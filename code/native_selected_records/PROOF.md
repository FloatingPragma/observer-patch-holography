# Native selection and complete observer records

## Integer endpoint selection

Let a finite connected graph have n≥2 vertices and m≥1 edges. At each attempt,
choose an edge uniformly and a fair bit. Replace its two integer loads by the
floor and ceiling of their mean, with their order set by the bit, and record
the oriented transferred amount j. The total Q and the initial minimum/maximum
box are preserved. For contrast d, the squared-load potential decreases by
(d²-r²)/2, where r is the parity of d. This is at least two if |d|≥2.

From an unbalanced state, choose a shortest path from a maximum to a load at
least two smaller. Its internal loads are maxima or one below. Appropriate
fair-bit outcomes propagate the maximum to the final edge in at most n-1
attempts, then strictly decrease the potential. The probability of this word
is at least (2m)^(-(n-1)). Every other attempt is nonincreasing. Consequently
the process almost surely reaches the invariant class whose entries are b or
b+1, where Q=nb+k and 0≤k<n. If Vmin=(n-k)b²+k(b+1)² and K=(Vinitial-Vmin)/2,
then E[tau]≤(n-1)K(2m)^(n-1). This bound is intentionally conservative.

The finite census contains 1,820 nonnegative twelve-port states of total at
most four, 109,200 actual endpoint branches, 794 binary states, no binary exit
and no state without a path into the binary class. A swap/hold alternative on
the same graph preserves doubles, so selection of the unit class is not an
identity of the integer alphabet. The alphabet's integrality itself receives
no emergence credit. A uniformly filled two-load graph is a fixed point;
the selection theorem gives adjacent levels for that preparation.

For |Q|≤n the selected loads are absolute 0/+1 or 0/−1. A public finite load
bound and sufficiently large initially empty reservoir realize that hypothesis
for arbitrary admitted loads in the input region. The zero reference is a
preparation datum; elimination of double occupancy is the dynamical result.
