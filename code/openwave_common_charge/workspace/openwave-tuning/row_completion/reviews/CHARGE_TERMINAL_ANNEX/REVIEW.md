## Actual source and the terminal channel

The actual source route updates its cell coordinate by plus or minus one
modulo two. The graph coordinates and all48 signed archived routes were
checked independently, including each path endpoint and actual seam.
Both signs on axis a therefore end at c xor 2^(2-a). This is special to
the declared size-two graph; the two signs end at different cells at
size three, retained as a rejecting control.

On the source's common Gauss-reduced field Hilbert space, each signed
Weyl transport is unitary. The spin projectors (I plus/minus alpha_a)/2
are orthogonal and complete, and act on a separate factor. Thus the
axis's two rail-dependent internal unitaries form a unitary block D_a,c.
Factoring out the common destination gives the axis as S_a D_a. The mass,
quadratic field and Coulomb operations are block diagonal in cell; their
noncommuting internal actions and all field modes remain in the source.
The full one-particle macro consequently has the exact form

    U = sum_c |c xor7><c| tensor V_c,

with unitary V_c, and a separate source clock tick. The source files
`interacting_gaussian/branches.py` and `spin/dirac_proposal.py` were read
at the pinned revisions. In particular the field word leaves `cells`
unchanged; its Coulomb phase is retained, rather than dropped as though
the interacting source were free.

For any terminal cell-diagonal instrument and any quantum reference,
the output cell trace removes off-diagonal input cell blocks. On each
surviving diagonal block, tracing the internal output removes conjugation
by V_c. This partial-trace identity also holds with initial correlations
among cell, internal field, clock and reference. The independent clock
instrument commutes with V_c, so the equality is an equality of the joint
record and retained-reference channel, not only of probabilities. The
same argument applies to the fine C/E terminal pointer records after
the cell-controlled Fourier meter: their effects remain cell diagonal.

The new mixed-input diagnostic retains a three-dimensional reference and
a correlated clock. It tests every one of256 cell subsets, both outcomes
of all three Pauli clock settings, and one/two macros:3072 reference
matrix comparisons, maximum discrepancy below1.13e-16. These finite
tests accompany the dimension-independent partial-trace proof; generic
internal rotations are not substituted for the physical field source.
If the internal output is retained, a fresh example differs by0.03245
in operator norm. Thus the stronger full-state equality is explicitly
rejected. Doubling the clock phase also fails a fresh Y-record control.

## Independent native replay and complete budgets

All36 archived preparation-prefix-meter tapes are replayed on all32 or64
input columns, including dirty probe and clock columns. The target
matrices are reconstructed from the integer Gauss flux
G_R minus n_R, the exact size-two cell permutation, each stated preparation
and the physical source clock. The pi-flux phase is evaluated as exact
integer parity in the target construction. No producer readout formula,
stored ideal matrix or executor determines that comparison.

For each raw pulse the review recomputes the coherent proper-code map
and every complement's positive first-export effect, preceded by all
earlier successes. Every stored basis-column failure and complete complex
failure effect is compared. The full Gram identity is checked. Native
pointer effects are independently reconstructed from literal endpoint
occupations, coin outcomes, integer current and spectator shell. All fine
successful C/E combinations and ordered first-pointer failures are
recomputed, with no success normalization. NATIVE_AUDIT.json records
the exact counts, maximum discrepancies and each experiment's bound.

For r regions, the reset count is3 cell registers, one clock, r probes
and2(r+1) pointer work registers, totaling6+3r. The source controller's
all78 input-column success probabilities are recomputed exactly. Every
archived fine successful reset and explicit timeout tape is replayed
using the integer event formula, and every seam/coin/column partial
injection is checked. A one-block timeout is at most35/2^31, hence two
blocks are bounded by its square uniformly on any subnormalized input
correlated with other registers or a reference. Sequential first failures
sum to at most(6+3r)(35/2^31)^2 without an independence assumption.

Successful private reset tapes are marginalized for comparison with an
ideal blank register and the original reference marginal. The complete
fine tapes remain in the reproducibility record; they are not equated
to the ideal instrument's absent private tape. All first timeout and
export outcomes remain failure outcomes, with their subnormalized mass.
This scope is necessary: retaining a fine reset record that resolves an
unknown input is a different output channel.

For every experiment the exact bound is the sum of all preparation,
permutation-prefix and meter pulse certificates, r+1 actual pointer-word
certificates, and the stated reset timeout bound. All36 sums are compared
as Fractions to the receipt. The maximum is approximately
3.18755438747e-15. Binary64 matrix residuals are numerical diagnostics;
they do not replace or numerically certify the much smaller individual
rational pulse bounds. The exact complete bound uses the verified source
certificate theorem and channel contract.

The clock tick is unchanged: each macro has physical time1/8 and phase
1/64. The ideal Heisenberg transfer adds zero field truncation or field
splitting error for this particular terminal channel. This does not remove
the errors of the original field implementation. A comparison between
the two native implementations requires both complete error bounds and
restriction to the common terminal record/reference output, marginalizing
private work records. No intermediate measurements, retained field state,
full native failure-tape equality or original huge-field execution is
covered.
