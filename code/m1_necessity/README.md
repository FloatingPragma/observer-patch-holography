# M1 necessity and explicit sparse alternatives

An explicit sparse family T preserves the named causal/count, scalar-field,
smooth free quantum, full thermal and leading planar crossing-entropy observables, with
one nat per unordered crossing pair and no fitted recalibration. A companion
lower bound proves that comparable thinning at the original radius cannot
retain the three coordinate-cut leading counts within symmetric
translation-invariant sub-stencils. T supplies a constructive escape by a
larger, still vanishing radius. These are completed constructions and
counterbounds, not a proposed physical replacement axiom.

## Results

* T has radius L*q^(-1/8), degree Theta(q^(9/8)) and fixed-horizon logical
  read count Theta(q^(17/4)). The dense comparison has radius L/sqrt(q),
  degree Theta(q^(3/2)) and read count Theta(q^5). T preserves the leading
  pi*q^4/(4L^2) crossing count per unit area in every fixed regular planar
  orientation. Its coarser radius converges more slowly; this is a fixed-q
  comparison, not equal-accuracy runtime or native cost savings.
* The sparse S stencil has at most 24 n^2+6 n+9 parents, with R=2^n.
  A charged constructive route gives the Euclidean light cone in the limit,
  including a boundary tube bound. Actual generated diamonds, strict pairs,
  ordering fraction 1/10 and retrospective count clocks have the same limits.
* Equal-cell masses admit the same positive continuous conformal weighting.
  This preserves the specified FLRW mass identities, not profile selection
  or a physical redshift law.
* S has a vanishing ratio of coordinate-cut crossings to the dense family.
  Adding every axial length gives A, whose diagonal/coordinate crossing
  density tends to sqrt(2), despite its round causal/count limit and exact
  cubic symmetry. One scalar entropy calibration cannot remove this bias.
* Exact moments define positive sparse scalar actions with O(a^2) spatial
  error and an explicit finite-time free-field/detector bound. Centered
  leapfrog at dt=a/c is unstable for the entire positive isotropic class,
  including the dense comparison. Exact semidiscrete evolution and the
  layered read program are different constructions.
* The same proofs work at q=R^k for every fixed integer k>=2. In particular,
  both a=L/sqrt(q) and a=L*q^(-2/3) give these limits. No successful cone or
  field convergence is assumed in choosing either schedule.
* The uniform-weight T action has m^3 modes approaching the mass frequency,
  including m^3-1 extra low-energy modes, despite agreeing on smooth probes. The completed action is the fixed positive
  half-sum of that action and the nearest-neighbor Laplacian, using existing
  reads. It preserves the same continuum normalization and cut statistics,
  and supplies uniform bounds on the entire spectrum.
* The completed actions have convergent full thermal partition functions,
  normal-ordered energies and Gibbs entropies. On the clipped box, ordered
  eigenvalues converge with multiplicity and obey a uniform j^(2/3)/(2L^2)
  lower bound. Both positive and zero mass are allowed there; a periodic
  oscillator vacuum requires positive mass.
* Smooth compactly supported Weyl observables and their causal commutators
  converge on the same clipped box. The limiting free quantum response cone
  agrees with the read/count cone, including observation at the declared
  ticks. This does not identify thermal Gibbs entropy with crossing entropy,
  assert an interacting theory, or execute a native quantum instrument.

The distinction between finite output and continuum observables matters:
deleting a one-layer parent changes the finite order and an independently
variable lossless tuple read. The sparse comparison is not a compiler for
the old complete-neighbor function.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, set `PYTHONPATH=code` and run:

```text
python -m m1_necessity.build
python -m m1_necessity.verify
python -m pytest -q code/m1_necessity
```

In the Lean project, `lake build Geometry.M1NecessityAxiomAudit` checks all
17 public reductions and rejects both `sorryAx` and compiler-trusted proof
axioms. The binary route subroutine and finite algebra are kernel checked;
the universal stencil, continuum and spectral arguments are analytic proofs.

The committed receipt is a compact candidate certificate. Verification
independently reconstructs the stencil with integer inequalities, executes
outgoing graph updates (the producer gathers incoming parents), recounts cut
pairs, checks moments and actual unstable Fourier modes, and executes every
route with charged repeats and a boundary-tube check. It does not import the
producer. Both Linux and Windows run the hostile controls in CI.

The reference contains 298 routes (including repeated catalog targets), 2215
charged route steps and six complete small graphs. The six baselines and
twelve initial/intermediate interventions execute 149760 event instances and
13479912 read incidences in total. These are software update counts, not
native twelve-port operations or physical events. The additive update is a
declared exact-integer probe of actual graph influence; it is not an asserted
physical law or a lossless tuple encoder. Full graph execution uses n=1,2;
cut and action certificates use nine levels through n=20 and the q=R^2
schedule. Larger cut counts are computed from exact finite formulas, not by
executing the astronomically large event population. Other k schedules are
covered by the analytic proof, not claimed as additional simulation runs.
T has an additional 161 checked routes and 815 charged steps, plus exact
column-summed cut and action certificates at t=1,2,3. The largest coarse
radius is 512; vertical columns are summed with integer formulas instead of
storing every offset. A miniature spaced-ball graph is checked by complete
pair enumeration, and the t=1 moments and cuts are also checked against its
fully expanded stencil. No full q^3 T history is claimed executed.
The spectral controls check all 4096 periodic alias modes of t=1, their
correction, and all 512 eigenvalues of a miniature zero-exterior action.
Universal spectral convergence and thermal domination are analytic proofs;
these finite controls do not stand in for those proofs.

The eight direct consumer rows and all 133 existing claims in their registered
dependency closure are bound by full-row hashes; the receipt also retains

Hashes provide custody, while independent computation supplies the semantic
check. No raw event archive or external storage service is needed.
