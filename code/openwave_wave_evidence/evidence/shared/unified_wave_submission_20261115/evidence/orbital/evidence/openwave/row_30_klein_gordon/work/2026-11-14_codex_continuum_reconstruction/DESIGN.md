components each use the same native endpoint event, a one-unit disclosed
uniform preparation, and globally IID weighted seam selection on a periodic
four-axis array of twelve-port graphs. Internal weight1 and external
weighta^(-2), a=1/m, are represented by repeated addresses. The only extra
events in the exact-Green version are independent native zero-load coin
records, with the normalization fixed by the native covariance identity.

The observer retains currents and the initial reference. Its internal
constant projections form the four-link readout whose nearest-neighbor curl
is F. Its nonconstant internal projections form massive scalar readouts.
The common reflection is vector on the constant sector, scalar on the
nonconstant sectors. Its record clock is the first hit of each next vertical
slice, with an unwrapped counter; the finite-cycle and large-depth comparison
is proved in `SOURCE_CLOCK_JOIN.md`. The same tape can be used for both the
clock and occupation observables. No independent clock dynamics is supplied.
Take the clock's origin to be the disclosed initial slice and its next plane
relative to that origin. This leaves the uniform occupation preparation
unchanged. The relative counter and orientation are declared memory, and
translation symmetry gives the same stopping law for every initial slice.

The finite graphs, rational test menus, moment degrees, tolerances and
doubling rule in `PROOF.md` specify the limiting construction. The exact
moment recurrence is computed from native transition records. Its
fixed-graph convergence proves termination; the covariance and Riemann
bounds give controlled volume and spacing limits. This is a fully specified
mathematical source family, not a claim that arbitrarily large resources
were used in the already retained finite experiments.

The photon and every scalar internal band use the same source graph,
rulers and reconstructed clock. Native eigenvalues determine the scalar
mass parameters. No measured mass, alpha or value of c is inserted. This
family gives the free-wave mathematical content; it does not assign the
observed particle species or derive their interactions.
