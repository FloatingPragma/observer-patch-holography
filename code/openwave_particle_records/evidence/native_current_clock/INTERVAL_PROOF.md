## Exact targets

The reflection has entries2/3 minus the identity on coordinates0,2,4 and
minus identity on their complement. Those entries are rational. The spin
axis is the difference of the two exact raw port vectors, divided by its

direction. Each raw component lies in Q(sqrt5), from the pinned source

## Removing interval wrapping

The first audit propagated rectangular matrix intervals through every
pulse. It obtained an enormous multiplex enclosure and failed its gates;
that complete attempt remains in attempts/interval01. A wide enclosure is
not evidence that the underlying matrix has a correspondingly large error.

The corrected method stores an exact dyadic center M and a rigorous
Frobenius error radius r, with ||U_previous−M||_F≤r. For the next ideal
binary-angle pulse G, the algorithm encloses G M by directed interval
arithmetic, then chooses an exact dyadic midpoint matrix M_next. The box
gives a fresh radius delta with ||G M−M_next||_F≤delta. Every ideal native
logical pulse is unitary, so

    ||G U_previous−M_next||_F
      ≤ ||G(U_previous−M)||_F+||G M−M_next||_F
      ≤ r+delta.

This is an additive proof, with no approximation to unitarity. It applies
to each exact ideal X/Y/Z/I/H pulse and the proper four-level J diagonal.
Their embedding on any declared register remains unitary. Integer square
roots round the accumulated squared enclosure errors upward. After the
last pulse, the exact target interval comparison is added to the retained
radius. Operator error is at most this Frobenius bound.
