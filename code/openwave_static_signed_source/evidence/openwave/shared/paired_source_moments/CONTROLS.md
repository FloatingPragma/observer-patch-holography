# Controls and non-empirical identities

All body controls use the same118 successfully read/restored paired inputs and
the same sixty edge/coin branches. Their altered body laws do not alter census
or source selection. Every zero-current branch is retained.

Frozen body records jX=jY=0. It erases both moments and fails the nonzero
source-axis predicate in118/118 cases. Deterministic exchange only on seam
(0,5) produces a nonzero mean in40/118 cases. At p0 it has jump vector
(1,1-phi,phi), whose covariance contains mixed z/xy entries. For the fixed
proper half-turn R=diag(-1,-1,1), R C R^T differs from C. It fails the source-axis
predicate for all118 inputs, including the78 zero-mean cases. The claim is not
that every fixed-seam input has a nonzero tensor.

The planted vector law has two equiprobable vectors (1,1,1) and (0,0,0).
Its sign-reversed copy has the same covariance. Both go through the same moment
accumulator and geometric detector as native records. This detector reports
tensor equality true and geometric equivariance false, detecting the desired
failure instead of accepting a hard-coded expected Boolean.

Swapping X and Y negates k under any body law applied identically to both.
Odd mean and even covariance under that exchange alone are identities and
supply no evidence for native geometric covariance. R^2=I is also a geometry
identity. The source-axis alignment, nonzero anisotropic covariance, measured
coefficients and fixed-R covariance are law-sensitive calculations. The frozen
and fixed-seam controls show that the apparatus can fail those calculations.

The pooled-source control erases preparation orientation using one complete
native branch law per measured axis. It reads zero mean and2I/9 covariance.
The experiment therefore does not infer a spontaneously selected or persistent
axis from its oriented preparations. A parity-even second moment by itself
is not a physical spin-1/2 angular momentum or central-phase measurement.
