## Which quantities belong to the consensus spacetime?

At resolution \(r\), the observers identify presentations of the same event through recorded emission, receipt, coincidence and overlap agreements. The physical quotient \(Q_r\) uses these identifications. Equal numbers on two unconnected devices do not establish a common event. A scalar observable must take the same value for every presentation of its quotient record. Conversely, such an invariant defines a scalar on the quotient. Tensor components transform between the observers' calibrated frames. A finite sampling interval expresses uncertainty about an observable. Overlapping intervals do not define an equivalence relation.

Observers exchange signals to set a time unit, a spatial scale and a common signal cone. In an inertial radar chart with a calibrated isotropic signal speed \(c\), an echo sent and received on one hand assigns the reflection event time \((\tau_++\tau_-)/2\) and radar distance \(c(\tau_+-\tau_-)/2\). Reciprocal ranging and checks with other clocks or probes establish its domain. Fitting a metric, associating a clock phase with proper time, converting spatial frequency to momentum and reconstructing proper volumes all require measurable agreements. A bare quotient supplies none of those calibrations. A matter candidate is a repeatably preparable and detectable propagating excitation with a common dispersion branch, identifiable under changes of reader, motion and resolution. Stronger species assignments such as a baryon require the corresponding charge, spin, stability and response records.

## Active mass without input radius labels

The spherical published-records comparison supplies pressureless source matter, a radial flow law and a common relativistic clock and ray action. Each stationary mirror records the arrival spacing of two pulses from the inner emitter, which also records a round-trip proper duration \(D_j\). Let \(y_j\) be the squared ratio of received to emitted spacings. With \(x=1-2\mu/R_0\), propagation in the stipulated exterior gives
\[
 R_j=\frac{2\mu}{1-xy_j},\qquad
 D_j=4\mu\sqrt{x}\,I(x,y_j),\qquad
 I(x,y)=\int_1^y \frac{du}{u(1-xu)^2}.
\]
The emitter has \(y_0=1\). Distinct positive lapse gradients give \(1<y_1<y_2\). Throughout \(0<x<1/y_2\), \(I(x,y_2)/I(x,y_1)\) is strictly increasing. For \(x'>x\), the ratio of the two integrands is strictly increasing in \(u\), so its average over \([y_1,y_2]\) exceeds its average over \([1,y_1]\). The ratio starts at \(\log y_2/\log y_1\) and diverges at the upper endpoint. A resolved duration ratio in that range fixes \(x\); either echo then fixes \(\mu\) and the three areal radii. With only one mirror a continuum of positive masses and radii gives the exact same two readings. On the ordinary source, 0.05204 and 0.11954 are two examples.
