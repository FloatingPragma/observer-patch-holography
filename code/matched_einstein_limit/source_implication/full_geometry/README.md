The ten separations of the five calibration stations admit a spherical realization. If the inverse curvature radius is \(z\), their spherical Gram has entries \(\cos(z d_{ij})\). Between \(z=0.6221\) and \(0.6224\) its determinant changes sign while its leading four minors stay positive. At a zero it is positive semidefinite of rank four, and all pairwise angles lie below \(\pi\). All off-diagonal entries are negative. Perron–Frobenius applied to the identity minus this Gram gives a strictly positive vector in its kernel. The five points therefore surround the origin of the embedding and cannot lie in a closed hemisphere. The sixth station tests the fitted curvature without changing the first five gains. The six-point Gram has uniform-direction Rayleigh quotient between −0.315 and −0.309 throughout the fitted bracket. A spherical Gram cannot be negative in any direction.

The exclusion covers every curvature in a common three-dimensional space form:

* **Positive curvature.** Compare the six-point cosine Gram with the matrix having all off-diagonal distances equal to three. On the centered subspace the comparison matrix is \((1-\cos 3z)I\). The maximum absolute row error is bounded by \((z^2/2)\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}|d_{ij}^2-9|\). For \(0<z\le0.65\), the quadratic and quartic Taylor terms for the cosine give a lower eigenvalue exceeding \(1.93z^2\) on this five-dimensional subspace. A spherical three-space has Gram rank at most four. From \(z=0.65\) to the maximum possible \(\pi/\max d_{ij}\), every off-diagonal cosine is nonincreasing and the uniform Rayleigh quotient is already below −0.70 at 0.65. Larger inverse radii violate the spherical diameter.
* **Flat and weak variable curvature.** Write \(J=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/5\). On the centered five-station subspace \(-JD^2J/2\) has all four eigenvalues greater than 3.49. Exact rational principal minors and an interval row-sum bound verify this inequality. Any Euclidean three-dimensional fit must alter some squared distance by more than 1.745, since an entrywise error \(e\) changes this centered Gram by at most \(2e\) in operator norm. Suppose the five stations are in a strongly convex normal three-ball of radius \(R\) where \(\sup |\mathrm{sec}|R^2\le1/2\). Normal coordinates have length distortion between \(11/12\) and \(12/11\), by radial isometry and the sine and hyperbolic-sine bounds for transverse Jacobi fields. Straight segments in the tangent ball and minimizing geodesics inside the ball give the same bounds on the distances relative to their tangent-space chords. The product of curvature and radius squared is dimensionless. This excludes the measured shape on any smooth small-ball sequence **that retains this normalized distortion**. For \(0<z\le0.2\) the centered five-point squared distance bound and the fourth-order cosh remainder give four negative directions.
