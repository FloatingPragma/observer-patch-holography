# How much space do six radar clocks enclose?

On the 80-cell Interval Echo equilibrium, six observers can represent all fifteen calibrated separations as shortest distances in a common smooth three-dimensional space. This includes the tetrahedron used in the finite entropy–area balance. The same station distances admit different fourth-order area responses on balls about an enrolled observer. These are conclusions about what the finite clock data determine. ## The measured clock error before projection

The ports in acquisition order are 0, 1, 38, 39, 5 and 6. The heads are prepared in the selected full-algebra reference. The mobile carries a fair choice of the two load charges. On each directed route, the sender records the first successful relay and return under the original half-unit inspection schedule. Let $r_{ij}$ denote the mean of half the return time, including all missed returns at the cap of three range units. There are thirty such means. The clock record is stochastic and includes propagation, detection and timeout.

In a stationary metric interpretation with fixed stations, scalar clock rates $N_i$ and instantaneous reflection, the elapsed coordinate time of a round trip is the sum of the two one-way times, the same sum in either order. Thus any common, time-independent reciprocal range predicts $r_{ij}=N_i d_{ij}$ for some symmetric $d$. Write $z_{ij}=\log(r_{ij}/r_{ji})$. The smallest possible worst absolute log error, allowing every positive station rate, is
\[
 \delta_*=\tfrac12\max_C |C|^{-1}\sum_{ij\in C}z_{ij}.
\]
For a given $a_i=\log N_i$, the best $\log d_{ij}$ is the average of $\log r_{ij}-a_i$ and $\log r_{ji}-a_j$. This leaves half the absolute mismatch $|z_{ij}-a_i+a_j|$. Summing these mismatches with their signs on any directed cycle gives the lower bound. Conversely the constraints $a_j\le a_i-z_{ij}+2\delta$ are feasible exactly when the directed costs $2\delta-z_{ij}$ have no negative cycle. Shortest-path distances from an auxiliary source provide potentials. The reversed edges give the other sign. It suffices to enumerate simple cycles because every closed walk decomposes into simple cycles.

The exact native means give $\delta_*$ between **0.003729 and 0.003734**, about 0.374% multiplicative error. A maximizing witness uses station indices $0,1,3,2$. The calibration allows a different clock at each observer and any symmetric range. Fixing $a_0=0$, the constraints against station zero also bound all five remaining potentials at the optimum. For the pairwise midpoint distances corresponding to each optimal choice of clock rates the centered Euclidean Gram is positive definite on the five-dimensional centered subspace, with lower eigenvalue above 2.9863 in squared range units. To see this, compare with the distance simplex having off-diagonal squared length nine. After centering its Gram is $9I/2$ on that subspace. The operator error is at most half the maximum absolute row sum of the difference of the two squared-distance matrices. This proves that the reciprocal optimum admits a metric realization. It does not produce an exact stationary scalar-clock description of the directed observations. [Clock comparison and acquisition](readout.py)

For the volume comparison keep the **four original survey gains** when adding two receivers. At each preparation use $a_i=\sum_{j<4}z_{ij}/4$ on the anchors and $a_i=\sum_{j<4}(z_{ij}+a_j)/4$ for either added receiver. The corrected squared length is $d_{ij}^2=r_{ij}r_{ji}\exp(-a_i-a_j)$. ## Smooth three-dimensional spaces with the observed lengths

The calibrated separations lie strictly between $13/5$ and $31/10$. The following explicit construction gives the station distances the meaning of intrinsic proper lengths. It is an interpolation of the data, with its chambers and tubes supplied by the construction.

Use six identical scaled round three-spheres, one per station. In each place five outlet centers evenly around a great circle and put the station at a point orthogonal to that circle in the unit-sphere embedding. Remove open geodesic balls of angular radius $\rho=\pi/20$ about the five outlets. On each remaining annulus $\rho\le u\le2\rho$ make the round angular radius constant near the boundary, keeping the radial coefficient fixed. For example replace $\sin u$ by $\sin(u+\chi(u)(2\rho-u))$, where the smooth cutoff is one near $\rho$, zero near $2\rho$, and lies in $[0,1]$. This increases lengths pointwise and gives a round product collar. The old radial arc to each outlet avoids the other annuli and keeps its length. Scale the sphere so that the station's distance to each outlet sphere is $c=1/10$. In each case the minimizing arc reaches a specified point perpendicularly.

Join each pair of chambers by a round product cylinder of length $d_{ij}-2c$, matching those two specified points at the same angular position. The product collars give a smooth closed three-manifold. The radial arcs and the axial cylinder path give distance at most $d_{ij}$. A path between stations must traverse at least one entire cylinder. Two full traversals cost more than $2(13/5-2c)=24/5>31/10$, even before any chamber length is counted. If there is just one full traversal it joins the required pair. The initial and final portions each cost at least $c$, since the path must reach its first boundary and leave its last boundary. The total therefore costs at least $d_{ij}$. All fifteen shortest distances are exact simultaneously. The argument holds on a neighborhood of source preparations because all the bounds are strict. For the minimax clock projection the same chamber argument works with any sufficiently small $c$ whenever $\max d_{ij}<2\min d_{ij}$, as verified by the interval bounds.

The same separations can even live on a smooth spatial **three-sphere of prescribed topology**. Carry out the construction in two dimensions using six round two-spheres, five outlet circles per sphere, and fifteen product cylinders. Use orientation-compatible gluings. The closed orientable surface has Euler characteristic $6(2-5)=-18$ and genus ten. Embed it as the boundary of a regular neighborhood of a bouquet of ten circles in the three-sphere. Equip its two-sided collar with the product metric $dt^2+g_{\rm surface}$ to width two in each direction and extend smoothly over both complements using positive metrics matching the collar near its boundaries. Projection to the central surface shortens any path inside the collar. A path between stations leaving the collar costs at least four in the outward and returning portions, greater than every station separation. Thus every observed pair retains exactly its measured distance. This also works inside Euclidean space equipped with a variable metric that agrees with the Euclidean one sufficiently far away. Chamber interiors about distinct station centers are isometric in both constructions. The three-sphere topology alone gives no unique proper volume.

Take the product spacetime $-dt^2+g$ with this spatial metric $g$. Light travel between the stationary points has the specified reciprocal distances in the common clock unit. If each site is assigned rate $N_i$, multiplying the spacetime metric by a positive conformal factor equal to $N_i^2$ near that site preserves the optical paths and implements those endpoint rates. The calibrated tetrahedral volume is the observer's Euclidean comparison functional of six edge lengths. The reported data do not measure the actual volume of a tetrahedral subregion of the smooth chamber construction. [Geometry and exact length inequalities](smooth.json)

### A curvature response at the measured station

At the station in chamber zero, let $r$ be distance in a round normal ball strictly inside the chamber. Take a smooth radial cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near the station and zero before the chamber outlets. For $e\ge0$ change the spatial metric to
\[
 g_e=g+e\chi(r)r^2(g-dr^2).
\]
The tensor extends smoothly through the center since $r^2dr^2=\tfrac14d(r^2)\otimes d(r^2)$ and $r^2$ is smooth there. This deformation increases lengths, preserves every radial segment in the ball and therefore preserves the fifteen direct minimizing paths. For an arbitrary smooth completion, a sufficiently small ball avoids the chosen finite set of minimizing paths not containing the station, while paths through the station are radial there. The chamber construction explicitly provides this separation.

Write $K$ for the original sectional curvature of the station neighborhood and $r$ in the common spatial unit. Near the origin the angular radius of its deformed two-sphere is $f_e(r)=(r-Kr^3/6+O(r^5))\sqrt{1+er^2}$. Its area is $4\pi f_e(r)^2$ and its volume the radial integral of that area. Between two choices of $e$, the leading differences per unit change in $e$ are
\[
 \Delta A|_r=4\pi r^4+O(r^6),\qquad
 \Delta V|_r=\tfrac{4\pi}{5}r^5+O(r^7),\qquad
 \Delta A|_V=\tfrac{12\pi}{5}r^4+O(r^6),
\]
where the common target volume in the last formula is $4\pi r^3/3$. The target-volume radius shifts by $-r^3/5$ per unit $e$ to leading order. At the center every spatial direction has Ricci curvature $-2 f_e''/f_e$ in the $r\to0$ limit. Its change is exactly $-6$ per unit $e$, giving scalar-curvature change $-18$. In the ultrastatic extension, a null vector $(1,n)$ with $|n|=1$ therefore has $\Delta R_{kk}=-6\Delta e$. The same increments hold for an arbitrary smooth reference, including the anisotropic product collars in the prescribed-topology construction. Near the center all sphere areas acquire the exact factor $1+er^2$, while their leading area is $4\pi r^2$. In orthonormal normal coordinates the metric increment has quadratic term $e(|x|^2\delta_{ij}-x_i x_j)$. Its constant and first jets vanish, so the change of Ricci at the center equals the linearized Ricci of this quadratic tensor, exactly $-6e\delta_{ij}$. The signed ambiguity therefore persists on a smooth three-sphere. The choice of $e$ has units of inverse squared range. [Symbolic coefficients and replay](smooth.py)

The ambiguity is signed even if the reference, the complete finite material measurements and one additional **total proper volume** are held common across the proposed descriptions. Choose a smooth native preparation path through the selected state with parameter $s$. Vary the cylinder lengths with its observed $d_{ij}(s)$. Use positive reference amplitude $e_0$ at stations zero and one, with otherwise identical profiles. In one continuation set their amplitudes to $e_0+\lambda s$ and $e_0-\lambda s$. In the other continuation reverse them. Take $|s|$ small enough that both are nonnegative. Both metrics reproduce the same station readings for every preparation and coincide at the reference. The spatial volume multiplier in either support is exactly $1+e\chi r^2$, because there are two rescaled transverse directions. The two integrals cancel **between continuations at each $s$**, including away from first order. The whole-space volume may depend on the common measured cylinder lengths. At station zero the fourth-order fixed-volume area slopes have opposite signs and the null Ricci slopes differ by $12\lambda$ in magnitude. Assigning the same measured head and flux records to both reconstructions leaves this geometric ambiguity intact. A native clock or interior count sensitive to the transverse proper ruler, or a dynamical law selecting that ruler, could distinguish the two. This is a finite inference result. A dense physical refinement supplying uniform small-ball control would require its own comparison.

### Directional null curvature with every small rest-ball volume matched

Even independent knowledge of the centered area and volume at **every sufficiently small radius in one rest frame** would leave a separate directional ambiguity. At a measured station in a round chamber, choose orthonormal normal coordinates $x$, with the first axis along a tagged radar segment. Let $B$ be a positive semidefinite symmetric matrix. Add the following tensor to the chamber metric, using a smooth nonnegative radial cutoff equal to one near zero and supported inside that chamber.
\[
 \delta g_x(v,w)=\chi(|x|)(x\times v)^{\mathsf T}B(x\times w).
\]
It is smooth, increases lengths, and vanishes on the radial tangent. All fifteen minimizing paths therefore retain their lengths. At the center its constant and first coordinate derivatives vanish. Contracting its quadratic derivatives gives the exact Ricci increment $3(B-\operatorname{tr}(B)I)$. The symbolic certificate checks this identity for all six independent entries of $B$. This map on symmetric tensors is invertible. A positive isotropic reference $B=bI$ permits any sufficiently small two-sided symmetric perturbation. In particular, for any desired even null curvature response $R'_{kk}=n^{\mathsf T}Mn$ set $B'=(M-\operatorname{tr}(M)I/2)/3$. This fits any chosen even stress slope and any chosen coupling, which shows why selecting a fit by itself supplies no evidence for the coupling.

Consider $B_+(s)=\operatorname{diag}(b+s,b-s,b)$ and $B_-(s)=\operatorname{diag}(b-s,b+s,b)$ for $|s|<b$. A quarter turn in the first two coordinates interchanges the two tensors in the round station neighborhood. The same quarter turn is a local isometry in the fixed three-sphere construction when its first two coordinates are tangent to the round station surface and its third follows the product collar. For each $s$ all sufficiently small centered balls in the two descriptions thus have exactly equal proper volumes and areas. The total proper volume is equal as well, since the perturbed support is round and both metrics agree outside it. The slope of $R_{kk}$ in the ultrastatic extension along the *same tagged ray* $k=(1,1,0,0)$ is $+3$ in the first continuation and $-3$ in the second. The station tags determine which direction is compared; rotating those tags would change the experiment. Scalar ball volumes in one frame cannot supply the directional contractions demanded by the tower. Independent angular measurements or other observer frames could distinguish these responses. ## A finite triangulation and a radius-one ball

A second, piecewise Euclidean representation makes the volume ambiguity directly calculable at finite radius. Positive definiteness of the centered Gram realizes the six distance vectors as the vertices of a nondegenerate Euclidean five-simplex. Split its six vertices into triples $P,Q$. Take every tetrahedron with two vertices in each triple and glue along common faces. This complex is the join of the two boundary triangles $S^1*S^1$, hence a triangulated three-sphere. For example the join sends $(u,v,t)$ to $(\cos(t)u,\sin(t)v)$ on the unit three-sphere for $u,v\in S^1$ and $0\le t\le\pi/2$. All edges are present. Every intrinsic path is at least as long as its ambient Euclidean chord and each edge realizes this lower bound. Six choices of the unordered partition retain the original four vertices as one tetrahedron. All six agree on its intrinsic Euclidean volume as well as all fifteen station separations.

For a vertex $i$, measure the volume of its intrinsic metric ball at radius $R\le1$. Every incident tetrahedron has altitude greater than one at each vertex. The ambient chord from $i$ to an opposite face therefore costs more than one, so these balls stay in the union of the incident tetrahedra. Each contributes its solid angle times $R^3/3$. For the Gram $G$ of three emanating edge vectors of lengths $l_1,l_2,l_3$, the solid angle is $2\operatorname{atan2}(\sqrt{\det G},l_1l_2l_3+l_1G_{23}+l_2G_{31}+l_3G_{12})$, the solid-angle formula for three unit rays. The certified interval arithmetic propagates all thirty native directed-clock uncertainties through gains, determinants and angles.

Use the partition in which the first triple contains indices $(0,1,5)$, with its complement as the other triple. At station zero the solid angle is [3.342461, 3.342769] steradians. For $(0,3,5)$ it is [3.394340, 3.394647]. The open gap between the intervals survives the simultaneous count uncertainty. The two total optical three-volumes are respectively [27.283009, 27.285634] and [26.944445, 26.947136] in cubic range units. Even a global rescaling cannot make all these local ball volumes agree, since the exact ratios of the solid angles at stations zero and one also have disjoint enclosures. These metric cones describe singular comparison spaces. The smooth construction above shows why removing cone singularities still leaves the fourth-order response undetermined.
