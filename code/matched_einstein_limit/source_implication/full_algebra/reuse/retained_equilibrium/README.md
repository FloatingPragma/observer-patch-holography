# Maintaining the entropy–area equilibrium on the same two head memories

Two observers can retain their original head memories after every four-station radar survey and maintain the already identified finite entropy–area equilibrium. They need one bit from the flight record in addition to the planned route, charge and measured reversal sector. If they erase that bit, **no lottery of two-head unitaries** controlled by the remaining settings can maintain the target. Keeping it permits an attracting controller made from native contact and rail rotations. Its convergence factor is at most 7/8 per cycle on the prescribed reversal mixture. ## What the observers read

Use the 80-cell board, twelve directed station routes, two equally probable probe charges, and the twelve half-ticks of the original survey. Choose the route and charge uniformly before each cycle. The sender saves one bit indicating whether the mobile completed its outward and return detections **strictly before tick 12**. A last-tick return, either timeout and every other unsuccessful trial stay in the complement. Remove the mobile and keep both head coins. Each sector starts with the preassigned probability of the original equilibrium. Separate ensembles or records of the two sectors are required to recover this mixture. Let $X$ be one unnormalized reversal block of the calibrated reference and $Y_h=\Phi_h(X)$ the unnormalized outgoing block for a readout $h$, including the probability of its route and charge. Write $s_k(Y)$ for the sum of the largest $k$ eigenvalues. The target can be restored by randomized, record-conditioned unitaries on the retained heads **if and only if**
\[
s_k(X)\le\sum_h s_k(Y_h),\qquad k=1,2,3.
\]
The total traces already agree. Necessity follows because a rank-$k$ projection extracts at most $s_k$ from each block. For sufficiency, rotate every output eigenbasis to one common basis, arranging eigenvalues in decreasing order. Their sum now has the decreasing eigenvalue vector $v=\sum_h\lambda(Y_h)$. The inequalities are exactly the majorization of $\lambda(X)$ by $v$. The convex hull of all permutations of $v$ is characterized by these inequalities: sorting any proposed separating linear functional shows its maximum is attained with both lists in the same order, and summation by parts uses just the first three partial sums. Thus a classical lottery over permutations produces the target in its own eigenbasis. The implementation solves the 24-permutation problem separately for each reversal.
