# Can a regional energy meter account for the retained survey response?

A material observer can keep the Hamiltonian energy of an entire twelve-cell region fixed for a vacancy probe. On the same incoming head source and at the same fixed radar volume, an independently launched excess probe then loses regional energy. The head entropy increases and the tetrahedral survey area decreases. Counting the energies of the parked heads is essential for determining the regional response.

Neither a change of radial weighting nor a contribution from the measured regional momentum inflow reconciles these energy readings with the ordinary-calibrated entropy response. The bounds allow a separate arbitrary weight from zero to one on every one of the 78 non-source cells of the federation. ## Computation and reproducibility

The native flight is evaluated on all 40 enrolled input columns in each of four charge/reversal sectors. The active integer matrix is $h=2H$ up to a scalar with absolute row sum below 64. [native.py](native.py) reuses the original degree-640 polynomial and rigorously bounded coefficients of $\exp(-2ih)$ expressed in $T_k(h/64)$; it keeps degrees through 180 and bounds the summed discarded coefficients, their rounding and the original Taylor tail. For a dyadic column grid $U=2^{55}$, each rounded Chebyshev recurrence injects Euclidean error at most $\lceil\sqrt{3840}\rceil/U$. Since the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind of degree $j$ has norm at most $j+1$ on $[-1,1]$, the resulting error in term $k$ is bounded by $k(k+1)\lceil\sqrt{3840}\rceil/(2U)$. Exact signed integer limbs evaluate each coefficient product. Coefficient conversion, accumulation and the output grid give final complex column error below $3\times10^{-10}$. The replay encloses $M$ separately from the integer columns. Thus error from a barely occupied distant cell reflects its small acceptance.
