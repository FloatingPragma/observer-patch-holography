## What four stations determine about interior curvature

For **every tetrahedron certified by the native survey**, there is a one-parameter family of smooth-in-parameter, three-times continuously differentiable ultrastatic metrics with the same six exact station distances, the same metric and clock rates near the entire boundary, and the same total proper spatial volume, whose interior null Ricci response varies. This is a geometric non-identifiability theorem about the measured records. It leaves the original flat-simplex volume estimator fully specified. [Exact algebra and small-ball check](metric_interiors.py)

To construct the family, first use the Euclidean comparison tetrahedron. Its six edges have length at most 3, each triangular face has area at most $9\sqrt3/4$, and the certified volume exceeds 1.57. Its inradius $3V/A$ therefore exceeds 0.30. Take one ball of radius $a=1/16$ about the incenter $x_1$, and a second of the same radius at distance $3a$ from $x_1$. Both are strictly inside. Let $b_i=u_i^2(1-u_i^2)^4$ for $u_i=|x-x_i|/a<1$, and zero elsewhere. These nonnegative profiles have disjoint supports and the same integral. Set $c=3a^2/8$ and, for $|s|<1$, define
$$
 w_s=cs(b_1-b_2),\qquad
 ds_s^2=-dt^2+(1+w_s)^{2/3}(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2).
$$
The supports have clearance $d>1/20$ from every face plane, hence from each edge line. Any curve from one edge endpoint to the other through a support has Euclidean length at least $\sqrt{L^2+4d^2}$ by reflection or the triangle inequality, where $L\le3$ is that edge's length. Its proper length is at least $(1-c)^{1/3}$ times this. The exact inequality $(1-c)^2(1+4(1/20)^2/9)^3>1$ shows it cannot be a shortcut. Every other curve retains its Euclidean length. Consequently all first-arrival station times, boundary clocks and the six straight-edge distances agree. The proper volume density is exactly $1+w_s$. The two bump integrals cancel, preserving even the original flat tetrahedron's total proper volume at every $s$.

At $x_1$ every member even has the same metric and vanishing first derivatives in the same coordinates. The spatial Ricci tensor there equals $-sI$ in native inverse squared range units. For any equally normalized $k=(1,n)$ with $|n|=1$, $R_{ab}k^ak^b= -s$. Relabeling $s$ by $-s$ reverses its response while preserving every stated survey measurement. A separate measurement on nested small proper balls about $x_1$ can distinguish the possibilities. Their volume as a function of measured proper radius $R$ obeys
$$
 \frac{V_s(B_R(x_1))}{4\pi R^3/3}=1+\frac{s}{10}R^2+O(R^4).
$$
This coefficient also follows directly by integrating the radial ruler and the shell volume, without using the Ricci calculation. The construction does not claim these metrics solve the native dynamics. To select a macroscopic metric, a theorem about those dynamics or independently calibrated interior measurements must supply information beyond this survey.

There is a refinement consequence if only length and total-volume errors are controlled. In a fixed bounded three-dimensional chart choose an interior point outside the countably many infinite lines through all measured pairs of a sequence of finite surveys. At each level this point has positive clearance from the finitely many lines enrolled so far. Choose $a$ small enough that the two supports fit inside that clearance and that the same strict detour bound holds for the longest edge at this level. This is always possible because $c=3a^2/8$ tends to zero and the Euclidean detour at positive clearance is strictly positive. With $a$ tending to zero, the metric perturbation is uniformly $O(a^2)$ and its first derivative is $O(a)$. All enrolled edge lengths and the full region's proper volume are exactly their flat values. For fixed nonzero $s$, its null Ricci remains $-s$ at this same point, while the limiting flat metric has zero null Ricci. The sequence does not have uniform second-derivative convergence. A physical refinement theorem must supply the required control of curvature or the matched entropy and area remainders. These are alternative continuum completions of the finite observations.
