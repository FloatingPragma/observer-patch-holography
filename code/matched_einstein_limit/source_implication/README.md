# Regional energy at constant density

A gravity test on smooth sources can use the regional response to a uniform energy-density change. It also needs a bound on how far the modular instrument samples and a bound on responses to changes that its proposed stress instrument misses. Two preparations of one thermal Interval Echo cell change the energy on every seam by the same amount. Their regional entropy derivatives differ by 0.149669 nats per unit preparation parameter. This tests a constant spatial energy profile throughout the cell, including the boundary of the observed region. [Uniform-energy experiment](regional_moments.py) · [Finite error bound](../../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/RegionalMoments.lean)

## What suffices for a small ball

Fix one properly calibrated observer, radius, reference and source tangent. Let $s$ denote the regional modular response, $t_i$ the stress in that observer's rest frame at sampling sites, and $t_0$ the central stress. Independent measures of geometry and stress decide the sampling locations. The signed weights $w_i$ include the volume and clock factors. Define the bounds

$$
 |s-\textstyle\sum_i w_i t_i|\le e,\qquad
 |\textstyle\sum_i w_i-m|\le d,\qquad
 \textstyle\sum_i |w_i|\le L,\qquad |t_i-t_0|\le\omega .
$$

On the admitted family $e$ includes responses to variations with identical measured $t_i$. Subtraction of $t_0$ inside the sum gives

$$
 |s-mt_0|\le e+L\omega+d|t_0|.
$$

For the Einstein small-ball theorem the target mass of the weights is $m=(8\pi^2/15)\ell^4$. If $e,d=o(\ell^4)$, $L=O(\ell^4)$, $\omega=o(1)$, and $|t_0|$ is bounded uniformly in the ten observers and admitted variations, this supplies its bulk bound at the required order. The shape of the weight at interior points can differ while giving this same leading response on smooth stress. Rapidly varying or singular sources need separate estimates. Recovering the full local conformal modular flow demands more information than this scalar leading moment. [Tensor estimate](../../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/QuantitativeSmallBall.lean)

If the readout has uniform positive response $\beta_{\rm eff}$ per physical energy and the ball volume is $4\pi\ell^3/3+o(\ell^3)$, the required moment gives $\beta_{\rm eff}/\ell\to2\pi/5$ in natural units. An inverse temperature held fixed in proper units cannot supply this gain through a shrinking family. The gain must be recovered on restrictions of the same reference. Choosing temperatures separately at each radius fixes it by construction.

## A uniform energy test on the native cell

Take the faithful, half-filled grand Gibbs state at inverse temperature $1/16$ of the 12-port blank binary cell, with the degree-five reference terms included. Retain the eight ports consisting of the enrolled seam (0,5) and all neighbours of either end. The region contains 15 internal seams. All 4,096 binary words contribute to its reduced density. Define symmetric and antisymmetric one-load states on a seam. One transfer replaces the symmetric state by the antisymmetric state. A second replaces an equal mixture of 00 and 11 by the symmetric state. The native energy changes are one and $\kappa$ respectively. Both one-port partial traces of each transfer vanish. Divide the second transfer by $\kappa$ to normalize the energies. [Native regional state and central transfer](../../../model/experiments/seam_program/collective_rate/README.md)

For each of the two transfers, sum its placement on all 30 cell seams, tensoring with the normalized spectator identity at each placement. Thus every seam energy has derivative exactly one for both summed preparations. Each term on a different seam has at least one traced-out endpoint. Every site reference energy has zero derivative. The full tangent has zero trace and a sufficiently small two-sided perturbation of the faithful reference is a density matrix. This specifies source-state variations, with no native preparation circuit asserted for these density matrices. Restriction to the eight ports removes the fifteen transfers with at least one exterior end. Diagonalizing the regional density gives the following predictions for its entropy derivative.

### Why a homogeneous source can fail on a finite region

At half filling the grand Hamiltonian, up to a scalar, is the sum of edge terms $-[XX+YY+(1+2\kappa)ZZ]/4$. Let $E$ denote the normalized partial trace over the exterior of the region. Expanding the regional logarithm near $\beta=0$ gives

$$
 K_R={\rm const}+\beta E(H)-\frac{\beta^2}{2}
 \big[E(H^2)-E(H)^2\big]+O(\beta^3).
$$

Products of two internal edges cancel from the second-order difference. A pair of edges meeting at an exterior vertex contributes to the interaction of its two interior neighbours. The normalized trace of distinct Pauli matrices at that vertex is zero. The two orders of the matching Pauli factors give $-\beta^2[XX+YY+(1+2\kappa)^2 ZZ]/16$ for each such pair. Terms with no source endpoint or just one vanish against either fixed-marginal tangent. Correlations induced between two interior ports that do not share a native seam also vanish against the summed transfers.

For the unit-energy swap, the changes in $XX+YY$ and $ZZ$ are $-4$ and zero. For the normalized aligned transfer they are $2/\kappa$ and $-2/\kappa$. Their difference in regional modular response therefore equals $b(1+2\kappa)\beta^2/4+O(\beta^3)$, with $b$ the number of triangles containing an internal seam and an exterior common neighbour. The eight-port region has $b=6$. At the active strength the coefficient is $25.5$. Analyticity around the finite full-rank reference gives strict inequality throughout a sufficiently small interval of positive inverse temperatures. The numerical value of the difference divided by $\beta^2$ at $1/4096$ is $25.56839$. The nonzero second-order term is a boundary contribution for the smooth, uniform seam-energy variation on this graph. A cofinal three-dimensional construction has a concrete way to suppress an unresolved term of this type. If it is confined to a layer of proper thickness $h$ and volume at most $C\ell^2h$, and its absolute modular response per unit proper volume is at most $C'\ell$ on admitted variations, its total error is at most $CC'\ell^3h$. This is $o(\ell^4)$ when $h/\ell\to0$. Those geometric and locality bounds require a common native volume, radius and source family. Conversely, a signed unresolved response bounded below by $c\ell$ per unit volume throughout a core of volume at least $c'\ell^3$, with the same sign elsewhere or an outside contribution $o(\ell^4)$, leaves a normalized error bounded away from zero. A thickness comparable with the ball radius gives no suppression from this argument. The single-cell measurement distinguishes the source preparations but supplies no cofinal estimate in either direction.

### Which globally conserved energy these transfers could represent

Consider assigning different constant weights to exchange and contrast on the closed cell, $Q=aX+bC$, with native generator $H=X+\kappa C$. Their commutator is $[H,Q]=(b-\kappa a)[X,C]$. On two binary loads, the sharp port words $(0,1)$ and $(0,8)$ have diagonal contrast energies 10 and 12, with exchange matrix element $-1/2$ between them. Thus $[X,C]_{(0,1),(0,8)}=-1$ exactly. Global conservation on every state forces $b=\kappa a$. Adding a scalar or a multiple of conserved load number leaves this conclusion intact. A phase-coherent combination of the two words distinguishes a nonzero commutator in the Heisenberg time derivative.

The difference between the uniform transfers has zero expectation against this entire class of conserved energies and nonzero regional modular derivative. Boundary fluxes can change a regional energy. Assigning their effect to an extra conserved global stress would require operators beyond this two-term class or a justified coarse-domain conservation law.

## A signed boundary error at every inverse temperature

Partition a finite screen into a core $A$, a buffer $B$ and its exterior $C$, covering the whole source. All four regions below use marginals of the same faithful reference $\sigma$ and the same traceless Hermitian tangent $D$. Write $k_X=-\operatorname{Tr}D_X\log\sigma_X$. The reference conditional mutual information and its first variation are
$$
 F=S_{AB}+S_{BC}-S_B-S_{ABC},\qquad
 L=k_{AB}+k_{BC}-k_B-k_{ABC}.
$$
Strong subadditivity gives $F\ge0$. If both $\rho_\pm=\sigma\pm tD$ are positive for $t>0$, write $d_X^\pm=D_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho_{\pm,X}\Vert\sigma_X)$. The exact finite entropy difference on each region gives
$$
 0\le I_{\rho_\pm}(A:C\mid B)=F\pm tL+d_{ABC}^\pm+d_B^\pm-d_{AB}^\pm-d_{BC}^\pm
 \le F\pm tL+d_{ABC}^\pm .
$$
The last inequality uses monotonicity under tracing out $C$ from $BC$ and positivity of $d_{AB}^\pm$. Thus $|L|\le(F+\max_\pm d_{ABC}^\pm)/t$. One may also use $q=\operatorname{Tr}(D\sigma^{-1}D)$. In eigenbases with eigenvalues $p_i,s_j$ of $\rho,\sigma$, average $\log(p_i/s_j)$ with weights $p_i|\langle i|j\rangle|^2$. Jensen's inequality gives $D_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho\Vert\sigma)\le\log\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^2\sigma^{-1})$. For either arm the trace equals $1+t^2q$. Hence
$$
 |L|\le F/t+tq.
$$
Put $c=\|\sigma^{-1/2}D\sigma^{-1/2}\|_\infty$. The two arms are feasible when $tc\le1$. Writing $D=\sigma^{1/2}Y\sigma^{1/2}$ gives $q=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma Y^2\le c^2$. For $0<F\le1$ choose $t=\sqrt F/c$ when $D\ne0$. Then $|L|\le2c\sqrt F$. For $F=0$ take $t\downarrow0$ to obtain exact equality $L=0$. These are finite-dimensional inequalities for any source and temperature. The Lean lemma checks the two-sign scalar reduction. The relative-entropy argument is given above. [Scalar bounds](../../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/RegionalMoments.lean)

For changes with $D_{BC}=0$, the signed error is exactly $k_{AB}-k_{ABC}$. For a grand Gibbs reference the latter full response equals $\beta\,\delta\langle H-\mu N\rangle$. For a changing bath, $k_{BC}-k_B$ must also be read. The condition $c_\ell\sqrt{F_\ell}=o(\ell^4)$ uniformly over the admitted transfers suffices for this part of the regional error. It requires both the information and the size of the physical perturbation relative to the reference; a nearly pure reference can make $c_\ell$ large. In spatial dimension at least two the finite-temperature bound in Kuwahara's Eq. (8) carries $\dim\mathcal H_{AC}$; it does not give this refinement rate from bounded contact degree alone. [Kuwahara, Eqs. (7)–(9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05835v3)

On the 12-port binary Interval Echo cell take $A=(0,5)$, half filling, contrast 8 and $\beta=1/16$. All 4096 basis words contribute. Two transfers keep $BC$ fixed. Exchange changes native energy by one. A four-port parity variation keeps every pair marginal fixed and changes native energy by zero. A third test maximizes $k_{AB}$ over diagonal tangents of trace norm at most one that preserve the *complete* $BC$ marginal and every single- and two-port marginal of the cell. On $AB$ this is a linear program. Each row constrains a buffer word or a one- or two-site binary moment. The dual minimizes the maximum absolute pointwise error of an additive predictor from those rows. The primal and dual agree to numerical tolerance. Tensoring the witness with normalized identity on $C$ preserves the constraints, and faithfulness gives a positive preparation step in both directions. These are source-level preparation comparisons. ## Transport excludes exact thermal screening through an arbitrarily thick buffer

Let $A,B,C$ partition the **entire** finite binary carrier. Assume a connected component contains a port $a\in A$ and a port $c\in C$. For any finite $\beta\ne0$ and any chemical potential for total load, the faithful grand Gibbs state of the active binary hopping generator satisfies $I_\sigma(A:C\mid B)>0$.

To prove it, suppose equality held. The equality theorem for strong subadditivity splits $B$ into a direct sum of spaces $B_{Lj}\otimes B_{Rj}$, with the state on block $j$ equal to $p_j\sigma_{A L j}\otimes\sigma_{R j C}$. Faithfulness lets us take each logarithm. Assign $-\log p_j$ to the left side of each block. The result is $-\log\sigma=U_{AB}+V_{BC}$ with $[U_{AB},V_{BC}]=0$. Evolution of an exterior observable under this sum uses $V_{BC}$ alone and stays on $BC$ for every time. Since $-\log\sigma=\beta(H-\mu N)+\log Z$, the same holds under $H-\mu N$. In particular a sharp load on $a$ and a vacuum launch, which have identical $BC$ marginals, would give identical readings of the occupation on $c$ at every later exposure. [Hayden, Jozsa, Petz and Winter, Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304007)

The vacuum gives zero. In the one-load sector each hop along an edge has matrix element $-1/2$. At graph distance $d$ let $P_d$ count shortest paths from $a$ to $c$. Diagonal energies change neither the shortest order nor its coefficient. The transition amplitude has leading term $i^d P_d t^d/(2^d d!)$, so the occupied launch gives strictly positive probability at sufficiently small positive exposure. This is the contradiction. The path counts and matrix coefficients can be checked in integer arithmetic at graph distances 3, 14 and 32 on the 12-port cell and two spherical federations. At antipodal ports on the cell the magnitude of the cubic amplitude coefficient is $5/24$. The theorem does not replace a marginal's logarithm by the generator of a larger source; every traced spectator must be assigned to a member of the partition used in the proof. At $\beta=0$ the Gibbs state is a product. Removing all hopping leaves the classically shielded control above.

There is an explicit source variation realizing a *strictly signed* error at every such buffer. Replace the core with its marginal from an independent copy, preserving its own state and the complete $BC$ marginal. The resulting state is $\tau=\sigma_A\otimes\sigma_{BC}$. Mixing the replacement arm into the reference defines $D=\tau-\sigma$. Exact entropy differences and $I_\tau=0$ give
$$
 k_{AB}-\beta\,\delta\langle H-\mu N\rangle
 =-F-D_{\rm rel}(\tau\Vert\sigma)+D_{\rm rel}(\tau_{AB}\Vert\sigma_{AB})\le -F<0 .
$$
The inequality follows by data processing under tracing out $C$. For the three buffers in the table the signed errors are $-1.15441086$, $-0.28913097$ and $-0.00014718082$ nats per unit of replacement probability. The global response is $+1.15441086$ in all three cases. For this preparation, uniform $o(\ell^4)$ error necessarily implies $F_\ell=o(\ell^4)$ with the same source normalization. Its applicability to macroscopic smooth matter depends on whether such replacements are admitted, and on their normalization per proper volume. Strict positivity on each finite graph supplies no positive lower bound uniform under refinement. Exact screening is also a sufficient shortcut to one thermal energy estimate, not a premise of the Einstein theorem itself.
