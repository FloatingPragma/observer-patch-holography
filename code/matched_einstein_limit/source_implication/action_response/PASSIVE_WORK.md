The asymptotic passive energy is the energy of the Gibbs state of the same entropy. For this two-level spectrum it is $16+q$, where $0<q<1/2$ and $\log4+h(q)=S(\chi)$, with $h$ the binary entropy in nats. The present preparation lies in the interior entropy range $(\log4,\log8)$. Here is a direct derivation. At temperature $1/\beta$ with $\beta=\log[(1-q)/q]$, relative entropy to the product Gibbs state bounds the final energy of every entropy-preserving control from below by $n(16+q)$. To see attainability per copy, choose any $q'>q$ below $1/2$, and then $\delta>0$ such that $\log4+h(q')>S+\delta$. With probability tending to one, an input eigenword has negative log probability at most $n(S+\delta)$ by the law of large numbers. There are at most $e^{n(S+\delta)}$ such words. The output shell of $\lfloor nq'\rfloor$ excitations has $4^n {n\choose\lfloor nq'\rfloor}$ words and fits all of them for sufficiently large $n$ by Stirling's formula. A permutation places them there. The remaining probability costs at most one excitation per copy. Let $q'\downarrow q$ to obtain the upper bound. This specializes the general theorem of Alicki and Fannes. [Alicki and Fannes, Phys. Rev. E 87, 042123](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.87.042123)
