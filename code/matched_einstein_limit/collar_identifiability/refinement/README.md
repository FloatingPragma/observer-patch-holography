# A visible collar under spatial refinement

A comparison bit made from two material round trips measures a signed modular response with no background subtraction. Weakening correlations gives a positive fourth-order term if their admission probability scales as the square of the proper radius. A bounded budget of randomly placed marks cannot achieve this throughout a three-dimensional volume. A growing bank can. A separate finite transport experiment identifies dynamics giving its radial profile on a supplied spatial chart. The two experiments do not supply a shared native metric.

## A material comparison with a balanced reference

The [formed collar](../material_algebra.py) uses an excess on an 80-cell federation to prepare correlated heads. Compare with independently prepared heads of the same one-head states. On the excess probe at two native units per leg, let \(p\) and \(q\) denote the three returned probability bins for formed and independent heads. With the admission bit hidden the register sees \(q_f=q+fd,\ d=p-q\). Strengthening pairing has tangent \(fd\). Its raw modular slope equals
\[
 -f d\cdot\log q_f=-f d\cdot\log q-f^2\sum_i d_i^2/q_i+O(f^3).
\]
The linear and quadratic coefficients on the complete read are \(-0.05870405\) and \(-0.00515505\) nats. Subtracting the independently evaluated reference generator cancels the linear term. This subtraction needs its own identification with the bulk observable.

A different terminal register avoids this issue. Run the tested return and a separate independent-head return. Report one if the tested completion bit exceeds the reference bit, zero if the order is reversed, and use a fresh fair rail outcome on a tie. The fair bit is a unit load on a blank seam after a half exchange and a sharp port read. Keep only the comparison bit. With \(z=p_{\rm complete}-q_{\rm complete}\), its yes probability is exactly \((1+fz)/2\), including every trial. This balances the independent reference for any \(q_{\rm complete}\). It uses no fitted threshold. In the weakening direction the fixed-reference modular first variation on this actual bit is
\[
 Q(f)=fz\,\operatorname{atanh}(fz),\qquad
 0\le Q(f)-f^2z^2\le (fz)^4/[3(1-(fz)^2)] .
\]
The bound follows by summing the power series on \(|fz|<1\). At two units, \(z=-0.01437036554\) and \(z^2=0.00020650741\). Switching off contrast in the return Hamiltonian at the same source reduces \(|z|\) below \(10^{-13}\) on every tested appointment. A strengthening tangent reverses the sign. Keeping both input transcripts or the admission flag defines a different output algebra. [Operator replay and admissions](admission.py)

The full two-head reference \(\sigma\) also has a measurement-dependent information loss. Let \(D\) be the formed state minus the independent state. A single-copy instrument with a classical output has effects \(M_i\), \(q_i={\rm Tr}\,\sigma M_i\) and \(d_i={\rm Tr}\,D M_i\). Write \(D=(L\sigma+\sigma L)/2\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(d_i^2/q_i\le {\rm Tr}\,\sigma L M_i L\). The spectral measurement of \(L\) attains equality after summing, fixing the greatest possible classical quadratic coefficient. The coefficient for the full quantum logarithm weights \(|D_{jk}|^2\) in the eigenbasis of \(\sigma\) by \((\log s_j-\log s_k)/(s_j-s_k)\), with diagonal value \(1/s_j\). The classical maximum uses \(2/(s_j+s_k)\). The former is strictly greater for unequal eigenvalues, by convexity of \(1/x\) on the interval between them. Thus there is a strict difference when \([\sigma,D]\ne0\). The measurement bound is uniform for weak admission on this source: \(|d_i|/q_i\le\|\sigma^{-1/2}D\sigma^{-1/2}\|\) controls the remainder for every instrument. The calculated 20-cell vacancy coefficients are 0.004952115 for the classical maximum and 0.005000189 for the full quantum generator. The 80-cell excess values are 0.132090509 and 0.132092888. The optimal spectral measurement is an independent mathematical bound. State tomography on an ensemble and subsequent evaluation of the logarithm recovers a different observable.

## A finite-intensity obstruction in three dimensions

Let the same positive admissions measure \(\mu_\ell\) serve all observers on a bounded proper three-dimensional patch \(U\). It may vary with refinement. Assume volumes of small balls on the relevant neighborhood obey \({\rm Vol}(B_\ell(y))\le C\ell^3\), and suppose \(f_\ell(x)\le\mu_\ell(B_\ell(x))\), as for the probability of at least one hit from a point process of intensity \(\mu_\ell\). If its total intensity on the neighborhood is \(M_\ell\), integration in either order gives
\[
 \int_U f_\ell\,dV\le C M_\ell\ell^3 .
\]
For any signed response obeying \(|Q|\le K f^2\) uniformly, this implies
\[
 {\rm Vol}\left\{x\in U:\ |Q|/\ell^4\ge\tau\right\}
 \le C M_\ell\sqrt{K/\tau}\,\ell,\qquad \tau>0 .
\]
The balanced bit obeys the bound for all \(0\le f\le1\) and the measured \(|z|<1\). At bounded total intensity the normalized response vanishes in volume measure. This excludes convergence to a nonzero smooth halo on an open annulus for this admission mechanism. Singular concentrations, a center-dependent measure and growing intensity fall outside the inference. Finite two-dimensional station balls can yield area-order hit counts and do not fill a proper three-dimensional patch at bounded distance distortion. [Clocked packing bounds](../../../../model/experiments/interval_echo_einstein_station/collar_scaling/README.md#which-metric-do-these-clocks-determine-at-large-scales)

The intensity order is sharp. Supply \(N_\ell=\lceil1/\ell\rceil\) independent marks in dimensionless chart units, each drawn from smooth probability density \(m(x)\) relative to the volume. Feed the detector one formed pair if at least one mark lands in its ball, otherwise feed independent heads. Then \(f_\ell=1-(1-\mu(B_\ell))^{N_\ell}=(4\pi/3)m(x)\ell^2+o(\ell^2)\) uniformly on compact regular patches. Consequently the weakening slope divided by \(\ell^4\) converges to \(z^2[(4\pi/3)m(x)]^2\). Neither the observer's area nor the placement policy was inferred from this scalar calculation. An exact-integer lattice census checks the limits on a unit flat address torus with spacing \(\ell^2\). At \(\ell=1/128\), the normalized balanced slope is 0.00362101 for 128 marks (limit 0.00362337) and \(2.21\times10^{-7}\) for one mark (limit zero).

## A dynamically generated radial shape in a comparison chart

Place a source at the center of a cubic address chart with absorbing walls at lattice distance \(R\). Three independent fair material bits per opportunity generate eight outcomes: step to one of the six neighbors or stay for either of the other two. Let \(P\) be the substochastic transition matrix, and \(g=(I-P)^{-1}e_0\) the expected visits from a single injection. Resetting escapes to the source gives stationary placement probability \(g/\sum g\), since each injected mark exits once. Routing, reset, chart, opportunities and placement of independent banks are supplied. The unknown proper geometry of a native galaxy is absent from this comparison. [Finite mark transport](transport.py)

These local transition probabilities produce a mesoscopic \(1/r\) visit law without assigning radial weights. On the infinite lattice the Fourier multiplier for visits is \(1/(1-\frac14[1+\sum_j\cos k_j])\), with leading part \(8/|k|^2\) near zero. The radial inverse transform of the leading part with a smooth cutoff has leading coefficient \(2/(\pi r)\), from the Dirichlet integral. The remaining multiplier is bounded near zero and its first derivatives are \(O(1/|k|)\), which are integrable. Integration by parts in a largest coordinate and the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma give \(o(1/r)\) for its Fourier coefficients. The strong Markov property bounds the difference between the infinite and killed Green functions by the maximum infinite-lattice value at an exit, \(O(1/R)\). Therefore for \(1\ll r\ll R\), visits scale as \(1/r\), and the balanced modular coefficient, proportional to squared normalized placement density on a resolved chart, scales as \(1/r^2\). Here the chart is first resolved on a ball small compared with the variation scale of \(m\); the near-source and wall limits do not apply. In a continuum sphere with absorbing radius \(R\), the corresponding solution of radial flux conservation is proportional to \(1/r-1/R\). Its square predicts an edge-dependent shape.

The complete \(127^3\) cube satisfies its local injection equation to \(2\times10^{-16}\), with total absorbed probability within \(6\times10^{-13}\) of one. An independent axial solution separates the two transverse sine modes and inverts the remaining Dirichlet tridiagonal matrix. At cube radii 128, 256, 512 and 1024, compare squared visits at \(r=\lfloor\sqrt R/2\rfloor\) with those at \(2r\). The resulting ratios are 4.379, 4.261, 4.171 and 4.119, against the mesoscopic limit of four. A control absorbs a mark in the bulk with probability \(1/8\) per opportunity before its step, keeping the chart, bit alphabet and diagonal symmetries. On the \(R=64\) cube the 4-to-8 ratio rises from 4.65 to over 17,000.

At fixed transport geometry and compact source position, changing the injection rate leaves the normalized visit distribution fixed. If the number of available marks is \(A(M_b)/\ell+O(1)\), the limiting coefficient is proportional to \(z^2 A(M_b)^2\). Thus simply adding equal mark intensities for each unit of baryonic mass produces a quadratic mass dependence. With a fixed material response \(z\), the deep-law target proportional to \(\sqrt{M_b}\) would require \(A(M_b)\propto M_b^{1/4}\). Such a constitutive relation is an independent hypothesis.

The established quantity is the shape of a signed information response. Identifying \(15Q/(8\pi^2\ell^4)\) as active stress requires the same regional algebra, vacuum normalization, proper radius and independently measured gravitational response. A positive weakening tangent, a chosen number of marks and the normalization of a visit distribution do not fix their dependence on baryonic mass, the acceleration \(a_0\), pressure or lensing. A particle interpretation is also outside this experiment.

## Mass dependence from shared seam signs

Independent equal-strength fair baryon signs have summed amplitude \(S_N\) with \(\mathbb E S_N=0\), \(\mathbb E S_N^2=N\), and \(\mathbb E S_N^4=3N^2-2N\). Forgetting the signs makes one comparison exactly uniform. Keeping them when taking the logarithm gives a quadratic coefficient proportional to \(N\). Taking the RMS of the positive quadratic statistic gives \(\sqrt{3N^2-2N}\). The RMS of the signed amplitude scales as \(\sqrt N\), but squaring it for the comparator restores \(N\).

The scaling obstruction only needs independence, zero means and a common finite variance \(\sigma^2>0\). The positive quadratic mean is \(N\sigma^2\) by variance addition. Its RMS, when finite, is at least that mean. For identically distributed signals of finite fourth moment \(\mu_4\), its squared RMS is \(N\mu_4+3N(N-1)\sigma^4\). These statements apply to the unchanged linear amplitude and quadratic comparison with fixed per-baryon intensity.

The executable board admits another preparation. Its external seams are disjoint pairs of ports, three per triangular cell. Load one unit on each blank seam, pulse for \(\pi/2\), and read which port holds the unit. Isolated pulses on product inputs give an independent fair sign \(T_e\) on each seam. Its two owners see opposite orientations. With oriented incidence \(\eta_{ie}\), the local sum \(X_i=\sum_{e}\eta_{ie} T_e\) has variance three. Neighbors have covariance \(-1\) and nonneighbors have covariance zero. For enrolled set \(A\), \(S=\sum_{i\in A}X_i\) is a sum of independent fair signs on the \(B\) cut seams, hence \(\mathbb E S^2=B\). A triangular ancestor of linear subdivision \(L\) has \(N=L^2\) cells and \(B=3L=3\sqrt{N}\). For disjoint sets, joining across \(k\) shared seams subtracts \(2k\) from their summed cuts. The full sphere has zero cut. Native fair bits, adjacency and the controlled collar return are established operations. Enrolling baryons on these cells, copying the signs, scheduling resets and choosing the source instrument below are supplied. The autonomous law would have to produce this preparation family and establish the relation between \(N\) and ordinary mass.

Each occupied cell independently hits with probability \(u=a(x)\ell^2+o(\ell^2)\), using the growing per-cell bank above with fixed intensity per enrolled cell. Select one admitted cell uniformly, then one of its three seams uniformly. Uniform selection from a finite list can be made with fair-rail bits by rejecting out-of-range words. Use its local oriented sign to choose the order of the two return bits on the balanced comparison. On admission, feed the tested input a paired sample with probability \(s\) and an independent-head sample otherwise. On a miss use independent heads on both arms. Use fresh comparison and tie trials and hold the sign preparation fixed in law across interventions. With \(f=1-(1-u)^N\) and \(y=z f S/(3N)\), the reported bit \(b=\pm1\) has \(p_s(b\mid S)=(1+b s y)/2\). Erase the address, hit and seam selector. The flux \(S\) suffices wherever cell placements are equal.

At an interior reference \(0<s_0<1\), both source orientations (increasing or decreasing \(s\)) are physically allowed. At the tested interior reference \(s_0=1/2\), dyadic forward and backward mixtures can be prepared with finite independent fair-rail words and approach the same analytic derivative. The signed first variation per unit decrease of \(s\) is
\[
 Q_{\rm flag}=-\sum_{S,b}(-\partial_s p_s)\log p_{s_0}
             =\mathbb E[y\,\operatorname{atanh}(s_0y)] .
\]
The sign distribution is independent of \(s\), so its own logarithm drops out. The weakening slope is nonnegative and the strengthening slope has the opposite sign. At the fresh reference \(s_0=0\) the slope is zero. For fixed finite source,
\[
 \lim_{\ell\to0}Q_{\rm flag}/\ell^4=s_0 z^2 a(x)^2 B/9 .
\]
Averaging out all signs before recording gives zero slope on the one-bit comparison. Two otherwise independent comparisons on one hidden flux have agreement probability \((1+s^2\mathbb E y^2)/2\) and weakening slope \(2s_0\mathbb E y^2\operatorname{atanh}(s_0^2\mathbb E y^2)=O(\ell^8)\). Joint refinement and growth of \(N\) requires a separate uniform estimate and rare admissions \(Nu\ll1\).

One communicated sign recovers a fixed fraction of the large-cut response. At the source compute \(h=\operatorname{sgn}S\) with a fresh fair bit on ties. Multiply the comparison bit by \(h\), then erase \(h\). Its marginal law is \(p_s(b)=(1+b s\bar y)/2\), with \(\bar y=z f\,m_B/(3N)\) and \(m_B=\mathbb E|S|\). The weakening slope \(\bar y\operatorname{atanh}(s_0\bar y)\) has coefficient \(s_0 z^2 a(x)^2m_B^2/9\). Telescoping the binomial sum gives
\[
 m_B=B{B-1\choose\lfloor(B-1)/2\rfloor}/2^{B-1}
\]
for \(B\ge1\), and \(m_0=0\). Stirling's formula gives \(m_B^2/B\to2/\pi\). The source must aggregate the sign per common preparation and deliver it to the selected comparator or alongside the return. The controlled construction supplies the sign channel. A finite-speed realization requires time for the contributing boundary portions to reach a common aggregation and alignment event. Its time, energy and capacity in a native galaxy have not been derived.

For any binary alignment based on the same preparatory word, \(hS\le |S|\) pointwise. Majority therefore maximizes this oriented one-bit coefficient. If the sign computation's causal record includes only \(k\) of the \(B\) independent cut signs, all inaccessible signs have zero conditional mean. Thus \(|\mathbb E[h S]|=|\mathbb E[hS_k]|\le m_k\), with equality for the accessible majority. The same bound holds for a stopping or adaptive sign query protocol with at most \(k\) distinct independent signs, by completing unused queries with independent signs. Its squared coefficient is at most \(m_k^2/m_B^2\) of the complete majority response. A bounded number of accessible cut signs therefore cannot retain the \(\sqrt N\) growth for a compact refining triangular source. This bound counts independent boundary inputs that influence the final orientation and permits an intermediate one-bit message summarizing many prior inputs. Large collections of disconnected bodies expose the distinction between source protocols. One shared alignment across bodies needs their joint flux and uses \(m_{\sum B_j}^2\) for disjoint, nonadjacent bodies at equal placement. If each body aligns its own word and the selected body's origin is erased, the pooled probability shifts add, so the coefficient uses \((\sum_j m_{B_j})^2\). If independent per-body return records are retained as a product and their modular charges summed, the coefficient uses \(\sum_j m_{B_j}^2\). All three constructions specify the same individual source intensity and a different physical handling of the records.

### Saturation at finite observation radius

At uniform \(u\), the exact ratio of either slope to its respective sparse forecast \(s_0 z^2 u^2 B/9\) or \(s_0 z^2 u^2m_B^2/9\) factors as \([f/(Nu)]^2 R\). For the one-bit case \(R=\operatorname{atanh}(s_0\bar y)/(s_0\bar y)\). Since \(m_B\le\sqrt B\) and \(B\le3N\), \(|\bar y|\le |z|/\sqrt{3N}\). For full signs write \(g=z f/(3N)\). The fourth moment \(3B^2-2B\) and the power-series remainder for \(\operatorname{atanh}\) bound
\[
 1\le R\le 1+\frac{s_0^2 g^2(3B-2)}{3(1-s_0^2 z^2)}.
\]
For both instruments \(R=1+O(1/N)\) uniformly at all admissions when \(B>0\). Thus as \(N\to\infty,\ u\to0\) with \(Nu\to c\in(0,\infty)\), the observed-to-sparse ratio tends to \([(1-e^{-c})/c]^2\). It tends to one if \(Nu\to0\) and zero if \(Nu\to\infty\). At \(c=1\) it is 0.3995764. Enrolling more baryons at fixed resolution competes for a single selected return. This saturation is observable without calibrating the stress to gravity.

The limits below use measured contrast \(z=-0.014370365536930434\), \(s_0=1/2\), and natural logarithms. The full sign and compressed columns share the same per-cell intensity. At \(u=1/4096\), the replay evaluates the exact finite output law and its normalized slopes differ from these limits by up to 0.37%.
