### Why the outskirts of a galaxy could show an excess

An “observer” here is a physical system that can retain a distinguishable outcome. The relevant records are made in and around the source. When neighboring patches interact, a record in their shared region can preserve which outcome occurred on both sides. Consider two patches carrying the same fair bit. If the shared region retains the bit on 80% of trials and has a blank on the others, the best reconstruction fails on 10% of all trials. The retained bits are read correctly and half the blanks are guessed wrongly. Reading a blank many times gives the same blank. This finite example makes precise the kind of local information the proposal needs. It assigns no mass to a missed observation. [Exact erasure model](../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/SparseRecordDefectWitness.lean)

For a real galaxy the proposal makes two additional assertions. First, enough *independent local records* in a fixed physical layer allow the neighboring state to be recovered to high accuracy. Under the paper’s mixing bound this suppresses the possible extra source. With sparse records the bound also permits zero. Second, certain long-lived correlations across galactic layers actually carry positive stress which the metric responds to at the ordinary coupling. This second assertion can be tested by changing a joint correlation at fixed local matter readings and comparing an independently measured clock or light-path response with the response to an ordinary source. Record coverage, sign and calibration have to be established independently of the orbit used to test them. Two physically identical galaxies viewed at different distances have the same intrinsic prediction in their own frames. Distance affects our inference of their radii and speeds. ## Geometric normalization and the three errors

At a smooth point p and future unit timelike vector u, take spacetime normal
coordinates with time axis u. The hypersurface `exp_p(u^perp)` has zero
second fundamental form at p: its coordinate embedding has zero second
derivatives there and the ambient Christoffel symbols vanish there.
This is a local construction for each u; it requires no globally
time-symmetric foliation. The source must admit the corresponding regional
readouts. The Gauss constraint gives `R^(3)(p)=2 G_ab u^a u^b`.

For an intrinsic three-dimensional geodesic ball of radius r, the normal
coordinate volume expansion gives

```
V(r)=4 pi r^3/3 * [1-R^(3) r^2/30+o(r^2)],
A(r)=4 pi r^2 * [1-R^(3) r^2/18+o(r^2)].
```

At fixed volume `4 pi ell^3/3`, solving for r gives
`r=ell+R^(3) ell^3/90+o(ell^3)`. Substitution gives
`A=4 pi ell^2-2 pi R^(3) ell^4/15+o(ell^4)`.
Subtract a common local vacuum reference with Einstein tensor
`G_ref=-Lambda g`, so its timelike contraction is Lambda. Thus the leading
fixed-volume area contrast is `-C_ell (G_uu-Lambda)`, where
`C_ell=4 pi ell^4/15`. On another prescribed slice the correction is
`R^(3)/2-G_uu=(K_ij K^ij-K^2)/2`; it must be included in the area error.
A fixed nonzero correction is order `ell^4`, so it does not disappear after
division by `ell^4`.

The radial diamond-kernel integral is
`integral_0^ell 4 pi r^2 (ell^2-r^2)/(2 ell) dr=C_ell`.
The geometrically normalized modular factor `2 pi` therefore gives the
bulk coefficient `2 pi C_ell=8 pi^2 ell^4/15`. These are the exact
