# Entropy stationarity driven by finite repair at equal proper volume

Use three centered geodesic balls in independent ultrastatic constant-curvature geometries. Fix the target proper volume at that of a flat unit ball. Select the curvatures so the geometric boundary areas are three, four and five tiles of area π, respectively. This tile dictionary and the rest-frame geometry are inputs. Check equality of volumes with independent interior counts and read area with a separate surface counter. Each tile carries one binary boundary register. The region has one independently readable material bit. Its coarse configurations are its geometry sector and material bit.

From each of two sharp preparations run 512 local bit flips, quiet attempts and reversible changes of sector chosen at random. Removing a tile requires its bit to be zero. Adding a tile initializes it quiet. Use the exact finite Markov kernel derived from these rules to generate 1,048,576 terminal observations, including the probability of retaining a quiet attempt. Measure entropy on the six coarse outcomes and area from independent geometric counters. At the endpoint test the first variation of entropy plus the log of two per tile on all five independent probability transfers. Require a maximum absolute variation below 0.03 nats per unit probability, and interior counts within six sampling errors of their target. Apply the identical test to a repair kernel that halves the chance of increasing the exposed tile count, preserving the geometry, overlaps, alphabet and microscopic connectivity. Require a residual above 0.6 nats for that control. An area selected from the observed entropy would invalidate the comparison.

This finite example supplies a geometric area per boundary bit on one volume and one rest frame. It isolates the additional identification a native realization needs. A small-ball limit, a matter stress law, and a source-selected physical coupling remain separate questions.
