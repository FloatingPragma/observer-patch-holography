# Record-driven selection and extraction without a supplied unit total

## What is repaired

The older common input supplied compartment totals +1 and −1. Selection was
law-sensitive, but its final particle count followed from those supplied
totals. Here neither a numerical total nor a one-unit sector is given to the
observer. A public bounded neutral family admits 0 through 12 units of either
orientation, including signed high loads. The process constructs a real
canonical pair and keeps its entire source history. This strengthens the
source-to-record premise; it does not turn a chosen final count into a new
quantization theorem.

## Native current reconstruction

For an oriented seam, native repair has current

    j=(d−(2c−1)(|d| mod2))/2,   d=q_a−q_b.

After its first repair, repeated identical coins give zero current. The first
opposite coin gives the remaining parity contrast, so the pre-probe contrast
is exactly `d=2j_first+j_opposite`. Subtract the contrast change from previously
recorded probe currents, then integrate these pre-probe contrasts on a spanning
tree. The relative initial state has one additive constant. The declared
initial vacuum at 0 or 14 fixes that constant. The accumulated probe currents
then give the actual post-probe state. This is the existing census theorem,
now used without any cell-total argument. The reference may become occupied;
it is its initial value, not its final emptiness, that is used.

The 22 tree edges each fail to see an opposite coin within 64 events with
probability 2^-63. Their union contributes at most 22·2^-63. On failure the
observer produces no state or success verdict. A secretly shifted vacuum datum
would be a different preparation, not something this current-only instrument
authenticates. That assumption is retained rather than hidden.

## Constructive balancing from the observed state

Each native repair preserves the total and the initial min/max box. Its
decrease of `V=Σq_v²` on an edge of contrast d is

    ΔV=(d²−(|d| mod2)²)/2.

Thus |d|≥2 gives a strict decrease of at least 2 on either coin, while |d|=1
gives a hold or exchange with equal probabilities. If the cell span exceeds
one, choose a maximum M and a shortest path to any vertex of value at most
M−2. Every interior value is M or M−1. An encountered M already supplies the
next maximum; an M−1 is exchanged with the current M by the native event.
The last edge then has contrast at least 2 and strictly lowers V. All choices
are functions of the reconstructed state and subsequent current records.

The minimum potential at fixed total is attained exactly by adjacent levels.
The bounded integer potential admits at most V_initial/2 strict decreases.
For both 12-site cells together, V_initial≤24·64², including the census since
native probes never increase V. Each decrease needs at most 11 requested
exchanges (a conservative bound). The controller therefore reaches the
adjacent-level class unless one of at most 540672 capped exchanges fails.
This is a constructive finite controller, not a burn-in assumption.

For total K with |K|≤12, adjacent levels are {0,1} if K>0, {−1,0} if K<0,
or all zero if K=0. The observer derives K from its reconstructed state only
after the census. A swap/hold body preserves V and every multiset; on the

nondecreasing-repair output and unselected state are retained. The native

## Extracting and clearing real source charges

Within a selected cell, find an occupied vertex nearest the target port.
The interior is vacant, since an interior occupied vertex would be closer.
Native movements therefore retain one actual unit at 0 and its opposite at
14. Their sum is zero. The remaining source on the other 238 ports is also
neutral and has values in {−1,0,1}. The packet includes a spanning-tree
certificate that the graph induced by those ports is connected.

Choose a shortest path among every pair of opposite-sign occupied vertices
of the remaining source. Its interior is vacant: if an interior site were
positive, it and the negative endpoint would give a shorter pair; if it were
negative, it and the positive endpoint would do so. Route the positive unit
through that vacant interior. The final adjacent pair has sum zero and
contrast 2, so either native coin annihilates it exactly. No resetting,
discarding of source charge or externally imposed annihilation is used.
Repeat. At most 11 surplus pairs exist. The cleanup keeps both protected
ports out of every event.

This lemma applies to any connected finite graph and any neutral binary
signed source. At most k(N−1) requested moves clear k pairs (a conservative
bound including the terminal contacts). With uncapped fair native movements,
the expected number of attempted movement events is at most twice that
number. With cap C, a union bound gives at most k(N−1)2^-C inconclusive
probability. It is a useful constructive alternative to a loose global
random-absorption bound. It does not change the uniform-schedule Green
response theorem: the schedules and observables are distinct.

For reversed orientation, the actual remaining negative unit moves 0→1,
the positive unit moves 14→0 avoiding 1, then the negative unit moves 1→14
avoiding 0. Connectivity certificates cover each allowed graph. The sources
do not cross or annihilate. Two further native moves 0→1→0 witness the
positive frame. No conjugation of an audit array substitutes for these moves.

The conservative total number of requested fair moves is

    540672 + 22 + 11·237 + 3·239 + 2 = 544020.

Each capped fair exchange fails with probability 2^-64 conditional on its
entire past. Thus every preparation in the declared domain has complete
inconclusive probability at most

    η = 22·2^-63 + 544020·2^-64 < 3e-14.

K=0 is an exact no-unit outcome, not a failure or a postselected particle.
On nonzero K and no timeout, the actual entire 240-site source equals
δ0−δ14. All outside ports are empty because their elimination was executed
and observed. The public |K| bound supplies capacity, not a unit total.
