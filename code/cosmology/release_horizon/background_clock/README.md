# A clock and a record census on the thermal release screen

Four pulses travel through a 320-cell Interval Echo screen. The periods read at distances eight and sixteen are twice and four times the period read by the emitter. On the same history, a census of angular addresses produces eight and sixty-four clock records per base face per layer. A fourth-power count at the two measured period ratios would give sixteen and 256. This comparison tests which aspect of a physical expanding background the finite screen can supply. ## Sending a crest and sharing a clock event

Each nonroot cell listens to its lowest-address neighbor one graph link closer to the root. Children are serviced before parents. At an external seam the sender offers one rail of its public bit and the receiver offers a vacancy. After a sharp read the receiver returns the borrowed load, restores the two allocations and sets its own rail to the sign it observed. It copies both the bright and the dark symbol. The complete port truth tables include the dark-to-bright and bright-to-dark overwrites and a parked external exchange. The source publishes four single-layer crests with offsets 0, 2, 4 and 6. A parent’s previous word reaches its child one layer later, so the entire sampled train arrives after the graph distance in layers. The [tree and clocks](measure.py) run on each registered board. Separate calibration and fault banks use the same declared addresses.

For a clock comparison at a receiving cell, route its two complementary public rails to the two ends of a single external seam and apply the registered native echo. The strict written state has both loads one. Each side unwrites it, measures its own sharp endpoint against a vacancy and restores the same written state before the other side reads. Endpoint polarity is complementary. Both owners decode the same sign, and the borrowed rails return to their original owners. Cutting the transport into this comparison seam yields four missing bright coincidences on every board, while the independent train at the receiver remains live. The recorded negative and invalid comparisons are available for every layer.

The receiver pre-runs two full marker strokes. Its neighbor pre-runs three flips, then operates a separate oscillator with a native half-period exposure per microstep. There is no intermediate projection of this oscillator between its two half exposures. Two half exposures give a total exposure of π and interchange its sharp rails. One clock tick counts that flip. The position returns to its starting rail after four half exposures. At any crest the observer reads its retained count of completed flips. The clocks share their exposure cadence as an apparatus input. Their states, local measurements and stored counts are separate. A crest between oscillator interrogations reads the count recorded at the last complete boundary.

## Pulse periods and the angular census

Every marker has the same native flip and is read locally. Let M(j) be its number of measured changes through layer j. A train emitted at layers u,v and read at distance d has period ratio (M(v+d)−M(u+d))/(M(v)−M(u)) on the common-cadence bank. A stationary pulse delay cancels from the layer interval. The material microsteps per layer are q=1 through layer 8, q=2 through layer 16, and q=4 afterwards on the largest board. The 20- and 80-cell boards cap q at 1 and 2 respectively. The earliest receiver on 320 cells measures ratios 1, 1 and 2 because its final period crosses a cadence boundary. The other two measure constant ratios 2 and 4. Parking only the far receiver's first marker stroke after its first crest leaves all arrival layers intact and moves its first ratio from 4 to 7/2. With static cadence all three ratios at every receiver are one. This measures the response of scheduled material pulses and local clocks. Photon transport and cosmological expansion require further identifications.

Every four-child angular address is connected internally by three seams to its central child. Exactly two external seams connect the children of each adjacent pair of parents. The quotient reproduces the smaller registered board at each refinement. The screen remains on one substrate throughout the experiment. The enrollment at scale q retains one distinguished finest descendant for each of the q² active addresses per base face. At every microstep each enrolled address publishes one marker reading, and the census counts actual changes in that marker.

Each of the twenty faces gives the same count. With fixed enrollment the counts fall to 1, 2 and 4 at the same pulse periods. Re-reading each unchanged marker twice adds no count. Parking the local exchange yields zero marker changes. The board, angular enrollment, and cadence are supplied. A nested angular address contributes two powers of q and the measured clock contributes one. A further multiplicity w would yield q² q w. For positive q this equals q⁴ exactly when w=q. No radial multiplicity or independently calibrated physical volume has been identified by these measurements.

A sharp guarded decision can temporarily use the marker and restore its position before its deterministic stroke. The [material heat source](../README.md) fixes the initial upper populations at 1/5 and 1/3, the energy gap at one and the swap probability at 1/4. The pointer responds to the designated public rail. Address permutations restore any temporarily displaced dimer before the next logical service. In the separate two-clock comparison the witness uses an independent coherent dimer.

The joint timing bank keeps every root age through the 36-layer deadline and its remaining probability as a single tail. A receiver reports all successive crests that reach it in that window. The fraction seeing all four at distance sixteen is about 0.98218. The thermal bank reads all four probes at layer 25, including those that receive no crest. Their local clock count is 60. Injecting one material crest at each of the 320 possible owners at layer 23, count 52, measures which inputs can reach a probe by the reading.
