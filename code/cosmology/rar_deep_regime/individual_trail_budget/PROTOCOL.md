# Finite-stock upper bounds on individual SPARC outer orbits

Freeze before extracting any per-galaxy budget ratios. Prior work inspected SPARC, including NGC 3741 and other individual disks, and all three trail yields were already compared with cosmological data. This comparison uses previously published measurements and targets. The native ratios come from a separate paid experiment. No galaxy data calibrate these ratios.

* Start from every galaxy in the original bundled SPARC tables. Require quality 1 or 2, catalogue inclination at least 30 degrees, positive stellar luminosity and measured positive total HI mass. Require a zero bulge contribution at every tabulated radius. Apply the existing outer-ring cuts: positive radius, rotation velocity and its uncertainty, relative error no greater than 10%, and positive resolved baryonic squared speed at nominal disk M/L 0.5. Use the largest radius meeting these criteria. Retain any sign of the observed excess and use a single point per galaxy. Report all eligible galaxies, the existing deep outer-ring subset below 1.2e-11 m/s², and the existing deterministic five-fold labels (fold zero separately). Every available SPARC galaxy has previously been available to this project.
* Obtain total nominal baryons from the catalogue 3.6 μm luminosity with stellar M/L 0.5 and HI mass with factor 1.33 for helium. The table's signed gas velocity already incorporates helium. The resolved disk contribution is supplied at stellar M/L 1. At the same outer radius, add the observed gas-plus-stellar squared speed to G times the total baryon mass times q divided by the radius. For spherical nonnegative additional matter this is the largest possible force from that stock. Set q=8.5N/50.5 for each actually supplied N=14,32,64. Also give the tighter exact net-work ratios, with no fit of galaxy mass, geometry or trail length. Report required q per galaxy and compare each with all three caps.
* Propagate distance changes as R proportional to D, total flux mass proportional to D² and each resolved squared-speed component proportional to D. Rescale observed velocity by sin(i_catalogue)/sin(i_test). At one and two catalogue errors move distance, inclination (at most 90 degrees), photometric normalization and measured speed in favorable directions, and allow M/L from 0.3 to 0.8 with gas from 0.7 to 1.5 times the published helium-corrected HI inventory. Vary stellar luminosity in its catalogue interval and scale the resolved stellar mass normalization consistently. For a further extreme sensitivity move all these quantities by up to three errors, allow M/L 0.3–1.0 and gas up to twice its catalogue value. Treat these as per-galaxy bounds, with no combined significance or assigned likelihood for the box. Preserve signed gas terms and explore endpoints to give the model its best allowed outcome. The gas allowance is an assumed multiplier, not a measurement of all diffuse or molecular baryons.
* Report the highest required ratio under the two-error favorable box and inspect that object's measurement provenance, possible systematics and missing baryon amount. Also provide all rows, split counts and multi-ring stability for that example if possible. The spherical rule tests gravitational inventory independently for each galaxy. It leaves nonspherical forces, a variable cosmological-to-galaxy fraction and other supplies open.

The two-error maximum identifies NGC 3741. After inspecting the original observers' paper, add a source-specific stress test using their quoted +2.7 and +4.8 km/s asymmetric-drift corrections at the last two points. Treat the catalogue rows as the reference, subtract the corresponding full correction, then repeat the existing boxes without reselection. The published velocity errors already include half the correction; this extra change intentionally favors the model and is not an independent random error. Also give the gas multiplier and distance at which the 32-seam point stops failing if every other input is held at its favorable two-error value.
