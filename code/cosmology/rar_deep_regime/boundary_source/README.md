# Can a boundary of ordinary material set the extra orbital gravity?

On ten nearby galaxies, excess speed squared shows little nominal association with the measured baryonic boundary after both are divided by the square root of source-aperture mass (Spearman −0.030). A pair selected from the visible matter alone gives a sharper test. With independent stellar distances, the measured NGC 3521 / NGC 3198 outer excess ratio is **1.29**. The optical radial boundaries predict **5.75**. Adding the full two-dimensional atomic-gas images gives **4.66**, or **4.02** if both images are normalized to their whole-galaxy catalogue H I masses. Using two-dimensional maps of all three measured phases gives **4.09**, against **1.54** when the ordinary orbital force is recomputed from the same maps. The atomic maps by themselves give **1.15**. Calibration on NGC 3198 then gives an 18.6 km/s median error for the mapped two-gas source on eight other galaxies, including one 99 km/s underestimate and two overshoots above 75 km/s. ## From neighbouring loads to a measured source

For two nonnegative surface densities below a common threshold ceiling T, a shared uniformly sampled threshold gives unlike binary loads with probability |Σ₁−Σ₂|/T. Integrating over level sets gives the total variation of the surface density. Independent thresholds for the two sites instead give p₁+p₂−2p₁p₂. A sharp disk of density Σ and radius R gives S=2πRΣ. In a monotone exponential disk, direct integration gives S=2πΣ₀Rᴅ=M/Rᴅ. Thus at equal mass the more compact disk emits more in this model. A single shared threshold in an individual galaxy can give a different realised perimeter. This estimator uses the threshold average with one common measure for every galaxy.

For circular rings with outer radii bⱼ and surface densities Σⱼ, the Euclidean radial source is $S=2\pi\sum_j b_j|\Sigma_j-\Sigma_{j+1}|$ and density beyond the mapped edge is set to zero. A four-neighbour square lattice replaces Euclidean perimeter by the axis-weighted perimeter. For a radial field the overall factor is 4/π and cancels in galaxy ratios. On images we calculate the actual horizontal and vertical edge sums. S has units of solar masses per kpc. The threshold ceiling of 10,000 M☉/pc² leaves every primary binned density below saturation. Finite pixel size, the physical coupling of occupancy to column density, the threshold law, and a linear gravity response Y=kS are assumptions. A field whose stress is quadratic in unnormalised summed amplitudes would require a separate source law. [Source construction](source.py) · [Direct acquisition and controls](measure.py)

Leroy et al. measured gas means and 3.6 µm stellar medians in 10-arcsecond tilted rings through about 1.2 optical radii. We use the independent light and line-intensity columns. Their kinematic fit is excluded. The source includes the published 1.36 helium correction, CO conversion and K-to-3.6 µm stellar calibration. Some molecular bins fall below the survey's sensitivity and some galaxies lack a resolved molecular profile. The primary assigns these bins zero. Five atomic bins require interpolation in NGC 2841. The regularly rebinned field represents observed radial structure at 0.8 kpc bin width, with instrumental smoothing originally corresponding to 0.4 or 0.8 kpc at the authors’ distances. It cannot recover density variations in individual arms or the far-side geometry. The radial stellar medians and restricted azimuths also prevent a general identification with a true two-dimensional mean. [Leroy et al., §§3.2, Appendix E](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-6256/136/6/2782) · [Machine-readable profiles and sample](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/AJ/136/2782)

We read one outermost SPARC rotation measurement per galaxy. The target is Y=Vobs²−Vgas|Vgas|−0.5 Vdisk|Vdisk|−0.7 Vbulge|Vbulge|−GMmol/R, in (km/s)². The last term is a spherical estimate for the measured molecular component. Dropping it changes the catalogue-distance pair ratio from 1.632 to 1.654. Every primary excess is positive. Source and endpoint use different physical observables, but share distance, deprojection and potentially the same 21-cm emission or infrared imaging. The project has used SPARC repeatedly. No direct measurement of microscopic record formation enters this comparison. [Lelli et al., SPARC mass models](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-6256/152/6/157) · [Original catalogue rotation references](../data/table1.dat)

## Which scale predicts held orbital speeds?

Of 23 Leroy galaxies, 13 have catalogue counterparts, including Holmberg II. Quality or inclination removes two and the aperture cut removes NGC 2976. Catalogued geometry places every selected outer ring outside the measured source edge and at least three fitted stellar scale lengths out. The alternating mass ranks supply five calibration galaxies and five different held galaxies. The only fitted parameter of each law is a common gain from the five calibration targets. The aperture factor F is a secondary model in which the source at each radial jump emits as a spherical shell. Its normalised stationary field u yields F=R⁻¹∫₀ᴿ[ru(r)]²dr. The primary boundary gain is 1.868×10⁻⁶ (km/s)² kpc/M☉. The held total-speed residuals (prediction minus measurement) are −16, −37, +26, −35 and +82 km/s for IC 2574, NGC 2403, NGC 3521, NGC 2841 and NGC 7331. Square-root mass improves IC 2574 and NGC 7331 and misses NGC 2841 by −113 km/s. This small cohort establishes no clear best population law. Among all ten galaxies the regression of ln(Y/√M) on ln(S/√M) gives slope −0.050 against +1 for a linear source, with Spearman coefficient −0.030. Dropping one galaxy at a time gives slopes −0.214 to +0.188. Letting both mass and compactness exponents float instead gives 0.267 and 0.510 in Y ∝ Mᵝ(S/√M)ᵠ. The slope conditioned on an assumed mass scaling is descriptive, with correlated mass errors and a small sample. Catalogue atomic mass fractions within the Leroy profiles range from 0.12 to 1.26. Values above one expose a cross-survey geometry or normalization difference. [All results and sensitivity variants](results.json)

## The gain-free pair and its stellar distances

The preregistered largest contrast in S/√M among pairs with measured masses within a factor of two selects NGC 3521 / NGC 3198. SPARC puts these galaxies at 7.70±2.30 and 13.80±1.40 Mpc. Their source apertures end at 11.19 and 15.45 kpc and their orbital measurements are at 17.74 and 44.08 kpc. The measured aperture-mass ratio is 1.84 and the observed excess ratio 1.632. Boundary, finite-boundary and square-root mass ratios are 3.600, 3.467 and 1.358. Moving both velocities independently by one quoted error gives 1.327–1.981. The old distance and inclination ranges admit a common gain at favourable parameter settings. Rebinning to 1.6 kpc gives a boundary ratio of 3.334. Halving or doubling the molecular conversion gives 3.582–3.634. [Fixed sample and input perturbations](results.json)

The follow-up fixed a choice of direct stellar distance indicators after seeing the original pair. Xi et al. measured the red giant branch tip from Hubble imaging in the outskirts of NGC 3521 at 11.3±1.1 Mpc, including their zero-point uncertainty. Freedman et al.’s metallicity-corrected Cepheids give 13.80 Mpc for NGC 3198. We use ±0.18 mag for a sensitivity box by adding the 0.08 mag random error and 0.10 mag LMC-anchor error linearly. A previous Tully–Fisher estimate of 13.24±1.97 Mpc for NGC 3521 depends on rotation and is excluded from this direct-distance check. [Distance protocol](DISTANCE_PROTOCOL.md) · [Xi et al., §3.1](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae2d06) · [Freedman et al., Table 4 and adopted LMC distance](https://doi.org/10.1086/320638) · [Anand et al., Table A2](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/501/3/3621/6006291)

With direct distances, the *same angular orbits* are at 26.03 and 44.08 kpc. The revised source-mass ratio is 3.97, outside the original mass match. A common linear gain must still give equal Y/S on these two locked galaxies. The ratio of their required gains is 0.224, using S₃₅₂₁/S₃₁₉₈=5.754 and Y₃₅₂₁/Y₃₁₉₈=1.291. The finite model gives 5.604, and square-root aperture mass gives 1.993. For fixed published source deprojections and adopted baryonic forces, the allowed required-gain ratio lies **inside [0.099, 0.463]** over the entire continuous one-error distance, speed and inclination boxes. Doubling every width expands this conservative enclosure to **[0.043, 0.864]**. The expected value is one. These are deterministic parameter boxes, with no assigned joint probability. Force decomposition, independent stellar mass-to-light errors, warps and intrinsic turbulent or streaming speeds can still affect the conclusion. [All-distance proof and numbers](DISTANCE.md)

## What can gas outside the optical profiles change?

At the direct distances and nominal speeds, retaining the visible NGC 3521 source requires at least another 2.092×10¹⁰ M☉/kpc of source strength in NGC 3198. For a nonincreasing exterior density whose inner value does not exceed the tabulated edge density, joining a tail adds 2π∫Σ dr to the radial source. Its mass is at least the optical edge radius times that increase, or **3.23×10¹¹ M☉** in NGC 3198 when holding the published ordinary force fixed. Additional large amounts of actual baryonic mass would themselves alter that force and the inferred excess, so this is a missing-source requirement under the stated subtraction. The catalogue minus measured atomic inventory is 1.04×10¹⁰ M☉ including helium. Assigning the entire 3.6 µm luminosity a stellar mass-to-light ratio of 0.7 gives a remaining stellar-plus-atomic budget of 2.23×10¹⁰ M☉ after subtracting the same measured components. The latter is a calibration scenario. For arbitrary nonmonotone rings each at least 0.8 kpc wide, TV ≤ 2M/width, so the corresponding mass requirement falls to 8.37×10⁹ M☉. Finer unresolved structure can have still larger perimeter per unit mass. [Analytic bounds and inventory](distance.json)

The image follow-up replaces the atomic rings with full, naturally weighted THINGS 21-cm images while retaining the measured stellar and molecular radial fields. Both galaxies keep their direct stellar distances. A square four-neighbour boundary count at 0.8 kpc gives a total-source ratio of **4.664** at the default origin. Changing to 1.6 kpc and moving the grid origin half a cell gives 4.66–5.04 across all four settings. Only 1.15–1.28 comes from the ratio of the two atomic-only perimeters. Atomic material alone is a physically different source choice and was examined after selection. The measured excess ratio is 1.291. A separate transfer applies the gas-only law to the other eight galaxies. [Full gas-source transfer](GAS_TRANSFER.md) [Map protocol](MAP_PROTOCOL.md) · [Square-grid results](maps.json) · [Walter et al. survey and atlas](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2125) · [Public FITS maps](https://www2.mpia-hd.mpg.de/THINGS/Data.html)

The NGC 3521 and NGC 3198 image fluxes are 297.30 and 155.75 Jy km/s after clipping negative pixels. The survey catalogues 297 and 227 Jy km/s. Globally renormalizing each image to the survey values gives a combined 0.8 kpc source ratio of 4.12. Scaling to the SPARC atomic inventories gives 4.02. Either rescaling preserves the measured gas morphology and therefore cannot recover the spatial distribution of missed flux. Approximately 39.5% and 66.5% of the respective mapped H I masses lie outside the optical profiles, and 7.9% and 0.37% beyond the respective orbital radii. Counting only seams inside the observed radius gives 4.63. Applying the same finite spherical-shell transfer to measured edge locations gives 4.91. The actual radio beam, elongated in the disk plane after deprojection, limits the recovered structure. The distribution of unobserved gas, true disk thickness and the law converting these loads to a shared gravitational stress remain open. ## Two-dimensional stars, atomic gas and molecular gas

Both galaxies also have S4G dust-separated stellar images and HERACLES CO(2–1) line-intensity and uncertainty images. We kept the atomic footprint and mapped the stars and molecules inside the original Leroy optical aperture at the direct stellar distances. S4G gives the calibrated 3.6 µm mass conversion per pixel at M/L=0.6. Masked stellar regions are filled with the valid mean in the same annulus. The old-star mask or missing coverage affects about 1% and 4% of the optical areas. For CO we use the released moment and noise maps, the common line ratio of 0.8, and a Galactic conversion including helium. The HERACLES integration window uses local H I velocity information in the survey reduction. None of these source amplitudes is fitted to the pair's speed. The observed dark excess remains dependent on a gravitational mass model. ## Replay

Run from the repository root with NumPy, SciPy and pytest available.

```sh
export PYTHONPATH=code:.
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.measure
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.distance
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.maps
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.stellar_maps
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.gas_transfer
python -m cosmology.rar_deep_regime.boundary_source.gas_image_transfer
python -m pytest -q code/cosmology/rar_deep_regime/boundary_source
```

The offline input grids contain integrated atomic mass in square 0.4 kpc cells. Summing adjacent cells produces each registered map resolution. To regenerate these grids from the primary FITS, install Astropy in a suitable environment and run each of the two map modules with `--rebuild-from-fits /tmp/oph-boundary-fits`. To reacquire the two canonical author-deposited VizieR tables, run `python code/cosmology/rar_deep_regime/boundary_source/fetch.py`.
