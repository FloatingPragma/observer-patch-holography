# Which experiment sets the anomalous acceleration scale?

Use no SPARC rotation calibration. Invert the published covariance-weighted shear on each independent lens selection, at the catalogue bin centres and baryonic masses. For the untrimmed spherical profile solve the single weighted mean equation for positive √a₀, with the compact baryonic contribution separated. Transform the measured mean plus or minus its quoted two statistical standard errors through the same inverse. As a sensitivity bound, use each bin's upper edge, +0.2 dex lens masses, the positive shear factor ceiling 4/π and the measured mean minus two statistical errors to infer the lowest scale that could support that signal. Return zero when the permitted compact matter already accounts for that lower mean. These quantities are conditional on the publicly measured covariance and the stated optics and samples.

Compare with the outer Figure 8 ring of AGC 114905 as a held observation. For an untrimmed spherical exterior at that radius attribute its entire squared speed to the anomaly, giving a conservative upper scale V⁴/(GM_b). Include central values and the generous upper-speed and lower-mass setting from the cross-data protocol. Separately infer the scale for the disk-force transfer g_obs=g_bar+√(a₀ g_bar) from the independently reduced stars-and-gas curve. The two dwarf mass conventions test different predictions. An arbitrary independent halo edge would remove the dwarf upper bound on an untrimmed amplitude; the cross-data sublinear-profile inequality tests a calibration-independent alternative. No data from observers beyond the published instruments enter any comparison. The diagnostic is retrospective.
