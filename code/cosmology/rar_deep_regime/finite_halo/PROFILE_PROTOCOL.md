# Dwarf-to-shear bound for a broader family of spherical profiles

Use the target ring, mass allowances, and published shear settings in TRANSFER_PROTOCOL. Drop the assumptions of a sharp edge, a particular edge acceleration, and even a specified value of a₀. In weak-field lensing with ordinary stress optics, a positive thin spherical shell outside projected radius R has nonpositive differential surface density, while enclosed shells contribute their mass divided by πR². Thus ΔΣ(R)≤M(<R)/(πR²). Assume the same normalized enclosed anomalous mass f(x)=M_A/M_b on the compared spherical haloes, x=R/√(GM_b). Impose only nonnegative mass, nondecreasing f(x), and nonincreasing f(x)/x on the radii linking the source and lens observations. These assumptions include the conditional compact-baryon linear exterior and universal sublinear tapers. A positive compact mass intercept is also permitted when it satisfies these inequalities. The dwarf's positive total speed gives f(x_d)≤V_max² R_d/(G M_d) without dividing by the difference of two rotation curves. The resulting upper bound is f(x)≤f_max max(1,x/x_d). Apply the positive stack weights and allow any positive pair weights within each published bin. The maximum of b[1+f_max max(1,√(g_d/b))] is at the upper edge in b. Report the gaps against observed shear in the same three sensitivity settings. These are conditional standard errors on two measurements using shared KiDS imaging; do not combine them into an independent detection. The check is retrospective. An individual dwarf drawn from a distribution of different profiles would supply no bound on the entire ensemble.
