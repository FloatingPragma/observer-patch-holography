# One edge acceleration for a gas-rich dwarf and two lens stacks

Use the same positive edge acceleration g_t=GM_b/R_t² in every galaxy, with the spherical excess M_A=√(M_b a₀/G) min(r,R_t) and a nonnegative separate baryonic contribution to squared circular speed. Use the outermost Figure 8 ring of AGC 114905, its published total baryonic mass and the registered SPARC normalization. Assign its entire circular speed to the excess to derive the weakest necessary cutoff. Bound the optically projected mass of every isolated lens by the resulting cutoff; use its largest published bin-edge acceleration rather than fitting a bin weight. Repeat after lowering the dwarf's mass by two quoted errors, increasing its radius in proportion to distance plus two quoted errors, and raising its speed by two plotted errors. Also test an independently generous tilt allowance of 27 degrees instead of 31 and a +0.2 dex shift for every lens mass. Use the low training scale to check that all lens bins lie outside the edge across the tested range of a₀. In that regime a₀ cancels from the upper bound. This is a retrospective test of one common cutoff and mass calibration across populations. The dwarf uncertainties are sensitivity settings with fixed membership and no asserted joint coverage.

Compare the weighted upper limit with the published shear using the same positive, covariance-only statistic as the finite-edge test. Record the local circular-speed allowance, radius, necessary edge acceleration, actual maximum lens bin acceleration, and observed, predicted and cross components. No dwarf endpoint or lensing amplitude is fitted to data from the other channel. This experiment tests the declared exterior extension of the deep law, including its matching mass, on both classes of galaxy. A core with a different anomalous mass intercept, nonspherical source, environment-dependent cutoff, or alternative constitutive law has a different prediction.
