# Lens aperture test of a finite compensating energy supply

Use the four published baryonic mass bins of Mistele et al. (2024) and their extended isolated-lens weak-field measurements. The qualitative result of nearly flat circular-speed profiles is already known and the underlying KiDS imaging was used by previous OPH comparisons. The physical-radius mass-binned stacks and their quantitative amplitudes will be read after selecting this test. No OPH mark source, gain, mass profile, duration or compensation radius is adjusted to these data.

Use the last physical radial sample within the authors' conservative isolation domain. Report all four mass bins and a predeclared nearer control (the last sample at or below half the isolation-domain cutoff). If the authors supply only tabulated speeds at selected radii, apply the same cuts to their table. Use the top of each published baryon mass bin as an allowance on ordinary stellar and gas mass, and double that allowance as a sensitivity test. An unconstrained additional ordinary inventory can weaken the result. Carry both the supplied statistical and deprojection errors conservatively. For a positive effective spherical excess mass at a measured radius, compute the deficit required outside it in a closed equal-conversion redistribution. Translate a bound on the negative density into an outer supply radius and energy. Separate (a) no claim on supply density, (b) homogeneous ordinary baryons at the present mean with a generous past-density variant appropriate to this source sample, and (c) an independently positive cold background as a conditional scenario. Keep the signed source distinct from a new gravitating mass baseline. If direct excess surface density is available, test the exterior zero-net prediction on an actual data bin without spherical deprojection. Report honest limitations of stacking, lens isolation, the mass sheet, exterior extrapolation and correlated uncertainties.
