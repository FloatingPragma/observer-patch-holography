# Where would the energy for a dark halo come from?

If a dark gravitational signal is produced by moving already gravitating energy, every positive mass excess in an aperture needs an equal net deficit outside it. At **291.07 kpc**, four isolated-galaxy lensing stacks require exterior deficits of at least **0.40, 0.77, 2.03 and 3.42 trillion solar masses** under the favorable allowances below. The published catalogue baryonic masses cannot themselves supply those deposits, even if their entire rest energy is made available. A finite local redistribution remains possible if it can access enough energy farther out. [Mistele et al., Table 1 and §III](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ad54b0) · [Frozen data selection](PROTOCOL.md) · [Calculation and results](results.json)

An isolated stationary lens can also draw optical mass from pressure. Integrating the radial force balance bounds that contribution by the exterior mass available to support it. The four measured stacks at the common outer radius still require additional-to-baryon ratios of 5.40–8.04 under the specified favorable shifts. [Derivation, external-pressure control and all four mass bins](pressure_budget/README.md)

A comparison at two radii constrains where the surviving energy lies. For 32 outward records with compact baryons, even an arbitrary hop length permits an equivalent speed ratio of at most 0.855 from 62.71 to 291.07 kpc. The four central observed ratios are 1.007–1.070. [Radial dependence and limits from the data](trail_profile/README.md)

## What was measured

Mistele et al. infer the spherical mass inside a physical radius from tangential shear around KiDS-bright galaxies. We selected the last published radius below their conservative isolation limit of 300 kpc and a nearer control below 150 kpc before reading the numbers. Their reported speed is $V=\sqrt{\langle V_i^2\rangle_w}$, so $rV^2/G$ gives the weighted mean enclosed mass at that radius. Their weights may differ between radii. We give every galaxy the **top of its mass bin** as its ordinary-mass allowance, then double that ceiling in the favorable case. The authors estimate stars from photometry and gas from scaling relations. An additional uncounted ordinary component could alter the budget. Their sample and shapes share KiDS imaging with previous OPH comparisons. These physical-radius, mass-binned amplitudes specify no mechanism parameters here. [Lens selection and mass estimates](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09685v1#S2) · [Authors’ complete machine-readable table](https://content.cld.iop.org/journals/2041-8205/969/1/L3/revision1/apjlad54b0t1_mrt.txt)

For the favorable case take $V^2-2V(2\sigma_{\rm stat}+\sigma_{\rm sys})$ before converting to mass, then subtract twice the catalogue bin ceiling. The authors propagate the statistical error of the acceleration linearly to $V$. Applying the same Jacobian back to the full systematic is more generous than squaring a downward velocity shift. These are sensitivity bounds conditional on the assigned systematics. Gaussian acceleration errors and a one-sided Bonferroni allowance across the four outer stacks give **0.319, 0.669, 1.940 and 3.337 trillion M☉** at joint 95% conditional coverage if the full systematic and doubled mass ceiling bound their respective biases. At the 115.88 kpc control, the two-error calculation still requires **0.159, 0.556, 0.651 and 0.602 trillion M☉**. We do not combine the two radii or treat individual lens galaxies as independent tests. ## Finite compensation

Let $D(r)=\int_{|x|<r}\delta\rho\,d^3x$ be the difference from the pre-transfer gravitating density. Assume an isolated weak field, equal gravitational mass per unit energy at the donor and receiver, negligible stress and slip corrections, and $\int\delta\rho=0$. Then a measured $D(r)>0$ implies an exterior net debit of exactly $D(r)$. Additional positive deposits outside the aperture only increase the gross removal required. The mass-budget inference uses the published spherical lens deprojection and the ordinary-mass allowance. It makes no choice of record birth rate, lifetime, force profile, transfer path or donor location.

The favorable deficits are **5.41–8.06 times** the already doubled ordinary masses allowed to the galaxies. Their rest-energy equivalents range from **7.19 × 10⁵⁸ to 6.12 × 10⁵⁹ J**. This expresses the amount of transported energy for a common $E/c^2$ gravitational conversion. Mechanical work to move material and a microscopic energy gap are different quantities. No calibrated conversion of native OPH ticks to these joules exists in this test.

If the initially extractable density is at most $\rho_{\max}$ everywhere outside $r$ and all donors lie within $T$, then
$$
D(r)\le \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_{\max}[T^3-r^3]_+.
$$
The table gives lower limits on a **common maximum** supply radius across each stack. If individual radii vary, it bounds $\langle\max(T_i,r)^3\rangle_w$, and at least one lens exceeds the stated lower reach or attains it. No individual radius distribution is measured. For an individual lens with a positive excess, the same inequality holds for its own aperture. An arbitrarily dense exterior reservoir can sit immediately outside it. A transport law with a maximum total origin-to-destination displacement similarly needs access to the mass in the exterior shell reached by that displacement.

For scale, Planck’s fitted physical baryon density $\omega_b=0.02242$ gives $6.22\times10^9\,M_\odot/\mathrm{Mpc}^3$ today. Using its proper density at the upper selected photometric redshift 0.5 makes the shell smaller. At the present mean, the four radii are **2.49–5.08 Mpc**. To compensate inside **1 Mpc** requires donors at average densities at least **4.69, 9.00, 23.66 and 39.89 times** the cosmic baryon density even at $z=0.5$. A galaxy’s local gas density need not equal the cosmic mean. A subunit accessible fraction of its rest energy increases the required volume inversely. A separate homogeneous cold reservoir at Planck’s fitted $\omega_c=0.11933$ could reduce the $z=0.5$ radii to **0.96–1.94 Mpc**. In that case the positive cosmic cold energy is an additional assumption. Internal zero-net rearrangements alone have zero spatially averaged density contrast and cannot generate the homogeneous abundance. [Planck VI, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910) · [Lens redshift selection](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15248#S3)

A sharpness control places the deposit in a uniform core and completely empties a uniform donor shell from $r$ to $T$. The final density stays nonnegative and the signed mass integral vanishes. Outside $T$, both excess orbital acceleration and azimuthally averaged excess shear vanish. Inside, projected shear can include a contribution from the negative shell. In the largest favorable case at $r=291.07$ kpc, $\pi r^2\Delta\Sigma$ is 1.00061 times the deposited mass. Consequently an arbitrary measurement of shear *inside* a signed source cannot simply be treated as a positive lower bound on enclosed projected mass. Integrating the synthetic shear with the authors’ independent spherical deprojection recovers the signed enclosed mass at six radii. [Saturating source and checks](test_compensation.py)

## Independent direct-shear check

Gavazzi et al. measured the shear around 22 strong-lens galaxies using Hubble images, including a final bin centered at $287.5\,h^{-1}$ kpc. Its tangential and cross signals are $60\pm17$ and $34\pm17\,h\,M_\odot/\mathrm{pc}^2$. The previous center at $164.5\,h^{-1}$ kpc bounds every pair radius in the ordered last bin from below, or 235 kpc for $h=0.7$. If every signed perturbation is confined inside 235 kpc, it gives zero azimuthally averaged exterior shear. For nonnegative ordinary matter, its lensing contrast is at most $M_{\rm ordinary}/(\pi R^2)$ there. A uniform mass sheet cancels from linear tangential shear. The strictest compact upper limit puts all the mass inside the minimum radius. We give each lens twice its heavier Salpeter mass plus one quoted log-mass error, $2\times10^{\log M_{\rm Salp}+\sigma}$. The largest ordinary budget is $2.518\times10^{12}\,M_\odot$. [Gavazzi et al., Table 2 and shear estimator](https://doi.org/10.1086/519237) · [Auger et al., Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/705/2/1099)

With the reported mean source-distance efficiency for each lens **in this bin**, the compact hypothesis predicts at most **20.73** in the measured units, 2.31 quoted errors below the observation. If each shape-weighted source population can have arbitrary positive efficiency up to one, the upper limit becomes **34.10**, 1.52 errors below the signal. The cross component itself is two errors above zero. This is a weaker independent check on the exterior field. These lenses were selected for strong lensing and are sensitive to their environments. Any aperture mass attributed to neighboring structure reduces the amount assigned to a host reservoir. The KiDS test likewise depends on isolation, line-of-sight structure, spherical averaging and the authors’ interpolation and extrapolation of shear. It rules out a closed compact source with the specified ordinary budget as the *sole* explanation on those assumptions. Compensation farther away, a different gravity law and an additional positive baseline imply distinct tests. [Independent matched stellar inventory](../data/slacs_weak_lenses.dat) · [Source-efficiency and radius bounds](results.json)
