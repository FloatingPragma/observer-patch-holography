# M31 orbits from distributed record formation

Can the stationary mark field generated by the observed stars and gas in Andromeda supply its outer orbital speeds? A fixed finite-survival model raises the baryons-only forecast at 38.09 kpc from 131.1 to **209.6 km/s**. Chemin et al. measure **275.1 ± 4.8 km/s** there. On a different H I survey, Corbelli et al. find **240.1 ± 38.2 km/s** at 36.5 kpc, compatible with the **211.6 km/s** forecast. A separate shape calculation shows why changing the mass calibration or the survival probability alone cannot recover the steep rise in the Chemin curve. [Chemin et al., H I profile and rotation curve](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/705/2/1395) · [Corbelli et al., Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/200913297) · [Predictions at all measured radii](results.json)

## Inputs and their independence

The source uses Chemin's photometry with color-based stellar masses of 71 and 64 billion solar masses for the disk and bulge. The exponential disk scale length is 5.6 kpc. The spherical Sérsic bulge has projected half-light radius 1.3 kpc and index 1.1. We deproject its surface brightness with the Abel integral. The 100 published H I surface-density rings integrate to 4.235 billion solar masses, consistent with Chemin's integrated flux measurement of 4.23 billion. We place the published 0.35 billion solar masses of molecular hydrogen in the same radial proportions and multiply the gas inventory by 1.3 for helium. These give 140.96 billion solar masses in the source. The direct circular force includes an additional 0.14 billion solar masses in the central black hole. [Chemin et al., §§4, 8 and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3846) · [Archived columns and source hashes](inputs.json)

The absolute forecast uses an acceleration scale fitted to the separate SPARC sample. M31 is absent from that catalogue. The registration enrolled all radii beyond five stellar scale lengths in each survey and specified the source, direct force, calibration and sensitivity cases before loading the individual outer speeds. The published summaries of a warp and outer rise were already known. Chemin's H I column densities share tilted-ring inclinations with their circular-speed inference. Both comparisons use the same photometric and gas inventory and an adopted distance of 785 ± 25 kpc. The Corbelli curve comes from another survey of this same galaxy. The forward model takes target radii as inputs. A test substitutes unrelated speeds, uncertainties and inclinations and reproduces every predicted speed. ## Stationary transport and absolute speeds

On the inherited chart a mark stays at its site with probability 1/4 and takes each of six neighboring steps with probability 1/8. With survival probability $s$, stationary visits obey $u=J+sPu$. The screened continuum kernel is $e^{-\mu d}/d$ with $\mu^2=8(s^{-1}-1)/a^2$ for spacing $a$. The sparse sign-resolved record construction gives a positive quadratic form in linear combinations of visits. This test chooses one coherent positive injection field proportional to the resolved spherical distribution of baryons, averages the field over directions, and squares that field. Distinct injection channels, a flattened dark response or incoherent local emission can change the resulting density. A physical coupling of this quadratic record to mass and a common galactic length scale are assumptions. [Finite mark transport](../../../matched_einstein_limit/collar_identifiability/refinement/transport.py) · [Quadratic record calculation](../record_shear/README.md)

Let $d\nu(t)$ be the normalized stellar and gas mass in spherical injection shells. The angular average of the Green function is
$$
u_\mu(r)=\int \frac{e^{-\mu\max(r,t)}\sinh(\mu\min(r,t))}{\mu r t}\,d\nu(t).
$$
Write $w_\mu=r u_\mu$. The assigned gravitating mass obeys $M_A'(r)=C w_\mu(r)^2$ and the orbital forecast is $v^2=v_b^2+GM_A(<r)/r$. The direct baryon force uses the planar exponential stellar disk, the spherical bulge and the gas annuli in one plane with 0.5 kpc Plummer softening. The last convention represents a finite disk thickness approximately. The sensitivity calculation also uses an all-spherical direct force. Changing gas softening to 0.25 or 1 kpc changes either Chemin endpoint prediction by less than 0.2 km/s. [Calculation](measure.py) · [Disk thickness adopted by Corbelli et al., §4](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.4133)

The existing SPARC scale $a_0=8.7504\times10^{-11}$ m/s² fixes $C=\sqrt{M_b a_0/G}=9.4071\times10^9\,M_\odot/\mathrm{kpc}$ for this galaxy. This population calibration supplies the mass dependence. A universal coherent amplitude per baryon with fixed quadratic-to-mass gain would instead give $C\propto M_b^2$. The absolute finite-range comparison further assigns the Planck cold-matter/baryon ratio $0.11933/0.02242$ to this individual galaxy. Solving $C\int_0^\infty w_\mu^2\,dr=5.32248M_b$ fixes $\mu=0.0058661\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$, or a point-source density e-folding length of 85.24 kpc. The positive kernel makes the integral strictly decreasing in $\mu$, from infinity to zero. This selects the lifetime once the total-mass assumption is supplied. [Independent acceleration calibration](../finite_halo/results.json) · [Planck 2018, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910)

At infinite survival $u_0(r)=\int d\nu(t)/\max(r,t)$. Consequently $0\le w_\mu\le w_0\le1$ at every radius. This gives $M'_A\le C$ and **$v^2(r)\le v_b^2(r)+GC$** for *every* positive radial injection profile and survival at this SPARC mass calibration. Infinite survival is an upper control with finite predicted speeds inside an aperture and infinite total mass.

## A shape bound independent of dark abundance and mark lifetime

After opening the velocities we compared the first and last registered ring in each survey. For the assumed source distribution the ratio $M_A(r_2)/M_A(r_1)$ cancels the unknown positive gain $C$. The excess mass required by the observed speed is $r(v_{\rm obs}^2-v_b^2)/G$. The shape test uses neither a Planck budget nor the SPARC scale. For a fixed source it covers every $\mu\ge0$, including infinite mark survival. Values below give outward-rounded upper bounds and favorable shifts that raise the first measured speed and lower the last.

| Endpoints (kpc) | Model upper mass ratio | Required ratio from measured velocities | Required at one quoted error | Required at two quoted errors |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chemin 28.17 to 38.09 | 1.443 | 2.727 | 2.404 | 2.126 |
| Corbelli 28.5 to 36.5 | 1.352 | 2.107 | 1.021 | 0.339 |

The same upper bound holds for any nonnegative mixture of these **mass profiles** with different lifetimes and gains if each channel traces the same measured source. The combined ratio is the average of the channel ratios weighted by their contributions to the inner mass. Mixing amplitudes before squaring, changing where a channel emits, or a conversion varying with radius requires another calculation.

Conditioning on the measured inner Chemin speed allows at most **219.4 km/s** at the outer ring, for any positive gain and lifetime. The nominal deficit is 55.7 km/s. The profile bound across all 11 tested stellar-mass/direct-force cases is below 1.476. Even with two favorable quoted speed errors, the required ratio is above 1.878 for each case. On the central photometric source, also assigning 735 or 835 kpc for the common distance gives 2.076 or 2.181 respectively. At fixed angular structure changing distance rescales the source and all apertures together, so the bound covering every inverse length is invariant. The alternate Corbelli reduction admits the nominal model bound within one quoted error of its endpoints. [All aperture comparisons](aperture.json)

For the numerical enclosure, write the growth ratio as $1+\int_{r_1}^{r_2}w_\mu^2dr/\int_0^{r_1}w_\mu^2dr$. The distributed source has a finite list of spherical shells with measured mass weights $p_i$ at radii $t_i$. Each integral decreases monotonically with $\mu$ by positivity of the kernel. On each of 160 intervals up to $1\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$ use the low-$\mu$ numerator and high-$\mu$ denominator. Between successive emitting shells, $w_\mu''=\mu^2w_\mu$ makes $w_\mu^2$ convex. After splitting at every shell and aperture boundary, the trapezoid bounds the outer integral above and the midpoint rule bounds the inner integral below. [Implementation](aperture.py)

For $\mu\ge\mu_*=1\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$ let $f_i=p_i/t_i$. Then $2\mu w_\mu=\sum_i f_i[e^{-\mu|r-t_i|}-e^{-\mu(r+t_i)}]$. By the triangle inequality its outer $L^2$ norm, multiplied by $\sqrt\mu$, is at most
$$
A=\sum_{t_i\le r_1} f_i e^{-\mu_*(r_1-t_i)}/\sqrt2+\sum_{t_i>r_1} f_i.
$$
For the inner integral keep the positive self-terms and integrate each over $[t_i,t_i+1/\mu]$ when the whole interval is inside $r_1$. Multiplying its squared norm by $\mu$ gives a lower bound
$$
B=\tfrac{1-e^{-2}}{2}\sum_{t_i\le r_1-1/\mu_*} f_i^2(1-e^{-2\mu_*t_i})^2 .
$$
Thus $1+A^2/B$ bounds the full high-loss mass ratio. It is below 1.00066 for central Chemin inputs. The low- and high-loss bounds apply to the declared shell representation. Halving shell width from 50 to 25 pc reproduces the central bound to 0.0002 and every primary speed to 0.012 km/s. An independent check integrates the original three-dimensional Green kernel over angular separation. The original finite mark chart on three successively finer grids approaches that continuum Green function to better than 0.09% at all three checked radii. [Independent numerical controls](test_measure.py)

## What the two surveys resolve

The comparison constrains the chosen coherent spherical source under circular-orbit dynamics and the stated baryon inventory. M31 has a warped gas disk. Chemin's errors combine fit errors with the difference between disk halves where both are available. Some outer annuli have only the receding side and receive formal fit errors. Corbelli combines the half-difference in side velocities with the mean within-side dispersions. Shared distance, ring geometry, noncircular streaming and the same disturbed outer disk limit both reductions. Neither the ring counts nor the error-box endpoints constitute a joint significance. Distinguishing a failure of this source profile from the inference of circular speeds requires an independent orbital tracer or a common three-dimensional velocity-cube treatment of both surveys. An empirical gain or longer lifetime alone cannot produce the central Chemin shape in this source model. [Chemin et al., §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3846) · [Corbelli et al., §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.4133)

## Varying the measured modular reference

A subsequent calculation allows the tested contact-bank reference to change arbitrarily with radius at fixed ensemble energy per contact. Even with a separate ordinary calibration at each radius, the six faithful banks permit at most a 1.124-fold outer-to-inner gain. Chemin requires at least 1.888 across the paired baryon variations and favorable two-error endpoints. Corbelli permits a common gain within its errors. ## Reproduction

From the repository root use `PYTHONPATH=code:.` and one BLAS thread. The first command checks SHA-256 on both authors' TeX sources, extracts the original data and recomputes the forecast. Omitting `--fetch` uses the archived input. The second command recomputes the independent-of-gain bound.

~~~sh
python -Bm cosmology.rar_deep_regime.m31_transport.measure --fetch
python -Bm cosmology.rar_deep_regime.m31_transport.aperture
python -Bm pytest -q -W error code/cosmology/rar_deep_regime/m31_transport
~~~

The subsequent Keck stellar-halo check measures 98.0 (+7.2, −6.6) km/s dispersion. At its median radius the unchanged finite and infinite-survival fields predict 105.5 and 121.6 km/s for isotropic tracers. Unknown stellar anisotropy and the contribution from radii beyond the outer gas limit affect how well this distinguishes the H I reductions.
