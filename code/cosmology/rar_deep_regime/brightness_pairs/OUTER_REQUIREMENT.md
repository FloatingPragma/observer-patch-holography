# Which outer rings require extra inward acceleration?

Use the archived SPARC catalogue, quality 1 or 2, inclination at least 30 degrees and radius at least three reported stellar disk scale lengths. Register all such rings before classifying their speeds. Compare the ratio of total circular acceleration to the signed baryonic prediction. Give each galaxy a common distance and inclination anywhere within twice the catalogue errors, constrained to positive distance and angles between 0 and 90 degrees. Use one stellar disk and one bulge mass-to-light ratio per galaxy in the broad chosen rectangles [0.1,1.0] and [0.1,1.4] in solar units. Keep the catalogue signed gas force. Allow every speed its entire twice-quoted error interval. Report lower and upper ratios per ring, the number of galaxies requiring a positive excess at one or more outer radii, and the strongest example. These error boxes are a sensitivity test, with the catalogue mass templates held fixed.

As a separate optical proxy hypothesis, allow an arbitrary nonnegative enhancement relative to baryonic acceleration as a nonincreasing function of tabulated stellar surface brightness in each galaxy. No function is shared between galaxies. Test all pairs whose positive reported brightness differs by at least a factor of two, leaving closer pairs unconstrained. The common inclination and distance cancel exactly from each ordering inequality. Intersect the resulting linear half-planes in the common disk/bulge mass-to-light rectangle. Compare its feasibility with allowing any inward enhancement at any radius. Exclude a ring only from ratio comparisons if its baryonic force can be nonpositive inside the chosen stellar rectangle, and retain its identifier. Photometry measures stellar light. The catalogue supplies no physical overlap-record production rate; the proxy test therefore concerns this specific radial identification. Published speeds and prior brightness-pair diagnostics were known when designing this test.
