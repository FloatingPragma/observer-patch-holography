# Does the same photometric enhancement transfer between galaxies?

Keep the outer-ring catalogue, signed baryonic templates, speed error intervals, common galaxy geometry and stellar ranges of the outer acceleration test. Compare one dimensionless function for all galaxies with the per-galaxy compatibility result. Use only positive reported brightness for ordering. Grant arbitrary nonnegative inward enhancement to the zero-brightness rings as they do not supply a positive stellar surface brightness. Freeze an astronomical split by the low bit of the first SHA-256 byte of the catalogue galaxy name. Even is calibration and odd is held. The catalogue and the per-galaxy outcome were known when defining the split. This is a retrospective transfer validation.

Permit every coefficient of the brightness law to vary subject to nonnegativity and monotonicity. Use two variants. The strict law orders every reported positive brightness and identifies equal values. The conservative variant compares only brightnesses differing by a factor of two and grants independent responses even at equal tabulated brightness. Keep only constraints necessary in the latter relaxation. First look for pairs with disjoint nuisance-bounded force ratios in reverse brightness order in the calibrators, in the held group, and across groups. A calibration ring bounds the response for another galaxy independent of its measured speeds. Report opposite-order witnesses with both names, original catalogue lines, nuisance intervals and brightness ratios. When the calibration set itself is incompatible, report that outcome directly. A passing shared fit would be a consistency check with freely adjusted coefficients, without identifying stellar light with physical observer-record density.
