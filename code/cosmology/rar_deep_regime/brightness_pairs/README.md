# Stellar surface brightness and the orbital excess at matched baryonic force

A spherical source reconstructed from pair midpoints predicts enclosed mass proportional to squared radius up to its edge. Letting each galaxy choose one mass and edge admits 117 of 136 outer SPARC curves. All positive concentric mixtures admit 133, with three-ring exclusions for NGC 5907, UGC 00128 and UGC 09133 under the declared observational allowances. The same tests on four independent lensing stacks admit three for one screen and four for mixtures at twice the summed errors. Under comoving screen expansion the published dark-density dilution also requires the total weighted pair contribution to grow by a factor 1.9901–2.0038 per doubling of the cosmic scale. ## Outer rings and one law across galaxies

At radii of at least three disk scale lengths, 816 of 1,288 catalogue rings still require extra inward acceleration. They occur in 106 of the 136 selected galaxies. At 7 kpc in NGC 3741 the required total is at least 8.7082 times the modeled baryonic acceleration. This bound allows one common distance, inclination and pair of stellar mass-to-light ratios per galaxy, twice the quoted distance, inclination and speed errors, disk mass-to-light ratios of 0.1–1 and bulge ratios of 0.1–1.4 in solar units. It keeps the signed SPARC gas template fixed. These are sensitivity boxes. The study uses quality 1 or 2 and catalogue inclination at least 30 degrees. [Selection and assumptions](OUTER_REQUIREMENT.md) · [Ring-level results](outer_requirement.json) · [Catalogue and published mass models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09251)

Each of the 109 galaxies offering ordered comparisons admits its own inward enhancement that grows towards fainter positive stellar surface brightnesses separated by at least a factor of two. For a common law, assign each ring a variable \(y=g_b/g_{\rm obs}\in[0,1]\). Set \(h=(D/D_0)(\sin i/\sin i_0)^2\), \(x=h\Upsilon_d\) and \(z=h\Upsilon_b\) per galaxy. The bounds \(v_-^2y\le h v_g|v_g|+x v_d|v_d|+z v_b|v_b|\le v_+^2y\) are linear, as are the shared stellar bounds and the brightness ordering. A nonnegative rational combination of the original inequalities has a lower bound over the allowed variable box strictly above its right side. This excludes even the relaxed common law on the calibration subset and separately on the retrospectively designated held subset. Catalogue rows 772, 778 and 779 (NGC 2403), 1006 (NGC 3109), and 1931 and 1932 (UGC 00891) suffice for the calibration contradiction. No isolated pair has disjoint nuisance-bounded ratios in reverse order. [Shared-law protocol](SHARED_OUTER_ORDER.md) · [Exact witnesses and catalogue references](shared_outer_order.json) · [Constraint and certificate reconstruction](shared_outer_order.py)

Stellar surface brightness measures light per projected area. Using it as a proxy for the production of physical overlap records is an extra assumption. The result gives a quantitative target for an independently derived galactic source law and rules out this optical proxy as its sole monotone input under the stated mass models. ## Matched baryonic acceleration within each galaxy

Does the fainter part of a galaxy consistently require more apparent extra gravity? In the SPARC rings selected below, the answer depends on the galaxy. At low baryonic accelerations, 70 galaxies supplied two annuli differing by at least a factor of two in local 3.6 µm stellar surface brightness, with baryonic accelerations matched within 10%. At twice the individual quoted speed errors, two fainter annuli had a larger relative acceleration than every constant-amplitude square-root model permits, eight had a smaller one, and 60 overlapped its prediction. All 70 fainter annuli were farther out in the same galaxy. Their common telescope distance cancels from the comparison. A smaller visible surface brightness therefore cannot by itself enforce the proposed monotone increase of the square-root amplitude on these nominal mass models. ## What is being observed?

In OPH, local record density $\rho$ counts **records made and retained by the source system per physical volume**. At fixed physical depth $\delta$ the dense-recovery premise bounds the defect by $\kappa B\exp[-(\delta\rho^{1/3}-r_0)/\zeta]$. On the sparse erasure model, the retention probability $p$ yields defect $(1-p)/2$. The bound permits different actual stresses at the same density; it is an upper bound and does not imply that every sparse patch gravitates more. Establishing a galaxy-level law requires an independent measurement of local retention, a calibration between recovery failure and gravitating stress, and the ordinary gravity response on the matching physical region. The paper gives no measured mapping from a stellar luminosity surface density to $\rho$ or $p$. Nor do the deposited galaxy orbital/lensing records contain the physical observer retention counts. A repeat observation from another telescope can refine the orbital or lensing measurement but leaves the records formed in the galaxy intact. [Dark-sector paper, galactic collar and dense-recovery sections](../../../../cosmology/oph_dark_matter_paper.tex) · [PR-100, PR-135–137](../../../../docs/registers/OBSERVABLES.json)

Here the operational proxy is $\Sigma_{3.6}={\rm SBdisk}+{\rm SBbulge}$, the local stellar light per projected area. It tracks starlight, while the catalogue separately models the gas force. Changing the assumed distance scales disk area and luminosity by the same factor in the nearby-galaxy catalogue convention. Measuring the luminosity field is distinct from acquiring neighbors' retained physical records. The authors of SPARC have already tested acceleration-relation residuals against galaxy properties. This additional retrospective comparison holds the galaxy fixed, matches two nominal baryonic accelerations, and uses a bound valid for **every** square-root acceleration scale. It supplies neither a second galaxy population nor a new survey. [SPARC catalogue and original mass-model paper](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-6256/152/6/157) · [Primary residual analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08981)

## Selection and falsifiable sign

SPARC's parent sample has 175 galaxies. Quality 1 or 2 and inclination at least 30° leave 153. For each acceleration cut the protocol picks the greatest positive brightness ratio from qualifying pairs using only the photometric/baryonic columns. Each galaxy then contributes at most one contrast. Speeds and speed uncertainties are first opened after both selections. The catalogue was used in earlier project studies, so this is a retrospective, velocity-blind selection, not a prospective new observation. The SPARC gas contribution retains its sign and its published helium correction. Disk and bulge mass-to-light ratios are fixed at 0.5 and 0.7. Published near-zero positive surface brightness is accepted; rounding can influence the largest contrast. Both selected annuli must individually satisfy the cut. [Data-column definitions](../data/ReadMe) · [Exact-rational selection and comparisons](measure.py)

Write $t=g_{b,f}/g_{b,h}$ for baryonic acceleration in the faint and bright annuli. At angular radii $\theta_f,\theta_h$ and observed rotation speeds $v_f,v_h$, the orbital acceleration ratio is
$$
 R=(v_f/v_h)^2\,\theta_h/\theta_f.
$$
A common inclination correction cancels from the speed ratio. Distance cancels from the radius ratio. At fixed angular mass and light profiles, $v_b^2$ and physical radius both scale linearly with the assigned distance, so the catalogue baryonic accelerations entering the cuts also stay fixed under this common distance rescaling. A local warp, pressure support or a radius-dependent change to the mass model can change the comparison.

For $g_{\rm orb}=g_b+\sqrt{a_0g_b}$ let $u=\sqrt{a_0/g_{b,h}}$. The predicted ratio is
$$
 R_0=\frac{t+u\sqrt t}{1+u},\qquad
 \min(t,\sqrt t)\le R_0\le\max(t,\sqrt t).
$$
This interval covers any $a_0\ge0$ and uses no fit. For the stronger brightness hypothesis allow a local multiplier of the excess, $g_{\rm orb}=g_b+A(\Sigma_{3.6})\sqrt{g_b}$, with $A_f\ge A_h\ge0$ when the annulus is fainter. Replacing the faint amplitude by the bright one can only decrease the predicted ratio. Hence $R\ge\min(t,\sqrt t)$ is a necessary condition even when each galaxy has its own normalization. An upper measured ratio below that threshold is a conditional counterexample to the monotone proxy. An excess above the upper shape bound is compatible with such a proxy but also with other changes to the orbital model. A ratio inside the interval does not demonstrate a shared global amplitude.

## How much can stellar calibration change the eight lower ratios?

Hold the eight original pairs and the two-error speed boxes fixed. Allow separate common disk and bulge mass-to-light ratios in $[0.4,0.6]$ and $[0.56,0.84]$ for each galaxy, with its signed gas contribution fixed. These ranges give a sensitivity test with no assigned probability. For a given annulus, the baryonic force is affine in the two mass-to-light ratios. Both annuli remain positive at all four corners. At an intermediate point, write the two affine forces as convex combinations of the corner values with the same weights. Their ratio is a weighted mean of the corner ratios, with weights proportional to the bright-ring forces. The extrema therefore occur at corners. The deep cut and both force-matching inequalities are also linear constraints on the forces. [Sensitivity protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Exact-rational calculation](sensitivity.py)

Five pairs remain below the monotone bound throughout this stellar range. Independently, five pairs have a two-error orbital upper endpoint below $10/11$, the smallest possible lower shape bound for *any* positive forces satisfying the original 10% match. Three pairs belong to both groups. Thus seven conflicts persist at every allowed stellar calibration that also keeps their two forces matched. In UGC07125 a disk ratio of 0.6 permits the lower shape inequality while retaining the original cuts. Reaching that inequality establishes a necessary condition only. UGC06667 alone stays deep and matched and remains discrepant throughout the entire stellar range. This calculation keeps the original annuli and does not ask whether another pair would become the largest brightness contrast at a changed calibration. [Results for all eight galaxies](sensitivity.json)

Even without a stellar model one can bound the force change needed. Let $H$ be the largest orbital ratio permitted by the two speed boxes. The lower shape bound can be reached only if the true force ratio is at most $q=\max(H,H^2)$. Independent relative changes of magnitude at most $e$ in both nominal baryonic accelerations can lower their ratio from $t$ to $t(1-e)/(1+e)$. Consequently the required tolerance is at least $(t-q)/(t+q)$, ranging from 0.807% in UGC12732 to 13.061% in UGC02259. This is the most favourable opposing adjustment at the two radii. Published speed errors alone supply no error budget for the inferred baryonic forces.

## Independent total hydrogen flux

A direct measurement tests the proposed repair in UGC06667. This is the only one of the eight original lower ratios that remains deep, matched and discrepant throughout the stellar calibration range. To meet the lower orbital shape inequality while keeping the measured gas profile and disk mass-to-light range, its gas mass must fall to at most **0.318861** of the catalogue normalization. SPARC gives $M_{\rm HI}=8.09\times10^8 M_\odot$ at 18 Mpc. The optically thin conversion $M_{\rm HI}/M_\odot=235600(D/\mathrm{Mpc})^2 S_{21}/(\mathrm{Jy\,km\,s^{-1}})$ corresponds to 10.598 Jy km/s and sets the greatest admitted flux at **3.38 Jy km/s**. The WSRT synthesis observation gave $11.0\pm0.4$ Jy km/s. A separate Fisher--Tully single-dish measurement gave $11.6\pm2.2$ Jy km/s. Its lowest value at twice the quoted error is **7.2 Jy km/s**. Even dividing by the *highest* two-error WSRT flux gives a single-dish/synthesis ratio of 0.610, above the orbital limit. The SPARC mass and resolved WSRT observations share their gas provenance; the single-dish flux supplies the independent comparison. Table 3 lists one single-dish measurement for this galaxy, with no caution flag. Its cited literature reference is 1. [Verheijen & Sancisi, Table 3](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J%2FA%2BA%2F370%2F765%2Ftable3) · [Original Fisher & Tully survey](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1981ApJS...47..139F/abstract) · [Deposited fluxes and quoted errors](ugc06667_flux.tsv)

If the flux scales the full gas mass profile uniformly, Newtonian gravity scales its signed radial force by the same factor. The measured total therefore excludes this repair for the specified stellar masses, fixed gas shape, adopted inclination and velocity boxes. SPARC gives an inclination of 89 degrees; line-of-sight projection, radial gas distribution, finite thickness, opacity and noncircular motion can affect that transfer. An unresolved flux supplies a single integral. A redistribution of gas between annuli can keep the total flux constant and change both radial forces. The single-dish measurement cannot resolve that alternative. Radially resolved gas columns with a separately determined geometry and an independent orbital tracer would test it. The quoted error boxes carry no combined confidence.

A second radio survey provides a uniform check over all the original 70 pairs that appear in Yasin & Desmond's published Table 2. The authors matched 40 SPARC galaxies and retained 20 with suitable ALFALFA profiles. Ten of these supply a frozen deep pair and 60 of the 70 pairs have no published match in that table. The authors used the same distance for both masses and assign a 10% calibration uncertainty to each. They document possible missed outer flux for extended or faint gas. The compact table here retains the printed precision of both logarithmic masses and the AGC identifier. Its source is published p. 2115, Table 2, extracted with Poppler from the institutional PDF (SHA-256 `702959545048a1d727b8620e42c691d6cf9c46d570eb0f4b548999fdb3a2b795`). [Primary article and published PDF](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/104814958/staf453.pdf) · [All 20 mass pairs](hydrogen_masses.tsv)

At the printed central mass ratios, all ten fixed pairs overlap the square-root shape interval at the nominal stellar masses, with no adjustment of either orbital speed beyond its predeclared two-error box. This check allows the pairs to leave their original baryonic-force selection. Requiring both original cuts at the new calibration, allowing the full stellar range and taking both reported 10% mass calibrations and mass rounding at their most favourable values, leaves **eight overlaps, one below and one outside the force selection**. None lies above. UGC05750 can no longer meet the cuts, and UGC12732 is the lower case. The latter has an independently measured common gas factor $10^{-0.02}=0.9550$. A factor this close to unity suffices to reach its shape interval if the two annuli may cease to be force-matched. UGC12732 is the only original lower-ratio case in the published 20-galaxy table. The stellar and gas ranges permit different values in each galaxy. These are shape compatibility statements; they determine neither an excess amplitude nor a galaxy's source.

**Computing the needed gas change.** Write the faint and bright baryonic forces as $G_j=c Q_j+d D_j$, where $c\ge0$ is one gas factor, $Q_j$ the nominal signed gas force, and $d\in[0.4,0.6]$ the disk mass-to-light ratio. The tabulated bulge force is zero in every annulus used for this gas calculation. For a positive orbital ratio box $[L,H]$, the closed constant-amplitude shape interval overlaps it precisely when $\min(L,L^2)G_h\le G_f\le\max(H,H^2)G_h$. The upper inequality is necessary for the monotone brightness prescription. The measured forces must also stay positive. The selection, when imposed, adds $G_j<1.2\times10^{-11}$ m/s² and $G_f/G_h\in[10/11,11/10]$. Every condition is linear in $c,d$. Eliminating $d$ by comparing each resulting lower bound to each upper bound yields the projected interval of $c$ exactly, including any excluded endpoint. The base-10 mass ratios are enclosed on a rational grid using exact integer powers. Each logarithm can move by half of its last printed digit, and the calibration box permits the mass ratio to move by factors of $9/11$ and $11/9$. [Exact projection and all 70 coverage flags](hydrogen.json) · [Implementation](hydrogen.py)

Five of the seven previous conflicts under the original cuts admit **no** common gas scale if the forces must still match. UGC04325 can reach the lower inequality for $0.56543536\ldots\le c<0.94761984\ldots$. UGC06667 requires $0\le c\le0.31886071\ldots$, and the measured flux excludes this range under the fixed-shape assumptions. The eighth original case, UGC07125, admits $c=1$. The archive gives the projected intervals for all eight both with and without the original cuts. An interval endpoint is a necessary shape condition. No absolute speed fit or radial gas redistribution follows from it.
