# Direct weak-lensing test of the midpoint source

Take the truncated spherical density proportional to inverse radius obtained from isotropic pairs on one round screen. Apply ordinary thin-lens projection and derive its excess surface density. On each ordered pair of radii, find the full range of the far to near signal ratio as the edge moves from zero to infinity. Positive sums of screens and a compact ordinary baryonic mass must obey the resulting two bounds. Use every pair in the six previously enrolled low-acceleration isolation bins of the KiDS-bright and GAMA samples from Brouwer et al. The datasets have been analyzed before. This is a retrospective shape test.

Use the measured tangential excess surface density and the released covariance after the multiplicative shear correction. Test at the centres and under arbitrary within-bin sampling of a common nonnegative profile. Also report the cross component as a zero-signal control using the same covariance. Retain both signed contrasts for all fifteen bin pairs. Use a one-sided Gaussian 5% Bonferroni threshold for the thirty contrasts in each tangential sample and keep the two overlapping surveys separate. The horizontal axis is the published point-baryon acceleration, proportional to inverse squared projected radius for each individual lens. The transfer to a stack assumes fixed lens weights, compact ordinary baryons and spherical midpoint components centered on those baryons. No comparison requires a halo amplitude, edge or cosmological length fitted from these shear bins.
