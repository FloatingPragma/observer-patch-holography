# Galaxy test of attraction to the local concentration of written records

The native cold release measures an inward response to the local number of incident written contacts. This quantity decreases away from an isolated trail. Test a conditional application in which a fixed physical volume has a fixed number of available sites, record incidence is proportional to the deposited shell density, and the rest-wave kinetic coefficient transfers to slow circular tracers. The additional specific potential is minus one positive constant times that density. This differs from assigning a Newtonian mass to each shell. The original held galaxy scores for the latter assignment are known.

Use all ten resolved surface-density maps and all 369 enrolled SPARC rings of the measured-source analysis. Use the same mass-weighted launch annuli, catalogue baryonic gravity, stellar length units, measured contact-end fractions (two ends per written seam, graph distance from the original launch port) and outward/folded direction prescriptions. In spherical averaging a seed in annulus [a,b] contributes a density proportional to the length of its intersection with [|R-h|,R+h] divided by R h (b²-a²), at observed radius R and shell displacement h. For a zero-hop end use the unshifted annular density. Measure the mean of minus its log-radius derivative in a fixed window [0.98R, 1.02R] by a density difference divided by the log-radius width. This includes jumps at annulus edges and assumes the tabulated speed represents that narrow window. At an exact endpoint of an unshifted annulus use half weight. Report cases with negative total squared speed as having no circular orbit. For the median speed residual only, score those cases at zero speed.

For each target galaxy and route, choose the shared hop length (the original 97 grid values) and a single positive potential conversion on the other nine galaxies. Minimize the galaxy-equal mean squared error in squared speed, with a nonnegative conversion and the smallest scale on ties. A predictive nonzero amplitude cannot be inferred from the native calculation in astronomical units. This fit grants a favorable calibration to the continuation. Report full held predictions, medians and counts within twice the quoted speed errors, with catalogue geometry fixed. Also ask if the shape alone can meet the measured speeds, allowing an independent positive length and normalization for each galaxy. For a separate continuous shape check use up to seven equally spaced ranks among rings whose entire extra squared-speed interval is positive. Widen the bounds by 0.001 squared km/s in numerical arithmetic. Test every interval between geometric breakpoints, including the zero-length limit and the asymptotic shape. A feasible subset is inconclusive for the whole curve.
