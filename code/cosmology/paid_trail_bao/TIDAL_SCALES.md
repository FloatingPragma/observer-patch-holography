# Physical scale of the measured exterior tide

Use the same exterior tide, the five ordinary single-insertion calibrations, the closed patch eligibility, the two image tracers, the seven capacities and all 369 enrolled speeds. The previous disk test chose one stellar scale length per hop. The comparisons below were specified after that test. Retain failed calibrations, radii and galaxies.

1. Rewrite the planar force integral about the observer. An imaged annulus has constant surface density, and its angular first moment at a specified separation is a difference of two disk arcs. Bound the signed acceleration over every positive hop length, including the zero- and infinite-length limits, and over mixtures with the same total patch mass. Also allow each source annulus its own scale distribution. Compare the resulting necessary bounds directly with the two-error rotation bands.
2. Use the imaged opportunities to give the length a physical reading. In the existing 0.8 kpc map an observer at a returned patch compares to its nearest other returned patch. Use that separation as one hop, then repeat with the six-hop peak spanning that separation. Conditional on a patch at the first site, compute the full distribution of nearest observed neighbors under independent enrollment at the other sites. If none returns in the imaged field, count the attempt in the complement and assign zero field in this protocol. Compute radial source weights and the signed force from all annuli, including exterior annuli. Fit no length, amplitude or radial distribution to an orbit. These are proposed correspondences between image opportunities and native comparison lengths.
3. Compare equal mass per logarithmic length interval with equal mass per linear length interval on a compact source. Derive the enclosed-field dependence and its normalization from the measured radial kernel. Count the material required to populate a band of scales. Distinguish a physical distribution demonstrated by the source from any distribution supplied for this sensitivity test.
