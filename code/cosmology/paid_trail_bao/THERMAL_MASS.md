# Does the measured thermal energy supply the gravitating abundance?

The equilibrium measurement on the fixed two-doubles source gives a positive internal-energy change. The outer galaxy requirements and the previous source profiles are known. Test the alternative in which each four-owner patch supplies one separately equilibrated ordinary pair and the measured change in its internal energy gravitates at the same mass per energy as an ordinary load. Calibrate one ordinary double by the phase of its uniform blank-board mode against an isolated storage rail, with the native six-slot completion already used in the material experiments. This is a declared insertion-energy standard. Its physical identification with baryonic rest mass and universal gravitational coupling are assumptions of this test.

Use the previously registered two tracer maps, seven common capacities, native two-head probabilities and separately conserved sector weights. Four independently prepared owners at filling p supply four times p ordinary doubles in expectation. Assign all of the observed tracer ordinary mass to that stock, giving each double one fourth of the full cell mass. Treat the ordinary reader bath as provided. Convert the measured thermal internal-energy difference per attempt using the measured ordinary energy standard, integrate over every observed aperture, and use ordinary Newtonian G on its spherical continuation. Fit no orbital velocities, per-galaxy gain or source profile. Keep signed masses even if negative. Count every ring and report the largest speed change from the baryon-only prediction and the full set of held residuals.

Also bound every recovery preserving both head marginals: a magnitude-two record on either head requires at least one initially loaded owner, so the change of its doubly positive probability cannot exceed the probability of that inventory. Derive a bound on excess energy per unit of ordinary material for arbitrary access in this one-bath preparation, test it on the Planck cold-to-baryon benchmark, and compare with the measured NGC 2841 outer mass requirement even granting the entire galaxy's detected baryon inventory. A positive Poisson flux conditional on that energy coupling and an actual common geometry can be distinguished by the ordinary-controlled measurements.
