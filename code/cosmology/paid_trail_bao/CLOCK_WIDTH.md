# Does a clock-rate distribution with the observed width explain the orbits?

Test two precise interpretations of a macroscopic stationary clock lapse. In a renewal clock the observer samples a local rate until the next tick. If the mean rate per renewal is fixed by the ordinary gravitational baseline, the fractional variance of rates sampled in time determines the slowdown. Also test a local proper-time clock whose mean squared rate is fixed by that baseline, as when a signed zero-mean field enters the squared lapse. Grant a common clock and orbital potential in each case.

Use all ten galaxies in the existing THINGS–SPARC image comparison. Compare the radial change of the inferred anomalous potential to the actual H I second moments from the naturally weighted THINGS maps. At each optical depth assume the emitting gas samples the timerate distribution, individual frequency offsets persist through an emission and the ordinary Doppler contribution is uncorrelated with the clock. Interpret the measured widths as upper limits on the contribution within the detected emission. Use the annulus nearest half the last measured rotation radius through the last point, retaining positive and negative speed-squared residuals. Keep the outer-boundary clock dispersion free and nonnegative. Reduce the observed speeds by two quoted errors before computing the lower-end integral, keep the published baryonic reduction fixed, and use linear interpolation of the speed-squared residual in log radius. Report the median, flux-weighted RMS and maximum recorded width in the starting ring, the mapped emission coverage, and the minimum number of equally weighted independent phase samples per emission that would reduce an intrinsic width to the measured RMS. Propagate every measured outer ring, including those where no positive lower bound follows. The per-ring speed shifts are a sensitivity box. The noise-masked moment maps give no bound on emission beyond their masks or on fluctuations coherent across an entire beam.

To avoid assuming a common zero for the fluctuations, also derive the exact bound from the difference of the two boundary widths, and test the response when the variance contribution accounts for only part of the potential. Keep the same source distribution on the laboratory clock comparisons. A match to a conditional clock rule alone would leave the physical identification of galaxy records open.
