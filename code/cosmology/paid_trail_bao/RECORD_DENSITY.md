# Do two observed seams predict a galaxy's radial source?

Use every ring in the existing ten-galaxy SPARC, THINGS and Leroy comparison. Keep its distances, disk baryonic speeds, face-on 0.8 kpc cell and spherical continuation. The earlier pair-abundance results on these galaxies are known. Freeze this source law before calculating its new galaxy scores.

One attempted four-owner patch per fixed area, with a fixed column depth and mass per loaded owner, gives material filling probability p equal to the measured tracer surface density divided by one common capacity. Compare atomic gas including helium and the same map plus the published stellar and molecular annuli. For each tracer the largest cell surface density within the last measured orbit across all ten images sets the minimum possible capacity. Use 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64 and 256 times that value. These are common sensitivity settings. A galaxy's measured orbital speeds never enter its filling law. Use the full cell values before annular sums.

Use the native source experiment's connected-log change per attempted patch as the density. Enroll the two-head receiver with either no other recovery record or the full third head available to the repair. Fix the native-to-recovered orientation. Integrate the signed source over each aperture, using zero for empty patches. Keep negative contributions. Fit one nonnegative mass conversion to squared speeds of the other nine galaxies with equal total weight per galaxy. Report undefined circular speeds as misses. Also intersect each withheld galaxy's individual two-error constraints to test whether any nonnegative conversion could reproduce its entire curve, even using that galaxy's velocities. Archive the measured peak densities, all integrated source profiles and held scores. Directly compare baryons alone.

The common capacity, access to the third head, fixed depth, mass conversion and spherical continuation are physical assumptions of this comparison. The radio and light images trace ordinary material. They do not measure which additional OPH records a repair can access. These assumptions bound the interpretation of either a success or a failure.
