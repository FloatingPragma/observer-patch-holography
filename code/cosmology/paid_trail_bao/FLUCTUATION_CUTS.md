# Do repeated fluctuation readings give the radial mass inferred from orbits?

Test the dark matter paper's proposal that each successive cut carries a positive gravitating contribution proportional to the amplitude of the unrecovered fluctuation. Use the native probability of material transit before return on each of the three measured rings. For uniform independent sampling its finite law is fixed by the local per-appointment crossing frequency. Compare also its long-ring limit. The radial rate of cuts and the conversion from the transit fraction to gravitating mass per unit length combine into one unknown gain. A fitted value is a test of the shape.

Use both previous tracers on all ten THINGS/Leroy images. Use the original 0.8 kpc cells, crop, two-error SPARC speed intervals and seven capacities. A pixel of filling p can supply one ordinary single with probability p. For the closed source use two independent four-owner preparations in the pixel. Both must pass the closed 2200 selection and their two middle flags must disagree. One preparation is eligible with probability e=6 p²(1-p)² (448/2110). Given eligibility the flag reads positive with probability f=3/28 in the original law and 5/28 in the repair. The native per-appointment transfer on their new written comparator is e² f(1-f)/2. Keep the original, repaired and repaired-minus-original transit responses. Rejects of either arm disable its comparator and consume the sampled address. This rule uses two independent inventories. One donor gives per-appointment probability p/4. These are ideal, synchronized samples of recorded surface density. Production of this stock and physical equilibration over each cut remain hypotheses.

At each cut, count only contributions at imaged pixel centers at smaller or equal radius. For a fixed-size pooled address book, use the same denominator in every galaxy and at every radius, the number of 0.8 kpc grid centers in the largest observed aperture among the ten galaxies (including empty addresses). Keep all unused addresses as disabled comparators. This choice isolates growth of the enclosed variance. Integrate the stepwise transit probability over radius, using the actual pixel radii without interpolation. Grant an arbitrary common nonnegative gain to convert this integral divided by radius into extra circular speed squared at the measured radii. Retain the best simultaneous count and any wholly accommodated galaxy. In addition fit one gain on each set of nine galaxies and predict all radii of the tenth. Choose the training coefficient by equal-galaxy mean squared residual in extra squared speed; keep the tracer, capacity and receiver fixed. Report the direct donor as an ordinary control. Existing orbital comparisons are known before this test.

The two-copy results are known before adding the direct middle-port write. For this read one eligible board suffices. Its signed middle contrast is n−1, and the native single transfers with probability e f/4 per attempt. Test the same maps, ring lengths, gains, and the three original, repaired and difference profiles. Treat this as a distinct forward prediction.

Independently test the entire class of nonnegative, nondecreasing per-cut amplitudes and constant cut density. Its enclosed mass is convex and nondecreasing. On all eligible rings of each original bulgeless or imaged galaxy, allow any such function through zero. Also test just the outer half of available radii with a free nonnegative initial mass and no constraint from the interior. Use observed ranges of r times extra squared speed at the existing fixed stellar and gas reductions. Keep all failures and their two- or three-radius certificates. This shape test covers unknown gains, source placement and normalizations only under the stated monotonicity and spherical force identification. ## A disjoint lensing comparison

Keep all ten mapped disks for an equal-galaxy least-squares normalization and the previously enrolled two outer SLACS shear bins. Test the mapped-baryon calibration for the one-donor control and the direct middle read, including its repaired-minus-original increment. Treat the published stellar masses as the compact baryonic stock and assume the same exterior potential affects light and orbital motion. At radii outside the stock, constant mass per radial interval gives a term proportional to 1/R in excess surface density and an unknown compact term proportional to 1/R². Difference the two radius-squared weighted bins to cancel that compact term. Use the catalog's representative radii, efficiency-squared weights constant across bins, untruncated exterior and linear shear. Report tangential and cross contrasts with one- and two-quoted-error boxes. The pair weights and covariance needed for a likelihood on that contrast are unavailable in these summary tables. These lens records were previously studied with other transfers.

The stars determine total filling in the common address book. Allow either nominal Chabrier or Salpeter masses for the ordinary control. For the direct read give every lens its full mass range between Chabrier minus one reported error and Salpeter plus one, and allow arbitrary pixel fillings in [0,1] with that mean. Bound the mean of p²(1-p)² by its concave envelope and retain zero as the lower bound. Apply the finite receiver, the gain determined only from the disks, and the common stack weights. The resulting bound relaxes the spatial distribution of stars inside the aperture.
