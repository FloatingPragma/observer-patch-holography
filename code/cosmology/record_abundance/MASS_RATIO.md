# A volume-independent mass budget for mismatching seams

Assign a fixed positive mass to one active ordinary double and another fixed mass to the finite response of one disagreeing seam. Count seams by their active endpoint. Use the stationary preparation's occupancy 0.16 and Planck 2018 VI Table 2's primary spectra, lensing and BAO central cold-dark to baryon density ratio when testing the hypothesis that these components supply all cold dark matter and baryonic matter. Derive the bound before any mass or length calibration, the exact change under splitting an edge equally between its two ends, and the correction upon subtracting a homogeneous background. Check sharpness and boundary signs by enumeration. Report conditions that make an exterior contribution observable.

Use the published extended-rotation inventory of NGC 3741 in Begum et al. (2008), listing the global helium-corrected H I and stellar masses and the outer dynamical mass. Count the whole inventoried baryonic mass inside the rotation aperture to favor the source. Calculate the total enclosed baryon mass that would suffice and the additional, uncounted mass needed. Account for changed observations in Begum et al. (2005), distance, geometry and unmeasured warm or hot gas. Compare to the complete Planck-cosmology aperture inventories in Gonzalez et al. (2013), keeping all measured gas-plus-star entries and the published high-mass mean, without treating clusters or earlier measurements as independent confidence claims. The newer dwarf inventory and the cosmic ratio were already known when choosing this test. No observational mass ratio may set a per-galaxy gain.
