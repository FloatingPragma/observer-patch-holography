# Can ordinary observations determine how often a galaxy loses its records?

Draco's stellar light and size require **at least 27,400 times as many native sites per proper volume as the cosmic preparation**, at a stellar $V$-band mass-to-light ratio of one. They leave the microscopic occupation undetermined. A separate comparison with stellar velocities gives the two-step witness model an occupation ceiling of **0.0487** under an explicit gas inventory and stellar conversion. Using this ceiling as the source input would use the gravitational signal to set the quantity meant to predict it. Draco was chosen after its velocity-resolved diffuse-gas limit and half-light mass were known. [Protocol](SPHEROIDAL_PROTOCOL.md) · [Full numerical results](spheroidal.json)

## What the ordinary census measures

If each double has the same baryonic mass $m_b$, then $\rho_b=m_b n_s p$ with site density $n_s$ and mean occupation $p$. Relative to the cosmic reference this gives
$$
 (n_s/\bar n_s)\,p=p_0\,\rho_b/\bar\rho_b.
$$
At $p_0=0.16$, $\omega_b=0.02242$, the 291 pc sphere contains only $0.6423\,M_\odot$ of baryons at the cosmic mean. Draco contains half its luminosity there, $1.10\times10^5 L_\odot$. For $\Upsilon_V=1$, stars alone give $n_s/\bar n_s\ge27{,}402$ because $p\le1$. This increases proportionally with the adopted stellar conversion. The result uses the deprojected half-light radius and cosmic baryon density without using orbital masses. [Wolf et al., Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2995) · [Planck VI, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06209#page=16)

For a finite counterexample, assign two native refinements the same physical aperture and the same eight doubles on every trial. Their stationary exchange laws make each eight-subset uniform. Each of the twenty equal ancestral faces has mean ordinary count $8/20$ in both preparations. A mismatched seam loses the accessible record exactly when no other occupied site lies within two steps of its endpoints. Given a specified active endpoint, the probability that the opposite endpoint and the $m$ witnesses are empty is $\binom{N-m-2}{K-1}/\binom{N-1}{K-1}$ at fixed total $K$. Three times the mean of this probability over edges gives the failed cuts per double.

| Sites in the same aperture | Occupation | Edges with 11 / 12 witnesses | Expected failed cuts per double |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 80 | 0.100 | 60 / 60 | 0.85301 |
| 320 | 0.025 | 60 / 420 | 2.24218 |

The lost signal changes by a factor of 2.63 at fixed total ordinary mass and identical mean baryonic map on the ancestral faces. The ensembles differ in microscopic vacancies and in the variance of repeated subaperture counts. A physical ruler for the native sites or direct measurements of these fluctuations could distinguish them. An integrated stellar luminosity supplies neither readout. ## Independent gas constraints

**Warm ionized gas.** Gallagher et al. reported a $3\sigma$ velocity-resolved upper limit of 0.024 Rayleigh in a one-degree beam centered on Draco for an emission-line dispersion of 15 km/s. At 8,000 K each Rayleigh corresponds to emission measure $2.5\ {\rm pc\,cm^{-6}}$. We add their full 0.003 R stellar-absorption allowance and allow 10% foreground attenuation. The beam contains the entire projected 291 pc sphere. For full beam area $A$ and sphere volume $V$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $M_{\rm ion}\le 1.33 m_p\sqrt{V\,A\,{\rm EM}}$ with consistent length units. Uniform filling maximizes mass. This permits **$1.075\times10^5\,M_\odot$** of warm gas including helium. Hotter gas, strong internal extinction, broad lines or gas with a different systemic velocity require separate tracers. [Gallagher et al., §§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0301228)

**Neutral gas.** The Green Bank Telescope upper limit of 133 $M_\odot$ of H I covers the smaller projected half-light ellipse. We extracted five original LAB survey longitude maps to cover the full 291 and 900 pc spheres. In the 59.77 km/s window centered at $v_{\rm LSR}=-274$ km/s, the summed integrals over the four inner and fourteen outer sightlines were 1.34 and 5.56 K km/s. The three essentially equal-width control windows gave sums from $-0.14$ to $0.60$ and from $-0.38$ to $1.74$ K km/s, respectively. We include any positive line strength without fitting a baseline or subtracting foreground emission. [Spekkens et al., Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.0028) · [LAB survey](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/VIII/76/ReadMe) · [Extracted sky spectra](draco_lab.npz)

For optically thin hydrogen each K km/s corresponds to $1.823\times10^{18}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}$. To bound flux throughout the aperture we use the FITS header's effective 40-arcmin northern beam, approximate its main lobe as Gaussian and bound its summed response over 0.01-degree sky patches. The minimum response sums are at least 1.42 and 1.31. Multiply the sum of upper column estimates by the beam area divided by this lower response. We allow five random standard errors with 0.09 K rms over 2 km/s independent spectral elements, a coherent 0.04 K baseline over the line, and fully correlated sky positions. The resulting H I ceilings are **$2.78\times10^5$** and **$1.06\times10^6\,M_\odot$**. Assuming fully correlated errors even across all velocity channels raises these to $1.08\times10^6$ and $4.10\times10^6\,M_\odot$. These are sensitivity calculations dependent on spectral covariance, optical depth, velocity coverage and the effective beam approximation of an undersampled survey. [Original FITS maps](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/VIII/76/bvmaps/) · [Instrument and control-window results](spheroidal.json)

## Stellar dynamics and local recovery

Wolf et al. give $M_{1/2}=(2.11\pm0.31)\times10^7\,M_\odot$ from 206 stellar velocities, 3D half-light radius $291\pm14$ pc, distance $76\pm5$ kpc and total luminosity $2.2^{+0.7}_{-0.6}\times10^5 L_\odot$. At 900 pc, Vitral et al.'s analysis of 18 years of HST proper motions and line-of-sight velocities gives $v_c=24.19^{+6.31}_{-2.97}$ km/s, equivalent to central total $1.224\times10^8\,M_\odot$ using their Eq. 14. Both are dynamical inferences dependent on equilibrium and geometry. We maximize the outer stellar inventory by including the whole galaxy's luminosity. WHAM covers only part of this sphere. If the warm emissivity is spherically distributed, the fraction visible on every shell is at least $1-\sqrt{1-(R_{\rm beam}/900\ {\rm pc})^2}=0.324$. The resulting warm-gas ceiling is $1.027\times10^6\,M_\odot$. Arbitrarily asymmetric emission outside the beam remains unconstrained. [Wolf et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/0908.2995) · [Vitral et al., Eq. 14 and §VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07769v1)

With Planck's cosmic cold-to-baryon ratio 5.32248 supplied externally, divide $5.32248/(1-0.16)$ by the cosmic loss fraction to obtain the largest possible local dark mass per baryon. The cosmic fractions on the reference 80-cell board are 1 for constant loss, $0.84^4$ for one-step access, and $(0.84^{11}+0.84^{12})/2$ for two-step access. The respective ceilings are **6.34, 12.73 and 46.88**. These assume source-owned mass or negligible net import of charge across the aperture. A nonlocal mass assignment requires its own transfer rule. These minimum stellar conversions assign all allowed gas to the aperture. Zero means gas at its upper allowance could account for the minimum required baryons. At $\Upsilon_V=3$, constant loss and one-step access still need $2.07\times10^6$ and $7.29\times10^5\,M_\odot$ of additional baryons at 291 pc. Moving to the favorable quoted half-light input endpoints gives thresholds of 13.18, 5.30 and zero. At 900 pc the lower quoted velocity gives 47.28, 20.10 and zero. With fully correlated spectral noise, the central constant-loss conversion thresholds are 12.13 and 46.43; for one step they become zero and 11.11. These corners do not represent joint confidence limits. [Inputs and individual sensitivities](spheroidal.json)

A stellar conversion of three is a published source-favorable benchmark for Draco. Recent integrated-color models give central values of 4.90 and 6.61 for different initial mass functions, the latter reaching roughly 11 at the upper quoted error. Stellar populations, undetected molecular, cold optically thick or hot gas, and imported source charge limit the interpretation. At both radii two-step access still has sufficient capacity under the generous gas allowances. Constant loss falls shortest and one-step access is sensitive to noise and stellar conversion. [Łokas et al., adopted stellar conversion](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/363/3/918/1147717) · [Recent stellar population estimates](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/553/2/stag1500/9026953)

For a region in the same homogeneous independent occupation family, the two-step charge per ordinary double is proportional to $[(1-p)^{12}+(1-p)^{13}]/2$. At $\Upsilon_V=3$ and with both gas phases allowed up to their ceilings, the 291 pc mass requires **$p\le0.04871$**, implying at least **$1.69\times10^6$ times cosmic site density** from the enclosed stars alone. The separate 900 pc calculation gives **$p\le0.01568$** and at least **$1.77\times10^5$ times cosmic site density** when only the inner half-light luminosity is used for the guaranteed stellar lower bound. These are necessary conditions inferred from kinematics. Each treats its aperture as homogeneous; a radially varying occupation distribution requires an explicit profile. A measurement of physical site capacity, occupation or failures in Draco could turn these conditions into a forward test. [Numerical inversion](spheroidal.py)
