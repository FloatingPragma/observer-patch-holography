# Could ordinary photons supply the work before recombination?

If the 32-record abundance were made by extracting energy from ordinary photons at redshift 10,000, the conversion would consume **31.7% of the pre-existing photon energy**, raise the baryon-to-photon number ratio by a factor of **1.332** after perfect blackbody restoration, and raise the effective neutrino count from 3.044 to **4.46**. Planck with BAO gives $N_{\rm eff}=2.99\pm0.17$ in its extra-radiation model. This checks a specified energy reservoir for a component with the measured finite excess-to-ordinary ratio 5.134. [Native work ledger](../../../../model/experiments/interval_echo_shell_flux/product_address/green_response/material_recoil/overlap_source/native_boundary/stock/trail.json) · [Planck cosmological parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209) · [Standard neutrino reference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07047)

Let the conversion take place after nucleosynthesis and neutrino decoupling, before recombination, with fixed baryon number, untouched decoupled neutrinos, complete conversion efficiency and no other source or sink. A positive fraction $f_i$ of the final pressureless comoving stock is created at scale factor $a_i$. Photon energy density counts as $a^4\rho_\gamma$ while the surviving stock counts as $a^3\rho_s$. If $q=\omega_s/\omega_b$, the past excess of comoving photon energy above its final value, in units of that final value, is
$$R=(q\omega_b/\omega_{\gamma,0})\sum_i f_i a_i.$$
Perfect blackbody restoration gives $\eta_{\rm after}/\eta_{\rm before}=(1+R)^{3/4}$ for $\eta=n_b/n_\gamma$. The neutrino-to-photon energy ratio increases by $1+R$. These conservation identities also apply to a distributed history of positive transfers. They require the assumed cold redshifting and photon sink.

Use the measured present photon temperature $2.72548\pm0.00057$ K, the nucleosynthesis baryon estimate $0.02218\pm0.00055$ used by DESI and the rounded Planck base-model baryon summary $0.0224\pm0.0001$. Varying the inputs by two quoted errors in favorable directions, the baryon-ratio comparison requires a single burst for the middle stock at $z\gtrsim48{,}100$; the separate neutrino comparison gives $z\gtrsim49{,}000$. For a distributed history, at most about **21%** of the stock can be made between redshift 10,000 and recombination within either sensitivity calculation. This follows by bounding the weighted mean scale factor below by the late mass fraction divided by 10,001. The two reference fits use established cosmological transfer functions. These intervals are parameter-sensitivity checks, not a joint likelihood for a new photon-cooling cosmology. A transfer during nucleosynthesis, a separate reservoir, photon replenishment, nonthermal spectra or a change in the gravitational conversion require different predictions.
