# A cosmological test of conserved dark charge

A conserved charge $Q_A$ carried in a comoving volume proportional to $a^3$ gives $\bar\rho_A(a)a^3=\mathrm{constant}$. The ratio $\bar\rho_A(a_2)/\bar\rho_A(a_1)=(a_1/a_2)^3$ is independent of the unknown abundance. In a separately conserved homogeneous fluid with $d\ln\bar\rho/d\ln a=-3(1+w)$ this branch predicts $w(a)=0$ in every bin. Applying the bound to the cosmological measurements below assumes that this branch supplies the dark matter component fitted in the observational analysis. A mixture with another dark component, exchange of charge with the environment, or appreciable evolving rest energy would change that identification. ## Precision of a constant dilution exponent

Thomas et al. varied the constant equation of state, sound speed and viscosity in a flat cosmology. Their Planck 2015 primary spectra, lensing and BAO fit gives a 99.7% credible interval of −0.000896 to +0.00238 for the dark equation of state. From the homogeneous continuity equation the power in $\bar\rho_A\propto a^{-n}$ is $n=3(1+w)$. Its interval is **2.997312 to 3.007140**. Doubling the scale factor leaves **0.12438 to 0.12524** of the density, rounded outwards. The conserved-charge prediction is exactly one eighth. The normalization, sound speed and viscosity require their own source predictions. Kopp et al. infer a scaled dark density of $0.1193^{+0.0036}_{-0.0035}$ at matter–radiation equality from primary spectra when the equation of state is allowed to vary. This constrains a proposal to generate the cosmological source only after galaxies form. [Thomas et al., Table 1 and results](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.05097) · [Kopp et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09541)

## Joint likelihood of inverse-volume dilution

Ilić et al. report best likelihoods in a flat cosmology with a cosmological constant and adiabatic initial conditions. In each pair the standard cosmological parameters, today's dark abundance and experimental nuisance parameters are fitted by the authors in both hypotheses. The free history introduces eight equation-of-state amplitudes. Their first dataset uses Planck 2015 temperature and polarization spectra (PPS). The second adds the Planck lensing reconstruction and baryon acoustic oscillations. The two data combinations overlap. [Ilić et al., Table IV, first row](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09572v2#page=18)

| Data | Perturbations in both hypotheses | $-\ln L_{\max}$ at $w=0$ | $-\ln L_{\max}$ with free history | $2\ln(L_{\rm free}/L_{w=0})$ |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PPS | Sound speed and shear viscosity zero | 6467.585 | 6462.040 | 11.090 |
| PPS + lensing + BAO | Sound speed and shear viscosity zero | 6475.238 | 6470.89 | 8.696 |
| PPS | Nine sound-speed and nine viscosity amplitudes fitted | 6467.722 | 6462.443 | 10.558 |
| PPS + lensing + BAO | Nine sound-speed and nine viscosity amplitudes fitted | 6475.377 | 6471.250 | 8.254 |

The second perturbation treatment tests the same background law while allowing freedom in clustering. The measured improvement of about eight to eleven for eight additional background parameters provides no decisive preference for evolving dilution in these analyses. Two larger perturbation models attained slightly worse reported optima than their nested submodels, by 0.274–0.806 in twice the log likelihood. The printed extrema are numerical fits. A calibrated significance or Bayesian odds for a dark-matter mechanism would require additional information. The authors discuss the prior dependence of early-time marginalized shifts when the sound-speed and viscosity bins are freed. [Model definitions, priors and fit interpretation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09572v2#page=5)

## Where a single bin challenges the prediction

Kopp et al. used eight smoothly joined equation-of-state bins with vanishing sound speed and viscosity. Their Table I gives 95% one-dimensional marginal intervals, and their Fig. 1 gives the wider 99% bands. In bin 6, nominally $10^{-4}<a<10^{-3.5}$ or $3161\lesssim z\lesssim9999$, PPS gives $w_6\in[-0.0003,0.0157]$. Adding BAO yields $[0.0005,0.0162]$. The latter favors a positive value at that individual marginal level, corresponding to a bin-amplitude dilution exponent $3(1+w_6)\in[3.0015,3.0486]$. The PPS+HST interval in the same bin also narrowly excludes zero. These marginal intervals overlap across datasets and bins; multiplying their probabilities cannot give a joint rejection. In the variable-history case the Planck-only inference near matter–radiation equality gives $\omega_g^{\rm eq}=0.1193^{+0.0036}_{-0.0035}$ at 95%, with $\omega_g$ proportional to $a^3\bar\rho_g$. The measurements determine that normalization, which the OPH dilution identity leaves free. [Kopp et al., Table I and equality-density measurement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09541v2)

The authors' results use the Planck 2015 likelihood and their stated external data. These measurements already existed when this comparison was selected.
