\begin{abstract}
A cosmological comparison requires a finite source model, compatible
transfer, an observable projection and a data likelihood with covariance
and nuisance parameters. We distinguish quantities derived from a chosen
source from imported scales, fitted parameters and diagnostic formulas.
A reproducible retrospective ledger compares conditional observer-patch
values with Planck, ACT, SPT, BICEP/Keck, DESI and SPARC measurements.
It records both agreement and tension, including the conditional tilt,
fixed-capacity equation of state and capacity normalization. These
comparisons use seen data and several share the standard cosmological
baseline. None supplies a joint source-derived cosmological likelihood or
a likelihood ratio for the full theory.

\end{abstract}


\section{Comparison Classes}

Every reported number belongs to one of four classes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{source-derived}: all physical inputs follow from the declared OPH
construction without using the comparison data;
\item \textbf{imported}: established background or microphysical inputs are
declared explicitly;
\item \textbf{fit}: one or more parameters are inferred from the same data;
\item \textbf{diagnostic}: the calculation tests shape, scale, code behavior, or
an interface without supporting a physical OPH claim.
\end{enumerate}

The cosmology comparisons in scope are diagnostic or imported. No source-derived
joint cosmology likelihood exists. The finite particle comparison computes no
particle abundance or early-Universe population distribution, so every species
density in these comparisons is imported or fitted~\cite{ophparticles}. The retrospective comparison ledger of
Section~\ref{sec:ledger} classifies every recorded comparison by these
classes and by whether the compared value is shared with the standard
baseline.

In a likelihood comparison, the event law of the spacetime
construction~\cite{ophEinstein}, its cone and count-clock hypotheses and
their physical calibration count as model information, alongside the
background, conserved stress, initial conditions and matter assumptions. A
geometric count clock by itself does not define the cosmological transfer or
observable likelihood.

\section{Model and data contract}
A comparison must specify the covariant action and source map, background
and species, gauge-invariant initial data, perturbation and collision
terms, observable projection, numerical errors, and every external input
or fitted parameter. Source, transfer and observable must use the same
physical clock and scale.

It must also identify the data release, selection, masks, covariance,
calibration, nuisance parameters, priors and combination rule. Sample
overlap and shared calibration enter the covariance; a sum of diagonal
residuals is not the joint likelihood when correlations are present.
A measured quantity that selects the formula, normalization or applicability
range is an input to that comparison. For example, conserved modular
charge in a comoving volume gives \(\rho_A\propto a^{-3}\), and with a
separate no-exchange continuity law gives a pressureless background. It
does not fix abundance, perturbations, lensing or nonlinear growth.

\section{Diagnostic Results}

The reported CMB overlays, compressed background comparisons, and galaxy
scaling checks are diagnostic. Their formulas or inputs are selected with knowledge of
the comparison data, omit required covariance or nuisance structure, or lack a
physical source map. They test numerical paths and conditional formulas and
do not establish a cosmological prediction.

\paragraph{Conditional observer spectra and native-source tests.}
The observer-spectrum calculation propagates a supplied primordial
amplitude and shape through a supplied cosmological background and standard
temperature/polarization transfer. A conditional sky realization adds
cosmic-variance sampling around that mean; it does not select the mean's
inputs. Agreement with a measurement-calibrated reference curve, including
equality when identical source and transfer inputs are used, verifies a
numerical interface and cannot be counted as independent source evidence.
Residual root mean squares formed from marginal band-power errors omit
inter-bin covariance and nuisance parameters; they are not a likelihood,
probability of the theory, or independent fit significance.

The native-repair tests have a different observable and evidence source:
absolute stationary load-record variance and inherited/fresh/settled
covariance in 61 retained refinement chains. They constrain those finite
mechanisms without using a CMB normalization, but do not identify load as
geometric curvature, set a physical background, or supply a primordial
tilt. In the executable observer construction, actual native density
snapshots on refining source screens have joint Gaussian limits with
covariance \(\exp(-|\tau-u|A)\) for a positive operator \(A\) of spectral
dimension two on the screen, with \(\tau\) a rescaled native attempt
count~\cite{ophtimefields,ophcontinuum}. That construction does not derive
the tilt branch \(n_s=1-P/48\) from its joint records~\cite{ophpublicdata}.
Their receipts and conditional observer comparisons are
archived~\cite{ophobsdyn}.
An observational test must first connect the native geometric readout to
the source, radial and transfer interfaces with their uncertainties.

Agreement has to be attributed to the tested model. Zero source
isocurvature and Gaussian source cumulants are shared with standard
adiabatic models; \((w_0,w_a)=(-1,0)\) is the \(\Lambda\)CDM point.
The tensor-zero branch additionally assumes zero initial tensor amplitude
and velocity and no forcing. Its compatibility with a finite upper bound
does not establish exact zero or exclude all slow-roll models. The
running-zero branch discriminates only alternatives resolvable by the data.

The tilt uses a declared full-collar generator density \(P_\star/24\),
evaluated at a pixel located from the measured fine-structure constant.
No native source emits that density. A conditional particle/memory root
also does not derive it. In the ACT DR6, Planck, lensing and DESI joint
chains, \(0.612\) percent of posterior mass lies below \(0.96602\),
versus approximately \(0.74\) percent in a Gaussian approximation; the
diagnostic \(0.96484\) branch lies at \(0.200\) percent~\cite{act2025,ophpublicdata}.
These are posterior positions conditional on the fitted baseline, not
probabilities of OPH or likelihood ratios.

The fixed-capacity point has a \(2.6\) to \(3.9\sigma\) Gaussian-moment
distance in the stated DESI combinations, and the monotone-capacity branch
has posterior fractions between \(0.007\) and \(0.11\) percent. The two
capacity displays differ from the DESI coordinate by \(-2.64\) and
\(-2.81\sigma\), while differing from the Planck-central value by
\(+0.62\) and \(+0.38\) percent. All these inputs were seen before the
registered comparison protocols. Only a complete prospectively fixed
protocol, its eligible release, statistic and thresholds defines a test;
the frozen prediction register specifies those separate contracts~\cite{ophladder}.
The compared \(w\) is the effective-fluid quantity of the continuity relation
read from \(\rho\propto1/N\), so a posterior on it is no measurement of screen
work: identical fixed-volume records admit \(H_\alpha(V)=Q(V/V_0)^{-\alpha}\) with
\(p/\rho=\alpha\), and an additive energy offset changes \(p/\rho\) while leaving
pressure unchanged~\cite{ophobservers}.
A failure of a conditional branch rejects the full proposal only if that
branch's premises are shown necessary.

\section{No-Data-Use Boundary}

A source-derived calculation must expose its dependency graph. The source side
may use mathematical constants, declared OPH finite artifacts, and explicitly
imported physical inputs. It may not use the observed quantity being claimed as
an output, a fit to that quantity, or a proxy calibrated from the same dataset.

This provenance rule is enforced through the declared dependency graph and
data-use classification.

\section{Theorem scope}

The screen-spectrum and radial-lift mathematics are conditional theorems.
One-shell inversion is nonunique; physical source dilation and radial
cross-covariance tomography are two established sufficient uniqueness routes.
Neither route is supplied by the native screen energy, which is not
boundedly congruent to the static boundary action of a smooth compact
three-dimensional Dirichlet bulk; a radial lift needs a radial observation
algebra as an added premise~\cite{ophcontinuum}.
Finite source instantiation, repair-charge cosmological source, Boltzmann
bridge, and joint likelihood are not supplied. All cosmological
data products are diagnostic. No cosmological result carries an OPH
falsification verdict.
