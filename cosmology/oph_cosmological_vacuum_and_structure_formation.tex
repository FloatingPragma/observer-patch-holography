\begin{abstract}
A finite quotient, a reference measure and an action determine an observer
ensemble. A vacuum interpretation needs additional reversible Euclidean
data and compatible refinement. For guarded material pairs the native energy gap and the two sharp transition
probabilities determine a reversible Euclidean step of \(\log 2\).
Keeping one child instrument per parent preserves every finite history law. An active free field
has vacuum covariance \((2\sqrt B)^{-1}\) under its declared preparation;
its flat massless spectrum grows as \(k^2\) per logarithmic interval and
therefore is not a scale-invariant primordial curvature spectrum.
Structure formation requires an expanding background, a curvature readout,
and transfer dynamics on the same observer history. Identifying the screen
capacity with horizon entropy gives \(\Lambda\ell_P^2=3\pi/N\).
A galaxy clock calibrates the matter contribution in the flat cosmology
with constant vacuum density. Two other redshift bins test that conditional
expansion curve. In a level-202 repair history, observer zero's material
beacons give pulse shifts of zero and one quarter.

\section{Ensemble, vacuum and background}
Observers can establish a reference by disabling the excitation source,
repeating a calibrated probe at specified clock intervals, and comparing
outcomes and their correlations. The same observers can agree just as
precisely on the arrival of an excitation. To identify a stationary field
vacuum, the reference also needs evidence that it occupies the lowest
accessible energy for the chosen clock evolution. On an evolving background
the preparation and detector trajectory enter the comparison.
The guarded two-rail apparatus below provides a finite example.
Its ground reference assigns equal probabilities to the two rail labels.
With both initial labels equally represented, the next sharp read changed
label in 40,939 of 163,840 trials on 320 cells, against the probe's
transition probability of $1/4$. These counts include the specified
intervention between reads.

If the capacity \(N\) is identified with de Sitter horizon entropy in
nats, then
\[
 \Lambda\ell_P^2=\frac{3\pi}{N}.
\]
This is the entropy relation read backwards; it determines \(\Lambda\)
only after \(N\) and the area interpretation are supplied. For a separately
conserved vacuum component with \(\rho\propto N^{-1}\), the continuity
equation gives \(w=-1+\tfrac13d\log N/d\log a\). Constant \(N\) gives
\(w=-1\); nondecreasing \(N\) gives \(w\ge-1\). Neither relation chooses
\(N\). This \(w\) is the effective-fluid quantity of the continuity equation:
consensus about identical fixed-volume records cannot select an unobserved strain
derivative, since \(H_\alpha(V)=Q(V/V_0)^{-\alpha}\) gives \(p/\rho=\alpha\). Three
geometrically spaced energy readings identify the response within a family with
\(\alpha\ge0\), which excludes \(w=-1\) at positive energy, and an additive energy
offset changes \(p/\rho\) while leaving pressure unchanged~\cite{ophobservers}.
The whole-screen horizon reading needs a region map. Target-informed capacity formulas
and their retrospective data comparisons belong to the likelihood
companion~\cite{ophcontracts}. A smooth Einstein interpretation also needs
the common-stress and tensor-curvature or small-ball identification
premises~\cite{ophEinstein}.

\paragraph{Galaxy clocks and a repeat-spectrum test.}

$H(z)^2=H_\Lambda^2+B(1+z)^3$. A single astronomical clock calibration

\[
 H(z)^2=H_\Lambda^2+
 (H_a^2-H_\Lambda^2)\left(\frac{1+z}{1+z_a}\right)^3 .
\]

$100.99,111.61$ in the same units. The corresponding measurements are
$119.45,108.28$. Propagating the published three-bin covariance through
the anchor gives observed-minus-predicted differences of $18.46\pm6.48$
and $-3.33\pm10.95$ to first order. A separate fit of one matter
coefficient to all three measurements at fixed $H_\Lambda$ gives

with that covariance. The middle bin drives the tension. The physical
screen was selected with a Planck horizon benchmark, and this comparison
also assumes the Friedmann background.%

\paragraph{Constrained scalar, matter and radiation transfer.}

Einstein gravity, ideal dust and ideal radiation. Put $N=\log a$ and $h=H_N/H$.

$A=V_\phi/(H^2M_{\rm Pl})$, $\mathcal M=V_{\phi\phi}/H^2$,
$Q=k^2/(aH)^2$, $U_i=aH\theta_i/k^2$, $y=\delta\phi/M_{\rm Pl}$ and
$z=y_N$. The background has
$h=-(3m+4r+s^2)/2$ and $s_N+(3+h)s+A=0$.
The full linear equations are
\begin{align*}
 P:=\Phi_N&=-\Phi+\tfrac12(3mU_m+4rU_r+sy),\\

\end{align*}

\[
 C=Q\Phi+3(P+\Phi)

\]

\[
 \Phi_{NN}+(4+h)\Phi_N+(3+2h)\Phi

\]

constraint projection is unnecessary. The regular zero-wave-number
adiabatic mode uses
$T=(3/2)(H/a)\int_0^a da'/H(a')$, $\Phi=3/2-T$, $U_m=U_r=T$,
