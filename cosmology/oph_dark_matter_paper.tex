\begin{abstract}
Observer Patch Holography proposes that correlations retained across
overlapping regions contribute to gravity. Finite probes detect a
correlation at fixed ordinary load. On one source, removing it reduces
the area of a fixed-volume clock survey at the coupling measured on
ordinary matter. A fifth station limits the geometric interpretation.
Complete record recovery suppresses the anomaly under a finite recovery
bound. If surviving fluctuations supply positive active mass, their
variance adds and the mean number of contributing layers per radial
length is constant, a compact spherical exterior gives flat rotation
curves and a fourth-power baryonic mass law. A fit to 121 galaxies,
applied to the local disk force, gives a mean speed of at least
62.9 km/s for every cut phase in the outer ring of AGC~114905, where
the measured speed is $27\pm2$ km/s.
The ring encloses about two thirds of the modeled gas. Three separately
selected outer orbits agree within two quoted errors. Weak lensing
retains discrepancies at the rotation calibration. In 53 galaxies,
stellar spectra and Einstein rings permit equal anomalous responses
for light and orbits under a spherical continuation into the stellar
interior. Identifying the source requires a signed stress measurement
that predicts geometry at the ordinary-matter coupling.
\end{abstract}

\section{What Sources Gravity in OPH}

The finite classification, recovery and profile identities are checked under
the stated hypotheses in the Lean formalization~\cite{ophlean}. Their physical
identification is an additional premise throughout.

In the executable studied here, finite observers occupy overlapping
patches of a spherical federation of icosahedral cells. Interval Echo
repairs disagreements at shared ports and retains a physical record on
each serviced seam~\cite{ophmodel}. Two prepared loads can interrogate
that record by interference. In the dark-sector hypothesis, the
candidate gravitational source is the anomalous modular charge of the
overlap. The probe reads the record on which a finite version of that
charge can be measured.

The conditional Einstein arrow of the spacetime paper ends in
\begin{equation}
G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G\langle T_{ab}\rangle,
\label{eq:einstein}
\end{equation}
where \(\langle T_{ab}\rangle\) is a supplied local, covariant, conserved
stress on the physical branch~\cite{ophEinstein}. Its smooth form requires
same-family tensor-curvature convergence or the continuum
small-ball/null-balance identification; scalar-curvature convergence alone is
diagnostic. Finite positive-null
tomography exactly reconstructs a quadratic form from directional charges,
but identifying that form with the same local stress additionally requires
charge--stress matching, locality and covariance, and a weak Ward law. In the
rest frame the relation then follows from three further premises on a small
geodesic ball of radius \(\ell\): generalized entropy is declared stationary,
the bulk entropy variation equals
\((8\pi^2\ell^4/15)\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle\), and the fixed-volume area
variation equals \(-(4\pi\ell^4/15)\,\delta G_{00}\). Exact arithmetic gives
\(\delta G_{00}=8\pi G\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle\).

On that conditional branch, the stress entering this relation is identified
with the same modular charge. The screen does not
consult the electromagnetic readout of a source before responding to it. That
is the universal-coupling premise, and it is the only input the dark-sector
question takes from the gravity derivation.

\section{The Generic Dark Sector}
\label{sec:generic}

Let a finite family of screen sources be indexed by \(i\), each with
rest-frame modular-charge contribution \(q_i\) and a flag recording whether
it carries the electromagnetic readout. Write \(Q_{\rm lum}\) for the sum over
flagged sources and \(Q_{\rm dark}\) for the sum over the others, so that
\(Q_{\rm tot}=Q_{\rm lum}+Q_{\rm dark}\).

\begin{premise}[Universal coupling]
\label{pre:universal}
The stress contraction entering the rest-frame Einstein relation equals
\(Q_{\rm tot}\), with no readout-dependent weight.
\end{premise}

\begin{proposition}[Total and non-luminous response]
\label{thm:all-charge}\label{thm:iff}\label{cor:excess}\label{prop:sign}
Under Premise~\ref{pre:universal} and \(G>0\),
\(\delta G_{00}=8\pi G(Q_{\rm lum}+Q_{\rm dark})\).
The luminous-only equation holds exactly when \(Q_{\rm dark}=0\).
If every non-luminous charge is nonnegative, its weak-field contribution
has the attractive sign.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the finite-sum decomposition of \(Q_{\rm tot}\) in the assumed
response law. Cancel \(8\pi G\) for the equivalence and sum nonnegative
terms for the sign.
\end{proof}
This classifies a geometric excess if one exists; it does not establish a
nonzero charge, its abundance, or a physical carrier.

\section{The Modular-Anomaly Source}
\label{sec:source}

On the support-visible branch the cap modular generator has the local form
\begin{equation}
K_C=2\pi B_C+K_C^{\rm anom}+\mathrm{const},
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
with \(B_C\) the bulk boost generator and \(K_C^{\rm anom}\) the nonadditive
part carried on the overlap collar~\cite{ophEinstein}. At finite regulator
stage the separately supplied collar-recovery model carries this part as a
remainder and bounds it:
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\delta\langle E_C^{(\delta)}\rangle\bigr|\le C_C\,\eta_\delta,
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_\delta\) is the collar recovery defect and \(C_C\) depends only
on the collar model and the bounded observable class.

\begin{definition}[Anomalous stress and density]
\begin{equation}
\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle
:=\frac{15}{8\pi^2\ell^4}\,\delta\langle E_C^{(\delta)}\rangle,
\qquad
\rho_A:=\frac{1}{c^2}\,\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle .
\label{eq:tanom}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

On a family of positive shrinking proper radii $\ell_n$, let $E_n$
denote the measured collar-energy variation on the corresponding ball
in one fixed observer frame. Equation~\eqref{eq:tanom} gives the exact
criterion for a finite rest-frame stress $\tau$,
\[
  \delta T^{\rm anom}_{00,n}\longrightarrow\tau
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \frac{E_n-(8\pi^2/15)\ell_n^4\tau}{\ell_n^4}\longrightarrow0 .
\]
This follows by multiplying the rescaled difference by the positive
constant $15/(8\pi^2)$. In particular, a recovery bound
$C_n\eta_n=o(\ell_n^4)$ forces $\tau=0$. For nonzero $\tau$ the
leading collar coefficient belongs in the total stress of the Einstein
equation, while the residual after its subtraction must satisfy the
small-ball error bound. A fluctuation measured on a galactic cut
requires a map to these local contractions, including their sign,
normalization and transport. The galactic radius in the enclosed-mass
profile and the local proper radius $\ell_n$ serve different measurements.

The dark-sector adapter identifies this anomalous modular energy with the
non-luminous charge of Section~\ref{sec:generic}. Its absence of an
electromagnetic readout is a source-localization hypothesis: it is assigned to
the part of the modular generator that no local record exposes. Neither
identification follows from the finite Einstein-shape theorem. Three consequences follow from
\eqref{eq:bound} and \eqref{eq:tanom}.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded source]
\label{thm:bounded}
\(\bigl|\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle\bigr|
\le 15\,C_C\,\eta_\delta/(8\pi^2\ell^4)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Full recovery switches the source off]
\label{thm:off}
If \(\eta_\delta=0\) then \(\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle=0\).
Along any family with fixed \(\ell\), bounded \(C_C\), and
\(\eta_\delta\to0\), the anomalous stress tends to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cold scaling]
\label{thm:cold}
A conserved anomalous charge in a comoving cell of physical volume
\(\propto a^3\) has density \(\propto a^{-3}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proofs]
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded} multiplies \eqref{eq:bound} by the positive kernel
inverse. Theorem~\ref{thm:off} is the case \(\eta_\delta=0\) of the bound and
a squeeze along the family. Theorem~\ref{thm:cold} is
\(\rho\,a^3=Q\).
\end{proof}

The split \eqref{eq:split} has a machine-checked finite counterpart. On a
finite carrier of microstates labelled bulk or collar, with strictly
positive weights, a declared boost datum vanishing on the collar, and
modular generator \(K=-\log w\), the decomposition
\(K=2\pi B+K^{\rm anom}+c\,\mathbf 1\) exists with \(c\) the bulk-weighted
mean of \(K-2\pi B\), is unique among splits whose anomalous part has
vanishing bulk mean, and is invariant under the additive-constant
quotient \(w\mapsto e^{-t}w\). When the bulk weights are exactly Gibbs for
\(2\pi B\), the anomalous part vanishes on the bulk and is carried
entirely on the collar, and its expectation is the remainder bounded in
\eqref{eq:bound}. The finite carrier, the boost datum, the normalization
clause, and the Gibbs condition are model premises; the receipt is the
finite counterpart of \eqref{eq:split}, at exactly that strength.

For a static weak field with pressures negligible compared with the
rest-energy densities, take $\Phi$ relative to the common vacuum
potential. The anomalous mass density then enters the Poisson equation
beside the baryons,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\,(\rho_b+\rho_A),
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
with the attractive sign of Proposition~\ref{prop:sign} whenever the
anomalous charge is nonnegative. In units $c=1$, the equation for the
unsubtracted potential includes $-\Lambda$, and appreciable principal
pressures add $4\pi G\sum_i p_i$ at this order. The identification
of the full stress enters before taking the slow-motion limit.
The ordinary and overlap terms partition the same regional modular
variation; counting the measured overlap variation in both terms would
overestimate the source.

\section{The Deep-Regime Profile}
\label{sec:deep}

Around a baryonic mass \(M_b\), the proposed collar-count interpretation
assigns a number of settled cuts proportional to the enclosing radius
\(r\). The precise deep-regime characterization uses the following two
premises, each independently falsifiable.

\begin{premise}[Deep-regime scale covariance]
\label{pre:cov}
In the deep regime the enclosed anomalous mass is degree-one homogeneous in
the radius: \(M_A(M_b,\lambda r)=\lambda\,M_A(M_b,r)\) for every
\(\lambda>0\). A sparsely recorded deep cut retains no length scale, so
rescaling the radius rescales the enclosed mass by the same factor.
\end{premise}

\begin{premise}[Quadrature composition]
\label{pre:quad}
The anomalous amplitudes of independent baryonic sources compose in
quadrature:
\(M_A(m_1+m_2,r)^2=M_A(m_1,r)^2+M_A(m_2,r)^2\). The collar defects of
distinct sources are independent, their variances add, and the gravitating
anomalous charge is the amplitude of the unrecovered record fluctuation.
\end{premise}

\begin{theorem}[Profile characterization]
\label{thm:char}
Let \(M_A(M_b,r)\) be nonnegative, monotone in the source, somewhere
nonzero, and satisfy Premises~\ref{pre:cov} and \ref{pre:quad}. Then there
is a unique constant \(a_0>0\) with
\begin{equation}
M_A(r)=r\sqrt{\frac{M_ba_0}{G}},
\qquad
\rho_A(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}\sqrt{\frac{M_ba_0}{G}}.
\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scale covariance factorizes \(M_A(M_b,r)=r\,f(M_b)\). Quadrature
composition makes \(f^2\) additive on positive sources, monotonicity makes
it monotone, and a function additive and monotone on the positive reals is
linear: \(f^2=\lambda M_b\) with \(\lambda>0\) by nondegeneracy. Setting
\(a_0=\lambda G\) gives \eqref{eq:profile}; uniqueness follows by
evaluating at \(M_b=r=1\). Each premise is load-bearing: an additive law
\(M_A\propto rM_b\) satisfies everything except quadrature composition, and
an \(r^2\) law everything except scale covariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear enclosed mass]
\label{pre:linear}
The deep-regime density is the shell derivative of the enclosed mass,
\(M_A'(r)=4\pi r^2\rho_A(r)\), with \(M_A\) linear in \(r\) and the
coefficient \(\sqrt{M_ba_0/G}\) forced by
Theorem~\ref{thm:char}.
\end{corollary}

Deep-limit scale invariance is the known symmetry of the modified-dynamics
limit~\cite{milgrom2009}, and square-root mass laws appear in
entropic-gravity proposals~\cite{verlinde2016}. The resulting law is the
deep limit of modified Newtonian dynamics, \(a_A=\sqrt{a_ba_0}\), with its
baryonic Tully--Fisher corollary; the characterization adds that the two
premises force it jointly, and it reads the square root as the variance
additivity of independent collar defects under source composition. It does
not fix \(a_0\).

Premise~\ref{pre:quad} itself follows from a more primitive per-cut model.
Suppose the \(nr\) nested cuts within radius \(r\) each carry anomalous
amplitude \(\sqrt{V(M_b)}\), the standard deviation of the collar defect
sourced by the baryons, with the variances of independent sources adding
and the variance monotone in the source. Then the variance function is
forced linear, \(V(M_b)=cM_b\) with a unique \(c>0\), quadrature
composition is a theorem rather than a premise, the induced law satisfies
Theorem~\ref{thm:char}, and its constant is exactly
\begin{equation}
a_0=G\,n^2c .
\label{eq:dictionary}
\end{equation}
The dictionary is exact but determines only the product \(n^2c\): two
models with different cut densities and equal \(n^2c\) induce the same
enclosed-mass law, machine-checked. The magnitude question is therefore
the value of the collar density and of the per-cut variance constant;
neither is derived here.

One sufficient realization of both premises rests one level deeper. Variance
additivity follows from probabilistic independence: for atomic defect
contributions with finite second moments, pairwise independence, and a
common per-atom variance on one probability space, the variance of the
compound defect of every finite atom family is the sum of the per-atom
variances, so disjoint-union composition is additive and \(V(m)=cm\) on
the modeled masses. Independence is stronger than necessary; pairwise zero
covariance and other dependent but uncorrelated constructions are viable.
An exact linear-asymptotic cut count follows from one rigid geometry: a
cut family with a positive first cut, consecutive gaps of at least \(s\),
and every length-\(s\) interval meeting a cut is exactly the arithmetic
progression with spacing \(s\), its count obeys
\(r/s-1\le N(r)\le r/s+1\), and the counted enclosed mass deviates from
the linear law by at most one per-cut amplitude at every radius, with
\(n=1/s\). A perfectly correlated pair and a one-cut family show only that
the conclusions do not follow after deleting every decorrelation or density
condition. They do not make independence or exact spacing necessary.
Irregular or ergodic cut families with asymptotic density and sublinear count
error are possible. The exact \(nr\) per-cut law is the continuum or
mean extension; the discrete count above differs from it by a bounded error.

\begin{theorem}[Deep radial-acceleration relation]
\label{thm:rar}
The anomalous acceleration of the profile \eqref{eq:profile} is
\begin{equation}
a_A(r)=\frac{GM_A(r)}{r^2}=\frac{\sqrt{GM_ba_0}}{r}=\sqrt{a_b(r)\,a_0},
\qquad a_b=\frac{GM_b}{r^2}.
\label{eq:deep}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Flat rotation curve and baryonic Tully--Fisher relation]
\label{thm:btfr}
The circular speed supported by \(a_A\) satisfies
\(v^2=a_A r=\sqrt{GM_ba_0}\), independent of \(r\), and hence
\begin{equation}
v^4=GM_b\,a_0 .
\label{eq:btfr}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proofs]
\(GM_A/r^2=G\sqrt{M_ba_0/G}/r=\sqrt{GM_ba_0}/r\), and
\(\sqrt{GM_ba_0}/r=\sqrt{(GM_b/r^2)a_0}\) for \(r>0\). Multiplying by \(r\)
removes the radius; squaring gives \eqref{eq:btfr}.
\end{proof}

The effect of orbital radius has a separate explanation. Compare two
orbits at $r$ and $2r$ in this smooth deep exterior. Both enclose the
same baryonic mass, so the baryonic acceleration at the outer orbit
is one quarter as large. The enclosed anomalous mass doubles between
the two orbits, so its acceleration is one half as large. Both
accelerations decrease, while their ratio doubles. Explicitly
$a_A/a_b=r/r_M$ with $r_M=\sqrt{GM_b/a_0}$. This radius is measured
from the galaxy's mass centre. Identifying a sparse physical collar
and establishing that its charge gravitates are additional source
measurements. A rotation curve alone cannot infer the local density of
retained records.

The exponents \(1/2\) in \eqref{eq:deep} and \(4\) in \eqref{eq:btfr} are
the parameter-free content of the profile. The constant \(a_0\) collects
\(C_C\), the collar density, and the small-ball normalization; none of these
is fixed by the source, so \(a_0\) enters every comparison as a fitted
value. The deep regime is the range \(a_b\ll a_0\), equivalently
\(r\gg r_M=\sqrt{GM_b/a_0}\). If the deep expression is algebraically
extended to the matching radius, both accelerations equal \(a_0\) there and
the anomalous term dominates outside \(r_M\); those algebraic statements are
machine-checked. The premises are asserted only in the deep regime, so they
do not determine the physical transition or the response inside \(r_M\).

\section{Comparison with Rotation-Curve Data}
\label{sec:data}

Theorems~\ref{thm:rar} and \ref{thm:btfr} are compared with the SPARC sample
of 175 disk galaxies with \(3.6\,\mu\)m photometry~\cite{sparc2016} under the
declared inclusive-error deep-law diagnostic: quality flag at most 2,
inclination at least \(30^\circ\), velocity error at most ten percent, and
stellar mass-to-light ratios \(0.5\) for disks and \(0.7\) for bulges. This
leaves 2700 points in 149 galaxies. The strict published-cut replay described
below is kept separate. Both computations are released with the
paper~\cite{mls2016,ophrar}.

\paragraph{Source-bound full-RAR calibration.}
Separate from the conditional OPH deep law, a deterministic replay fits the
published empirical interpolation
\[
g_{\rm obs}=\frac{g_{\rm bar}}
 {1-\exp[-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/a_0}]}
\]
to a committed CDS/VizieR snapshot whose provenance, catalogue description,
and two table files are pinned by SHA-256. The catalogue cuts reproduce the
published 153-galaxy parent selection. The strict point cuts retain 2696
points in 147 contributing galaxies, three more than the published 2693-point
census. These counts are distinct from the preceding 2700-point, 149-galaxy
deep-law diagnostic: that diagnostic uses the inclusive cut
\(dV/V\le0.1\), whereas this full-RAR replay uses the strict cut
\(dV/V<0.1\) and starts from the 153-galaxy parent set. With disk and bulge
mass-to-light values 0.5 and 0.7, the unweighted
point-level log-residual fit gives
\[
a_0=(1.1613\mathbin{\pm}0.0802)\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},
\qquad \sigma_{\log_{10}g}=0.1327\ \mathrm{dex},
\]
where the width is the standard deviation of a galaxy bootstrap. Equal total
weight per contributing galaxy shifts \(a_0\) by \(-9.58\%\); weighting only
by inverse fractional velocity variance shifts it by \(+15.49\%\). These are
estimator-sensitivity controls, not calibrated likelihood alternatives. The
interpolation and acceleration scale were known before this replay, no OPH
source value enters the fit, and distance, inclination, gas, stellar
population, intrinsic scatter, and correlation uncertainties are not jointly
marginalized. The result certifies public-data custody and arithmetic, not
independent evidence for OPH~\cite{mls2016,ophrar}.

\paragraph{Does fainter starlight predict more apparent gravity within a galaxy?}
We selected one pair of annuli per galaxy before reading their orbital speeds,
using the same SPARC catalogue~\cite{sparc2016}. Of 153 galaxies passing
the quality and inclination cuts, 70 supplied a pair whose local
$3.6\,\mu{\rm m}$ surface brightness differed by at least a factor of two.
Both nominal baryonic accelerations lay below
$1.2\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ and agreed within 10\%.
Write $t=g_{b,f}/g_{b,h}$ for the baryonic acceleration ratio in the
fainter and brighter annuli. For $g_{\rm orb}=g_b+A\sqrt{g_b}$ with a
common $A\ge0$, the predicted orbital ratio lies between $t$ and $\sqrt t$.
Allowing $A_f\ge A_h\ge0$ preserves the lower bound.
The measured ratio $(v_f/v_h)^2\theta_h/\theta_f$ cancels the common
distance and inclination, where $\theta$ is angular radius.
Allowing each speed twice its quoted error placed two pairs above the
constant-amplitude interval, eight below and 60 overlapping.
The eight lower ratios contradict the monotone brightness prescription
at the adopted baryonic forces. Keeping these annuli and the gas force
fixed, five conflicts persist for all disk and bulge mass-to-light ratios
in $[0.4,0.6]$ and $[0.56,0.84]$, respectively, shared between the two
annuli. Seven persist when the revised forces must also satisfy the
original 10\% match. UGC~06667 remains deep, matched and discrepant
throughout both mass ranges. Every fainter annulus was also farther
from its galaxy centre. The comparison cannot distinguish these two

\paragraph{Finite collar spacing against resolved rotation and lensing.}
\label{par:empirical-cut-spacing}
The arithmetic-progression construction predicts an individual enclosed
mass that departs from the smooth profile by at most one cut amplitude.
As a specified galaxy model set $N_s(R)=\lfloor R/s+\phi\rfloor$ with
one uniform phase $\phi$ per galaxy and common spacing $s$. On the
local-force disk prescription multiply the smooth anomalous squared
speed by $s N_s(R)/R$. The disk prescription extends the compact-source
theorem. A shared phase fixes the counts on every ring; integrating it
exactly gives a mixture with breakpoints at the measured fractional
radii. The average multiplier equals one at every radius.

Using all positive-error rings in the deep cut with quality at most two
and inclination at least $30^\circ$ gives 1,240 measurements in 121

ten-value grid including the smooth limit. The full-sample fit gives
$a_0=8.37\times10^{-11}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ and fractional scatter
$0.049$ per ring and $0.162$ per galaxy. The held mean log predictive
density gain over the smooth profile is $0.470$ nats per galaxy
(fixed-prediction galaxy-bootstrap interval $[0.092,0.895]$).
A smooth comparison allowing additional inner-ring scatter proportional
to inverse radius reduces that difference to $0.052$ nats with interval
$[-0.481,0.735]$. The score improves in four of the five held folds. The bootstrap
resamples held galaxies at fixed predictions and therefore does not
propagate new training fits or a fresh choice of spacing. The
small-radius scatter comparison was chosen after examining the spacing
fit. These retrospective comparisons condition on the catalogue disk
force and model the speed uncertainties. The spacing has no independent
measurement in the source.

model predicts a mean speed of at least $62.97$ km/s for every phase;
the reported speed is $26.94\pm1.83$ km/s. At fixed fitted scale and

and plotted measurement error, the conditional lower-tail probability

fiducial distance of 78.7 Mpc, integrate the published gas surface

The central outer-ring excess $v^2-v_b^2$ is $6.26\%$ of the least
value from the fitted disk prescription. The equivalent spherical

against a minimum of $61.20$ km/s at that geometry.

Even a spherical exterior leaves the interior acceleration undetermined.
Let $M_A(r)=Cr$ for $r\ge R_s$ with $C,R_s>0$. Inside $R_s$, the
profiles $C R_s(r/R_s)^3$ and $C R_s(r/R_s)^5$ both match the
exterior continuously and have finite nonnegative density. The first
gives four times the acceleration of the second at $R_s/2$. An
interior disk prediction therefore requires an independent relation
between the extended baryonic distribution and the proposed collar
stress. The measured galaxy constrains such a relation. It cannot

\paragraph{Galaxy type and a finite radial-pressure test.}
Brouwer et al.\ released the cross-covariance between galaxy types
for their Figure~8 excess surface density profiles. Six full bins satisfy
the sample isolation and deep-acceleration cuts. Project the early-minus-late
tangential excess surface density on the outer profile calibrated on SPARC
using the full covariance. At the published bin centres the contrasts
are $0.586\pm0.121$ by colour and $0.467\pm0.122$ by S\'ersic index.
The corresponding cross-component projections are $-0.016$ and $-0.068$.
The two splits reuse galaxies. A separate bin-edge test holds the nominal
compact baryons fixed and allows a shared nonnegative square-root envelope
and arbitrary positive source--lens pair weights within each bin. In
place of $Y_{\rm high}-Y_{\rm low}$ it projects
$Y_{\rm high}-kY_{\rm low}$ with its own covariance weights, where
$k=\sqrt{b_{\rm upper}/b_{\rm lower}}$. It exceeds its common-envelope
upper bound by $1.51$ and $0.50$ statistical standard errors for the two
splits. The first statistic quantifies the type dependence at bin centres.
The second tests how much a common envelope can vary with pair sampling.
Type-dependent circumgalactic gas can also affect these profiles.
Neither statistic measures local collar pressure.

In units $G=c=1$, consider a static spherical anomalous stress
conserved separately through its outer transition. If $p_r(r_0)<0$,
$p_r(R_t)=0$, $\rho\ge0$, $\rho+p_r\ge0$, $\Phi'\ge0$ and
$p_t\le\rho$, conservation gives
\[
 (r^2p_r)'=2rp_t-r^2(\rho+p_r)\Phi'\le2r\rho .
\]
Hence $r_0^2 |p_r(r_0)|\le2\int_{r_0}^{R_t}r\rho\,dr$ and
$\Delta m=4\pi\int_{r_0}^{R_t}r^2\rho\,dr
\ge2\pi r_0^3|p_r(r_0)|$. In the scale-free weak-field interior,
$m_A=hr$ and $p_r=w_r h/(4\pi r^2)$. Where the same scale-free
projection applies, the lens excess per unit orbital excess is
$q=(2+w_r)/[2(1+w_r)]$. Assigning the entire red-stack bin-centre
value $q=1.413\pm0.080$ to this mechanism would require
$w_r\simeq-0.452$ and at least $0.226\,m_A(r_0)$ in the transition.
This cost assumes smooth matching without a surface layer. Neither the
pressure nor its outer transition was measured in these galaxies.
The source and both covariance calculations are in
\path{code/cosmology/rar_deep_regime/pressure_transfer/}.

The three-guide material experiment gives a microscopic check on a
different step in the proposed explanation. A change of the joint
corner probabilities toward equal weights raises the entropy of the three-guide records
at fixed one- and two-guide marginals. Its leading occupation response
is an octupole. The total outward current has exactly zero derivative
at every enclosing radius. Thus a change visible only in joint records
can carry an angular signal without adding any monopole to the
transport measured by these observers. Obtaining a galaxy-scale source
requires an independent positive active stress and its gravitational
coupling on the same physical regions. Local record density in the
source enters this proposal. Distance from the telescope enters the
observation, and provides no such coupling.

\paragraph{Covariance and nuisance sensitivity.}
A separate penalized Gaussian objective profiles disk mass-to-light,
distance and inclination, and scans an equal-correlation family
\(\rho\in\{0,0.2,0.4,0.6\}\). A log-normal mass-to-light prior centered
at \(0.5\) is a penalty rather than an auxiliary-data likelihood.
The fixed catalogue-frame mean is
\(v_{\rm model}\sin i/\sin i_{\rm cat}\). Transforming observations and
errors by \(s(i)=\sin i_{\rm cat}/\sin i\) also requires the density
Jacobian \(s(i)^n\), which cancels the Gaussian factor \(s(i)^{-n}\).

On the \(f=0.1\) subset the optimum shifts from \(0.804\) to
\(0.582\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), or \(0.140\) dex,
as \(\rho\) increases from zero to \(0.6\). The data quadratic form per
nominal fixed-nuisance degree of freedom lies between \(3.6\) and \(16.4\);
the family has no intrinsic scatter and understates observed variation.
Its threshold sets are uncalibrated sensitivity contours, not confidence
intervals. The paired comparison contains zero at every scanned setting,
but channel-B nuisances are held at full-sample estimates while channel A
is refitted, so it is only partially paired. Independent replay and synthetic
controls verify the subtraction, fixed observation frame, covariance algebra
and component enumeration~\cite{ophrar}. A physical test requires a
calibrated covariance and intrinsic-scatter likelihood.

\paragraph{A matched orbit and shear prediction.}
Let $u(R)=v_{\rm obs}^2(R)-v_{\rm bar}^2(R)$ and
$D(R)=\Delta\Sigma_{\rm obs}(R)-\Delta\Sigma_{\rm bar}(R)$
be the excess squared circular speed and surface-density contrast
of the same galaxy. Suppose the excess has $\rho=A/r^2$ outside
$R_0$, extends along the line of sight, is spherical and has negligible
gravitational slip. For $R>R_0$, the compensating annular lens statistic
of Baldauf et al.\ \cite{baldauf2010} and the orbital mass difference obey
\[
 4G\,[R^2D(R)-R_0^2D(R_0)] = R u(R)-R_0u(R_0).
\]
Indeed $d(Ru)/dR=4\pi GA$, while
$d(R^2D)/dR=-R^2\Sigma'(R)=\pi A$ since $\Sigma(R)=\pi A/R$.
Differencing removes an arbitrary spherical mass confined inside $R_0$.
Neither $A$ nor $a_0$ is needed for the equality. If the outer density
cuts off sharply at $L$, then $\Sigma(R)=2A\arccos(R/L)/R$ and the
ratio of the two sides for $(R_0,R)=(L/4,L/2)$ is $1.014799$.
Direct numerical projection of spherical shells recovers both predictions
with two different compact cores. The comparison requires matched physical
radii, a common galaxy sample, measured pair weights and baryonic
subtraction, including extended disks and gas. A particle halo with
the same density and metric response obeys the same relation.

predicted-to-observed ratio of $1.101$ and a median absolute fractional

\cite{bolton2008}.

The 35 eligible lenses outside the weak-shear sample give a median

common rescaling raises the median absolute error on 18 held inner
lenses from $11.6\%$ to $15.8\%$. For the 22-lens outer stack, extending
the spectroscopic circular speeds flat and applying the same factor
gives central surface-density contrasts of $44.02$ and $25.19$ in
$h\,M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$. This
increases the two-bin discrepancy from $\chi^2=3.23$ to $4.41$.
The calibration and held galaxies are disjoint. The comparison tests
the transfer of a single multiplicative correction within this
spherical stellar and lensing model.

The same experiment extends outward in 22 lenses with stellar spectra,

\cite{gavazzi2007,auger2009}. At $164.5$ and $287.5\,h^{-1}$ kpc the

With only the individual efficiency range $[0,1]$, the upper bound

\paragraph{A pressure measurement from orbits and light.}
Consider the static spherical weak-field branch in physical units.
Let $m(r)$ be the mass in the radial metric coefficient,
$M_o=rv_c^2/G$ the circular-orbit mass and $M_w$ the mass that
deflects light. To leading order the radial Einstein equation gives
$M_o=m+4\pi r^3p_r/c^2$ and weak deflection gives
$M_w=(m+M_o)/2$~\cite{pressureOptics}. A spherical shell at $r>R$
places the fraction $1-\sqrt{1-R^2/r^2}$ of its mass inside the
cylinder of radius $R$. Integrating by parts gives
$M_{\rm cyl}(R)=\int_0^{\pi/2} M(R\sec t)\cos t\,dt$ for either
profile, provided the outer boundary term vanishes. Projecting
$M_o-M_w$ and substituting $z=R\tan t$ gives the signed pressure column
\begin{equation}
 P(R)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_r\!\left(\sqrt{R^2+z^2}\right)dz
 =c^2\,[\overline\Sigma_o(<R)-\overline\Sigma_w(<R)],
 \qquad \overline\Sigma=\frac{M_{\rm cyl}}{\pi R^2}.
 \label{eq:radial-pressure-column}
\end{equation}
Thus an optical excess over the projected orbital mass measures a
negative column of total radial pressure. The orbital projection
requires the force along the ray. Since
$d\overline\Sigma/dR=-2\Delta\Sigma/R$, the contrast
$P(R_1)-P(R_2)=2c^2\int_{R_1}^{R_2}(\Delta\Sigma_o-\Delta\Sigma_w)\,dR/R$
cancels an additive mass sheet. Absolute ring masses depend on
independent lens calibration and external convergence.

The 53 lens galaxies permit a conditional test with one spectrum and
one ring per object. Assume steady nonrotating spherical tracers with
deprojected de Vaucouleurs luminosity density $\nu$. Their radial
and one-component tangential second moments per unit volume,
$j_r$ and $j_t$, obey $(r^2j_r)'=2rj_t-r\nu v_c^2$. Positivity,
with $r^2j_r\to0$ at infinity, implies
$0\le j_r\le J(r)=r^{-2}\int_r^\infty s\nu v_c^2\,ds$.
For a circular fibre of radius $a$, set
$u=\sqrt{\max(0,1-a^2/r^2)}$ and $B=1-u-(1-u^3)/3$.
Integrating the line-of-sight stellar moment over the accepted
solid angles, substituting for $j_t$ and integrating $j'_r$ by
parts gives its luminosity-weighted numerator divided by $4\pi$,
\[
 \frac12\int_0^\infty r^2 B\nu v_c^2\,dr
 +\frac{a^2}{2}\int_0^\infty j_r(\sqrt{a^2+z^2})\,dz .
\]
The circular and radial stellar moment solutions ($j_r=0$ and $j_r=J$)
bound every radial variation of stellar orbital anisotropy. Atmospheric
blurring preserves the bound because a centred circular fibre with
Gaussian seeing has a decreasing radial acceptance, a positive mixture
of circular top hats. We use the 1.5 arcsec fibre radius and a common
1.4 arcsec seeing FWHM. A nonnegative stellar distribution function
can tighten these bounds.

Continue the square-root acceleration calibrated on SPARC into the
stellar body and along the ray, with $v_A^2=\sqrt{GM_*(<r)a_0}$,
$v_*^2=GM_*(<r)/r$ and $a_0=8.7504\times10^{-11}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$.
Light fixes the shape of $M_*$ and the bounded spectral moment constrains
its normalization. If stars lens at the ordinary coupling and $q_A$
is the anomalous optical weight, the ring mass is the projected stellar mass
plus $q_A$ times the projected anomalous orbital mass. Allowing each
measured stellar dispersion two quoted errors yields the following
intersections of the individual necessary intervals~\cite{bolton2008,auger2009}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
Sample and stellar mass constraint & Isothermal ellipsoid & Light traces mass\\
\hline
53 lenses, free stellar normalization & $[0.955,1.441]$ & $[0.925,1.342]$\\
18 lenses, stellar-population bracket & $[1.038,1.233]$ & $[0.943,1.174]$\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The columns use two reconstructions of the same arcs. For the 18
matched galaxies, the stellar mass ranges from the Chabrier estimate
minus one quoted logarithmic error to the Salpeter estimate plus one.
These population brackets set both active endpoints. For J2300+0022
the isothermal-ellipsoid ring gives $P/c^2$ between $-1872$ and $-52$
in $M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$ at 4.534 kpc. The alternative reconstruction
gives $[-1796,+79]$ at 4.424 kpc. Reducing the ellipsoid ring radii
by $2\%$ also admits unit weight across the 18 galaxies. These
intervals describe parameter boxes with no assigned joint confidence
level. The central continuation, spherical light profile and
external mass calibration enter the comparison. Assigning a measured
$P$ to the collar requires an independent prediction of its sign and
size after ordinary stresses and foreground mass are accounted for.
The per-lens values and sensitivity calculations accompany the

\paragraph{Pressure between two rings of the same galaxy.}
SDSS~J0946+1006 lenses two sources at spectroscopic redshifts 0.609
and 2.035. The rings at $1.43\pm0.01$ and $2.07\pm0.02$ arcsec sample
the foreground galaxy at 5.115 and 7.404 kpc for the stated flat
distance calibration~\cite{jackpot2008,jackpot2021}. Consider their
aligned circular two-plane interpretation. Write $C_i$ for the critical
surface density of the primary lens for source $i$, $S$ for a constant
external surface density on the primary plane, and $b_{12}$ for the
reduced radial bend of the outer ray by the intervening source.
The equations are $\overline\Sigma_{w1}+S=C_1$ and
$\overline\Sigma_{w2}+S=C_2(1-b_{12}/\theta_2)$. For a centered
positive secondary with the outer ray on the same side of the axis,
$b_{12}\ge0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:radial-pressure-column} therefore gives
\[
 \frac{P(R_1)-P(R_2)}{c^2}
 \le \overline\Sigma_o(<R_1)-\overline\Sigma_o(<R_2)-(C_1-C_2).
\]
The primary sheet cancels. An exterior spherical mass shell places
more mass per unit aperture area inside the outer cylinder than
inside the inner one. Indeed, for $r\ge R_2$ its fractional
contribution divided by $\pi R^2$ is
$[\pi r^2(1+\sqrt{1-R^2/r^2})]^{-1}$, increasing with $R$.
Consequently, at fixed interior orbital mass profile, deleting all
mass outside $R_2$ gives an upper bound over every nonnegative
exterior orbital mass density.

Use the two observed stellar light components~\cite{jackpot2012} and
the preceding square-root force law only out to $R_2$. Over the
individual Chabrier stellar-mass and light-profile error ranges,
both quoted ring errors and the SPARC bootstrap scale interval,
the truncated orbital contrast is at most
$941\,M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$. The critical-density difference is
$1055.58\,M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$. Hence the inner pressure column
must lie below the outer by at least $114\,c^2 M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-2}$
on this branch. To obtain a nonnegative pressure contrast by
adding a positive spherical orbital source requires at least
$1.72\times10^{10}M_\odot$ inside $R_2$, since its maximum weight
per unit mass is $(R_1^{-2}-R_2^{-2})/\pi$. Doubling both ring
errors gives a minimum contrast deficit of 76 in the same surface-density
units. Including the alternative inner-ring fits of
Ref.~\cite{bolton2008} lowers that bound to 10.

The nominal Salpeter component masses admit an explicit zero-pressure
alternative if the square-root continuation extends along the ray.
It requires $S=0.145\,C_1$ and $b_{12}=0.108$ arcsec, equivalent to
an isothermal secondary of dispersion $80.8\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$.
The secondary luminosity gives $59\pm13\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ on a
Tully--Fisher calibration~\cite{jackpot2008}. In the same Salpeter
orbital field an isotropic Jeans model for the two measured light
components gives a primary fibre dispersion of $259.4\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$,
against the independent spectrum at $263\pm21\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$.
The nominal Chabrier field gives $196.9\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ under
isotropy. The observed arcs are
partial and the two fitted stellar components have projected axis
ratios 0.66 and 0.71. Departures from circular lensing, additional
lens planes and the stellar mass calibration affect the pressure
inference. The pressure contrast constrains a proposed total stress;
its assignment to a collar requires independent material data.

The optical contrast can also be read from an elliptical fit to both

\paragraph{A resolved stellar test of the orbital field.}
The same galaxy has independent MUSE stellar spectra~\cite{jackpot2024}.
At the central bin the observed root-mean-square line-of-sight speed is
$276\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. The nominal spherical isotropic Jeans predictions,
after $0.5$ arcsec seeing, are $208$ and $278\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ for
the Chabrier and Salpeter mass scales. A second stellar bin centred at
$1.01$ arcsec limits the freedom to increase the central speed by adding
gravity beyond $S=2.07$ arcsec. A spherical Jeans shell at $s\ge S$
contributes the nonnegative kernel
$\sqrt{(1-R^2/s^2)_+}$ at sky radius $R$. After Gaussian seeing of
width $\sigma$, that kernel lies between
$1-(x^2+2\sigma^2)/S^2$ and one at projected position $x$.
With an upper reading of $247\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ in the second bin,
even the two upper Chabrier component masses and upper SPARC acceleration
give a central upper limit of $262\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ over arbitrary
nonnegative exterior force. The observed central bar extends down to
$270\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ after outward rounding. Thus the lighter branch
that requires the signed pressure contrast also misses a separate observable
under the adopted assumptions. To cover both bars with an added spherical
force, its enclosed orbital mass equivalent $r\,\delta v_c^2/G$ must
reach at least $8.4\times10^8 M_\odot$ somewhere inside the outer ring.
This follows since the seeing-averaged kernel of any shell is bounded by
the Gaussian probability of its disk, at most $\min(1,s^2/2\sigma^2)$.
The comparison uses fixed published light
scales, a spherical isotropic stellar tracer, and positive weights within
$R\le0.16$ and $0.64\le R\le1.37$ arcsec for the two bins.
These footprints allow for a spaxel and for reading the plotted bin widths.
The two graphical error bars carry no joint significance level here.
Flattening, stellar orbital anisotropy or an extra central mass change
this test. The
\href{https://github.com/FloatingPragma/observer-patch-holography/tree/main/code/cosmology/rar_deep_regime/double_ring_pressure/resolved_kinematics}{archived comparison}
gives the shell bound and the measured stellar bins.

A second telescope tests the stellar-orbit assumption. The Keck/LRIS
central $1.00\times0.21$ arcsec slit measurement gives
$287\pm11\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ for the dispersion~\cite{jackpot2012};
including the mean velocity leaves the lower endpoint at 276.
Combining this slit with the outer MUSE bin gives a central upper
sum of $265.4\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ under isotropy. Here both light
scales are fixed, the Chabrier component masses and acceleration may
vary over their quoted ranges, and exterior gravity is free and
attractive. Allow any spherical stellar anisotropy profile
$0\le\beta(r)\le r^2/(r^2+a^2)$, with $\beta=1-\langle v_\theta^2\rangle/
\langle v_r^2\rangle$. The bound stays below 276 for $a\ge3.11$ arcsec,
even when the outer orbits become radial. The calculation uses the
separate $0.8$ arcsec Keck and $0.5$ arcsec MUSE seeing and allows
a $0.03$ arcsec slit-centering error. Using the complete frozen force
and nominal Salpeter stellar masses without adjustment predicts
$272.9\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ centrally. Three of the 11 signed slit bins
bracket this profile. Seven predictions stay below their observed lower endpoints
even after the centering allowance. Nominal Chabrier gives
$205.0\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ centrally and falls below all 11 bins.

Stronger radial anisotropy illustrates the ambiguity of two speeds.
An Osipkov--Merritt tracer with $a=0.4$ arcsec, the upper Chabrier
