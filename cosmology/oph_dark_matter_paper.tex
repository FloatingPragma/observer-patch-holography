\begin{abstract}
Under a supplied universal coupling, geometry responds to modular charge
without regard to electromagnetic visibility. An overlap-collar recovery
defect provides a conditional non-luminous source whose stress is bounded
by that defect and vanishes at full recovery. A common exponential recovery
envelope suppresses compact sources; an erasure-record model supplies an
explicit defect law. For nonnegative anomalous mass, deep-regime scale
covariance and quadrature composition uniquely give
\(a_A=\sqrt{a_ba_0}\) and \(v^4=GM_ba_0\), with one undetermined
constant. A per-cut model expresses it as \(a_0=Gn^2c\).
These are conditional source and profile results, not a derivation of the
physical cut density, variance or relativistic stress. Retrospective galaxy
fits test the profile and reveal material weighting and covariance sensitivity.
Lensing, cosmological abundance and perturbation dynamics require a common
covariant completion.

\end{abstract}


\section{What Sources Gravity in OPH}

The finite classification, recovery and profile identities are checked under
the stated hypotheses in the Lean formalization~\cite{ophlean}. Their physical
identification is an additional premise throughout.


The conditional Einstein arrow of the spacetime paper ends in
\begin{equation}
G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G\langle T_{ab}\rangle,
\label{eq:einstein}
\end{equation}
where \(\langle T_{ab}\rangle\) is a supplied local, covariant, conserved
stress on the physical branch~\cite{ophEinstein}. Its smooth form requires
same-family tensor-curvature convergence or the continuum
small-ball/null-balance identification; scalar-curvature convergence alone is
diagnostic. Finite positive-null
tomography exactly reconstructs a quadratic form from directional charges,
but identifying that form with the same local stress additionally requires
charge--stress matching, locality and covariance, and a weak Ward law. In the
rest frame the relation then follows from three further premises on a small
geodesic ball of radius \(\ell\): generalized entropy is declared stationary,
the bulk entropy variation equals
\((8\pi^2\ell^4/15)\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle\), and the fixed-volume area
variation equals \(-(4\pi\ell^4/15)\,\delta G_{00}\). Exact arithmetic gives
\(\delta G_{00}=8\pi G\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle\).

On that conditional branch, the stress entering this relation is identified
with the same modular charge. The screen does not
consult the electromagnetic readout of a source before responding to it. That
is the universal-coupling premise, and it is the only input the dark-sector
question takes from the gravity derivation.

\section{The Generic Dark Sector}
\label{sec:generic}

Let a finite family of screen sources be indexed by \(i\), each with
rest-frame modular-charge contribution \(q_i\) and a flag recording whether
it carries the electromagnetic readout. Write \(Q_{\rm lum}\) for the sum over
flagged sources and \(Q_{\rm dark}\) for the sum over the others, so that
\(Q_{\rm tot}=Q_{\rm lum}+Q_{\rm dark}\).

\begin{premise}[Universal coupling]
\label{pre:universal}
The stress contraction entering the rest-frame Einstein relation equals
\(Q_{\rm tot}\), with no readout-dependent weight.
\end{premise}

\begin{proposition}[Total and non-luminous response]
\label{thm:all-charge}\label{thm:iff}\label{cor:excess}\label{prop:sign}
Under Premise~\ref{pre:universal} and \(G>0\),
\(\delta G_{00}=8\pi G(Q_{\rm lum}+Q_{\rm dark})\).
The luminous-only equation holds exactly when \(Q_{\rm dark}=0\).
If every non-luminous charge is nonnegative, its weak-field contribution
has the attractive sign.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the finite-sum decomposition of \(Q_{\rm tot}\) in the assumed
response law. Cancel \(8\pi G\) for the equivalence and sum nonnegative
terms for the sign.
\end{proof}
This classifies a geometric excess if one exists; it does not establish a
nonzero charge, its abundance, or a physical carrier.

\section{The Modular-Anomaly Source}
\label{sec:source}

On the support-visible branch the cap modular generator has the local form
\begin{equation}
K_C=2\pi B_C+K_C^{\rm anom}+\mathrm{const},
\label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
with \(B_C\) the bulk boost generator and \(K_C^{\rm anom}\) the nonadditive
part carried on the overlap collar~\cite{ophEinstein}. At finite regulator
stage the separately supplied collar-recovery model carries this part as a
remainder and bounds it:
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\delta\langle E_C^{(\delta)}\rangle\bigr|\le C_C\,\eta_\delta,
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_\delta\) is the collar recovery defect and \(C_C\) depends only
on the collar model and the bounded observable class.

\begin{definition}[Anomalous stress and density]
\begin{equation}
\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle
:=\frac{15}{8\pi^2\ell^4}\,\delta\langle E_C^{(\delta)}\rangle,
\qquad
\rho_A:=\frac{1}{c^2}\,\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle .
\label{eq:tanom}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The dark-sector adapter identifies this anomalous modular energy with the
non-luminous charge of Section~\ref{sec:generic}. Its absence of an
electromagnetic readout is a source-localization hypothesis: it is assigned to
the part of the modular generator that no local record exposes. Neither
identification follows from the finite Einstein-shape theorem. Three consequences follow from
\eqref{eq:bound} and \eqref{eq:tanom}.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded source]
\label{thm:bounded}
\(\bigl|\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle\bigr|
\le 15\,C_C\,\eta_\delta/(8\pi^2\ell^4)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Full recovery switches the source off]
\label{thm:off}
If \(\eta_\delta=0\) then \(\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle=0\).
Along any family with fixed \(\ell\), bounded \(C_C\), and
\(\eta_\delta\to0\), the anomalous stress tends to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cold scaling]
\label{thm:cold}
A conserved anomalous charge in a comoving cell of physical volume
\(\propto a^3\) has density \(\propto a^{-3}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proofs]
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded} multiplies \eqref{eq:bound} by the positive kernel
inverse. Theorem~\ref{thm:off} is the case \(\eta_\delta=0\) of the bound and
a squeeze along the family. Theorem~\ref{thm:cold} is
\(\rho\,a^3=Q\).
\end{proof}

The split \eqref{eq:split} has a machine-checked finite counterpart. On a
finite carrier of microstates labelled bulk or collar, with strictly
positive weights, a declared boost datum vanishing on the collar, and
modular generator \(K=-\log w\), the decomposition
\(K=2\pi B+K^{\rm anom}+c\,\mathbf 1\) exists with \(c\) the bulk-weighted
mean of \(K-2\pi B\), is unique among splits whose anomalous part has
vanishing bulk mean, and is invariant under the additive-constant
quotient \(w\mapsto e^{-t}w\). When the bulk weights are exactly Gibbs for
\(2\pi B\), the anomalous part vanishes on the bulk and is carried
entirely on the collar, and its expectation is the remainder bounded in
\eqref{eq:bound}. The finite carrier, the boost datum, the normalization
clause, and the Gibbs condition are model premises; the receipt is the
finite counterpart of \eqref{eq:split}, at exactly that strength. The
executable observer does not supply these premises: its full configuration
reference approaches the normalized trace at fixed modular parameter, so it
identifies no quantum vacuum and its tracial limit has trivial modular flow,
and a bulk/collar split of its source is not
constructed~\cite{ophtimefields,ophentropy}. Its regional bulk/edge entropy split
on one seam carries no boost generator or geometric normalization~\cite{ophEinstein}.

In the weak-field limit the anomalous density enters the Poisson equation
beside the baryons,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\,(\rho_b+\rho_A),
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
with the attractive sign of Proposition~\ref{prop:sign} whenever the
anomalous charge is nonnegative.

\section{Galactic-Scale Collars and the Solar System}
\label{sec:solar}

The collars in \eqref{eq:split} are overlap collars between observer
patches, and the recovery defect \(\eta_\delta\) measures how far the
repaired record on a collar falls short of determining its complement. Where
records are dense and repair saturates, the defect is small; where the
screen is sparsely recorded, over the settled outskirts of a galaxy, the
defect is carried on long-lived cuts. The consensus-layer provenance package
supplies a narrower finite statement~\cite{ophconsensus}: an injected overlap
mismatch persists at full strength through any continuation in which no later
commit writes a register in that overlap support. This is an unwritten-support
persistence theorem. It does not show that a physical galactic cut satisfies
the no-write hypothesis or remains long-lived. The proposed physical
identification assigns this anomalous record state to galactic collars.
The native continuum of one repair history is a two-dimensional spatial
operator on the screen with no radial domain, and its internal rank-three
modes vanish from bounded-energy families; a bulk reading needs a radial
observation algebra that the native energy does not
supply~\cite{ophcontinuum}. The map from screen collars to nested
three-dimensional galactic shells is therefore part of this identification.

\begin{premise}[Collar scale]
\label{pre:scale}
The collars carrying \(\delta\langle E_C^{(\delta)}\rangle\) are
galactic-scale settled cuts. A compact source inside the galaxy does not
generate anomalous charge of its own; it sits in the ambient anomalous
density \(\rho_A\) of its neighbourhood.
\end{premise}

A conditional saturation theorem supports limiting suppression in the
compact-source part of this premise. The collar-recovery claim of the
spacetime paper~\cite{ophEinstein} bounds the
defect on its conditional mixing branch by an exponential envelope, which we
write in the local record density \(\rho\) as
\begin{equation}
\eta_\delta\le\kappa\,B\,
\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\delta\rho^{1/3}-r_0}{\zeta}\Bigr),
\label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
with \(\kappa\) the mixing prefactor, \(B\) the boundary size in UV cells,
\(r_0\) an offset, \(\zeta\) the decay constant, and \(\delta\rho^{1/3}\)
the collar depth counted in UV record spacings.

\begin{theorem}[Compact-source saturation]
\label{thm:saturation}
Under the envelope \eqref{eq:envelope}, fix common parameters
\(\kappa\geq0\), \(B\geq1\), \(r_0\geq0\), and \(\zeta>0\).
Along a family of collars with fixed radius \(\ell>0\) and uniformly bounded
nonnegative collar constant \(C_C\), the recovery defect and anomalous stress
tend to zero as the depth tends to infinity at fixed positive record density,
or as the record density tends to infinity at fixed positive depth.
Quantitatively,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\delta\langle T_{00}^{\rm anom}\rangle\bigr|
\le\frac{15\,C_C}{8\pi^2\ell^4}\,\kappa B\,
\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\delta\rho^{1/3}-r_0}{\zeta}\Bigr),
\label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
For \(\kappa B>0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), the defect envelope bound falls
below \(\varepsilon\) exactly when
\(\delta\rho^{1/3}>r_0+\zeta\log(\kappa B/\varepsilon)\).
For \(\kappa=0\), the defect and stress vanish at every admissible depth.
Thus the bounded stress tends to zero in these fixed-parameter limits.
\end{theorem}

The delimitation is exact in both directions and machine-checked. The
identically zero defect satisfies every envelope hypothesis, so the envelope
never forces a nonzero defect: that the galactic-scale settled cuts carry an
order-one defect is a premise, not a consequence. And an explicit sparsely
recorded instance sits at the bound with an order-one defect and strictly
positive stress, so the same hypotheses that screen dense regions admit a
nonzero dark source on sparse cuts. What is not established is that physical
collars satisfy \eqref{eq:envelope}: the envelope is the conditional mixing
branch of the collar-recovery claim, taken here as a hypothesis. The
executable observer's native repairs have an exact quantum mixing gap
\((5-\sqrt5)/60\) per uniform seam attempt on the twelve-port
carrier~\cite{ophmixing}; that gap concerns relaxation in native event count
and supplies no envelope in collar depth.

The delimitation has a positive counterpart in which the defect is computed
rather than admitted. In the erasure-record model, a source bit perfectly
correlated with its complement and a collar record carrying the bit with
probability \(p\), the minimum prediction error over all predictors,
deterministic or randomized, is exactly \((1-p)/2\): an unrecorded cut
carries an order-one defect necessarily, a saturated record carries none,
and the defect falls below \(\varepsilon\) exactly when
\(p>1-2\varepsilon\), the mechanism-side counterpart of the settled-cut
threshold. With unit collar radius and collar constant, and anomalous energy
equal to the model defect, the unrecorded instance carries stress
\(15/(16\pi^2)\) exactly. The identification of the model
defect with \(\eta_\delta\) is a modeling premise, and the record density
of a physical galactic-scale cut is not specified; the model shows the
mechanism class exists, with an exact defect-density law.

Under Premise~\ref{pre:scale} the Solar System is a test of the ambient
density alone.

\begin{proposition}[An isotropic ambient density has no quadrupole]
\label{prop:isotropic}
The potential of a locally uniform density is \((2\pi G\rho_A/3)\,r^2\). Its
projection on the quadrupole weight \(P_2(\cos\theta)\) vanishes,
\(\int_{-1}^{1}(\mu^2-\tfrac13)\dd\mu=0\), so the quadrupole coefficient
\(Q_2\) is zero. The only footprint is the isotropic tidal scale
\(4\pi G\rho_A/3\).
\end{proposition}

With the local dark density
\(\rho_A\simeq6.8\times10^{-22}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), the value inferred
from Galactic kinematics~\cite{desalas2021}, the tidal scale is
\begin{equation}
\frac{4\pi G\rho_A}{3}=1.9\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}},
\label{eq:tidal}
\end{equation}
For a dimensional scale comparison, the Cassini trace-free quadrupole result
is \(Q_2=(1.6\pm1.8)\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}\)~\cite{park2026}, four
orders of magnitude above the isotropic scale. It is not a direct bound on
the traceful isotropic channel, whose quadrupole is identically zero. The
anomalous mass enclosed within ten astronomical units is
\(5\times10^{-15}\,M_\odot\). Both numbers are machine-checked inequalities
in the formalization. The Solar System constrains the premise of
Section~\ref{sec:solar} and nothing else in the construction.

\section{The Deep-Regime Profile}
\label{sec:deep}

Around a baryonic mass \(M_b\), the proposed collar-count interpretation
assigns a number of settled cuts proportional to the enclosing radius
\(r\). The precise deep-regime characterization uses the following two
premises, each independently falsifiable.

\begin{premise}[Deep-regime scale covariance]
\label{pre:cov}
In the deep regime the enclosed anomalous mass is degree-one homogeneous in
the radius: \(M_A(M_b,\lambda r)=\lambda\,M_A(M_b,r)\) for every
\(\lambda>0\). A sparsely recorded deep cut retains no length scale, so
rescaling the radius rescales the enclosed mass by the same factor.
\end{premise}

\begin{premise}[Quadrature composition]
\label{pre:quad}
The anomalous amplitudes of independent baryonic sources compose in
quadrature:
\(M_A(m_1+m_2,r)^2=M_A(m_1,r)^2+M_A(m_2,r)^2\). The collar defects of
distinct sources are independent, their variances add, and the gravitating
anomalous charge is the amplitude of the unrecovered record fluctuation.
\end{premise}

\begin{theorem}[Profile characterization]
\label{thm:char}
Let \(M_A(M_b,r)\) be nonnegative, monotone in the source, somewhere
nonzero, and satisfy Premises~\ref{pre:cov} and \ref{pre:quad}. Then there
is a unique constant \(a_0>0\) with
\begin{equation}
M_A(r)=r\sqrt{\frac{M_ba_0}{G}},
\qquad
\rho_A(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}\sqrt{\frac{M_ba_0}{G}}.
\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scale covariance factorizes \(M_A(M_b,r)=r\,f(M_b)\). Quadrature
composition makes \(f^2\) additive on positive sources, monotonicity makes
it monotone, and a function additive and monotone on the positive reals is
linear: \(f^2=\lambda M_b\) with \(\lambda>0\) by nondegeneracy. Setting
\(a_0=\lambda G\) gives \eqref{eq:profile}; uniqueness follows by
evaluating at \(M_b=r=1\). Each premise is load-bearing: an additive law
\(M_A\propto rM_b\) satisfies everything except quadrature composition, and
an \(r^2\) law everything except scale covariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear enclosed mass]
\label{pre:linear}
The deep-regime density is the shell derivative of the enclosed mass,
\(M_A'(r)=4\pi r^2\rho_A(r)\), with \(M_A\) linear in \(r\) and the
coefficient \(\sqrt{M_ba_0/G}\) forced by
Theorem~\ref{thm:char}.
\end{corollary}

Deep-limit scale invariance is the known symmetry of the modified-dynamics
limit~\cite{milgrom2009}, and square-root mass laws appear in
entropic-gravity proposals~\cite{verlinde2016}. The resulting law is the
deep limit of modified Newtonian dynamics, \(a_A=\sqrt{a_ba_0}\), with its
baryonic Tully--Fisher corollary; the characterization adds that the two
premises force it jointly, and it reads the square root as the variance
additivity of independent collar defects under source composition. It does
not fix \(a_0\).

Premise~\ref{pre:quad} itself follows from a more primitive per-cut model.
Suppose the \(nr\) nested cuts within radius \(r\) each carry anomalous
amplitude \(\sqrt{V(M_b)}\), the standard deviation of the collar defect
sourced by the baryons, with the variances of independent sources adding
and the variance monotone in the source. Then the variance function is
forced linear, \(V(M_b)=cM_b\) with a unique \(c>0\), quadrature
composition is a theorem rather than a premise, the induced law satisfies
Theorem~\ref{thm:char}, and its constant is exactly
\begin{equation}
a_0=G\,n^2c .
\label{eq:dictionary}
\end{equation}
The dictionary is exact but determines only the product \(n^2c\): two
models with different cut densities and equal \(n^2c\) induce the same
enclosed-mass law, machine-checked. The magnitude question is therefore
the value of the collar density and of the per-cut variance constant;
neither is derived here.

One sufficient realization of both premises rests one level deeper. Variance
additivity follows from probabilistic independence: for atomic defect
contributions with finite second moments, pairwise independence, and a
common per-atom variance on one probability space, the variance of the
compound defect of every finite atom family is the sum of the per-atom
variances, so disjoint-union composition is additive and \(V(m)=cm\) on
the modeled masses. Independence is stronger than necessary; pairwise zero
covariance and other dependent but uncorrelated constructions are viable.
An exact linear-asymptotic cut count follows from one rigid geometry: a
cut family with a positive first cut, consecutive gaps of at least \(s\),
and every length-\(s\) interval meeting a cut is exactly the arithmetic
progression with spacing \(s\), its count obeys
\(r/s-1\le N(r)\le r/s+1\), and the counted enclosed mass deviates from
the linear law by at most one per-cut amplitude at every radius, with
\(n=1/s\). A perfectly correlated pair and a one-cut family show only that
the conclusions do not follow after deleting every decorrelation or density
condition. They do not make independence or exact spacing necessary.
Irregular or ergodic cut families with asymptotic density and sublinear count
error are possible. The exact \(nr\) per-cut law is the continuum or
mean extension; the discrete count above differs from it by a bounded error.

\begin{theorem}[Deep radial-acceleration relation]
\label{thm:rar}
The anomalous acceleration of the profile \eqref{eq:profile} is
\begin{equation}
a_A(r)=\frac{GM_A(r)}{r^2}=\frac{\sqrt{GM_ba_0}}{r}=\sqrt{a_b(r)\,a_0},
\qquad a_b=\frac{GM_b}{r^2}.
\label{eq:deep}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Flat rotation curve and baryonic Tully--Fisher relation]
\label{thm:btfr}
The circular speed supported by \(a_A\) satisfies
\(v^2=a_A r=\sqrt{GM_ba_0}\), independent of \(r\), and hence
\begin{equation}
v^4=GM_b\,a_0 .
\label{eq:btfr}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proofs]
\(GM_A/r^2=G\sqrt{M_ba_0/G}/r=\sqrt{GM_ba_0}/r\), and
\(\sqrt{GM_ba_0}/r=\sqrt{(GM_b/r^2)a_0}\) for \(r>0\). Multiplying by \(r\)
removes the radius; squaring gives \eqref{eq:btfr}.
\end{proof}

The exponents \(1/2\) in \eqref{eq:deep} and \(4\) in \eqref{eq:btfr} are
the parameter-free content of the profile. The constant \(a_0\) collects
\(C_C\), the collar density, and the small-ball normalization; none of these
is fixed by the source, so \(a_0\) enters every comparison as a fitted
value. The deep regime is the range \(a_b\ll a_0\), equivalently
\(r\gg r_M=\sqrt{GM_b/a_0}\). If the deep expression is algebraically
extended to the matching radius, both accelerations equal \(a_0\) there and
the anomalous term dominates outside \(r_M\); those algebraic statements are
machine-checked. The premises are asserted only in the deep regime, so they
do not determine the physical transition or the response inside \(r_M\).

\section{Lensing and the Relativistic Source}
\label{sec:lensing}

The anomalous charge must enter \eqref{eq:einstein} through one stress tensor;
Premise~\ref{pre:universal} supplies no readout-dependent coupling. That fact
does not imply equality of masses inferred from null geodesics and timelike
orbits. Those observables contract different components of the metric response
and can differ in the presence of pressure, anisotropic stress, or gravitational
slip. No relativistic refinement limit is supplied that turns the weak-field
density \eqref{eq:profile} into a full \(T_{ab}^{\rm anom}\). A lensing law and
the relation between lensing and dynamical mass are therefore physical
constructions that this paper does not supply.

\section{Comparison with Rotation-Curve Data}
\label{sec:data}

Theorems~\ref{thm:rar} and \ref{thm:btfr} are compared with the SPARC sample
of 175 disk galaxies with \(3.6\,\mu\)m photometry~\cite{sparc2016} under the
declared inclusive-error deep-law diagnostic: quality flag at most 2,
inclination at least \(30^\circ\), velocity error at most ten percent, and
stellar mass-to-light ratios \(0.5\) for disks and \(0.7\) for bulges. This
leaves 2700 points in 149 galaxies. The strict published-cut replay described
below is kept separate. Both computations are released with the
paper~\cite{mls2016,ophrar}.

\paragraph{Source-bound full-RAR calibration.}
Separate from the conditional OPH deep law, a deterministic replay fits the
published empirical interpolation
\[
g_{\rm obs}=\frac{g_{\rm bar}}
 {1-\exp[-\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}/a_0}]}
\]
to a committed CDS/VizieR snapshot whose provenance, catalogue description,
and two table files are pinned by SHA-256. The catalogue cuts reproduce the
published 153-galaxy parent selection. The strict point cuts retain 2696
points in 147 contributing galaxies, three more than the published 2693-point
census. These counts are distinct from the preceding 2700-point, 149-galaxy
deep-law diagnostic: that diagnostic uses the inclusive cut
\(dV/V\le0.1\), whereas this full-RAR replay uses the strict cut
\(dV/V<0.1\) and starts from the 153-galaxy parent set. With disk and bulge
mass-to-light values 0.5 and 0.7, the unweighted
point-level log-residual fit gives
\[
a_0=(1.1613\mathbin{\pm}0.0802)\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},
\qquad \sigma_{\log_{10}g}=0.1327\ \mathrm{dex},
\]
where the width is the standard deviation of a galaxy bootstrap. Equal total
weight per contributing galaxy shifts \(a_0\) by \(-9.58\%\); weighting only
by inverse fractional velocity variance shifts it by \(+15.49\%\). These are
estimator-sensitivity controls, not calibrated likelihood alternatives. The
interpolation and acceleration scale were known before this replay, no OPH
source value enters the fit, and distance, inclination, gas, stellar
population, intrinsic scatter, and correlation uncertainties are not jointly
marginalized. The result certifies public-data custody and arithmetic, not
independent evidence for OPH~\cite{mls2016,ophrar}.

\paragraph{Radial acceleration.}
On fixed absolute subsets \(g_{\rm bar}<f a_{\rm ref}\), with the
comparison-only value
\(a_{\rm ref}=1.2\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) fixed before fitting,
the density profile predicts the total acceleration
\(g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}+\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}a_0}\). The fixed-exponent
diagnostic gives
\[
a_0=0.843\times10^{-10},\quad 0.875\times10^{-10},\quad 0.924\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]
for \(f=0.3\), \(0.1\), and \(0.03\), with 1519, 960, and 203 points in 141,
115, and 40 galaxies and rms scatter between \(0.14\) and \(0.17\) dex. Fitting
an anomalous power inside the total model, using every retained point rather
than selecting on the sign of the noisy excess, gives exponents \(0.454\),
\(0.461\), and \(0.239\). Galaxy-cluster bootstrap intervals contain
\(1/2\) for the first two cuts; the deepest cut is sparse and its interval is
wide. The computation uses equal retained-point weighting and does not
marginalize distance, inclination, stellar-population, or correlated
rotation-curve uncertainties, so it is a diagnostic rather than a full
measurement likelihood.

\paragraph{Tully--Fisher and matched channels.}
The 123 quality-selected galaxies with positive flat velocity give
\(a_0=v_f^4/(GM_b)=1.55\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), with
\(0.26\) dex scatter, for \(M_b=0.5L_{[3.6]}+1.33M_{\rm HI}\).
The ordinary least-squares exponent is \(3.75\pm0.10\), placing the
fixed exponent \(4\) at \(+2.66\sigma\) in this diagnostic. Catalogued
flat speeds retain a baryonic contribution at finite radius and need not
measure the anomalous speed entering the theorem.

A matched comparison uses
\(v_A^2=v_{\rm obs}^2-v_{\rm bar}^2\) at the outermost radius,

\paragraph{Covariance and nuisance sensitivity.}
