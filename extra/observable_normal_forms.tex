\begin{abstract}
Confluence asks whether reductions from one source rejoin. Many reconstruction
problems ask a different question: whether states with the same observable
boundary settle to the same public output. This paper gives certificates for
that stronger comparison after silent representation changes are quotiented
away. Two moduli turn residual and observation errors into an endpoint bound.
The bound survives arbitrary refinement depth, and compatible finite levels
define a solver-independent projective limit. The paper also proves repair and
symmetry obstructions, gives a checkable conditional-expectation resampling
projector, and classifies two succinct decision problems as coNP- and
\(\Pi_2^P\)-complete. For a declared finite scheduler, four nested
protected-behavior tests separate empty fibers, failed positive reach,
non-almost-sure reach, and ambiguous first-hit endpoint classes, and an exact
finite-state morphism preserves the resulting profile. On the canonical OPH
adaptive repair branch, every attempt stream is eventually constant;
pathwise weak fairness makes the stable state normal, while work conservation
gives a normal horizon bounded by the initial mismatch count. Under the
separately stated completeness and confluence hypotheses, weak-fair runs reach
one canonical raw and public endpoint. Counting every scheduler invocation,
including stutter, gives a genuinely restrictive cumulative capacity: no
initial-mismatch-only finite attempt horizon exists for arbitrary normalizing
schedulers, whereas bounded waste gives a quantitative upper horizon, with an
exact sharp threshold in the work-conserving case, and a capacity-bounded public endpoint. On the separate input-independent compiler branch, one emission-order round-robin cycle reaches the exact output in \(n\) attempts, arbitrary accepted repair order has order-sharp \(\Theta(n^2)\) paths, and weak fairness alone has no uniform finite attempt horizon. The Markov-chain and fair-termination
ingredients are standard; the contribution is their source-bound assembly
around protected observations, canonical repair, and the existing behavior-cut
interface. These results supply the observable-normal-form machinery used by the
Observer-Patch Holography consensus and Yang--Mills papers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A confluent rewrite system has a familiar geometry: paths may split, but paths from the same source eventually rejoin.  In many applications the source itself is hidden.  What is fixed is an interface, input, conserved sector, sensor record, or boundary condition.  The relevant question is then not
\[
 x\to^*y,\quad x\to^*z\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\downarrow z,
\]
but rather
\[
 B(x)=B(x')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{the settled observable outputs of }x\text{ and }x'\text{ agree}.
\]
The two statements have different proof obligations.  Confluence is a property of paths.  Observable reconstruction is a property of the consistent set and of the observation map on that set.

We use \emph{observation-determined normal form} for a settled state determined
through an observation map on the consistent set.  This is distinct from the
control-theory use of \enquote{observable normal form} for a state-space form
obtained by coordinate transformation, including in Boolean control networks
\citep{ChengLiQi2010}.

Let $Q$ be a state space after quotienting representation changes that are regarded as silent, let $C\subseteq Q$ be the consistent states, and let
\[
 B:Q\longrightarrow\cB
\]
be protected observable data.  For $b\in\cB$, set
\[
 C_b:=C\cap B^{-1}(b).
\]
The three possibilities
\[
 |C_b|=0,\qquad |C_b|=1,\qquad |C_b|>1
\]
mean, respectively, that the observation is unrealizable, reconstructing, or ambiguous.  Termination and confluence do not alter this trichotomy.  They control how a computation reaches $C$; they do not determine how many points of $C$ share one observation.

The distinction becomes more consequential under perturbation.  Exact injectivity of $B|_C$ says nothing about conditioning.  A sequence of finite systems may have injective observation maps at every stage and nevertheless become arbitrarily unstable under refinement.  To capture the missing information, we use two moduli:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item an error-bound modulus carrying a constraint residual to distance from $C$;
\item an inverse-observation modulus carrying observation discrepancy within $C$ to state discrepancy.
\end{enumerate}
The first is an error-bound or metric-subregularity object in the sense of
variational analysis
\citep{Hoffman1952,Kruger2015,CibulkaDontchevKruger2018,DontchevRockafellar2014}.
The second is the modulus of continuity of the inverse of $B|_C$.  Both notions
are standard.  Their use as an \emph{observation-determined normal-form
certificate} for constraint and rewrite systems yields the uniform and
multi-stage refinement calculus developed here.

\subsection{Main contributions}

The manuscript has six technical contributions.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item \textbf{Correct exact interface.}
We state a canonical partial normalizer and a boundary-indexed unique-normal-form theorem with the empty-fiber case separated from weak normalization.  An audited-terminal theorem further separates computational quiescence from semantic consistency.  Identification modulo a silent equivalence is characterized exactly by cross-source endpoint agreement.  For a separately declared finite scheduler, we also distinguish the static fiber question from positive first hit, almost-sure first hit, and endpoint-class selection.  The exact confluence implications are explicitly treated as classical.

\item \textbf{Sharp observational stability.}
For a residual $\Phi$ and an observation map $B$, we define intrinsic moduli $\eta_\Phi$ and $\omega_B$.  For arbitrary residual levels $\delta_x,\delta_y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-master}
\begin{split}
 d_Q(x,y)\le{}&\eta_\Phi(\delta_x)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_y)\\
 &+\omega_B\!\left(
 d_{\cB}(Bx,By)+L_B[\eta_\Phi(\delta_x)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_y)]
 \right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
At zero residual the bound is exact by definition.  Across all finite metric systems, the coefficient $2$ in the equal-residual specialization cannot be lowered.

\item \textbf{Uniform refinement towers.}
One-step approximate naturality follows from consistency and observation defects.  We distinguish same-level receipt-conditioned agreement from genuinely cross-level comparison.  An anchor-optimized theorem compares independently computed outputs at arbitrary refinement levels after restriction to a common level.  Compatible nested inputs leave only the intervening defect segment, and summable pathwise defects yield an implementation-independent projective limit.  A counterexample shows that stagewise injectivity is not enough without a uniform inverse modulus.

\item \textbf{Repair and selection obstructions.}
A total exact local repair preserving an exterior collar exists if and only if the local consistency relation projects surjectively onto that collar.  For a finite scheduler, four nested protected-behavior sets and their complementary cuts isolate fiber, positive-reach, almost-sure, and endpoint-selection obstructions.  On the canonical OPH adaptive branch, every attempt stream stabilizes after finitely many genuine changes, pathwise weak fairness is sufficient for normality, and work conservation gives an initial-mismatch bound on the normal horizon.  Counting stutter as positive attempt cost separates that bound from cumulative capacity: no single mismatch-only attempt horizon uniformly bounds all normalizing schedulers, bounded waste gives a quantitative upper horizon, and two nonisomorphic canonical finite sources make the work-conserving threshold sharp.  Completeness and confluence remain explicit requirements for a consistent canonical endpoint.  The underlying finite Markov, weighted-rewriting, and fair-termination facts are standard; the contribution is the source-bound protected-observation certificate, the adaptive attempt/cost classification, and their exact connection to the existing behavior-cut and public-endpoint interfaces.  On a finite full-support probability space, observation-fiber resampling is the weighted orthogonal conditional-expectation projector and has a finite matrix recognition receipt.  A stabilizer obstruction prevents an equivariant selector on ambiguous fibers.

\item \textbf{Constructive and linear classes.}
Ranked functional-audit systems have singleton-or-empty fibers, terminate under every effective asynchronous schedule with an explicit update bound, and settle synchronously in dependency depth; acyclic Boolean circuits compile directly into this class.  Linear quotient systems admit rank tests and singular-value bounds that instantiate \eqref{eq:intro-master}; finite linear dynamics yield the usual observability matrix as a special case.

\item \textbf{Receipts and complexity.}
A standard finite Markov drift lemma turns a row-wise kernel inequality into endpoint, stationary, and expected-occupation receipts, without asserting infinite-horizon confinement under persistent noise.  Machine-checked first-hit laws, exact finite-state transport, and native TwoBit, fixed-relation, and proper-target counterexamples provide separate receipts for the protected-behavior profile.  For succinct Boolean systems, we record tight coNP- and $\Pi_2^P$-completeness classifications in this formulation; these supporting results are not part of the principal novelty claim.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Relation to existing theory}

The exact theory overlaps several established areas.  Abstract rewriting supplies Newman's lemma, Church--Rosser properties, unique normal forms, and normalization theory \citep{Newman1942,Huet1980,BaaderNipkow1998,Terese2003,AotoToyama2018}.  Rewriting modulo equivalence and confluence under invariants address quotient and relevant-state restrictions \citep{ChristiansenKirkeby2016,GallFruehwirth2018,CampbellPlump2021,Lucas2025}.  Quantitative rewriting enriches rewrite relations themselves and proves metric analogues of classical confluence theorems \citep{GavazzoDiFlorio2022}.  Error bounds and inverse stability are mature subjects in variational analysis \citep{Kruger2015,CibulkaDontchevKruger2018}.  Functoriality of rewriting is also established in categorical graph transformation \citep{DuvalEchahedProst2011}.

Cumulative and nonuniform rewrite costs, derivation-height bounds, failed-attempt accounting, and self-stabilizing enabled-move convergence are established theory \citep{Dijkstra1974,KopMiddeldorpSternagel2017,AvanziniYamada2025}.  The contribution below is not a new weighted-rewriting formalism.  It locates the quantitative boundary on the repository's broader chosen-site attempt semantics, where a scheduler may spend capacity on a quiescent site, and then proves the exact additional condition and finite OPH witnesses that recover a sharp bound.

The triangle inequality, error bounds, inverse continuity, section criteria,
boundary-indexed unique normal forms, and the Lipschitz telescope are standard.
The integrated certificate architecture combines them with an explicit
anchor-optimized cross-level receipt comparison and its cofinal-limit
consequence.  Approximate inverse-system theory supplies the neighboring
framework, while the result here is an organizing synthesis for automated
reasoning, rewriting, and semantics.

\section{Relation to existing theories}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Rewriting, invariants, and quotients}

The classical properties of an abstract reduction system are standard \citep{Huet1980,BaaderNipkow1998,Terese2003}.  Confluence restricted to an invariant or taken modulo an equivalence has been developed in Constraint Handling Rules and graph transformation \citep{ChristiansenKirkeby2016,GallFruehwirth2018,CampbellPlump2021}.  Those frameworks are close to the first arrow in
\[
 \text{presentations}\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow\cB:
\]
they identify states that should count as the same or restrict attention to relevant states.  Our observation map is the second arrow.  It may be strictly coarser than equality in $Q$, and it is required to be injective only on the consistent subset when exact reconstruction is desired.

Unique-normal-form properties weaker than confluence are well known and have
been automated for term rewriting \citep{AotoToyama2018}.  The fiber
trichotomy and partial-normalizer universal property here are elementary set
theory; only the reachable-normal-form and fiberwise-confluence corollaries
specialize classical rewriting theory.

\subsection{Quantitative rewriting versus output stability}

Gavazzo and Di Florio enrich rewriting relations over quantales and derive quantitative Newman, Church--Rosser, and critical-pair theorems \citep{GavazzoDiFlorio2022}.  Here the rewrite relation remains ordinary.  Distances are attached to two semantic interfaces: residual-to-solution error and inversion of observable data on the solution set.  Thus the theory does not estimate a quantitative rewrite path; it estimates disagreement between settled outputs of possibly unrelated schedules or initial interiors.

\subsection{Error bounds and inverse mappings}

The inequality
\[
 \dist(x,C)\le \eta(\Phi(x))
\]
is an error-bound property.  In variational analysis, local linear versions are equivalent to metric subregularity or calmness of suitable set-valued mappings \citep{Kruger2015,CibulkaDontchevKruger2018,DontchevRockafellar2014}.  The inverse-observation modulus is a global finite or compact analogue of inverse Lipschitz/calmness information.  Our use of these notions is intentionally transparent: the novelty claim concerns their operational consequences for schedules and refinement towers, not their analytic origin.

\subsection{Approximate inverse systems}

Approximate inverse systems and almost-commuting diagrams have a substantial
topological literature, including stability and resolution theorems for
compacta \citep{MardesicSegal1989,MardesicRubin1989,Mardesic1993}.  Those
works perturb or relax compatibility of bonding data in topological or cover
terms.  Our setting keeps the ambient inverse system exact and instead gives
pointwise metric bounds for normalizer defects, solver receipts, and
protected-observation mismatch.  The connection limits the novelty claim:
the contribution is an explicit certificate adapted to
observation-determined normalizers, not the invention of approximate inverse
systems.

\subsection{Static reconstruction and functional observability}

Control-theoretic observability reconstructs a state, or a prescribed function of state, from an output history.  Linear observability and functional observability supply rank and conditioning tests; nonlinear work characterizes when a nonlinear functional can be reconstructed from measurements and studies the quality of the inverse map \citep{Kailath1980,MontanariEtAl2023,Kravaris2024}.  This is the closest neighboring language for the map $B|_C$.  Our setting is nevertheless static at the theorem interface: $C$ is the settled consistent set, $B$ may be any protected quotient observation, and the questions are how residual error and inverse conditioning control schedule disagreement and how these certificates propagate through refinement towers.

\subsection{Constraints, databases, and global sections}

Acyclic database schemes and positive-semiring relations provide local-to-global consistency results \citep{BeeriEtAl1983,AtseriasKolaitis2020}.  Constraint satisfaction studies local consistency as an algorithmic reduction \citep{DalmauOprsal2023}.  Database repair semantics distinguishes the existence of many consistent repairs from the answers invariant across them \citep{ArenasBertossiChomicki1999}.  Sheaf-theoretic contextuality similarly turns failure of a global section into an obstruction \citep{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}.  Our empty-fiber certificate belongs to this broad lineage.  The additional layer is the interaction with normal-form stability and refinement.

\subsection{Fair scheduling and asynchronous Boolean-network fixing}

Fairness is a qualitative liveness condition rather than a latency bound;
Lamport formulates the general closure issue explicitly
\citep{Lamport2000Fairness}, while the self-stabilization daemon taxonomy
separates fairness, boundedness, and enabledness as independent scheduling
axes \citep{DuboisTixeuil2011}.  Asynchronous Boolean-network work studies
fixing words and their minimal lengths, including monotone, acyclic, tree, and
conjunctive families \citep{AracenaEtAl2019,GadouleauRichard2018}.  We assign
no novelty credit to weak fairness, round robin, fixing words, or quadratic
fixing phenomena themselves.  The source-specific contribution below is the
joint machine-checked classification for the actual nonmonotone
\texttt{go} compiler: its first emission-order cycle, taken in the order in
which the compiler emits nodes, is the existing sweep and
therefore has a linear exact-output horizon, its adversarial genuine
accepted-step paths are order-sharp quadratic in the same emitted-node metric,
and its Tower endpoint distinguishes both from arbitrary weak-fair attempts
and from a separately supplied bounded-waste contract.  General well-formed
federations remain sharply exponential, in line with the order of the acyclic
fixing-length results \citep{GadouleauRichard2018}; the source-specific delta
is that the compiler's single-consumer shape is exactly what removes the
exponent.

\subsection{Asynchronous functional networks}

Kahn process networks provide timing-independent determinacy for networks of
functional components, while generalized Kahn principles treat broader
abstract asynchronous, including nondeterministic, models under their stated
hypotheses \citep{Kahn1974,Abramsky2011}.  Ranked functional systems below
are a finite terminating special case with residual audits.  Their purpose is
to give a class in which the exact boundary hypotheses, update bound, and
obstruction output are explicit.  They do not reprove those principles.

\subsection{Finite Markov reachability and lumping}

First-hitting probabilities, the decomposition into transient and closed
classes in a finite chain, least nonnegative solutions of reachability Bellman
equations, and strong lumpability are standard
\citep{Norris1997,KemenySnell1976,BaierKatoen2008}.  Markov functions and
intertwining provide neighboring language for law pushforward
\citep{RogersPitman1981}.  We use these facts without a novelty claim.  Their
role here is architectural: declared active sources and a protected
observation index four separate failure modes; those modes recover the
pre-existing OPH behavior cuts through explicit supported traces; and exact
transport retains the active-source, target, observation, and silent-quotient
data in addition to the kernel law.

\section{Exact observable fibers}\label{sec:exact}

\begin{definition}[Observable quotient system]
An \emph{observable quotient system} is a tuple
\[
 \mathfrak S=(Q,C,\cB,B),
\]
where $Q$ is a set, $C\subseteq Q$ is the consistent subset, $\cB$ is an observation set, and $B:Q\to\cB$ is the protected observation map.  In applications, $Q$ may itself be a quotient $\Sigma/\Gamma$ of presentations by gauge, implementation, or representation changes.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fiber trichotomy]
For $b\in\cB$, define $C_b=C\cap B^{-1}(b)$.  The observation $b$ is \emph{unrealizable}, \emph{reconstructing}, or \emph{ambiguous} according as $|C_b|$ is $0$, $1$, or greater than $1$.  The system is \emph{observable-determined} if $B|_C$ is injective.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical partial normalizer]
If $B|_C$ is injective, define
\[
 \mathsf N_B:Q\longrightarrow C\sqcup\{\bot\}
\]
by returning the unique member of $C_{B(x)}$ when the fiber is nonempty and $\bot$ otherwise.  The symbol $\bot$ denotes a mathematically proved no-extension outcome, not a failure of search.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Proof-carrying interpretation]
For finite data with decidable membership and equality, exhaustive search
returns the dependent alternative
\[
 \left(\sum_{c\in C} [B(c)=B(x)]\right)
 \;\sqcup\;
 [C_{B(x)}=\varnothing].
\]
Thus \enquote{obstructed} carries a proof of empty fiber.  In the ordinary
classical setting the same alternative is defined by excluded middle and
choice, but injectivity alone is not an algorithm for deciding which side
holds.  Even for finite succinct inputs, such a proof need not be
computationally cheap; \cref{sec:complexity} shows that realizability is
NP-complete.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Universal property]\label{thm:universal}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $B|_C$ is injective.
\item There is a unique map $N:Q\to C\sqcup\{\bot\}$ such that:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $N(x)=c\in C$ implies $B(c)=B(x)$;
  \item $N(x)=\bot$ if and only if $C_{B(x)}=\varnothing$;
  \item $N(c)=c$ for all $c\in C$;
  \item $B(x)=B(y)$ implies $N(x)=N(y)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\item $B|_C:C\to B(C)$ is a bijection.
\end{enumerate}
If $B(Q)\subseteq B(C)$, then $N$ is a total idempotent retraction $Q\to C$ preserving $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $B|_C$ is injective, every nonempty fiber contains exactly one point, so $\mathsf N_B$ has all listed properties and any competing map is forced to agree with it fiber by fiber.  Conversely, if $c,c'\in C$ have $B(c)=B(c')$, then boundary extensionality and the fixed-point property give $c=N(c)=N(c')=c'$.  The equivalence with (c) is immediate.
\end{proof}

Let $\to$ be a relation on $Q$, write $\to^*$ for its reflexive transitive closure, and let $\NF(\to)$ be the normal forms.

\begin{definition}
The relation $\to$ is \emph{observation preserving} if $x\to y$ implies $B(x)=B(y)$, and \emph{complete for $C$} if $\NF(\to)=C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Reachable normal forms live in the observable fiber]\label{prop:reachable-fiber}
If $\to$ preserves $B$ and is complete for $C$, then every normal form reachable from $x$ belongs to $C_{B(x)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Completeness puts the reachable normal form in $C$, and observation preservation puts it in the fiber of $B(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-source endpoint identification modulo equivalence]
\label{thm:cross-source-modulo}
Assume that $\to$ preserves $B$ and that $\NF(\to)=C$.  Let $\approx$ be
an equivalence relation on $C$.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for all $c,d\in C$, $B(c)=B(d)$ implies $c\approx d$;
\item whenever $B(x)=B(y)$, $x\to^*n_x$, $y\to^*n_y$, and
      $n_x,n_y\in\NF(\to)$, one has $n_x\approx n_y$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus observation identifiability modulo $\approx$ is exactly agreement modulo
$\approx$ of reachable endpoints from \emph{possibly different} sources with
the same observation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under (i), \cref{prop:reachable-fiber} puts $n_x,n_y$ in $C$ and observation
preservation gives $B(n_x)=B(x)=B(y)=B(n_y)$, hence $n_x\approx n_y$.
Conversely, take $c,d\in C$ with $B(c)=B(d)$.  Since $C=\NF(\to)$ and
$\to^*$ is reflexive, apply (ii) to $c\to^*c$ and $d\to^*d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Silent-quotient criterion]\label{cor:silent-quotient}
In addition to the hypotheses of \cref{thm:cross-source-modulo}, suppose
$c\approx d$ implies $B(c)=B(d)$.  Then $B|_C$ induces a surjection
\[
 \overline B:C/{\approx}\longrightarrow B(C),\qquad [c]\longmapsto B(c),
\]
and $\overline B$ is injective, hence bijective, if and only if either
condition of \cref{thm:cross-source-modulo} holds.  This includes quotients by
silent representation or gauge changes; equality recovers ordinary endpoint
uniqueness.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Silence makes $\overline B$ well defined and it is surjective by its
codomain.  Its injectivity says exactly that $B(c)=B(d)$ implies
$[c]=[d]$, equivalently $c\approx d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three separate hypotheses]
Same-source confluence compares arms of a peak from one state;
\cref{thm:cross-source-modulo} compares endpoints from all sources in one
observation fiber.  Weak normalization is a separate existence hypothesis
ensuring that every source has at least one endpoint.  Strong normalization,
fairness, or another liveness argument is needed to conclude that every
allowed schedule settles.  None of these three hypotheses may be substituted
for either of the others.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Static fibers versus a stochastic scheduler]
The fiber $C_b$ is determined by $(Q,C,B)$ alone.  Statements that a source
reaches $C_b$ with positive probability, hits it almost surely, or selects one
endpoint class require additional data: a transition kernel, a nonempty set of
declared active sources, and an equivalence on first-hit endpoints.  None of
those dynamic conclusions follows from singleton fibers or confluence alone;
conversely, a stochastic reachability statement does not construct a local
repair with prescribed write support.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Correct empty/singleton alternative]\label{cor:empty-singleton}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:reachable-fiber}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if $C_b=\varnothing$, no state of $B^{-1}(b)$ reaches a normal form;
\item if $C_b=\{c_b\}$, every reachable normal form from $B^{-1}(b)$ equals $c_b$;
\item if, in addition, every state of $B^{-1}(b)$ is weakly normalizing, then every such state reduces to $c_b$, and $\to$ is confluent on that fiber.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first two claims follow directly from \cref{prop:reachable-fiber}.  For (iii), normalize both arms of any peak.  Their normal forms are both $c_b$, providing a join.
\end{proof}

The completeness hypothesis $\NF(\to)=C$ is sometimes too strong: a
computation may halt uniquely and then fail a separate semantic audit.  The
next theorem separates those two layers.

\begin{theorem}[Audited terminal alternative]\label{thm:audited-terminal}
Let $T=\NF(\to)$ be the computational terminal set and let $C\subseteq T$
be the semantically consistent terminals.  Assume that $\to$ preserves $B$,
every state is weakly normalizing, and $B|_T$ is injective.  Then every
$x\in Q$ reaches a unique terminal $t_x\in T$, and
\[
 B(t_x)=B(x),
 \qquad
 t_x\in C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{B(x)}\ne\varnothing.
\]
Thus a realizable observation has a unique reachable terminal at its
consistent extension, whereas an unrealizable observation can have a
unique reachable terminal only outside $C$.  The theorem does not assert
strong normalization of every execution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weak normalization supplies a reachable $t_x\in T$, and observation
preservation gives $B(t_x)=B(x)$.  Any other reachable terminal has the same
observation, so injectivity of $B|_T$ makes it equal to $t_x$.  If
$t_x\in C$, it witnesses a nonempty consistent fiber.  Conversely, if
$c\in C_{B(x)}$, then $c\in T$ and $B(c)=B(t_x)$; injectivity on $T$ gives
$c=t_x$, hence $t_x\in C$.
\end{proof}

When $T=C$, \cref{thm:audited-terminal} reduces to the realizable part of
\cref{cor:empty-singleton}.  When $T\supsetneq C$, it prevents an audit
failure from being misreported as nontermination or as a consistent normal
form.

\begin{remark}[Classical scope]
Part (iii) is the invariant-fiber form of the classical implication from weak
normalization and uniqueness of normal forms with respect to reduction to
confluence.  It identifies the exact hypotheses needed for observable
reconstruction.
\end{remark}

\section{Observational stability moduli}\label{sec:stability}

Let $(Q,d_Q)$ be a finite metric space, let $(\cB,d_{\cB})$ be any metric
space, let $\varnothing\ne C\subseteq Q$, and let
$\Phi:Q\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ satisfy
\[
 C=\Phi^{-1}(0).
\]
All maxima below therefore exist.  The same formulas extend to compact spaces
by replacing maxima with suprema and imposing the continuity hypotheses stated
in \cref{prop:compact-extension}; the displayed Lipschitz estimates continue
to assume a finite Lipschitz constant for $B$.

\begin{definition}[Residual error-bound modulus]
Define
\[
 \eta_\Phi(t):=
 \max\{\dist_Q(x,C):\Phi(x)\le t\}.
\]
An \emph{error-bound modulus} is any nondecreasing $\eta$ satisfying
\[
 \dist_Q(x,C)\le\eta(\Phi(x)).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inverse-observation modulus]
Define
\[
 \omega_B(r):=
 \max\{d_Q(c,c'):c,c'\in C,\ d_{\cB}(Bc,Bc')\le r\}.
\]
The system is \emph{stably observable-determined} if $\omega_B(r)\to0$ as $r\downarrow0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimality of the intrinsic moduli]\label{prop:minimal-moduli}
The functions $\eta_\Phi$ and $\omega_B$ are nondecreasing. The following also hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\eta_\Phi(0)=0$;
\item $\eta_\Phi$ is the least nondecreasing error-bound modulus on the attained residual values;
\item $\omega_B(0)=0$ if and only if $B|_C$ is injective;
\item $\omega_B$ is the least nondecreasing modulus satisfying
\[
 d_Q(c,c')\le\omega(d_{\cB}(Bc,Bc'))\qquad(c,c'\in C).
\]
\end{enumerate}
For finite $C$, injectivity of $B|_C$ implies that there is an $r_0>0$ such that
$\omega_B(r)=0$ for $0\le r<r_0$.  When $|C|>1$, one may take $r_0$ to be
the minimum distance between distinct points of $B(C)$; when $|C|=1$, any
positive $r_0$ works.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity follows from inclusion of the defining sets.  Since $C=\Phi^{-1}(0)$, residual zero has distance zero from $C$.  If $\eta$ is any nondecreasing error-bound modulus and $\Phi(x)\le t$, then $\dist(x,C)\le\eta(\Phi(x))\le\eta(t)$; taking the maximum gives $\eta_\Phi(t)\le\eta(t)$.  The proof for $\omega_B$ is identical.  The statement at zero is exactly injectivity, and finiteness gives a positive minimum separation among distinct boundary images.
\end{proof}

Let $L_B$ be any Lipschitz constant for $B$ on $Q$.

\begin{theorem}[Heterogeneous two-output estimate]\label{thm:master-bound}
If $x,y\in Q$ satisfy
\[
 \Phi(x)\le\delta_x,\qquad \Phi(y)\le\delta_y,
 \qquad d_{\cB}(Bx,By)\le\eps,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master-bound}
\begin{split}
 d_Q(x,y)\le{}&\eta_\Phi(\delta_x)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_y)\\
 &+\omega_B\!\left(
 \eps+L_B[\eta_\Phi(\delta_x)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_y)]
 \right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose nearest points $c_x,c_y\in C$.  Then
\[
 d_Q(x,c_x)\le\eta_\Phi(\delta_x),\qquad
 d_Q(y,c_y)\le\eta_\Phi(\delta_y).
\]
Lipschitz continuity and the triangle inequality give
\begin{align*}
 d_{\cB}(Bc_x,Bc_y)
 &\le d_{\cB}(Bc_x,Bx)+d_{\cB}(Bx,By)+d_{\cB}(By,Bc_y)\\
 &\le \eps+L_B[\eta_\Phi(\delta_x)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_y)].
\end{align*}
The definition of $\omega_B$ bounds $d_Q(c_x,c_y)$.  A final triangle inequality yields \eqref{eq:master-bound}.
\end{proof}

The symmetric form used most often is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-bound}
 d_Q(x,y)\le
 2\eta_\Phi(\delta)+
 \omega_B\!\left(\eps+2L_B\eta_\Phi(\delta)\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Rate transfer]\label{cor:rate-transfer}
Suppose, on the residual and observation ranges under consideration,
\[
 \eta_\Phi(t)\le a t^p,
 \qquad
 \omega_B(r)\le b r^q
\]
for constants $a,b\ge0$ and exponents $p,q>0$.  Then
\[
\begin{split}
 d_Q(x,y)\le{}&a(\delta_x^p+\delta_y^p)\\
 &+b\!\left(\eps+L_Ba(\delta_x^p+\delta_y^p)\right)^q
\end{split}
\]
under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:master-bound}.  In particular, linear
error bounds and a Lipschitz inverse give a linear output certificate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the two rate bounds into \eqref{eq:master-bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimality and the exact observational modulus}

\begin{definition}[Settled-output modulus]
For $\delta,r\ge0$, define
\[
 \Theta_{\Phi,B}(\delta,r):=
 \max\left\{
 d_Q(x,y):\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\le\delta,
 \ d_{\cB}(Bx,By)\le r
 \right\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sharpness]\label{thm:sharpness}
The following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item At zero residual,
\[
 \Theta_{\Phi,B}(0,r)=\omega_B(r).
\]
Thus the inverse-observation term in \eqref{eq:symmetric-bound} is exact.
\item No universal estimate of the form
\[
 \Theta_{\Phi,B}(\delta,0)\le a\,\eta_\Phi(\delta)+\omega_B(0)
\]
can hold for all finite metric systems with a constant $a<2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
When $\delta=0$, the admissible points are precisely $C$, so the two defining maxima coincide.

For (ii), fix $s>0$ and let $Q=\{c,x,y\}$ with distances
\[
 d(c,x)=d(c,y)=s,\qquad d(x,y)=2s.
\]
Let $C=\{c\}$, let $B$ be constant, and set $\Phi(c)=0$, $\Phi(x)=\Phi(y)=1$.  Then $\eta_\Phi(1)=s$, $\omega_B(0)=0$, and $\Theta_{\Phi,B}(1,0)=2s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem does not assert that every term in \eqref{eq:master-bound} can be attained simultaneously in every structured class.  It states the correct universal boundary: the observation term is forced on $C$, and the coefficient $2$ on two independent residual errors is globally sharp.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Approximate schedule independence}

A finite execution is a sequence $x=x_0\to x_1\to\cdots\to x_n$.  It is $(\delta,\beta)$-\emph{settled} if its endpoint $z$ satisfies
\[
 \Phi(z)\le\delta,
 \qquad d_{\cB}(Bz,Bx)\le\beta.
\]
No exact observation-preservation assumption is made.

\begin{corollary}[Approximate schedule independence]\label{cor:schedule}
Suppose two executions start at the same state $x$ and end at $z_1,z_2$, where the $i$th execution is $(\delta_i,\beta_i)$-settled.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schedule}
\begin{split}
 d_Q(z_1,z_2)\le{}&\eta_\Phi(\delta_1)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_2)\\
 &+\omega_B\!\left(
 \beta_1+\beta_2+L_B[\eta_\Phi(\delta_1)+\eta_\Phi(\delta_2)]
 \right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The same bound holds for executions starting from two different states with equal initial observations.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint observation discrepancy is at most $\beta_1+\beta_2$.  Apply \cref{thm:master-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[High-probability schedules]\label{cor:probabilistic}
Let $Z_1,Z_2$ be random endpoints of two schedules from a common initial state in finite $Q$.  Suppose schedule $i$ is $(\delta_i,\beta_i)$-settled with probability at least $1-p_i$.  Let $D$ denote the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:schedule}.  Then
\[
 \mathbb P[d_Q(Z_1,Z_2)\le D]\ge1-p_1-p_2
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E[d_Q(Z_1,Z_2)]
 \le D+(p_1+p_2)\diam(Q).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the union bound for the two settling events.  On their intersection apply \cref{cor:schedule}; on its complement use the diameter bound.
\end{proof}

The preceding result consumes a probabilistic settling receipt.  The following
standard drift calculation is one finite mechanism for producing such a
receipt.

\begin{proposition}[Finite Markov drift receipt]\label{prop:markov-receipt}
Let $K$ be a Markov kernel on finite $Q$, write
\[
 (Kf)(x)=\sum_{y\in Q}K(x,y)f(y),
\]
and let $V:Q\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$.  Suppose $0\le\kappa<1$,
$\xi\ge0$, and
\[
 (KV)(x)\le\kappa V(x)+\xi
 \qquad(x\in Q).
\]
For the chain $(X_n)$ started at $x$,
\[
 \mathbb E_x[V(X_n)]
 \le
 \kappa^nV(x)+
 \xi\frac{1-\kappa^n}{1-\kappa}.
\]
Consequently, for every $\delta>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P_x[V(X_n)>\delta]
 \le
 \frac{\kappa^nV(x)+
 \xi(1-\kappa^n)/(1-\kappa)}{\delta}.
\]
If $V=\Phi$ and every transition with positive probability preserves $B$,
then the complementary event supplies a $(\delta,0)$-settling receipt for
\cref{cor:probabilistic}.  When the kernel is meant to sample rewrite
schedules, its support must additionally consist of permitted rewrite steps
or stuttering moves.  This is a one-time endpoint bound; pathwise confinement
over a time interval requires an additional excursion or concentration
hypothesis.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The tower property and the drift inequality give
$a_{n+1}\le\kappa a_n+\xi$ for
$a_n=\mathbb E_x[V(X_n)]$.  Induction solves this scalar recurrence and
gives the displayed geometric sum.  The probability bound is Markov's
inequality.  Positive-probability transitions preserve the initial
observation along every sampled path.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stationary and occupation receipts]
\label{cor:markov-occupation}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{prop:markov-receipt}, every stationary
distribution $\pi$ satisfies, for $\delta>0$,
\[
 \mathbb E_\pi V\le\frac{\xi}{1-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \pi\{V>\delta\}\le\frac{\xi}{(1-\kappa)\delta}.
\]
For a chain started at $x$ and $N\ge1$,
\[
 \mathbb E_x\!\left[\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}
       \mathbf1_{\{V(X_n)>\delta\}}\right]
 \le
 \frac{V(x)}{N\delta(1-\kappa)}
 +\frac{\xi}{\delta(1-\kappa)}.
\]
The same right-hand side bounds the probability of being outside the
$\delta$-tube at an independent uniformly sampled time in
$\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$.  When $\xi>0$, these expectation and occupation bounds do
not imply infinite-horizon pathwise confinement; persistent noise can permit
repeated excursions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Integrate the drift inequality against $\pi$ and use stationarity.  For the
occupation bound, use
$\mathbf1_{\{V>\delta\}}\le V/\delta$, sum the estimate in
\cref{prop:markov-receipt}, and bound
$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\kappa^n\le(1-\kappa)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Compact and uniform families}

\begin{proposition}[Compact-space extension]\label{prop:compact-extension}
Let $Q$ be compact metric, let $C\subseteq Q$ be nonempty and closed, let $\Phi:Q\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ be continuous with $C=\Phi^{-1}(0)$, and let $B:Q\to\cB$ be continuous.  Then $\eta_\Phi(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.  If $B|_C$ is injective, then its inverse on $B(C)$ is uniformly continuous and $\omega_B(r)\to0$ as $r\downarrow0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\eta_\Phi(t)$ did not tend to zero, there would be $t_n\downarrow0$ and $x_n$ satisfying $\Phi(x_n)\le t_n$ and $\dist(x_n,C)\ge a>0$.  A convergent subsequence has limit $x$ with $\Phi(x)=0$, hence $x\in C$, contradicting the distance bound.  The second statement follows because a continuous bijection from compact $C$ to the Hausdorff space $B(C)$ is a homeomorphism, whose inverse is uniformly continuous.
\end{proof}

Let $\{\mathfrak S_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a nonempty family of compact or finite metric systems with a common Lipschitz bound $L$ for the maps $B_i$.  Define
\[
 H(t):=\sup_i\eta_i(t),\qquad
 \Omega(r):=\sup_i\omega_i(r).
\]
We assume these suprema are finite on the ranges under consideration; this
uniform boundedness is not automatic when the diameters of the systems are
unbounded.

A \emph{uniform settled-output modulus} is a function
$\Psi(t,r)$ such that
$\Theta_{\Phi_i,B_i}(t,r)\le\Psi(t,r)$ for every $i\in I$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform observational stability]\label{thm:uniform-family}
If $H(t)\to0$ and $\Omega(r)\to0$ at zero, then every member of the family satisfies the uniform estimate
\[
 d_i(x,y)\le
 2H(\delta)+\Omega(r+2LH(\delta))
\]
whenever $\Phi_i(x),\Phi_i(y)\le\delta$ and
$d_{\cB_i}(B_ix,B_iy)\le r$.  Conversely, any uniform settled-output modulus restricts at zero residual to a modulus dominating $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:master-bound} stagewise and take suprema.  At zero residual, \cref{thm:sharpness}(i) gives the converse statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Stagewise uniqueness without uniform stability]\label{ex:nonuniform}
For $n\ge1$, let $C_n=Q_n=\{0,1\}$ with discrete state distance, and let $B_n(0)=0$, $B_n(1)=1/n$ in $\mathbb R$.  Every $B_n$ is injective, yet for every $r>0$ one has $\sup_n\omega_{B_n}(r)=1$.  Thus exact uniqueness at every finite stage does not supply a stable refinement limit.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Stagewise error bounds without a uniform error bound]\label{ex:nonuniform-residual}
For $n\ge1$, let $Q_n=\{0,1\}$ with the discrete metric, let $C_n=\{0\}$, let $B_n$ be
constant, and put
\[
 \Phi_n(0)=0,\qquad \Phi_n(1)=1/n.
\]
For every fixed $n$, $\eta_n(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$, and the unique
consistent fiber is perfectly conditioned.  Nevertheless, for every
$t>0$ one has $\sup_n\eta_n(t)=1$.  Thus uniform residual control and uniform
inverse-observation control are independent requirements.
\end{example}

\subsection{Parallel composition and observation design}

\begin{theorem}[Exact product calculus]\label{thm:product-calculus}
Let $I$ be nonempty and finite.  For each $i\in I$, let
\[
 \mathfrak S_i=(Q_i,C_i,\cB_i,B_i,\Phi_i)
\]
be a finite metric system with $C_i\ne\varnothing$.  Form the parallel product
\[
 Q=\prod_{i\in I}Q_i,
 \quad C=\prod_{i\in I}C_i,
 \quad B=(B_i)_{i\in I},
 \quad \Phi(x)=\max_i\Phi_i(x_i),
\]
with maximum metrics on $Q$ and $\prod_i\cB_i$.  Then
\[
 \eta_\Phi(t)=\max_i\eta_{\Phi_i}(t),
 \qquad
 \omega_B(r)=\max_i\omega_{B_i}(r).
\]
Consequently, parallel composition preserves a uniform stability certificate without a dimension-dependent loss in the maximum metric.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the maximum product metric,
\[
 \dist_Q(x,C)=\max_i\dist_{Q_i}(x_i,C_i).
\]
The constraint $\Phi(x)\le t$ is equivalent to $\Phi_i(x_i)\le t$ for every $i$.  Taking maxima gives the upper bound for $\eta_\Phi$; equality is attained by choosing a maximizing component and arbitrary consistent points in all other components.  The proof for $\omega_B$ is identical: the product observation discrepancy is at most $r$ exactly when every component discrepancy is at most $r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Adding observations cannot worsen conditioning]\label{cor:sensor-enrichment}
Let $D:C\to\mathcal D$ be an additional observation and equip
$\cB\times\mathcal D$ with the maximum metric.  For the enriched map
$B^+=(B,D)$,
\[
 \omega_{B^+}(r)\le\omega_B(r).
\]
Thus extra quotient-visible sensors can only improve the intrinsic inverse-observation certificate; they may, of course, carry their own implementation error outside $C$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The pairs of consistent states satisfying
$d_{\cB\times\mathcal D}(B^+c,B^+c')\le r$ form a subset of those satisfying
$d_{\cB}(Bc,Bc')\le r$.
\end{proof}

\section{Naturality and refinement towers}\label{sec:refinement}

For an observable system define its reconstructing, realizable domain by
\[
 Q^{\mathrm{adm}}
 :=
 \{q\in Q:\text{there exists a unique }c\in C
              \text{ with }B(c)=B(q)\}.
\]
Every normalizer in this section is understood to act on a chosen metric
subspace of $Q^{\mathrm{adm}}$, with the consistent set restricted to the
corresponding normalizer image; every coarse map is assumed to preserve the
chosen admissible subspaces.  To avoid carrying restriction notation through
every formula, we relabel those subspaces and restricted consistent sets as
$Q_s,Q_r,Q_n$ and $C_s,C_r,C_n$.  Thus $B_n|_{C_n}$ is injective on the
declared domain.  Obstructed and ambiguous fibers are governed by
\cref{sec:exact}; they are not silently assigned a finite normalizer value in
the metric estimates below.

Let
\[
 \mathfrak S_s=(Q_s,C_s,\cB_s,B_s,\Phi_s),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak S_r=(Q_r,C_r,\cB_r,B_r,\Phi_r)
\]
be finite or compact observable systems on the admissible domains declared
above.  Their normalizers $N_s$ and $N_r$ are therefore total on these
domains.  Let $f:Q_s\to Q_r$ be a coarse state map and
$h:\cB_s\to\cB_r$ a coarse observation map.

\begin{definition}[Morphism defects]
The pair $(f,h)$ has observation defect $\eps_B$ and consistency defect $\delta_C$ if
\begin{align}
 d_{\cB_r}(B_r(fq),h(B_s q))&\le\eps_B &&(q\in Q_s),\label{eq:obs-defect}\\
 \Phi_r(fc)&\le\delta_C &&(c\in C_s).\label{eq:cons-defect}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[One-step approximate naturality]\label{thm:one-step-naturality}
Assume $B_r$ is $L_r$-Lipschitz.  Under \eqref{eq:obs-defect}--\eqref{eq:cons-defect},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-step-naturality}
 d_{Q_r}(fN_s q,N_rfq)
 \le
 \eta_r(\delta_C)+
 \omega_r\!\left(2\eps_B+L_r\eta_r(\delta_C)\right)
\end{equation}
for every relevant $q\in Q_s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $y=fN_s q$ and $z=N_rfq$.  By \eqref{eq:cons-defect}, $\Phi_r(y)\le\delta_C$.  Choose $c\in C_r$ with $d_r(y,c)\le\eta_r(\delta_C)$.  Since both normalizers preserve observations,
\begin{align*}
 d_{\cB_r}(B_rc,B_rz)
 &\le d_{\cB_r}(B_rc,B_ry)
      +d_{\cB_r}(B_rfN_sq,hB_sN_sq)\\
 &\qquad+d_{\cB_r}(hB_sq,B_rfq)\\
 &\le L_r\eta_r(\delta_C)+2\eps_B.
\end{align*}
Both $c$ and $z$ lie in $C_r$, so the inverse-observation modulus bounds $d_r(c,z)$.  Add $d_r(y,c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact naturality from uniqueness]\label{cor:exact-naturality}
If $f(C_s)\subseteq C_r$ and $B_r\circ f=h\circ B_s$, then
\[
 f\circ N_s=N_r\circ f.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both sides are consistent coarse states with the same coarse observation.  Reconstructing fibers make them equal.  Equivalently, set both defects to zero in \cref{thm:one-step-naturality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stability under perturbation of the consistency model]\label{cor:model-perturbation}
Let $(Q,d_Q)$ carry two nonempty compact consistent sets $C$ and $\widetilde C$, two observation maps $B,\widetilde B:Q\to\cB$, and total maps $N,\widetilde N:Q\to Q$ satisfying
\[
 N(Q)\subseteq C,
 \qquad
 \widetilde N(Q)\subseteq\widetilde C,
 \qquad
 B\circ N=B,
 \qquad
 \widetilde B\circ\widetilde N=\widetilde B.
\]
Suppose
\[
 d_H(C,\widetilde C)\le\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sup_{q\in Q}d_{\cB}(Bq,\widetilde Bq)\le\beta,
\]
where $d_H$ is Hausdorff distance.  If $B$ is $L$-Lipschitz and $B|_C$ has inverse modulus $\omega_B$, then
\[
 d_Q(Nx,\widetilde Nx)
 \le
 \alpha+\omega_B(2\beta+L\alpha)
 \qquad(x\in Q).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose $c\in C$ with $d_Q(c,\widetilde Nx)\le\alpha$.  Since $N$ and $\widetilde N$ preserve their respective observations,
\begin{align*}
 d_{\cB}(Bc,BNx)
 &\le d_{\cB}(Bc,B\widetilde Nx)
      +d_{\cB}(B\widetilde Nx,\widetilde B\widetilde Nx)\\
 &\qquad+d_{\cB}(\widetilde Bx,Bx)\\
 &\le L\alpha+2\beta.
\end{align*}
Both $c$ and $Nx$ lie in $C$, so $d_Q(c,Nx)\le\omega_B(L\alpha+2\beta)$.  Add the Hausdorff error.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Categorical functoriality of rewrite steps is an established subject \citep{DuvalEchahedProst2011}.  \Cref{cor:exact-naturality} is a different and simpler statement: it concerns the terminal normalizer, and uniqueness allows it to be proved without simulating each fine rewrite.  The nontrivial content for applications is the defect estimate, its accumulation across a tower, and the perturbation certificate of \cref{cor:model-perturbation}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Accumulation through arbitrary depth}

Let
\[
 Q_0\xleftarrow{\rho_{1,0}}Q_1
 \xleftarrow{\rho_{2,1}}Q_2\xleftarrow{}\cdots
\]
be an inverse sequence of nonempty metric spaces with Lipschitz restriction
maps, with
$\rho_{m,n}=\rho_{n+1,n}\circ\cdots\circ\rho_{m,m-1}$.  Let
$N_n:Q_n\to Q_n$ be normalizers.  This subsection deliberately restarts with general metric spaces; the
finite-or-compact ambient assumption used for the preceding uniform-family
examples is not retained.  Completeness is imposed only where a Cauchy
estimate is converted into a coordinate limit.
Assume the following pathwise and uniform one-step normalization defects are
finite:
\[
 \alpha_n(q)
 :=
 d_n(\rho_{n+1,n}N_{n+1}q,N_n\rho_{n+1,n}q),
 \qquad
 a_n:=\sup_{q\in Q_{n+1}}\alpha_n(q).
\]
For $j\ge n$, write
\[
 K_{j,n}:=\Lip(\rho_{j,n}),\qquad K_{n,n}=1.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Telescoping refinement bound]\label{thm:tower}
For every $m>n$ and every $q\in Q_m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tower}
 d_n(\rho_{m,n}N_mq,N_n\rho_{m,n}q)
 \le
 \sum_{j=n}^{m-1}K_{j,n}a_j.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $j=n,\dots,m$, define
\[
 p_j:=\rho_{j,n}N_j\rho_{m,j}q.
\]
Then $p_m=\rho_{m,n}N_mq$ and $p_n=N_n\rho_{m,n}q$.  For $j<m$, put $r=\rho_{m,j+1}q$.  The difference between consecutive terms is
\begin{align*}
 d_n(p_{j+1},p_j)
 &=d_n\bigl(
 \rho_{j,n}\rho_{j+1,j}N_{j+1}r,
 \rho_{j,n}N_j\rho_{j+1,j}r
 \bigr)\\
 &\le K_{j,n}a_j.
\end{align*}
Summing the telescoping chain proves \eqref{eq:tower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Summable-defect asymptotic naturality]\label{cor:summable}
If all restriction maps are nonexpansive, then
\[
 d_n(\rho_{m,n}N_mq,N_n\rho_{m,n}q)
 \le\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}a_j.
\]
If $\sum_ja_j<\infty$, the defect is uniformly small when the comparison
level tends outward along the refinement index.  More generally, the
sufficient condition is
\[
 \sup_{m>n}\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}K_{j,n}a_j\longrightarrow0
 \quad(n\to\infty).
\]
\end{corollary}

Combining \cref{thm:one-step-naturality} with \cref{thm:tower} gives an explicit tower certificate in terms of residual, observation, and Lipschitz data at each stage.

\begin{corollary}[Modulus-generated tower certificate]\label{cor:modulus-tower}
Suppose the $n$th restriction map has consistency defect $\delta_n$ and observation defect $\eps_n$, and let
\[
 a_n^*:=\eta_n(\delta_n)+
 \omega_n\!\left(2\eps_n+L_n\eta_n(\delta_n)\right).
\]
Then \eqref{eq:tower} holds with $a_j$ replaced by $a_j^*$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Run-specific approximate solver receipt]
At level $m$, an output $\widehat x_m\in Q_m$ produced from
$q_m\in Q_m$ has receipt $(\delta_m,\beta_m)$ when
\[
 \Phi_m(\widehat x_m)\le\delta_m,
 \qquad
 d_{\cB_m}(B_m\widehat x_m,B_mq_m)\le\beta_m.
\]
Set
\[
 e_m(\delta,\beta)
 :=
 \eta_m(\delta)+
 \omega_m\!\left(\beta+L_m\eta_m(\delta)\right).
\]
Uniform receipts for a solver map mean that the two inequalities hold for
every admissible input, but the comparison results below require only the
displayed run-specific evidence.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Receipt-to-exact comparison]\label{lem:receipt-to-exact}
If $\widehat x_m$ has receipt $(\delta_m,\beta_m)$ relative to $q_m$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:receipt-to-exact}
 d_m(\widehat x_m,N_mq_m)
 \le e_m(\delta_m,\beta_m).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact normal form has residual zero and preserves the observation of
$q_m$.  Apply the heterogeneous two-output estimate to $\widehat x_m$ and
$N_mq_m$, using $\eta_m(0)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Same-level implementation agreement]
\label{prop:same-level-implementations}
Let $n\le m$.  If $\widehat x_m$ and $\widehat y_m$ are outputs on the same
input $q_m$ with receipts $(\delta_m,\beta_m)$ and
$(\delta'_m,\beta'_m)$, then
\[
 d_n(\rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m,\rho_{m,n}\widehat y_m)
 \le
 K_{m,n}\bigl(
 e_m(\delta_m,\beta_m)+e_m(\delta'_m,\beta'_m)
 \bigr).
\]
No refinement-defect term occurs in this same-level comparison.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compare both outputs with the same exact state $N_mq_m$ using
\cref{lem:receipt-to-exact}, then apply the $K_{m,n}$-Lipschitz restriction
map.
\end{proof}

For $n\le h\le r$ and $q_r\in Q_r$, define the amplified pathwise defect
segment
\[
 \mathsf A_{r,h}^{(n)}(q_r)
 :=
 \sum_{j=h}^{r-1}
 K_{j,n}\,
 \alpha_j\!\left(\rho_{r,j+1}q_r\right),
\]
with an empty sum equal to zero.  By definition of $a_j$,
\[
 \mathsf A_{r,h}^{(n)}(q_r)
 \le
 \sum_{j=h}^{r-1}K_{j,n}a_j.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Pathwise telescoping to an anchor]\label{lem:pathwise-anchor}
For $n\le h\le r$ and $q_r\in Q_r$,
\[
 d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{r,n}N_rq_r,
 \rho_{h,n}N_h\rho_{r,h}q_r
 \right)
 \le
 \mathsf A_{r,h}^{(n)}(q_r).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $j=h,\ldots,r$, put
$p_j=\rho_{j,n}N_j\rho_{r,j}q_r$.  Consecutive terms satisfy
\[
 d_n(p_{j+1},p_j)
 \le
 K_{j,n}\alpha_j(\rho_{r,j+1}q_r).
\]
Summing these inequalities proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fine-to-coarse solver certificate]
\label{cor:fine-to-coarse-solver}
For $m\ge n$ and an output $\widehat x_m$ with receipt
$(\delta_m,\beta_m)$ relative to $q_m$,
\[
\begin{split}
 d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m,
 N_n\rho_{m,n}q_m
 \right)
 \le{}&
 K_{m,n}e_m(\delta_m,\beta_m)
 +\mathsf A_{m,n}^{(n)}(q_m).
\end{split}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use \cref{lem:receipt-to-exact}, apply $\rho_{m,n}$, and then use
\cref{lem:pathwise-anchor} with $h=n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anchored cross-level solver comparison]
\label{thm:anchored-cross-level}
Let $n\le h\le\min\{m,\ell\}$.  Let $q_m\in Q_m$ and
$q_\ell\in Q_\ell$, and suppose $\widehat x_m$ and $\widehat y_\ell$
have receipts $(\delta_m,\beta_m)$ and
$(\delta'_\ell,\beta'_\ell)$, respectively.  Define the protected
observation mismatch at the anchor by
\[
 \gamma_h
 :=
 d_{\cB_h}\!\left(
 B_h\rho_{m,h}q_m,
 B_h\rho_{\ell,h}q_\ell
 \right).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchored-cross-level}
\begin{split}
&d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m,
 \rho_{\ell,n}\widehat y_\ell
 \right)
\\
&\quad\le
 K_{m,n}e_m(\delta_m,\beta_m)
 +\mathsf A_{m,h}^{(n)}(q_m)
 +K_{h,n}\omega_h(\gamma_h)
 +\mathsf A_{\ell,h}^{(n)}(q_\ell)
 +K_{\ell,n}e_\ell(\delta'_\ell,\beta'_\ell).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The two pathwise segments may be replaced by their uniform $a_j$ bounds.
Because the estimate holds for every admissible $h$, the right-hand side may
be minimized over the possible anchors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compare each approximate output with its exact normal form using
\cref{lem:receipt-to-exact}, and apply the corresponding restriction to
level $n$.  Use \cref{lem:pathwise-anchor} to move both exact branches to
level $h$.  The two anchor normal forms are consistent, preserve the
observations of their anchor inputs, and hence differ in $Q_h$ by at most
$\omega_h(\gamma_h)$.  Applying $\rho_{h,n}$ contributes $K_{h,n}$.
The triangle inequality gives \eqref{eq:anchored-cross-level}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nested compatible solver levels]
\label{cor:nested-cross-level}
Under the output and receipt hypotheses of
\cref{thm:anchored-cross-level}, let $n\le m\le\ell$ and suppose
\[
 B_mq_m=B_m\rho_{\ell,m}q_\ell.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{split}
 d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m,
 \rho_{\ell,n}\widehat y_\ell
 \right)
 \le{}&
 K_{m,n}e_m(\delta_m,\beta_m)
 +\mathsf A_{\ell,m}^{(n)}(q_\ell)\\
 &+K_{\ell,n}e_\ell(\delta'_\ell,\beta'_\ell).
\end{split}
\]
In particular, the middle term is at most
$\sum_{j=m}^{\ell-1}K_{j,n}a_j$.  The compatibility hypothesis holds when
$q_m=\rho_{\ell,m}q_\ell$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $h=m$ in \cref{thm:anchored-cross-level}.  The first pathwise segment
is empty and $\omega_m(0)=0$ on the observation-determined consistent
domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cofinal projective implementation independence]
\label{cor:cofinal-projective}
Assume every $Q_n$ is complete and every restriction map is nonexpansive.
Let $q=(q_n)\in\varprojlim Q_n$ be a compatible admissible input and suppose
its pathwise exact-normalizer defects are summable:
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\alpha_j(q_{j+1})<\infty.
\]
For $i\in\{1,2\}$, let $\widehat x_m^{(i)}$ be outputs at $q_m$ whose
receipt errors
\[
 e_m^{(i)}
 :=
 e_m(\delta_m^{(i)},\beta_m^{(i)})
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Then, for every fixed $n$, the limits
\[
 x_n
 =
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m^{(i)}
\]
exist, are independent of $i$, and are unchanged by passage to a cofinal
subsequence.  The family $x=(x_n)$ belongs to $\varprojlim Q_n$, and
\[
 d_n(x_n,N_nq_n)
 \le
 \sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\alpha_j(q_{j+1}).
\]
If all pathwise normalizer defects vanish, then $x_n=N_nq_n$ for every $n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $s\ge r\ge n$, compatibility gives $q_r=\rho_{s,r}q_s$.
\Cref{cor:nested-cross-level} and nonexpansiveness give, for either pair of
implementations,
\[
 d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{r,n}\widehat x_r^{(i)},
 \rho_{s,n}\widehat x_s^{(i')}
 \right)
 \le
 e_r^{(i)}+e_s^{(i')}
 +\sum_{j=r}^{s-1}\alpha_j(q_{j+1}).
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero as $r,s\to\infty$.  Completeness gives
the coordinate limits, and the same estimate makes them independent of the
implementation and of a cofinal subsequence.  Continuity of the restriction
maps passes compatibility to the limits.  Finally,
\cref{cor:fine-to-coarse-solver} gives
\[
 d_n\!\left(
 \rho_{m,n}\widehat x_m^{(i)},N_nq_n
 \right)
 \le
 e_m^{(i)}
 +\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\alpha_j(q_{j+1});
\]
let $m\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

The uniform hypotheses used earlier imply $e_m^{(i)}\to0$ whenever
$\delta_m^{(i)},\beta_m^{(i)}\to0$, the observation maps have a common
Lipschitz bound, and $H(t),\Omega(t)\to0$ at zero.  Uniform summability of
$\sum_j a_j$ implies the pathwise summability condition.  Thus raw receipt
parameters alone are not enough: uniform conditioning and the amplified
restriction factors are load-bearing.

\subsection{Exact inverse limits}

Assume that all one-step defects vanish and that
$\rho_{n+1,n}(C_{n+1})\subseteq C_n$.  Let
\[
 Q_\infty:=\varprojlim Q_n
 =\{(q_n):\rho_{n+1,n}q_{n+1}=q_n\},
\]
and similarly let $C_\infty=\varprojlim C_n$.
Here $Q_\infty$ is the inverse limit of the admissible domains fixed at the
start of the section.  It is the full ambient inverse limit only when every
observation value is reconstructing and realizable.

\begin{theorem}[Inverse-limit normalizer]\label{thm:inverse-limit}
For each $n$, let $N_n:Q_n\to C_n\subseteq Q_n$ be an idempotent
observation-preserving normalizer that fixes every point of $C_n$.  Assume the
restriction maps form an inverse system, preserve the consistent subsets, and
satisfy
\[
 \rho_{n+1,n}N_{n+1}=N_n\rho_{n+1,n}
 \qquad(n\ge0).
\]
Assume also that the observation spaces carry compatible restriction maps
$\beta_{n+1,n}:\cB_{n+1}\to\cB_n$ with
\[
 B_n\rho_{n+1,n}=\beta_{n+1,n}B_{n+1}.
\]
Then
\[
 N_\infty((q_n))=(N_nq_n)
\]
defines an idempotent map $Q_\infty\to C_\infty$ preserving the induced
inverse-limit observation.  If, for every $n$ and every
$b_n\in B_n(C_n)$, the fiber $C_n\cap B_n^{-1}(b_n)$ is a singleton, then
every realizable inverse-limit consistent observation fiber is a singleton.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Naturality makes $(N_nq_n)$ a compatible family.  Because each coordinate lies in $C_n$ and each $N_n$ is idempotent, the resulting map lands in $C_\infty$ and is idempotent.  Compatibility of the $B_n$ makes observation preservation coordinatewise.  If two inverse-limit consistent families have the same inverse-limit observation, their $n$th coordinates lie in the same finite consistent observation fiber for every $n$, hence agree at every coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not prove]
The theorem supplies a normalizer on a declared inverse system.  It
does not prove compactness, nonemptiness, a continuum dynamics, or adequacy of
the chosen refinement maps.  Those are separate mathematical or
model-specific premises.
\end{remark}

\section{Repairability and selection obstructions}\label{sec:repair}

The existence of a unique consistent state with a given observation does not imply that a prescribed local write support can construct it.  This section separates extension, uniqueness, and repair support.

Let $X_W$ be an interior write space, let $X_D$ be a protected collar space, and let
\[
 R\subseteq X_W\times X_D
\]
be the locally consistent relation.

\begin{definition}[Strong local repair]
A \emph{strong local repair} is a map
\[
 \mathsf R:X_W\times X_D\longrightarrow R
\]
such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\pi_D\mathsf R(x,d)=d$ for every $(x,d)$;
\item $\mathsf R(r)=r$ for every $r\in R$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus every local state is repaired, the collar is preserved, and consistent states are fixed.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Collar-section criterion]\label{thm:collar-section}
For nonempty finite $X_W$ and finite $X_D$, a strong local repair exists if and only if the collar projection
\[
 \pi_D:R\longrightarrow X_D
\]
is surjective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A repair sends every $(x,d)$ to a point of $R$ with collar $d$, so surjectivity is necessary.  Conversely, choose a section $s:X_D\to R$ of $\pi_D$ and define
\[
 \mathsf R(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 z,&z\in R,\\
 s(\pi_Dz),&z\notin R.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is a strong repair.
\end{proof}

If $R=\varnothing$ while $X_W\times X_D\ne\varnothing$, nonexistence of a
repair is immediate; when the domain is empty, the unique empty map is a
vacuous repair.  The next proposition assumes $R\ne\varnothing$ because
ordinary real-valued distance to the empty set is convention-dependent; an
extended-distance formulation may instead assign that case margin $+\infty$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite no-repair certificate]
For nonempty $X_W$, a single collar value
$d\notin\pi_D(R)$ certifies that no total exact repair preserving that collar
exists.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Stable no-repair margin]\label{prop:stable-no-repair}
Suppose $X_W$ and $X_D$ are metric spaces, $\varnothing\ne R\subseteq X_W\times X_D$, and the product metric dominates collar distance:
\[
 d_{X_W\times X_D}((w,d),(w',d'))\ge d_{X_D}(d,d').
\]
For $d\in X_D$, set
\[
 \mu_R(d):=\dist_{X_D}(d,\pi_D(R)).
\]
Then for every $w\in X_W$,
\[
 \dist_{X_W\times X_D}((w,d),R)\ge\mu_R(d).
\]
If $R$ is compact and $d\notin\pi_D(R)$, then $\mu_R(d)>0$.  Hence no procedure whose output preserves the collar value $d$ exactly can approximate consistency to error below $\mu_R(d)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every $(w',d')\in R$, the product-metric assumption gives
\[
 d((w,d),(w',d'))
 \ge d_{X_D}(d,d')
 \ge \mu_R(d).
\]
Take the infimum.  Compactness makes $\pi_D(R)$ compact and therefore
closed, so a point outside it has positive distance.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Repair width]
Let $\mathscr W$ be a finite family of admissible write supports with a cost function $w:\mathscr W\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$.  For each $W\in\mathscr W$, let $R_W\subseteq X_W\times X_{D(W)}$ be the induced local consistency relation and let $D_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\subseteq X_{D(W)}$ be the declared admissible collar domain.  The \emph{repair width} is
\[
 \min\{w(W):D_{\mathrm{adm}}(W)\subseteq\pi_{D(W)}(R_W)\},
\]
with value $+\infty$ when no admissible support has the required projection property.
\end{definition}

The definition prevents a one-site no-go from being misreported as a global inconsistency: increasing the transaction support may restore surjectivity.

\subsection{A protected-behavior first-hit profile}
\label{sec:protected-first-hit}

This section adds stochastic data without identifying it with the static
repair question.  Let $Q$ be finite and let $K:Q\times Q\to[0,1]$ be a row-stochastic
kernel.  Fix a protected carrier $P\subseteq\cB$.  For every $b\in P$, let
$I_b\subseteq Q$ be a nonempty set of declared active sources and put
$T_b=C_b$.  Let $\approx_b$ be an equivalence relation on $T_b$.  The first
entrance time
\[
 \tau_b:=\inf\{n\geq0:X_n\in T_b\}
\]
includes time zero.  For $q\in I_b$ and $c\in T_b$, define the endpoint mass
\[
 h_b(q,c):=\Pr_q[X_{\tau_b}=c,\ \tau_b<\infty]
\]
and its total $h_b(q)=\sum_{c\in T_b}h_b(q,c)$.  These are determined by the
increasing finite-horizon sums of first-hit path weights; no arbitrary initial
probability law is part of the qualitative definition.

\begin{definition}[Protected-behavior layers and cuts]
Define nested subsets of $P$ by
\begin{align*}
 L_0&=\{b\in P:T_b\ne\varnothing\},\\
 L_1&=\{b\in L_0:\exists q\in I_b,\ h_b(q)>0\},\\
 L_2&=\{b\in L_1:\forall q\in I_b,\ h_b(q)=1\},\\
 L_3&=\left\{b\in L_2:
   \begin{array}{l}
   h_b(q,c)>0,\ h_b(q',c')>0\\[-2pt]
   \Longrightarrow c\approx_b c'
   \quad(q,q'\in I_b)
   \end{array}\right\}.
\end{align*}
The cumulative cuts are $K_j=P\setminus L_j$.  The disjoint obstruction
strata are
\[
 \Delta_0=K_0,\qquad
 \Delta_j=K_j\setminus K_{j-1}\quad(1\leq j\leq3).
\]
Thus $\Delta_0$ is an empty-fiber obstruction, $\Delta_1$ a failure of any
declared source to supply positive reach, $\Delta_2$ a failure of all-source
almost-sure reach, and $\Delta_3$ a failure of endpoint-class selection.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Profile partition and structural characterizations]
\label{thm:protected-profile}
The layers satisfy
\[
 L_3\subseteq L_2\subseteq L_1\subseteq L_0\subseteq P,
\]
and
\[
 P=\Delta_0\sqcup\Delta_1\sqcup\Delta_2\sqcup\Delta_3\sqcup L_3.
\]
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $b\in L_1$ exactly when a positive-support finite path from some
      $q\in I_b$ first enters $T_b$ at its endpoint;
\item $b\in L_2$ exactly when $b\in L_1$ and no active source can reach,
      before hitting $T_b$, a nonempty closed positive-support set disjoint
      from $T_b$;
\item $b\in L_3$ exactly when $b\in L_2$ and the union of positive first-hit
      endpoint supports over $I_b$ is one $\approx_b$-class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Nesting is immediate from the definitions, after which successive set
differences give the displayed disjoint partition.  For (i), expand the
finite-horizon first-hit mass as the sum of products of transition weights;
nonnegativity makes the sum positive exactly when one summand is positive.
For (ii), the states reachable before the first hit form a finite directed
support graph.  A positive probability of avoiding $T_b$ forever reaches a
bottom strongly connected component disjoint from $T_b$, while any reachable
closed such component retains positive mass away from the target.  This is the
standard finite-chain closed-class argument.  Part (iii) simply rewrites the
pairwise definition in terms of the union of positive endpoint supports.
\end{proof}

The all-source quantifier here means all members of the declared $I_b$, not
all states of $Q$.  Positive reach, almost-sure hit, and endpoint selection are
different predicates.  In particular, $h_b(q)=1$ supplies neither a rate nor
an expected hitting-time bound, and singleton support modulo $\approx_b$ need
not mean literal endpoint equality.

\begin{proposition}[Exact behavior-cut recovery]
\label{prop:behavior-cut-recovery}
Construct traces from positive-support paths, observe each trace by its
protected behavior, and define four admissibility levels respectively by
target existence, positive supported reach, absence of a reachable closed
trap for every active source, and one endpoint class over all active sources.
If support-to-rewrite soundness, rewrite-to-support completeness, and target
normality hold, then the four realized-behavior sets are exactly $L_0,L_1,L_2,
L_3$ and the corresponding behavior cuts are exactly $K_0,K_1,K_2,K_3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At level zero the trace witness reduces to nonempty target fiber.  Level one
uses part (i) of \cref{thm:protected-profile}; level two uses part (ii); and
level three uses part (iii).  The two scheduler laws identify supported paths
with observation-preserving rewrite paths in both directions.  Taking
complements inside $P$ gives the cut equalities.  The structural certificates
are explicit parts of this adapter; the proposition does not claim that an
arbitrary admissibility profile has these four meanings.
\end{proof}

Consequently, variant comparison is coordinatewise:
$(K_0,K_1,K_2,K_3)\leq(K'_0,K'_1,K'_2,K'_3)$ when every coordinate is
included.  This is a product preorder, not a scalar score or total order.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-state profile transport]
\label{thm:protected-transport}
Consider two finite protected profiles and maps $f:Q\to Q'$ and
$g:P\to P'$.  Suppose protected membership is reflected and covered; active
sources are reflected and covered along $f$; target membership is reflected
and every target endpoint is covered; observations commute with $f$ and $g$;
the target equivalences satisfy
\[
 c\approx_b d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 f(c)\approx'_{g(b)}f(d);
\]
and the kernels obey exact lumping
\[
 K'(f(q),y)=\sum_{x:f(x)=y}K(q,x).
\]
Then first-hit endpoint mass pushes forward along $f$, total hit probability
is preserved, and for every $b\in P$ and $0\leq j\leq3$,
\[
 b\in L_j\Longleftrightarrow g(b)\in L'_j,
 \qquad
 b\in K_j\Longleftrightarrow g(b)\in K'_j.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on the first-hit time gives the exact endpoint-mass pushforward:
the base case uses target reflection and coverage, and the step uses kernel
lumping.  Monotone limits preserve the equality for total and endpoint hit
probabilities.  Active-source coverage and target coverage reflect positive
mass and almost-sure mass in both directions, while quotient exactness
reflects the singleton endpoint-class condition.  The four layer equivalences
and then their complementary cut equivalences follow.  Observation
commutation records that the transported target is the same protected
behavior; it is not a model-to-code refinement claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite observation-conditioned resampling}

There is nevertheless a canonical \emph{averaging} operation once a reference
measure is supplied; unlike a section, it does not select one state from an
ambiguous fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Fiber averaging is conditional expectation]
\label{thm:fiber-conditional-expectation}
Let $Q$ be finite, let $\mu(x)>0$ with $\sum_x\mu(x)=1$, and let
$B:Q\to\cB$.  For $b\in B(Q)$ put
$F_b=B^{-1}(b)$ and $\mu(F_b)=\sum_{y\in F_b}\mu(y)$.  Define
\[
 (P_Bf)(x)
 =\frac{1}{\mu(F_{B(x)})}
   \sum_{y\in F_{B(x)}}\mu(y)f(y).
 \]
Then $P_B=\mathbb E_\mu[\,\cdot\mid\sigma(B)]$.  In the weighted inner
product $\langle f,g\rangle_\mu=\sum_x\mu(x)f(x)g(x)$ it satisfies
\[
 P_B^2=P_B,\qquad P_B^*=P_B,\qquad
 \|P_Bf\|_{2,\mu}\le\|f\|_{2,\mu},
\]
and its fixed space is exactly
\[
 \mathcal M_B=\{f\in\mathbb R^Q:B(x)=B(y)\Rightarrow f(x)=f(y)\},
\]
the observation-measurable functions.

A Markov matrix $K$ on $Q$ equals the matrix of $P_B$ if and only
if the following finite receipt holds:
\begin{align*}
 K(x,y)&=0 &&\text{when }B(x)\ne B(y),\tag{R1}\\
 K(x,\cdot)&=K(x',\cdot)&&\text{when }B(x)=B(x'),\tag{R2}\\
 \mu(x)K(x,y)&=\mu(y)K(y,x)&&\text{for all }x,y.\tag{R3}
\end{align*}
Equivalently, its entries are
\[
 K(x,y)=\mathbf1_{\{B(x)=B(y)\}}
 \frac{\mu(y)}{\mu(F_{B(x)})}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed average is constant on each fiber and fixes every
fiber-constant function, which proves the conditional-expectation and fixed
space statements.  Grouping both variables by fibers gives
\[
 \langle P_Bf,g\rangle_\mu
 =\sum_{b\in B(Q)}
 \frac{(\sum_{x\in F_b}\mu(x)f(x))
       (\sum_{y\in F_b}\mu(y)g(y))}{\mu(F_b)},
\]
which is symmetric in $f,g$.  Hence $P_B$ is an orthogonal projection and is
$L^2$-contractive.  Its matrix plainly satisfies (R1)--(R3).  Conversely,
(R1)--(R2) give one probability row $k_b$ on each fiber.  By (R3),
$\mu(x)k_b(y)=\mu(y)k_b(x)$ there, so normalization forces
$k_b(y)=\mu(y)/\mu(F_b)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{thm:fiber-conditional-expectation} recognizes the exact finite
resampling projector from checkable matrix identities.  It neither chooses a
representative of a fiber nor supplies a spectral gap or a convergence rate
for a different repair dynamics; those require additional structure.  A
noncircular receipt enumerates the states and fibers, derives $K$ independently
from the declared transition table or transition counts, and then verifies
(R1)--(R3); constructing $K$ from the target entry formula is not a
verification.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Symmetry and hidden tie-breaking}

Let a group $G$ act on $C$ and $\cB$, and assume $B|_C$ is equivariant.

\begin{theorem}[Equivariant-section criterion]\label{thm:equivariant-section}
Let a finite group $G$ act on $C$ and $B(C)$, and let $B|_C:C\to B(C)$ be an equivariant surjection.  A $G$-equivariant section $s:B(C)\to C$ exists if and only if, for one (equivalently every) representative $b$ of each $G$-orbit in $B(C)$, the fiber $C_b$ contains a point fixed by the stabilizer
\[
 G_b=\{g\in G:gb=b\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Necessity follows from $gs(b)=s(gb)=s(b)$ for $g\in G_b$.  Conversely, choose an orbit representative $b$ and a $G_b$-fixed point $c_b\in C_b$.  Define $s(gb)=gc_b$.  If $gb=g'b$, then $g^{-1}g'\in G_b$, so $gc_b=g'c_b$; hence the definition is well posed.  It is visibly a section and equivariant.  Repeat independently on each orbit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stabilizer obstruction]\label{thm:stabilizer}
If some fiber $C_b$ has no $G_b$-fixed point, there is no equivariant boundary-only selector on that orbit.  In particular, a transitive ambiguous fiber with no fixed point cannot be canonically collapsed without adding symmetry-breaking data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[No selection by normalization]
Lexicographic order, an energy, a prior, maximum entropy, or an external sector label can select an ambiguous fiber.  None is entailed by consistency and the observation alone.  The theorem is elementary, but it is a useful firewall: a normal-form map cannot silently become a physical or probabilistic selector without additional structure.
\end{remark}

\section{Ranked functional-audit systems}\label{sec:functional}

We give a broad constructive class in which the partial normalizer, asynchronous dynamics, and obstruction output are all explicit.

\begin{definition}[Ranked functional-audit system]
Let
\[
 V=V_0\sqcup V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_m
\]
be a finite ranked site set with $V_0\ne\varnothing$.  Every site $v\in V$
has a nonempty finite state set $X_v$, and sites in $V_0$ are boundary sites.
Every generated site $v\in V_k$, $k>0$, has a parent set
\[
 P(v)\subseteq V_0\cup\cdots\cup V_{k-1},
\]
and a function
\[
 F_v:\prod_{u\in P(v)}X_u\longrightarrow X_v.
\]
A state satisfies the generating constraints when
\[
 x_v=F_v(x|_{P(v)})\qquad(v\notin V_0).
\]
A finite family of audits $A_j(x)\in\{0,1\}$ imposes residual global constraints.  A state is globally consistent when it satisfies all generating constraints and every audit returns $1$.
\end{definition}

Write
\[
 Q=\prod_{v\in V}X_v,
 \qquad
 B:Q\longrightarrow\prod_{v\in V_0}X_v
\]
for the full state space and the boundary-coordinate projection.  For a
boundary value $b\in\prod_{v\in V_0}X_v$, define $E(b)$ recursively by
rank.

\begin{theorem}[Singleton-or-empty fiber]\label{thm:functional-fiber}
For every boundary $b$, exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item every audit passes on $E(b)$ and the consistent fiber is $\{E(b)\}$;
\item some audit fails on $E(b)$ and the consistent fiber is empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rank recursion constructs one state satisfying all generating constraints.  Any other such state with boundary $b$ agrees at rank $0$ and therefore agrees inductively at every rank.  The audits either accept this sole candidate or eliminate it.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Asynchronous functional update]
For $v\notin V_0$, let $T_v$ replace $x_v$ by $F_v(x|_{P(v)})$ and leave every other coordinate unchanged.  An update is \emph{effective} when it changes $x_v$.
\end{definition}

Define recursively
\[
 M(v)=0\quad(v\in V_0),
 \qquad
 M(v)=1+\sum_{u\in P(v)}M(u)\quad(v\notin V_0).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite asynchronous normalization with an update bound]\label{thm:functional-termination}
In every sequence of effective asynchronous updates, site $v$ is updated at most $M(v)$ times.  Consequently every such sequence has length at most
\[
 \sum_{v\in V}M(v),
\]
and every maximal sequence terminates at $E(Bx)$ independently of update order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proceed by induction on rank.  Boundary sites never change.  Assume the bound
holds below rank $k$.  The parent tuple of a rank-$k$ site can change at most
$\sum_{u\in P(v)}M(u)$ times.  Before the first parent change, between
consecutive parent changes, and after the last parent change, at most one
effective update at $v$ is possible on each fixed-parent interval: after that
update, $v$ equals the value prescribed by the fixed parent tuple.  Hence $v$
changes at most $M(v)$ times.  Summing over sites proves termination.

At a maximal endpoint no functional update is enabled, so all generating equations hold.  The endpoint has the original boundary and therefore equals the unique generated extension $E(Bx)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Synchronous settling by dependency depth]\label{cor:functional-synchronous}
Let $x^{(0)}=x$.  In one synchronous round, keep the boundary coordinates
fixed and replace every generated coordinate using the values from the
previous round:
\[
 x_v^{(k+1)}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 x_v^{(k)},&v\in V_0,\\
 F_v\bigl(x^{(k)}|_{P(v)}\bigr),&v\notin V_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
For $0\le k\le m$, after round $k$ every site in
$V_0\cup\cdots\cup V_k$ agrees with
$E(Bx)$.  Consequently $x^{(m)}=E(Bx)$.  This is a depth bound for the
explicit ranked system; correspondence with a separately specified compiler
or update rule requires its own theorem.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Induct on $k$.  Boundary values are fixed.  If all sites of rank at most
$k-1$ are correct after round $k-1$, then every parent of a rank-$k$ site is
correct, so its round-$k$ update evaluates the defining function at the same
parent tuple as $E(Bx)$.  Sites of lower rank remain correct because their
parents were correct in the preceding round.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Acyclic Boolean circuit compiler]
\label{cor:boolean-circuit-compiler}
Every nonempty finite acyclic Boolean circuit compiles to a ranked functional
system with one $\{0,1\}$-valued site per input or gate.  Inputs and constant
gates form $V_0$, with the constant coordinates restricted to their prescribed
values; every other gate is ranked by its longest source-to-gate path, has its
incoming wires as parents, and uses its truth function as $F_v$.  The
extension $E(b)$ is ordinary circuit evaluation on the admissible input
$b$.  Updating all nonconstant gate sites in parallel from the preceding
round reaches $E(b)$ in at most the circuit depth, independently of the
initial gate-register values.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The topological ranking makes every nonconstant gate depend only on lower
ranks, so the compilation satisfies the definition on the boundary domain
where constants have their prescribed values.  Recursive evaluation is $E$,
and the depth bound is \cref{cor:functional-synchronous}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certified solver]
The procedure ``settle the generating equations asynchronously, then evaluate the audits'' returns either the unique consistent extension or a named failed audit.  It cannot confuse an empty fiber with nontermination.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Propagation of boundary perturbations]\label{thm:functional-lipschitz}
Equip every $X_v$ with a metric $d_v$.  Suppose that for each generated site $v$ there are coefficients $L_{vu}\ge0$ such that
\[
 d_v(F_v(p),F_v(p'))
 \le
 \sum_{u\in P(v)}L_{vu}d_u(p_u,p'_u).
\]
For a boundary site $r\in V_0$, define coefficients recursively by
\[
 K_{rr}=1,
 \qquad K_{vr}=0\ (v\in V_0,\ v\ne r),
 \qquad
 K_{vr}=\sum_{u\in P(v)}L_{vu}K_{ur}\ (v\notin V_0).
\]
Then for all boundary values $b,b'$ and every site $v$,
\[
 d_v(E(b)_v,E(b')_v)
 \le
 \sum_{r\in V_0}K_{vr}d_r(b_r,b'_r).
\]
With the $\ell^1$ product metrics,
\[
 d_Q(E(b),E(b'))
 \le
 \Lambda_E d_{\cB}(b,b'),
 \qquad
 \Lambda_E:=\max_{r\in V_0}\sum_{v\in V}K_{vr}.
\]
Thus the generated extension supplies an explicit inverse-observation modulus
$\omega_B(t)\le\Lambda_Et$ on every accepted audit branch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sitewise estimate follows by induction on rank.  Boundary sites satisfy it by definition.  At a generated site, apply the local Lipschitz inequality and substitute the induction hypotheses; regrouping coefficients gives the recursion for $K_{vr}$.  Summing over $v$ and then bounding each boundary coefficient by $\Lambda_E$ proves the $\ell^1$ estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The local updates need not commute when one site depends on another.  Order independence follows from well-founded dependency and unique extension, not from a global pairwise-commutation hypothesis.  \Cref{thm:functional-lipschitz} additionally exposes the amplification carried by every dependency path.
\end{remark}

\section{Linear quotient systems and observability}\label{sec:linear}

Let $V,W,Z$ be finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces.  Let
\[
 A:V\to Z,
 \qquad C=\ker A,
 \qquad G\subseteq C
\]
be a gauge subspace.  Let $B:V\to W$ vanish on $G$, so it descends to $V/G$.  Equip $V/G$ with the quotient norm.

\begin{proposition}[Linear information-boundary criterion]\label{prop:linear-criterion}
The induced map $C/G\to W$ is injective if and only if
\[
 \ker(B|_C)=G.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \rank(B|_C)=\dim C-\dim G.
\]
The same statement holds over any field.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kernel of the induced map is $\ker(B|_C)/G$.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 H=C\cap G^\perp.
\]
Under the injectivity hypothesis, $B|_H$ is injective.  Let
\[
 \kappa_A:=\|A^\dagger\|,
 \qquad
 \kappa_B:=\|(B|_H)^\dagger\|,
\]
where $\dagger$ denotes the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse.  Thus, when
$H\ne\{0\}$, $\kappa_B$ is the reciprocal of the smallest singular value
of $B|_H$; on the zero space the pseudoinverse convention gives
$\kappa_B=0$.  The constant $\kappa_A$ is the global linear error-bound
constant for $C=\ker A$.

\begin{theorem}[Linear stability certificate]\label{thm:linear-certificate}
For all $x,y\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-certificate}
\begin{split}
 d_{V/G}([x],[y])\le{}&
 \kappa_A(\|Ax\|+\|Ay\|)\\
 &+\kappa_B\!\left(
 \|B(x-y)\|+
 \|B\|\kappa_A(\|Ax\|+\|Ay\|)
 \right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c_x=P_Cx$ and $c_y=P_Cy$ be the orthogonal projections onto $C$.  The pseudoinverse estimate gives
\[
 \|x-c_x\|\le\kappa_A\|Ax\|,
 \qquad
 \|y-c_y\|\le\kappa_A\|Ay\|.
\]
Let $h_x=P_Hc_x$ and $h_y=P_Hc_y$.  Since $C=G\oplus H$, the vectors $h_x,h_y$ are the minimal-norm representatives of the quotient classes $[c_x],[c_y]$.  Because $B$ vanishes on $G$,
\[
 B(h_x-h_y)=B(c_x-c_y).
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
 \|B(h_x-h_y)\|
 &\le \|B(x-y)\|+\|B\|(\|x-c_x\|+\|y-c_y\|),
\end{align*}
and injectivity of $B|_H$ gives
\[
 d_{C/G}([c_x],[c_y])=\|h_x-h_y\|
 \le\kappa_B\|B(c_x-c_y)\|.
\]
Finally,
\[
 d_{V/G}([x],[c_x])\le\|x-c_x\|,
 \qquad
 d_{V/G}([y],[c_y])\le\|y-c_y\|,
\]
so the quotient triangle inequality yields \eqref{eq:linear-certificate}.
\end{proof}

The finite-field version retains the rank criterion but not singular values.  It is the quotient form of an information-set condition from coding theory \citep{HuffmanPless2003}.

\subsection{Finite linear dynamics}

Let $T:U\to U$ be a linear update, let $S:U\to Y$ be a sensor, and let
$G\subseteq U$ satisfy $T(G)\subseteq G$ and $S(G)=0$.  Equip
$Y^{k+1}$ with its product inner product.  The length-$k$ observation map is
\[
 \mathcal O_kx=(Sx,STx,\ldots,ST^kx).
\]

\begin{corollary}[Observability modulo gauge]\label{cor:observability}
The time tube $\mathcal O_k$ reconstructs $U/G$ if and only if
\[
 \ker\mathcal O_k=G.
\]
Over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ and when $G^\perp\ne\{0\}$, the smallest
singular value of $\mathcal O_k$ on $G^\perp$ is the separation margin; its
reciprocal (equivalently, the norm of the restricted pseudoinverse) is the
inverse-stability constant.  On the zero space only the pseudoinverse
convention is used.  The static Rule-90 example
in \cref{sec:examples} instead instantiates the earlier finite-field quotient
rank criterion; it is not asserted to instantiate this time-tube corollary.
\end{corollary}

This is the classical observability matrix viewed through the quotient-boundary language; no novelty is claimed for the control-theoretic rank test \citep{Kailath1980}.

\section{Complexity of succinct boundary problems}\label{sec:complexity}

The finite theory is elementary when $Q$ and $C$ are listed explicitly.  It becomes computationally nontrivial when consistency is given by a Boolean circuit or formula.

\begin{definition}[Succinct Boolean boundary instance]
A succinct instance consists of a Boolean circuit $F:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ and a coordinate set $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.  The consistent set is $C=F^{-1}(1)$ and the observation is the projection $B_S(x)=x|_S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary realizability is NP-complete]\label{thm:realizability-complexity}
Given $(F,S,b)$, deciding whether
\[
 \{x:F(x)=1,\ B_S(x)=b\}
\]
is nonempty is NP-complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A satisfying extension is a polynomial witness.  SAT reduces to the problem by taking $S=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary determination is coNP-complete]\label{thm:injectivity-complexity}
Given $(F,S)$, deciding whether $B_S|_C$ is injective is coNP-complete, even when $S$ contains all but one variable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Non-injectivity has an NP certificate: two distinct satisfying assignments agreeing on $S$.  For coNP-hardness, reduce UNSAT while keeping the consistent set nonempty.  Given a formula $\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$, introduce bits $z,t$ and define
\[
 F(x,z,t)=
 \bigl(\neg t\wedge[x=0^m]\wedge\neg z\bigr)
 \vee
 \bigl(t\wedge\varphi(x)\bigr),
 \qquad
 S=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m,t\}.
\]
The baseline assignment $(0^m,0,0)$ is always consistent.  If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, any satisfying $x$ gives the two distinct consistent assignments $(x,0,1)$ and $(x,1,1)$ with the same boundary.  If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, the baseline is the only consistent assignment, so the boundary restriction is injective.  Thus boundary determination is equivalent to unsatisfiability even for nonempty $C$, and $S$ observes all but one variable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strong repair existence is $\Pi_2^P$-complete]\label{thm:repair-complexity}
Let a Boolean circuit $R(w,d)$ represent a local consistency relation on write bits $w$ and protected collar bits $d$.  Deciding whether an unrestricted total strong collar-preserving repair (equivalently, a set-theoretic section of the collar projection) exists is $\Pi_2^P$-complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:collar-section}, a strong repair exists exactly when
\[
 \forall d\ \exists w\quad R(w,d)=1.
\]
This is in $\Pi_2^P$ and is $\Pi_2^P$-hard by the canonical quantified-Boolean-formula problem with one universal and one existential block \citep{Stockmeyer1976,Papadimitriou1994}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
These results explain why an abstract finite normalizer can be mathematically canonical yet computationally difficult.  The ranked functional class avoids the hardness by supplying a directed generative presentation; the linear class avoids it through rank computation.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Explicit-table algorithms]
If $Q$, $C$, $B$, $d_Q$, $d_{\cB}$, and $\Phi$ are given by explicit finite tables, then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the fiber trichotomy and partial normalizer are computable in time $O(|Q|+|C|)$ after hashing observations;
\item $\omega_B$ is computable from $O(|C|^2)$ pairs;
\item $\eta_\Phi$ is computable from all-pairs distances to $C$ and a sort by residual value.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\section{Examples and separation results}\label{sec:examples}

\begin{example}[Confluence does not imply observable reconstruction]
Let $Q=C=\{a,b\}$ with the empty rewrite relation and a constant observation map.  Every state is a normal form and the relation is confluent, but the sole observation fiber contains two consistent states.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Two-bit witness]\label{ex:two-bit-positive}
Let $Q=\mathbb F_2^2$, let $B(u,v)=u$, and set
$C=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$.  For each $u\in\mathbb F_2$, add the rewrite
$(u,1)\to(u,0)$.  This preserves $B$; the normal forms
are exactly $C$; and every source $(u,v)$ terminates at $(u,0)$.  Thus sources
with the same observation have the same endpoint, jointly witnessing
normalization, same-source confluence, and the stronger cross-source property
of \cref{thm:cross-source-modulo} for equality.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Observable determination does not imply realizability]
Let $C=\{c\}\subset Q$ and let $\cB=\{0,1\}$ with $B(c)=0$.  The restriction $B|_C$ is injective, but the fiber over $1$ is empty.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Termination does not imply completeness]
Let $Q=\{x,n\}$, let $x\to n$, and declare $C=\{x\}$.  The relation terminates, but its only normal form is not consistent.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A singleton fiber does not replace observation preservation]
Let $Q=\{x,c,d\}$, let $C=\{c,d\}$, set
$B(x)=B(c)=0$ and $B(d)=1$, and take $x\to d$ as the sole rewrite step.
Then the normal forms are exactly $C$ and the consistent fiber over $0$ is the
singleton $\{c\}$.  Nevertheless, the boundary-$0$ state $x$ normalizes to
$d$, not to $c$, because the rewrite changes the observation.  Thus the
observation-preservation hypothesis in \cref{cor:empty-singleton} is
load-bearing.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Native protected-profile separations]
The machine-checked artifact instantiates \cref{thm:protected-profile} and
\cref{prop:behavior-cut-recovery} on four reader-facing boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
\item A fine TwoBit observation reaches the final layer, while its coarse
      observation is almost surely reachable but has two endpoint classes;
      the inherited coarse rewrite remains confluent.
\item A full fixed relation satisfies the static collar criterion, whereas a
      relation missing one collar value activates the empty-fiber cut; filling
      that value is a negative control.
\item A proper-target three-state chain with a reachable closed trap has
      positive hit probability strictly below one; another hits almost surely
      and has two inequivalent positive endpoints.
\item Two profiles reverse their strict comparison between the first and
      second cut coordinates, so neither is below the other in the product
      preorder.  A separate nonidentity state map activates exact transport.
\end{itemize}
These fixtures establish nonvacuity and sharp separation.  They do not claim
empirical representativeness, stochastic rates, or a global mechanism rank.
\end{example}

\subsection{Rule 90 as a regression test}\label{sec:rule90}

No cellular automaton is used in the general theory.  Rule 90 is retained because a three-cell instance simultaneously exhibits a hidden kernel, a proper information boundary, a deficient boundary, and a reverse-repair obstruction.

Over $\mathbb F_2$, define
\[
 F(a,b,c)=(b,a+c,b).
\]
A one-step spacetime record is a pair $(s,t)$ satisfying $t=F(s)$.

\begin{proposition}[Width-three Rule 90]
The kernel and image are
\[
 \ker F=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0,1)\},
 \qquad
 \im F=\{(u,v,u):u,v\in\mathbb F_2\}.
\]
The coordinate observation $B_{01}(u,v,w)=(u,v)$ is injective on $\im F$, while $B_{02}(u,v,w)=(u,w)$ is not.  No total exact repair can preserve an arbitrary target row and alter only the seed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kernel and image follow immediately from the formula for $F$.  On the image, the first two coordinates determine $(u,v,u)$, whereas the first and third coordinates repeat the same bit.  A reverse repair preserving every target would require $F$ to be surjective by \cref{thm:collar-section}; for example $(0,0,1)$ is not in the image.
\end{proof}

The width-three example is a regression test, not a rule selected by the
theory or a load-bearing assumption.

\section{Application: set-valued public-capacity readback}
\label{sec:public-capacity-application}

The empty/singleton/ambiguous fiber distinction has a direct application to
finite observer-record capacity. This subsection records the application; it
does not make capacity an implication of confluence. Fix a supplied positive
carrier dimension \(D\), a trial system with terminal physical quotient fiber
\(\widetilde\Omega_{r,D}\), and a separately constructed functional
\[
M_\varepsilon:\widetilde\Omega_{r,D}\longrightarrow\mathbb N_{>0}.
\]
In the OPH capacity application, \(M_0(q)\) is the independence number of the
compound confusability graph built from reachable compatible public record
atoms and globally coupled checkpoint kernels. That construction, its
publicness policy, and its physical carrier are external data for the present
normal-form theorem.

Define the capacity readback fiber
\[
\mathfrak F_{r,\varepsilon}(D)=
\{M_\varepsilon(q):q\in\widetilde\Omega_{r,D}\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Capacity-readback trichotomy]
\label{prop:capacity-readback-trichotomy}
The readback is unavailable when the functional is undefined on any required
terminal class or the terminal fiber is empty, scalar when
\(\mathfrak F_{r,\varepsilon}(D)=\{m\}\), and ambiguous when
\(|\mathfrak F_{r,\varepsilon}(D)|>1\). Stable exact closure at \(D\) is the
singleton condition
\[
\mathfrak F_{r,0}(D)=\{D\}.
\]
Confluence from each fixed initial state does not imply this cross-source
singleton condition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first three statements are the image-set alternatives for the declared
functional. Stable closure requires every admissible terminal source class to
have full readback \(D\). Confluence identifies repair schedules from one
initial class; it does not identify distinct source classes in the unclosed
fiber.
\end{proof}

If an implementation isomorphism preserves observer lineage, record atoms,
interface atom maps, reachable public sections, publicness policy, every
global checkpoint kernel, and capacity-carrier projection ranks, then it
transports the compound confusability graph and every approximate decoder.
The functional \(M_\varepsilon\) and the readback set are therefore invariant
under that full packet isomorphism. A normal-form hash, stagewise singleton,
or implementation relabeling alone supplies no such theorem. A
refinement-limit statement additionally requires uniform stable separation or an explicit
capacity-packet naturality receipt, in accord with the general refinement
results above.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why the factorization is useful}

The estimate
\[
 \text{residual}\xrightarrow{\ \eta\ }
 \text{distance to consistency}
 \xrightarrow{\ \omega\ }
 \text{observable uniqueness}
\]
separates two burdens that are often mixed.  A rewrite algorithm, numerical solver, or physical implementation controls $\eta$ by showing that a small residual means closeness to $C$.  The observation architecture controls $\omega$ by showing that $B|_C$ is well conditioned, a property strictly stronger than injectivity of $B|_C$.  Critical-pair arguments may help establish exact completion or small residuals, and they are not required in the final output comparison.

\subsection{Why finite uniqueness is not enough}

In a refinement program, proving $B_r|_{C_r}$ injective for every $r$ does not prevent the inverse condition number from diverging.  \Cref{ex:nonuniform} is deliberately minimal.  The correct limit-facing statement is uniform stable separation, not a list of stagewise singleton claims.  This is the main stability requirement emphasized for refinement-sensitive applications.

The protected first-hit profile has the same discipline at a different
interface.  Finite hitting, closed-class, Bellman, and lumpability arguments
are standard; the OPH-specific step is to assemble them around protected
observations and declared sources, recover the existing behavior cuts, and
retain every source, target, quotient, and scheduler premise during transport.

\subsection{A declared scheduler class and its capacity split}

The machine-checked development fixes one declared admissible scheduler
class on the two-bit repair carrier. A scheduler in the class is a finite
Markov kernel whose supported steps do not increase a declared defect
mismatch, write at most one of the two registers, and spend at most one
unit of expected declared capacity per step. Reading the observed register
as the shared network record and the defect flag as a private register
splits each step cost into a shared and a private part and cuts out two
sub-classes: the self-update-only schedulers, which never write the shared
register, and the network-update-only schedulers, which never write the
private one. Three results hold on this carrier. Under every scheduler in
the class the defective fiber is unreachable from every consistent state,
while an explicit mismatch-violating kernel outside the class reaches it
with probability one. The fiber with the shared register flipped is
unreachable under every self-update-only scheduler and is hit with
probability one by an explicit network-update-only member, so a positive
hit prices at least one shared write. The almost-sure hitting hypothesis of
the fine two-bit fixture is discharged by the declared repair kernel, and
the in-class identity scheduler witnesses that the class constraints do not
force it. The class is a declaration on this finite carrier: the locality
and capacity clauses are typed and unconsumed by the obstruction theorems,
and no scheduler-selection claim is made.

\subsection{Adaptive canonical repair: stutter, fairness, and endpoint promotion}

The declared finite Markov profile above and the repository's canonical
adaptive repair answer different questions.  Let \(C\) be a finite OPH
carrier, let \(\sigma\) choose one site from the active record at each attempt,
and write
\[
x_n=\operatorname{adaptiveRun}_C(n,\sigma,x_0),
\qquad r_n=\operatorname{mismatchCount}(x_n).
\]
An attempt may stutter when the chosen site has no effective repair.  Every
nonstuttering attempt is an accepted repair step and strictly lowers \(r_n\),
while every stuttering attempt leaves the record unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive repair stratification]
\label{thm:oph-adaptive-repair-stratification}
For every \(x_0\) and every adaptive site scheduler \(\sigma\), there is an
\(N\) such that \(x_n=x_N\) for all \(n\ge N\).  If \(\sigma\) is pathwise
weakly fair, meaning that a site which remains enabled along a tail is
eventually selected on that tail, then \(x_N\) is a normal form.  Under the
stronger work-conserving condition, which requires the selected site to fire
whenever some site can fire, one may choose
\(N\le\operatorname{mismatchCount}(x_0)\), and the run is constant from
\(N\) onward.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The mismatch count never increases and strictly decreases at every genuine
change, so only finitely many genuine changes are possible.  If the eventual
constant state were reducible, one site would remain enabled throughout the
constant tail; pathwise weak fairness would select it and force another
genuine change, a contradiction.  Under work conservation every nonnormal
state takes a genuine decreasing step, so the initial natural-number rank
bounds the first normal index.
\end{proof}

The machine-checked provider also proves that every \(x_n\) is reachable from
\(x_0\) by accepted repair steps.  A downstream endpoint theorem then adds the
separately named completeness and confluence hypotheses: every pathwise
weak-fair adaptive run reaches the same raw endpoint as the canonical repair,
and therefore the same public fixed-point object.  This promotion supplies no
stochastic kernel, active-source law, hitting probability, rate, physical
scheduler, or refinement theorem.

\subsection{Cumulative attempt capacity and sharpness}

Every scheduler invocation receives one attempt unit, including an
equality stutter.  Let \(A_N=N\) be this cumulative attempt cost, let \(G_N\)
count the genuine changes among the first \(N\) attempts, and let
\(S_N=N-G_N\).  Thus \(A_N=G_N+S_N\); the initial mismatch rank controls
\(G_N\), but it does not control \(S_N\).

\begin{definition}[Bounded waste]
For \(q\in\mathbb N\), an adaptive scheduler has \emph{waste bound} \(q\) if,
from every scheduler index and every reducible record, at least one genuine
change occurs among the next \(q+1\) attempts.  Work conservation is the
special case \(q=0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cumulative attempt-capacity classification]
\label{thm:oph-cumulative-attempt-capacity}
The canonical OPH adaptive attempt semantics has the following three
properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There is no single mismatch-only finite attempt horizon uniformly valid
for all normalizing schedulers.  For every \(d\), a pathwise weak-fair scheduler on the committed
TwoCell source has initial mismatch one, stutters through attempt \(d\), and
reaches a normal form at attempt \(d+1\).
\item A scheduler with waste bound \(q\) reaches a stable normal form at some
\[
N\le(q+1)\operatorname{mismatchCount}(x_0).
\]
Consequently an attempt budget \(B\) at least this large cannot be exhausted
before normality.
\item The work-conserving coefficient is sharp.  For every \(m\ge1\), a
canonical independent-defect carrier with \(m\) self-loop mismatches has a
work-conserving scheduler whose first normal index is exactly \(m\).  Budget
below \(m\) fails and budget at least \(m\) succeeds.  The committed TwoCell
source is a second sharp instance with a different patch cardinality.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For (i), the delayed scheduler selects the edge-free TwoCell site for its
first \(d\) indices and selects the enabled probe next.  All delayed attempts
are literal equality stutters, while the final probe repair is genuine.
For (ii), if the current record is reducible, bounded waste produces a genuine
change within \(q+1\) attempts.  That change strictly lowers the natural
mismatch rank.  Strong induction on the rank gives the displayed product
bound, and normality makes the remaining tail constant.  For (iii), each site
of the independent-defect carrier owns one self-loop mismatch.  A canonical
local repair sets exactly that site to its unique consistent value, so every
genuine change lowers the mismatch count by exactly one.  The state-dependent
scheduler chooses a broken site whenever one exists.  Its rank after \(n\)
attempts is \(m-n\) for \(n\le m\), proving both necessity and equality.  The
TwoCell calculation gives the second, nonisomorphic threshold-one instance.
\end{proof}

Under the same budget and waste premises, the downstream Tower theorem adds
the separately named completeness and confluence hypotheses and returns the
existing canonical raw and public fixed-point endpoint at an index
\(N\le B\).  The capacity is a mathematical count of scheduler invocations.
It is not a physical clock, rate, energy, bandwidth allocation, hardware
quota, fee, or stochastic policy.

\subsection{Fixed computation federations}

The computation compiler has a second, distinct asynchronous surface.  For a
Boolean formula \(\varphi\) on \(k\) inputs, the generated node list
\(\operatorname{go}(\varphi,k)\) is independent of the input.  Its registers
start at \(k\); the initial state supplies the lower input ports.  A canonical
accepted step chooses a failing node and performs exactly that node's declared
write.  The historical patch-frame relation remains unchanged and is not used
as the accepted relation.

Read the node defects in increasing register order, with every earlier bit
more significant than all later bits together.  This superincreasing natural
rank strictly decreases at every canonical accepted step.  An upstream repair
may expose several later defects: the machine-checked fanout control raises
the raw failing-node count from one to two, so ordinary defect count is not the
measure.  The dependency rank nevertheless falls, and the accepted relation
is well founded without a fairness premise.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed computation-federation progress]
\label{thm:fixed-computation-federation}
For every finite Boolean function there is one input-independent generated-node
federation such that every canonical attempt run is eventually constant.  If
every node that remains failing on a tail is eventually selected on that tail,
the stable state is a consensus and its output equals the function value for
the preserved initial input.  Any two normal endpoints with the same input
observation agree on the output register.
\end{theorem}

The fairness premise removes attempt stutter; it does not prove accepted-step
termination.  Its positive singleton instance and a root-only starvation
schedule inhabit the two sides.  The broader historical relation admits a
sharper one-member control: the only member and site are selected at every
attempt and remain continuously enabled, yet the run stutters forever outside
consensus because the relation does not require the declared repair to execute.
Thus fairness cannot compensate for a step semantics that counts a no-op.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-federation execution classification]
\label{thm:fixed-federation-execution-classification}
For a well-formed generated-node federation and a fixed attempt run, pathwise
weak fairness holds if and only if the run eventually reaches a stable
consensus.  The reverse implication is vacuous after consensus: no node can
remain failing on that stable tail, so it does not imply recurrent selection.
At the compiler's present node granularity, member recurrence and register-site
recurrence coincide because generated output registers are unique; recurrence
is nevertheless strictly stronger than tail fairness.  A state-blind
round-robin scheduler is member- and site-recurrent and reaches the stable
correct output.  More sharply, its first \(n\) attempts are exactly the
emission-order sweep, so it reaches stable consensus and the correct output
within one emitted-node cycle.

If the emitted program has \(n\) nodes, every canonical genuine accepted path
has length at most
\[
  \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.
\]
An actual recursively nested NAND family emitted by the same compiler has a
consensus-reaching accepted path of length \(m\) satisfying
\[
  8m+1=n^2+8n.
\]
Thus the accepted-step classification is order-sharp
\(\Theta(n^2)\) in one node-count metric; the finite upper and lower constants
are not claimed equal.  Well-formedness alone does not yield this: every
well-formed node list admits fewer than \(2^n\) canonical accepted steps, an
explicit fanout chain family attains \(2^n-1\), and at three nodes that
family realizes seven accepted steps against the triangular budget six, so
the compiler's single-consumer structure is what collapses the exponential
regime to a quadratic one.  Weak fairness alone supplies no uniform finite attempt
horizon.  If a supplied fixed-node scheduler instead has bounded waste \(q\),
stable consensus is reached within
\((q+1)n(n+1)/2\) attempts.  Round robin discharges this premise with
\(q=n-1\), but its direct one-cycle theorem gives the sharper \(n\)-attempt
bound for that scheduler.  The Tower consumer returns the same-input unique
output.
\end{theorem}

The bounded-waste counting theorem is shared with the canonical adaptive
repair route through a neutral ranked-attempt interface.  Each domain proves
its own run and quiescence equivalence, and each Tower receiver calls the
shared theorem.  This is code and proof reuse, not a claim that adaptive
scheduler waste and fixed-compiler repair work are the same scientific
quantity.

The endpoint statement uses the immutable input prefix as the protected
observation and output-register equality as the endpoint equivalence.  Unused
natural-number registers are not identified.  The selected round-robin object
is a mathematical function, not a physical scheduler source.  No physical
device, finite \texttt{PublicWorld} presentation, clock, rate, energy,
bandwidth, fee, hardware realization, or raw-state uniqueness is constructed.

\subsection{Limitations}

The theory assumes a declared meaningful quotient, consistent set, residual, observation metric, and refinement map.  It does not select those objects.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item a normalizer is not a probability measure;
\item an inverse-limit normalizer is not a continuum field theory;
\item a finite update bound is not a wall-clock liveness theorem;
\item a boundary pseudometric does not acquire additional semantics without a separate interpretation theorem;
\item a small Rule-90 example does not establish cellular-automaton universality or generality.
\item a finite protected profile is relative to one declared scheduler and
      active-source family; it gives no rate, expected hitting time, mixing
      bound, stationary law, infinite-state extension, or refinement-tower
      conclusion; results quantified over the declared admissible class are
      relative to that class declaration and to the two-register
      capacity-split reading;
\item canonical adaptive repair is eventually constant without a fairness
      premise, but normality requires pathwise weak fairness and a
      canonical consistent endpoint requires completeness and
      confluence;
\item cumulative attempt capacity counts every chosen-site invocation,
      including equality stutter; bounded waste and the budget are supplied
      premises, weak fairness alone gives no uniform mismatch-only finite attempt bound, and the
      sharp lower bound is family-specific rather than universal over all
      carriers;
\item fixed-federation pathwise weak fairness is exactly stable consensus only
      relative to one attempt run and the source-derived eventual-constancy
      theorem; its reverse is vacuous after stable consensus and does not give
      recurrence, weak fairness alone has no uniform finite attempt horizon,
      the accepted-step result is order-sharp rather than constant-sharp, and
      bounded-waste attempts have no physical unit;
\item coordinatewise cut comparison is not a scalar score, total order, or
      family-wide optimum, and exact finite transport is not implementation or
      product refinement.
\end{itemize}
These exclusions are necessary for both mathematical correctness and journal review.

\section{Conclusion}

Confluence is about paths from one state.  Observable normalization is about what partial data determine after consistency is enforced.  Their exact intersection is classical unique-normal-form theory: under observation preservation and $\NF(\to)=C$, identification modulo any silent equivalence is equivalent to agreement of normal endpoints across all sources with the same observation.  This does not by itself provide normalization or all-schedule settlement.  Their stable intersection is governed by two analytic objects: an error bound to the consistent set and an inverse modulus for the observation map on that set.

On the canonical OPH adaptive branch, finite genuine-change stabilization is
source-derived rather than postulated as a scheduler property.  The remaining
steps are deliberately separate: pathwise weak fairness supplies normality,
while completeness and confluence promote the stable state to one consistent
canonical raw and public endpoint.

On the separate fixed compiler branch, the emission-order round-robin schedule
reaches the exact output in one \(n\)-attempt cycle, arbitrary genuine accepted
order has an order-sharp \(\Theta(n^2)\) worst-case family, weak fairness alone
has no uniform finite attempt horizon, and bounded waste supplies the general
conditional attempt bound.  These four statements classify distinct
scheduler and cost regimes rather than attaching a physical duration to an
attempt.

The resulting estimate is universal and sharp in its two unavoidable components. It survives refinement: one-step defects telescope, same-level solver agreement needs no tower term, and compatible cross-level computations differ by their receipt errors and the intervening normalizer-defect tail.  For a compatible admissible input in complete spaces, nonexpansive restrictions, vanishing receipt errors, and summable pathwise tails give a common projective limit; exact compatibility recovers the inverse-limit normalizer.  On finite probability spaces, fiber averaging supplies the canonical conditional-expectation projector, while its matrix receipt keeps that averaging distinct from representative selection or a spectral-gap claim.  Empty fibers and ambiguous fibers stay visible; a repair operator does not absorb them.  The theory therefore supplies the determination and stability question, a four-stage protected-behavior ladder, and an orthogonal local-support question:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Does a consistent extension exist?}\\
\text{Is it observably unique and stable?}\\
\text{Does some declared source reach it with positive probability?}\\
\text{Do all declared sources reach it almost surely?}\\
\text{Do first-hit endpoints select one silent-equivalence class?}\\
\text{Can the declared local support construct it?}
\end{array}}
\]
No one of these questions answers the others.  Keeping them separate is the central mathematical lesson and the reason the framework applies across rewrite, reconstruction, and distributed-inference models.
