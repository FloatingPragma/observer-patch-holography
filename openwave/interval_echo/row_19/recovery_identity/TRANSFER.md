# Does the measured overlap source predict a second observer's image?

Use the 80-cell Interval Echo reference and the two physical preparations selected by the joint-record meter. The preparation changes the two-head correlation at fixed conditional one-head records, reversal weights and parked energy. Its original four-station volume is fixed to first order. The source selection used that survey and the reference modular operator. The optical measurements use separate copies of the same preparations. [Source selection](../selected_collar/PROTOCOL.md)

Test one specific interpretation of the source. Let the reference be $R$ and its parity-conditioned product of marginals be $P$. On the held pair the connected modular contrast is the expectation change of $L=\log P-\log R$. The product term cancels because both marginal states are fixed in each parity sector. This equals the change of the full reference generator $K=-\log R$, and equals the declared recovery remainder in the right-erased arm. Take the signed contrast and counting interval from that arm's eighteen detector settings. For ordinary calibration compute the change of the same $K$ on the ordinary energy-changing endpoints. Use the calibrated model value for this ordinary source contrast. Divide each endpoint contrast by its full preparation step.

The working hypothesis is that the signed transverse image of each probe responds linearly to this single source reading with a probe-specific constant gain over the specified steps. It predicts that the held-to-ordinary image slope ratio of each probe equals the held-to-ordinary source slope ratio. This tests the scalar reading and these particular instruments together. A general stress tensor, a source with a different spatial profile, or material refraction can give different image responses.

Acquire the fixed three-sender, three-receiver camera on both sources with each observer's clock and radar calibration. Use the receiver triangle at ports 39, 47 and 42, the appointment at 4.375 ticks, and the source and ordinary half-steps registered by their respective preparation routines. Compare the signed image area. Keep reference shots, all missed returns and outside images. Use seeds 191127 and 191128 for interaction strengths 8 and 0 respectively with the registered counting budget. The zero-strength control tests whether the held image contrast is distinguishable from zero. Publish unresolved denominators and both probes irrespective of the result. Overlap of the counting intervals leaves the prediction compatible at this resolution. Disjoint intervals reject this finite scalar transfer. Prior experiments measured a different focusing observable on this source and this camera on a different held source.
