# Can spatially resolved source records account for equal-volume ball areas?

At the end of the registered sparse and dense flights, retain the receiver address together with parity and the two memory readouts. Enroll the six clock stations and a seventh outcome containing all other addresses. Sum over all launches. In a separate ordinary control use the same dense flight and the original ordinary parity write. In the geometry trial all three returned memories replace the same stationary source on one in 256 draws. The local source observer measures the active copies and applies that known probability. Complete signed transfer into a subregion includes its probability of occurrence.

Test the candidate identification of the terminal receipt at each station with the source of the fixed-volume vertex ball around that station. Give every filling, center and window its own free ordinary normalization in one control. Check the reference modular increment, the contribution from the separate head and parity records, and ordinary parked-energy increments. For each score subtract the expected price of a copy of the undisturbed reference so relocating such a copy carries zero excess. Use the actual ordinary response to test whether one nonnegative coefficient multiplying the connected charge, added either to the marginal score or to excess ordinary energy, accounts for the sparse response in all balls. Compare also the connected score alone. Use 30 native capped returns to measure the areas in all six preassigned triangulations at radius one half on the dense arm and the exact same enclosed volume in both other arms. Keep the complement and both signs. The site-to-ball assignment and the absence of spatial weighting within a receiver are hypotheses tested here.
