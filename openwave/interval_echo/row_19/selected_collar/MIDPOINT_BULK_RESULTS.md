### A direct shear prediction without fitting the source

For one spherical screen with density \(A/r\) up to \(L\), an ordinary thin lens has column density \(\Sigma(R)=2A\,\operatorname{arcosh}(L/R)\) inside its edge. Integrating the mass in the projected cylinder and subtracting this column gives

\[
 \Delta\Sigma(R)=
 \begin{cases}2A/(1+\sqrt{1-R^2/L^2}),& R<L,\\
 2AL^2/R^2,& R\ge L.\end{cases}
\]

The orbital and lensing observables must therefore obey \(g_{\rm extra}/2\le\pi G\Delta\Sigma_{\rm extra}\le g_{\rm extra}\) at every radius for any positive concentric mixture. A compact baryonic mass contributes the upper equality outside its support. Comparing the two observables in one identified population would test this prediction without fixing a screen size. The SPARC galaxies and lensing stacks here do not supply such matched records.

There is also a test entirely on measured shear. Let \(s=(R_{\rm near}/R_{\rm far})^2\) and \(S=\Delta\Sigma\). Every positive mixture satisfies
\[
 s S_{\rm near}\ \le\ S_{\rm far}\ \le\
          (1+\sqrt{1-s})S_{\rm near}.
\]
For one screen the ratio attains its minimum when both radii are exterior and its maximum when the edge is at the far radius. For an edge between the radii it increases as \(L^2(1+\sqrt{1-R_{\rm near}^2/L^2})\). Beyond the far radius its logarithmic derivative in \(L\) is \(1/\sqrt{1-R_{\rm near}^2/L^2}-1/\sqrt{1-R_{\rm far}^2/L^2}<0\). Both linear inequalities survive addition. They also survive fixed lens weights when both bins use the same distribution of relative positions in logarithmic acceleration. For arbitrary effective positions within the two bins the conservative bounds use the smallest possible \(s\).

### What does the same charge normalization require in an expanding universe?

Restoring the physical screen radius \(L\) gives another observable requirement. The rest-ball kernel has one power of length. Holding a boundary aperture at fixed fraction of \(L\) and keeping the ordinary conversion fixed therefore gives total midpoint mass \(M=15\kappa/(32\pi L)\) in natural units. Equivalently \(\rho(r)=15\kappa/(64\pi^2 L^3r)\) inside the sphere. Here \(\kappa\) is the total additive modular response, measured locally for a single enrolled pair. This argument uses the midpoint reconstruction and assumes its kernel is the physical stress instrument.

Consider screens expanding with \(L\propto a\) and an inventory in a fixed comoving volume whose summed pair price is \(K(a)\). For unequal expanding screens with fixed comoving sizes the sum is weighted by each inverse comoving size. Their mean physical density is then proportional to \(K(a)/a^4\). If the total price is fixed, doubling \(a\) leaves one sixteenth of the density. If it doubles, the surviving fraction is one eighth. A population of bound screens with fixed physical radii, conserved masses and conserved comoving number also gives one eighth. Maps at different angular resolutions of one screen do not measure cosmological growth.

Thomas, Kopp and Skordis used Planck temperature and polarization, lensing and baryon acoustic oscillations to constrain the constant equation of state of the fitted dark component. Using their reported 99.7% interval, doubling \(a\) leaves a density fraction 0.12438–0.12524. For expanding midpoint screens to account for the whole dark component in this analysis, the total weighted pair price must grow by a factor **1.9901–2.0038** per doubling, if the power law holds. A fixed word inventory per comoving volume misses that requirement. A physical law producing this growth or conserving the mass of bound screens remains unmeasured. The data constrain dilution separately from the absolute abundance and the growth of perturbations.
