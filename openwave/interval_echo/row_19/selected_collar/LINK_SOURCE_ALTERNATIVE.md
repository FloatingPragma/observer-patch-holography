# Can the same recorded transfer and volume accompany an expanding area?

Enroll the complete three-outcome detector for the positive probe transferred from port 0 to 3 and the original four-anchor survey. Keep the held source and reference from the blinded link experiment. Remove only the promise that the source must be drawn from three specific calibration stocks. Keep all one-head marginals and parity weights. Seek six reachable competing preparations using the independently computed detector responses to choose them. This is an adversarial identifiability test.

Make two triangles of competing lotteries. In each, test whether the held detector's joint signed mean and hit probability lie strictly inside the triangle of the three measured alternatives under all allowed counting errors. Together these two numbers determine the entire plus, minus and missed-port law. If the inclusion succeeds, an exact lottery in each triangle has the same underlying terminal law as the held source. Test whether the two triangles lie on opposite sides of the held survey volume, including variation along mixtures. If so, continuity supplies an exact matching volume between them. Test the signs of the reference modular increment and the anchor-area change on that whole convex hull. Record all calibration choices, populations, detector counts, clock histograms and inconclusive comparisons. A counterexample to a universal source identification can be chosen with the observables in view.
