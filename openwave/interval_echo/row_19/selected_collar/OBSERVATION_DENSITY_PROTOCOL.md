# Does omitting a physical interrogation produce an attractive source?

Use the ordinary-calibrated paired reference and its six sending stations. A randomly chosen sender releases either signed probe with equal probability. For half-window lengths 1, 2 and 4 half-ticks, compare two experiments with identical launch, Hamiltonian, total flight time and final discard of probe position. In the dense experiment, ask halfway whether the probe occupies its launch port and record every yes and no. In the sparse experiment, omit that physical interrogation. As a control, erase the dense report after it has been made. Trace over the probe position at the end and keep both native memory heads. Parity is conserved. Apply the procedure to one in 256 copies, preserving the calibrated reference on the others. This declared dose tests response near equilibrium.

Use separate launches to measure the completed source with paired-head counts and the four-anchor geometry with all twelve return-time histograms. Also attempt the blinded free-position source measurement. For sparse minus dense, measure the change in the original connected modular price, the contribution of the single-head and parity records, total entropy, raw anchor area and volume. Test the stationarity reading obtained by subtracting the calibrated response to the change in volume. Report its sign across the whole interval of interrogation probabilities. Retain all three windows and the sham even if the signs differ. A candidate physical law that an omitted interrogation necessarily produces a positive connected source and additional ordinary-calibrated contraction fails on this defined family if an enrolled case gives the opposite sign. A result about this family alone does not decide the sign on an astronomical observer net.
