# Regional stationarity with the records retained after a pulse

Use the 80-cell native pulse instrument and the six stations and 30 directed routes of the six-station read. Fix the existing equilibrium, ordinary parity redistribution, positive and negative right-head rotations, and the two matched one-head source endpoints. Draw new independent banks. Use the ordinary and rotation contrasts to calibrate each quartet and reserve the matched joint change for prediction. For the geometry keep the full cap-six timing and the original six-clock enrollment. Include all 15 quartets. Use shared, public preparation and read settings without conditioning the source on an echo arriving.

One observer chooses the route uniformly. A second records the first completed return tick or a timeout at the cap. Test the regional connected modular variation between the route and the return category. Give each deterministic grouping of the ten successful ticks its own calculation, always retaining the distinct timeout. Include groupings that do not preserve time order. Also test public random mixtures of these reporting conventions by enclosing the extrema for each quartet. For comparison test the entropy variation of the timing distribution conditional on route. Include the full time read and the binary echo read separately.

A successful return brings back the two head records in a definite parity block. On separate copies, at the sending station read parity and the two signs along each of the nine local Pauli-axis pairs. Pool the successful times, with every missed return retained in the denominator. Construct the connected modular reading of each returned pair at the equilibrium measured for its route and parity. Compare the returned contribution and the difference between the incoming and returned contributions. Include the selected source, ordinary calibration and rotation for each reading. Do not assign an outgoing head reading to a timeout. Check the returned trace against the ordinary timing instrument. Compare all three source variations with the same geometry. Record statistical and propagation uncertainty, ill-conditioned calibrations and unresolved signs. These are finite surveys. A common positive area coefficient and an extension to proper balls, light and ordinary material are needed for the gravitational interpretation.
