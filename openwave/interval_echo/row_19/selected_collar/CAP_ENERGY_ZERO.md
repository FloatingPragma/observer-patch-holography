# Can an energy-zero convention remove the positive local cap remainder?

At the declared native energy calibration, all six terminal receivers give a positive full modular increment after subtracting the centered-ball flight-energy reading at radius one half. The Ramsey comparison measures energy relative to its bypass. Adding a scalar to either parity or flight-sign sector leaves free trajectories unchanged. On a spatially resolved energy read it adds that scalar times the local change in sector population. Test whether the positive remainder survives this freedom.

Repeat the programmed and dense flights on fresh copies for each sign. Retain the terminal address and parity, averaging equally over the three times and six launchers. Enroll every complement outcome. For each station use the actual local modular score and full symmetric flight energy measured previously. Allow an unrestricted common energy origin, then independent origins per parity, then independent origins for all four sign and parity sectors. Test whether all six remainders can vanish, or all six can be nonpositive, with the original centered boost coefficient. Finally test the same questions while also granting a common nonnegative coefficient multiplying the measured flight-energy changes. Independently variable energy shifts times that coefficient only enlarge the admissible family. Use all sixteen sign orthants for the four offsets and rational interval certificates; in a compatible relaxation distinguish uncertainty from an explicit setting that makes all upper remainder bounds negative. A nonzero residual across sites is a statement on these finite cap instruments. A positive density at every physical location and the Einstein response require their own evidence.
