Give each time the same menu of twelve unit pointers: the four real and imaginary axes and the signed permutations of (3/5,4/5). On a separate sample read all thirty clock routes and all six signed material meters. Use 2^72 active starts per material setting. Compare the inferred ordinary-energy equivalents using the pre-existing independently measured ordinary controls. Also ask whether any complex overlap, of any nonzero strength, could simultaneously reproduce one positive ordinary-energy increment on these instruments. On each window the sparse change of every linear meter is a times its real quadrature plus b times its imaginary quadrature. If the target were k times the measured ordinary change for any k>0, every meter would satisfy (a/k) R+(b/k) I = O. Treat these two normalized coefficients as unbounded, split their signs, and allow each measured endpoint its entire interval. Test both impulses, all six material meters, the three charge-averaged material meters, the thirty returns and the full collection. Report exact separating inequalities whenever possible, and preserve compatible and unresolved cases. Extend the test to arbitrary common flight-time mixtures and separate pointer phases at the three times with the same positive ordinary amplitude. That extension permits arbitrary timing distributions on both sides as long as the same distribution sets the ordinary calibration; allowing independent signed coefficients even on absent times only enlarges the tested set. A failure elsewhere on the physical Einstein tower does not follow from these finite-chart tests.
