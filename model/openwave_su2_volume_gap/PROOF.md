# A volume-independent pure-gauge gap at fixed spacing

This is a sufficiently strong coupling result on periodic spatial lattices with
full Haar link spaces and continuous Hamiltonian time. The coupling threshold
is not evaluated. No spatial-continuum or Clay mass-gap conclusion follows.

## Exact local decomposition

Assign the three outgoing links at each site x to H_x=L²(SU(2)³,Haar). All
Peter–Weyl labels k=2j≥0 remain. On one link C² has eigenvalue k(k+2)/4 with
multiplicity (k+1)², a unique Haar-constant zero vector, and gap 3/4. The bulk
source is H=αΣ_e C_e²+βΣ_p(1−W_p), with α=g²/(2a), β=2/(g²a), and
W_p=Tr(U_p)/2. Its periodic boundary differs from the executed open-cube control.

For S={0,e₁,e₂,e₃}, set h_x=(4/3)Σ_(y∈x+S)Σ_i C²_(y,i). This classical local
operator has a unique product zero state and gap at least one. Every link
occurs in four stars, hence Σh_x=(16/3)ΣC². Every plaquette rooted at x uses only
links assigned to x+S, and its complete boundary has zero Gauss divergence.

Subtract the scalar 3βL³ and divide H by c=3α/16. The result is exactly
Σ_x(h_x+φ_x), where φ_x=−(16β/(3α))Σ_(i<j)W_(x,ij). Since |W_p|≤1,
||φ_x||≤16β/α=64/(g²)². Equality follows because the three loops all equal one
at the identity configuration, and continuity supplies neighborhoods of positive
Haar measure approaching that supremum. The norm has no volume or spacing
factor. No representation cutoff is used.

## Stability theorem and physical restriction

[Yarotsky, math-ph/0412040](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0412040), Theorem 1,
permits infinite-dimensional local spaces, periodic cubes, a classical local
product ground state and sufficiently small relatively bounded perturbations.
It supplies a unique gapped finite-volume ground state, a volume-independent
gap, a thermodynamic ground state and exponential clustering.

The exact map verifies those premises with relative bound zero and bounded
local norm 64/(g²)². Therefore a finite strong-coupling threshold exists. Above
it, multiply the theorem's normalized gap by 3g²/(32a) to obtain a positive
source gap independent of volume. Neither that threshold nor the normalized
gap is computed here. No displayed numeric coupling is thereby admitted.

Casimirs and closed traced loops commute with every local SU(2) transformation.
A unique ground transforms by a continuous one-dimensional representation of
each local SU(2), which must be trivial. Thus it lies in the Gauss subspace;
restriction cannot reduce its gap. Finite ground states, and hence their
thermodynamic state on local observables, are gauge invariant. The closed cyclic
space generated by gauge-invariant observables is invariant under time
evolution. For every local observable A in the local energy-form core, the
finite-volume inequality is
ω_L(A* [H_L,A])≥Δ(ω_L(A*A)−|ω_L(A)|²), with the same Δ>0.
Only finitely many interaction terms enter the commutator, so the local-state
limit preserves this inequality. Closure on the local form core gives the
gap of the Gelfand–Naimark–Segal Hamiltonian. Its restriction to the physical
cyclic space preserves the gap.

At finite volume, compact ellipticity and a bounded real potential give a
positivity-improving heat kernel and a strictly positive ground wavefunction.
Multiplying it by a nonconstant Wilson loop produces a nonzero centered vector.
The physical algebra has not been reduced to the vacuum projection. More
strongly, at zero perturbation the independent Haar state has ω(W)=0 and
ω(W²)=1/4 for a fundamental plaquette loop. The latter follows from the
SU(2) fundamental character orthogonality and W=χ_(1/2)/2. Theorem1(4)
gives analyticity, hence continuity, of these local expectations in the
bounded perturbation coefficient. Consequently, after decreasing the
existential strong-coupling interval if necessary, the infinite-volume
variance ω(W²)−ω(W)² is greater than1/8. Its centered vector is a nonzero
physical excitation. No numerical threshold for this interval is supplied.

## Time and spatial limits

The existing full-Haar transfer argument applies to this finite graph: electric
heat kernels have positive character coefficients, the magnetic multiplier is
bounded and positive, and symmetric transfer products are positive and Gauss
invariant. Trotter refinement reaches the same continuous-time Hamiltonian at
fixed spatial spacing. The uniform-volume gap is compatible with that limit.

For fixed g² the energy unit scales as 1/a. The smallness map controls a
strong-coupling region and neither supplies a weak-coupling renormalization
trajectory nor Euclidean rotational invariance. A physical finite mass, a GeV
scale, and the Clay spatial-continuum construction remain separate requirements.
