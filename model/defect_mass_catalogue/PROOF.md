# A common energy calibration and a complete target catalogue

A GeV calibration chooses one positive constant a for a specified source
and clock: E_GeV=a E_source. Every energy interval, including complementary
records when their energies are known, uses that same constant. Outcome
probabilities, their normalization and failure probabilities are unchanged.
This unit conversion supplies no particle dictionary or rest-mass theorem.

For positive source interval [x_-,x_+] and positive target interval
[y_-,y_+], the admissible scales are exactly

\[
 A=[y_-/x_+,\ y_+/x_-].
\]

A collection of interval anchors admits a common scale if and only if the
intersection of these intervals is nonempty, when the intervals specify an
independent Cartesian uncertainty set. This is a feasibility test, not a
likelihood or a confidence interval. Correlated feasible sets can only be
smaller than this box test; nonempty box intersections do not establish
feasibility for unknown correlations. The implementation uses exact rational
arithmetic and rejects transfers between unmatched source clocks. Signed
energy-transfer intervals are converted by all four endpoint products.

## Quark assignment test

The complete native quark receipt gives equal up-conjugate and
down-conjugate resolvents at equal primitive couplings. Its dominant
record-zero energy bins coincide near 0.781574 source units. The candidate
dictionary tested here assigns these two bins to the u and d parameters
of the fixed pure minimal-subtraction Standard Model calibration at the
same scale 91.1876 GeV. The two allowed scale intervals are disjoint.
No unit conversion makes this dictionary agree with both targets.

The target entries are frozen decimal calibration outputs; their joint
experimental uncertainty is not supplied or inferred. The source bin
intervals are certified finite-source intervals inherited from the bound
receipt. A running quark mass requires a renormalization map from the native
observable to the quoted scheme and scale. That map is absent. The test
therefore excludes a specified naive dictionary, without claiming that a
coloured finite-volume energy is a physical quark pole or excluding all
other constructions. Selecting different spectral lines as different
particles requires a source-derived dictionary. All native bins are retained
in the displayed u-anchored energy conversion; none is promoted to a species
by the conversion.

## All particle families

The target table contains nine charged fermions, three neutrino mass
eigenstates and the five boson types W, Z, Higgs, photon and gluon. W denotes
both charge signs; gluon denotes the eight colour directions. Antiparticles,
colour components and spin do not count as additional independently fitted
mass parameters. This is a Standard Model target inventory, not an assertion that the
native source has this particle spectrum. Gravitational radiation and a
possible spin-two quantum interpretation are recorded separately, with no
source mass assignment; they are outside the seventeen-type Standard Model
count.

are quadratic coefficients of the explicitly stated tree comparison; the
loop-matched vacuum is not automatically a stationary point of the tree
potential. Neither those coefficients nor a gauge null direction identify
measured poles of the interacting observer source.

The common defect source has global native currents and scalar/fermion
operators. A current label alone supplies no dynamical gauge boson. Its
complete carrier, scalar profiles and isolated spatial modes are retained
separately from the seventeen physical targets. The catalogue states null
physical mass entries wherever the propagation, family selection or
physical identification is unproved. This prevents a calibrated parameter
or a count of modes from silently closing the physical spectrum claim.
