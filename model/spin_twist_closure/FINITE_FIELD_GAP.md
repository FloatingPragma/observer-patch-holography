# A uniform finite-field gap and a native preparation path

This construction prepares the actual finite selected-field spin ground.
It uses an external proved gap theorem with its source hypotheses checked
explicitly, and the existing native coherent services. It does not assume
a gap or an effective state-preparation rate at zero field.

## The actual source meets the gap theorem

The integer producer `native_ordered_ground.source.matrix` is the Marshall
representation

\[
 H_B^{\rm M}={J\over4}\sum_{xy}
       (-X_xX_y-Y_xY_y+Z_xZ_y-I)-{B\over2}\sum_xX_x.
\]

Here J,B>0 and every listed bond joins opposite parts of the source graph.
The graph can be disconnected; open boxes and even periodic boxes are
allowed. Conjugating all spins by Hadamard gives

\[
 \widetilde H_B={J\over4}\sum_{xy}
       (X_xX_y-Y_xY_y-Z_xZ_y-I)-{B\over2}\sum_xZ_x.       \tag{1}
\]

In [Rayudu and Takahashi, Definition 1 and Theorem 11]
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10765v1), the nonconstant bond coefficients
are `w_z=J/4`, `w_x=J/4`, `w_y=-J/4`, with both cross coefficients zero.
Their required inequality holds at equality. Their field is `h=B/2`;
the scalar `-J|E|/4` is irrelevant to the gap. The theorem therefore gives
a unique full finite ground and

\[
                     g_B\ge B/8.                        \tag{2}
\]

This imports the mathematical theorem, not a numerical estimate. The
producer checks the complete integer conjugation of the actual source
matrices for one site, a bond, a path, a square and the eight-site cube.
The local identity (1) proves the substitution on every admitted graph.
The theorem controls the full spectrum rather than one visible channel.
Its proof obtains decay for every diagonal Pauli product, then rules out
a two-dimensional low-energy subspace using the complete diagonal algebra.

## A gapped source ramp with an explicit preparation error

In the physical spin frame use

\[
 H(s)=s H_J-B\sum_x\epsilon_x S_x^1,\qquad0\le s\le1.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

The same substitution applies with J replaced by sJ, including s=0.
Thus the entire path has gap at least g=B/8. At s=0 its ground is the
literal staggered X product. At s=1 it is the finite source ground
used in the observer construction. No eigenvector proposal or
ground-energy estimate is needed to choose this path.

Write e=|E| and D=Je. Since each shifted exchange bond is a negative
projector of norm J, `||H'(s)||<=D` and `H''(s)=0`. Apply
[Jansen, Ruskai and Seiler, Theorem 3, Eq. (6)]
(https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0603175v3) to the rank-one ground band.
For the actual Schrödinger evolution generated by H(t/T), its final pure
state has half trace distance to the target ground at most

\[
 \eta_{\rm ad}={1\over T}
            \left({2D\over g^2}+{7D^2\over g^3}\right).       \tag{4}
\]

The two endpoint terms each contribute D/g^2. The integral term is
7D^2/g^3 because the path is linear. All operators act on the full
finite spin space, so their domains and smoothness conditions hold.
This is a controlled preparation intervention; the ramp parameter is
the source time divided by T. It is not asserted to be unread repair.

## Complete implementation using the existing native gates

Partition [0,T] into M slices of width delta=T/M and freeze s at each
left endpoint. Duhamel's inequality gives operator error at most
`DT/(2M)` between this evolution and (3). In each slice apply every
exchange bond, followed by every onsite selection rotation. Set
`A=Je+Bn/2`, the sum of the term norms. The first-order unitary product
bound is `delta^2 sum_(u<v)||h_u||||h_v||<=delta^2 A^2/2`.
After M slices, both errors together are at most

\[
                  \eta_{\rm split}
                  ={DT+A^2T^2\over2M}.                  \tag{5}
\]

`preparation_word` emits this literal sequence lazily through the existing
`native_spin_observer.circuits` templates. Encoding costs five pulses per
site, the encoded Y rotation preparing +X costs eleven, and the odd-site
phase-exact Z costs two. Each bond evolution costs 62 pulses including
its scalar phase; each onsite X rotation costs eleven. Consequently

\[
 N\le16n+2n_{\rm odd}+M(62e+11n).                       \tag{6}
\]

The first slice has zero bond strength and skips those gates, so (6) is
an upper bound. The circuit uses the admitted native addressed-control
templates.
The single-occupancy spin code is preserved by every completed ideal
gate. Any optional repair must occur between complete words and retain
its records; no protection during intermediate pulses is assumed.

The existing dyadic port compiler gives complete isometry error at most
`29N 2^-b` for b bits of port precision. All first-failure records are
retained. The bound includes their probability, and no successful branch
is renormalized. Subsequent complete instruments and untouched references
preserve the bound by channel contraction. On the specified fresh blank
input, the total output half trace distance to the target ground plus
its ideal records is bounded by

\[
       \eta_{\rm ad}+\eta_{\rm split}+29N2^{-b}.          \tag{7}
\]

The exact rational resource planner allocates at most one third of the
requested error to each term. For e>0 it takes
`T=3(2D/g^2+7D^2/g^3)/error`, chooses the integer M from (5), and then
chooses b from (6). For e=0 the target is the prepared product
and no ramp is required. For fixed B and bounded-degree graphs this
resource bound is polynomial in the graph size and inverse requested
error; the inverse-field dependence is explicit. The large certified
tapes are specified by the lazy compiler, not claimed to have been
executed in the finite host witness.

## The subsequent field limit

Equation (2) holds at each strictly positive B. The gap estimate vanishes
as B decreases. It does not supply a rate relating the finite ground to
the selected infinite zero-field marginal at growing supports and times.
The required order remains volume first, selection field second, and
the continuum scaling third. A finite-volume adiabatic preparation error
cannot replace either of the latter state limits. Nor does a gap at
positive B imply a sharp zero-field dispersion, physical particle mass,
Lorentz covariance or a scattering identification.
