## Are federations necessary?

**No federation is needed for the present local derivations.** One twelve-port
carrier supports overlapping observer regions, the C/E instruments,
conditional histories, local clocks, response geometry, coherence tests and
repair-derived actions. Agreement between regions can be tested inside this
carrier. A proof can quantify over such regions without implementing a
network of larger observers.

A federation becomes useful when a claim concerns larger operational regions,
inter-carrier interventions, scalability, frame compatibility or refinement.
For an extended effective spacetime, some construction must support the
claimed observation domain with controlled approximation errors. A theorem
about an abstract family can provide this if the actual source satisfies its
hypotheses. The present spherical federation is one candidate family; it is
not a prerequisite for every geometry, action or clock theorem.

The [federation evidence](federated_observer/README.md) is valuable as a test
of composition and coarse memory. It also exposes the particular family's
limitations: routing is supplied, coarse blocks retain memory, and existing
ancestry maps do not preserve every seam. The approximately quadratic growth
of native-hop balls on its levels over part of the measured range, retained by
the [relative observer geometry](relative_observer_geometry/README.md), does
not establish a three-dimensional bulk. Building larger copies before
specifying the needed observable correspondence would leave the physical
question unchanged.

The separate [OL-A1 signature replication instrument](../code/instrument_specs/OL_A1_SIGNATURE_REPLICATION_SPEC.md)
also remains `FAILED`. Its threshold-sensitive inertia and nondiscriminating
ancestry control do not establish physical signature. That result is not
overturned by successful finite native-carrier calculations or by this audit.
