# Particle criteria and the actual finite charged edge

The [original-weight threshold theorem](../../../spin_shell_readout/THRESHOLD_READOUT.md)
allows a spectral boundary to be measured without assuming an isolated
one-particle pole. This package supplies its tail premise for the actual
charged response of the [open two-cell source](../two_cell/READOUT_PROOF.md).
It reuses that theorem and its non-atomic control; it does not introduce
a different source or a guessed continuum mass.

## A finite source instance of the edge theorem

Let nu be the subprobability energy-transfer measure of successful
preparation and the chosen addition or removal branch, on the original
attempt law. Its complementary outcome includes every other preparation
and apparatus record. The complete constrained spectrum and exact probe
selection rule exclude energies below the first reachable energy window.
That window has certified spectral rank one and strictly positive original
probability lower bound a. Consequently its exact energy theta is the
lower support edge and, for every 0<epsilon<=1,

    nu([theta,theta+epsilon]) >= a >= a epsilon.

The source supplies the threshold theorem's amplitude a, power 1 and
radius 1. The original probability is the certified conditional spectral
weight multiplied by the ground preparation probability 1/1053. A
conditional success weight cannot replace a. The complement is retained.

If [l,u] is the first certified energy enclosure, the cumulative
probability below l-epsilon is exactly zero, and the cumulative probability
at u is at least a. Feeding these bounds into the existing theorem gives

    l-2 epsilon < theta <= u.

The direct finite eigensystem has the tighter enclosure [l,u]. The wider
bracket verifies the reusable threshold interface with its actual source
premises. No observed zero count is used to exclude hidden lower weight.
A cluster of two unresolved energies cannot use its combined weight as
the weight of the lowest atom; the executable rejects that substitution.

This establishes a finite original-weight threshold for the given source,
state, reference and clock. It establishes no tail modulus uniform in
volume, link cutoff or scalar cutoff. The stationary periodic source has
a different Hamiltonian and requires a separate response calculation.

## Different physical sectors need different tests

An isolated stable branch and controlled nonzero residue are useful
criteria for a gapped stable particle. They are not a universal definition
of every particle appearing in physical measurements. Under the stated
Gauss-law hypotheses, an electrically charged sector can lack a sharp
mass eigenstate: [Buchholz, Physics Letters B 174 (1986), 331-334](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(86)91110-X).
The existing OPH threshold theorem explicitly preserves this possibility.
It does not prove that the source charge is electromagnetic charge or
that the source satisfies those continuum hypotheses.

The practical targets are sector-dependent:

| Sector being claimed | Observable and physical attachment required |
|---|---|
| Stable gapped excitation | A persistent energy-momentum branch, controlled field residue, current and transformation identification |
| Charged sector with long-range radiation | A controlled spectral boundary and dressed or inclusive records, with the infrared resolution and charge reference retained |
| Unstable excitation | A controlled resonance or finite-time decay response, including its width and preparation/detector dependence |
| Confined color field | Gauge-invariant color-singlet records and an explicit renormalized parameter convention, rather than a freely isolated colored detector particle |

The distinction between resonance poles and real spectral atoms is
described in the [PDG resonance review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2021/reviews/rpp2021-rev-resonances.pdf).
The [PDG quark-mass review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-quark-masses.pdf)
specifies the renormalization scheme and scale needed to compare quark
mass parameters. A single table of unlabeled real energy gaps would not
implement all these physical mass definitions.

Infrared dressing is a dynamical construction, not the act of attaching
an arbitrary ancilla. [Kulish and Faddeev](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/tmf4141)
alter the asymptotic state space and dynamics to treat long-range
electromagnetism. [Hirai and Sugishita](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11716)
show why gauge and asymptotic-charge compatibility impose further dressing
conditions. The present finite compensator implements local Gauss
constraints; it is not a proof of either continuum dressing construction.

The existing exact non-atomic control has an edge with no atom and
vanishing near-edge record weight as resolution is refined. Its edge
brackets nevertheless converge. It is an analytic acceptance control,
not a source output. Requiring a fixed positive isolated-atom weight for
every charged candidate would reject that allowed threshold mechanism.

The charged channels in the computed two-cell source have finite positive
atoms. Their field identity, relativistic joint energy-momentum relation,
continuum infrared character and observed species remain separate tests.
The correct acceptance route depends on those source results; it is not
selected by the particle name one hopes to assign.
