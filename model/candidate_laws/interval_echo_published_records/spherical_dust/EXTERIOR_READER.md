# Two exterior measurements of the same gravitating count

The spherical candidate assigns a carrier speed \(v(r)=-\sqrt{2\mu/r}\) to the areal-radius labels in the observer survey. A clock with rest mass \(m\) and radial momentum \(p\) follows the local action \(H=vp+\sqrt{m^2+p^2}\). This experiment asks whether pulse observers and released clocks infer the same \(\mu\) on copies of the same posted source. The two held clocks use radii 2 and 4. The inner clock emits twice, one proper-time unit apart, with different pulse momenta. The outer clock records the reception dates and reflects the first pulse at fixed local frequency. The inner clock dates its return. Hamilton's equations give \(dr/dt=v+\operatorname{sgn}p\) for light and \(dp/dt=vp/(2r)\). For a clock held at fixed radius, \(v+p/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}=0\) and \(d\tau/dt=m/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}\). The ratio \(z\) of emitted to received pulse spacings yields \(\mu=(1-z^2)/[2(1/r_{\rm in}-z^2/r_{\rm out})]\). Reading the propagated wave's local frequency gives an independent check within \(1.3\times10^{-10}\) of the pulse-spacing ratio.

For free release, the inner observer removes the holding force. Probes of masses 1 and 3 report their radii at their own clock appointments. The two shortest appointments have \(\tau=1/16,1/32\). Combining the measured \(2[r(\tau)-2]/\tau^2\) at the two times gives the initial acceleration to fourth order in the time interval. Each mass reproduces all four short-arc radius records for the ordinary and held sources. The ordinary records calibrate \(G=0.2499999999998\) by clock spacing and \(0.249999999884\) by free release. The posted counts \(1/3+8/33\) then predict \(\mu=19/132\) to the displayed precision of each calibration. [Short-arc radius records](results.json)

## Transferring the calibrated count to orbital records

The ratio of the joint to ordinary gravitating inventory in this sphere is \(19/11\). A galactic test of this ratio requires a spatial law for the joint count. If it follows the three visible tracer forces with constant nonnegative component gains, the outer squared speed of a two-ring system with positive tracers cannot exceed \(V_{\mathrm{in}}^2\max_j(S_{j,\mathrm{out}}/S_{j,\mathrm{in}})\), where \(S_j\) is the inward squared-speed contribution of tracer \(j\). This follows by writing the ratio of the two summed forces in squared-speed units as a weighted mean of the individual ratios with weights fixed by the inner ring.

In NGC 1003 the bulge contributes zero at the two tested radii, and both gas and disk contributions decline between 14.49 and 26.08 kpc. Their largest squared-speed ratio is 0.584857. Letting each speed range over five quoted errors and a further 10 km/s gives an outer speed of at most 86.95 km/s under the tracer law, against an observed lower bound of 88.00 km/s. Across 136 selected galaxies, separate arbitrary nonnegative gas, disk and bulge gains conflict with the orbital records in 23 galaxies at five quoted errors and seven with an extra 5 km/s per ring. The extra allowance describes a deterministic error box. These comparisons test transferring a native count to the visible radial templates. Identifying a distinct gravitating profile requires an independent regional source census and the same coupling to clocks, free trajectories and light.
