### Did the observed expansion ever accelerate?

A flat expanding FLRW geometry relates the transverse and radial acoustic distances without specifying matter, vacuum energy, or a numerical acoustic ruler. Write $x=\log(1+z)$, $d=D_M/r_d$ and $u=d'(x)=(1+z)D_H/r_d$ for a common comoving ruler. The deceleration parameter is $q=-d\log u/dx$. If the expansion never accelerated from the present back to a given redshift, $u$ is nonincreasing over that interval. Consequently the mean slope from the origin and both endpoints of every transverse-distance chord bound later or earlier radial measurements. A constant $u$ corresponds to coasting, which saturates all the bounds.

The nearby isotropic BAO distance gives an additional bound. For $z_a\le z_b$, both $d(x_a)\ge x_a u(x_b)$ and $u(x_a)\ge u(x_b)$. Substituting them into $(D_V/r_d)^3=z_a d(x_a)^2u(x_a)/(1+z_a)$ proves

$$
\left[\frac{1+z_a}{z_a\log^2(1+z_a)}\right]^{1/3}\frac{D_V(z_a)}{r_d}\ \ge\ (1+z_b)\frac{D_H(z_b)}{r_d}.
$$

At $z_a=0.295$ the left side is 32.04368, while the radial measurement at $z_b=0.934$ gives 34.11865. The difference is $-2.07497\pm0.49542$ using the DESI Gaussian covariance. The one-sided Bonferroni bound for a deviation this large anywhere in this family is $p\le0.001012$. Under the flat metric and constant-ruler interpretation, this favors some acceleration between today and $z=0.934$. This retrospective family bound assumes the released Gaussian distance likelihood; it does not distinguish a positive vacuum term from other expansion histories with acceleration.
