# Can the volume measured by one observer stay constant?

## Measurement

Use the eight existing 20-cell dense preparations with their existing repair schedules. This follow-up has access to the earlier range results. Supply one distinguishable reflector per port and enroll the source at port zero on every board. At each of the source clock ticks 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 52 and 56, measure whether each tag can go out and its reflection come back within twelve ticks. Record all 240 results, including the source's own marker with zero delay. Use separate matched preparations for different targets. A port reflects on first arrival during ongoing local repairs. The source reads its own send and first return. Missing returns have their own outcome. One responding marker is the volume standard on this detector. Repeat with the same records and a changed local transfer law that passes both written signs. Keep a settled, continuously signalling run: as a single query ages, report the first return of every distinct address and the cumulative count every two ticks until saturation. Apply the cube root only as a proposed three-dimensional effective scale. Inspect every pair of successive shell increments, including ties and reversals. These are retrospective native survey predictions on finite boards under the supplied protocol.

The marker census and the proper-volume and count-density constructions use different physical standards. A claim of universal acceleration must specify which quantity and which class of observers its quantifier covers.

A second follow-up holds four material markers fixed. Enroll every triple of distinct targets in the source cell in addition to origin zero. On the blank reference select the lexicographically first tetrahedron with a positive Euclidean volume. The terminal graph and existing clocks were available in selecting this follow-up. Keep all 165 triples and all geometrically inconsistent or degenerate records. The sender times an echo to each target and two triangle tours through each pair in opposite orders. A stationary reciprocal distance assignment must give equal tour durations in both orders. Infer the intertarget distance from the triangle duration and the two measured source-to-target ranges. Reconstruct the tetrahedral Gram determinant and keep its sign. A passing tetrahedron supplies a local Euclidean interpretation of those six ranges only.

## Results

On preparation 280210 the source times its echoes to ports 1, 4 and 6 at two, four and four ticks, respectively. Triangle trips through (1,4), (1,6) and (4,6) take five, five and six ticks in either direction. These six recorded durations recur for all ten emissions. In relay lengths the six reconstructed distances are (1,2,2,2,2,2), in the order of the three source links followed by the three opposite sides. In a Euclidean reconstruction relative to the source, the Gram matrix of the three radius vectors has diagonal (1,4,4) and off-diagonal (1/2,1/2,2). Its determinant is 11. The tetrahedral volume is **√11/6**. The same four fixed markers give this value at every enrolled epoch on six of the eight boards. The clock hand and the reflected messages continue throughout. Each clock toggle is an actual π transfer on an independently held, blank native dimer. The controller supplies one such toggle per relay round. The count bank retains all complementary outcomes of the native local transfer calibration.

On 280217 the same stationary marker quartet first gives √11/6, then gives unequal durations in opposite tour orders, then gives **2√2/3** in each later survey. On 280215 the first comparison is unresolved and every subsequent reading gives 2√2/3. The test can detect a change and it can fail to assign any volume at all. Only 10 of the 165 quartets on 280210's first survey admit strictly positive Euclidean tetrahedra; 20 are degenerate and 135 are incompatible with Euclidean distance. The six-distance fit establishes the displayed four-point volume within the observer's declared Euclidean ruler convention. Larger neighbourhoods and a continuum proper-volume interpretation require independent geometric evidence.

The same source on 280210 receives **31, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24** marker IDs in the successive twelve-tick windows. On 280214 it receives **12** each time, while 94 native writes occur on that board. The comparison that permits transmission across the written signs gives **77** per window on every preparation. Under the native transmission rule, later starts can only lose available routes. Any time-ordered outbound and return path valid for a later emission can be traversed by the earlier signal at the same relative offsets, since each edge was also available earlier. The finite-timeout count is therefore nonincreasing under this strict-write service on any finite board. The scope is an accessible, fixed-duration survey with an enrolled one-marker standard.

There is also an example in which the measured region **grows**. In the settled world of 280210 one origin waits progressively longer after launching its queries. At waits 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 ticks the number of distinct responding markers is **1, 5, 12, 18, 23, 24, 24**. Through waits 2, 4 and 6 the volume increases while its second difference is −1. Through waits 0, 2 and 4 the second difference of the count is +3; the second difference of its cube root is approximately −0.131. More time for signals to travel enlarges the observed region even in this stationary medium. In a three-dimensional homogeneous interpretation with a *fixed comoving region*, V=V₀a³ would instead give a″/a=V″/(3V)−2(V′/V)²/9. Even a positive V″ would not by itself establish positive a″. The expanding survey boundary here does not track a fixed comoving region.

## Why this repair service stops

On the fixed sharp host each successful 02→11 or 20→11 commit removes one vacancy and one double. The smaller of their two initial counts bounds the number of commits by at most half the ports. If any ready edge remains, a lowest-priority-number ready edge is enabled. Under the supplied independent per-window attempt probability p>0 the chance of at least one commit at a nonterminal window is at least p. The expected number of windows to settle is at most min(initial vacancies, initial doubles)/p, and settlement occurs almost surely. Random waiting times can be arbitrarily long. This bound assumes a fixed population, strict irreversible writes, continuing service at this cadence and no fresh sources. In the eight histories the 81–95 accepted writes finished by ticks 22–35. On a settled time-independent reciprocal network a reflected tag returns after twice its shortest open path length. Further starts on this clock still produce signals and identical ranges. These conclusions concern this source and scheduler. They do not bound a capacity-changing construction or the size observers might attribute to a continuum universe.
