# A threshold test across signed inventories

The 12-port signed-pair experiment found an all-excess fragment and an all-vacancy fragment cheaper than its putative bound source. Test whether the competition holds for arbitrary inventories and graphs on the same native blank-contact material. Embed a sharp signed word into two equal boards, one carrying its excesses and the other its vacancies. Keep all edges, private references and the host energy convention fixed. Compress the sum of the two separate-board generators back to the joint inventory. Check, entry by entry on all nine two-port sharp inputs, whether their difference is supported only on unlike adjacent defects and equals a scalar plus their exchange parity. If it fails on any entry, drop the proposed universal bound. Determine which native comparison strengths admit a lower energy with joined matter, and distinguish an inequality relative to the embedded input from one relative to the lowest separated energy.

Enroll independently prepared signed stocks on the OW-14/16 12-port cell with its 30 active blank seams and nine surviving private references, exterior parked. At ports 0 and 1 start with an excess and a vacancy; add the remaining excesses on the lowest unused ports, then the remaining vacancies likewise. Enroll inventories (1,1), (2,1), (2,2), (4,3). Also prepare the two real swap-parity superpositions of the original and 0–1 swapped words by an isolated calibrated half swap and a port-0 private phase. On two separate cells prepare the same marginal stock mixtures (the partner word carries a distinguishing reversed address). Each trial reads one appointed local ordinary energy on the joined or either separate cell. Record every terminal energy including complements, using 2^20 independent simulated starts per term, with separate original-start counts for every bank. Repeat the native strength 8, half contrast 4 and weakened contrast 1/4 with identical stock and analyzer: for the single unlike pair the proposed same-state comparison predicts a sign change on the symmetric branch below 1/2. The expectation difference, prior to looking at results, must agree within simultaneous 99% counting bounds with the prediction from a separate census of adjacent unlike sites and parity at ports 0–1. Compare all 36 signed inventories/stocks/strength settings, including the sharp-launch value. For the actual competing separated ground use the finite variational principle. A lower matched-state energy need not beat the independent fragment grounds.

The nominal masses, identities and observed nuclear binding targets play no role in selecting these stock words. This is a deliberate follow-up to the earlier segregation example and tests only its open blank-contact domain.
