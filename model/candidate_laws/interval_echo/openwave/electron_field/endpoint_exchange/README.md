# Do two charges with field endpoints have a common exchange sign?

Two vacancies exchange on opposite sides of a physical six-port loop. Each carries a proposed field pattern, with one end of a parity string on a face touching each charge. After every hop the two occupied endpoint faces can be kept vertex-disjoint. Among 2,818 such settings with measurable energy on both ends at every read, the amplitude for the measured exchange is negative in 1,466 and positive in 1,352. The largest probability of completing all six reads with negative amplitude is 0.0016564%. This is a phase-sensitive, filtered exchange of material charges. A universal fermionic sign for a freely bound excitation would require a preparation whose exchange retains that sign when the apparatus phases are controlled. One comparison fixes the two source vacancies at ports 11 and 8, the two initial field endpoints at faces 2 and 7, the final detector and all six material pulses. The two charges pass through ports 10, 9, 7 and 4. Changing only the face read when a vacancy occupies port 9 from face 15 to face 5 changes the exchange amplitude from −0.00223949 to +0.00296045. The corresponding probabilities of completing the six successful joint reads are 0.0005015% and 0.0008764%. The endpoints remain distinct and share no vertex at every read. Across the geometry and direction choices, 80 fixed-source cases admit both signs. Holding the initial field endpoints parked can itself give a negative exchange amplitude when the material paths enclose one end. ## Sources and controls

The word on the thirty seams, all twenty physical triangles, the material pulse and the field-energy read come from the one-cell winding experiment. Each signed source has a separate frozen compensator. At fixed material occupancy the equal-amplitude field is multiplied by the parity of the heads along a dual path joining the two selected faces. Ring energy is positive at both endpoints for every admitted read and zero on other faces in that template. The uniform field and a field with endpoints held at their source addresses are measured with the same material pulses. A detector that projects on a moving field template requires coherent access to the whole cell. The probability of every projection failure is included through the unconditional success probability. The two-source readout supplies no autonomous field transport. [One-cell field preparation](../flux_string/README.md)

The perimeter scan covers both material signs, six starts, two directions and all five incident-face choices at each of six sites, giving 375,000 settings before eligibility checks. There were 9,060 eligible settings, of which 2,818 met the vertex-disjoint condition. All eligible settings on this fixed background had the vacancy sign. Reversing both the material sign and every reference head reproduces the closed-exchange signs and probabilities. The geometry and reads restrict this result to the stated preparation.

On a three-arm junction the two charges exchange through a spare arm. Across 22,500 enumerated choices, 8,733 have distinct, active endpoints at all six steps. The exchange amplitude is negative in 4,729, positive in 3,969, and zero in 35. The six physical material transfers alone restore every head bit on every successful input with amplitude +1. Keeping the endpoint faces stationary gives a positive product of the projected transfer amplitudes. The moving-face filter distinguishes signs on this junction even though the parked charge and field reference has a positive exchange.
