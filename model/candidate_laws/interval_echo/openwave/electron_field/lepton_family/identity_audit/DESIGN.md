# Distinguishing the three guided charges

Freeze the twelve-port, seven-cell degree-six path, head words, external compensator, incident-edge grade assignment, unit comparison mass coupling and register effect of the incidence experiment. Prepare each of the three uniform positive rest states separately. Keep the register label at the sender. The receiving cell copies one binary answer to a fresh pointer at model times 1, 2, 4, 8 and 12. A one means the region contains the signed unit. Every zero acts by the complementary projector and remains part of the record. Born sampling, the observation schedule and the ideal projective apparatus are supplied. Examine the entire word distribution and reproducible finite sampled records at equal prior weights for the three sources. Do the same from a sharp charge at the first port to test the effect of a different source. Use both signs and check the detector against a separate count of inward written heads.

Compare with three spinless orbital branches obtained by reducing the same generator in the Fourier basis. Test the coherent spin-spectator extension by checking the operator factor on all branches. Check the positive rest multiplicities, the first nonuniform level and the commutators of all five instrument stages with the three character projectors. As a sensitivity control, turn on a distal address-phase kick cycling the characters and read out the daughter on the same charged cell. As a changed law, give all three register characters the mean effect, preserving the same graph, charge, cyclic symmetry and receiver. The detector’s ability to distinguish branches, the eligibility of a resting spin doublet and evidence for a spontaneous heavy-to-light transition are separate questions.
