# Threefold orientation of a single dressed charge

The geometric question is whether a proper threefold cell rotation provides a cyclic mixing operator and unequal level energies for a single mobile signed charge in the archived written-field extension. No measured lepton energies, Koide parameters or fitted coupling enter the experiment.

Inspect the 20-, 80- and 320-cell native federations in ascending order and cells in label order. Use the first cell whose physical twelve-port degrees admit a proper rotation of order three. Among eligible generators use the lexicographically first port permutation. For the broken-degree control use the first cell whose degrees admit only the identity proper rotation.

For each charge sign, start with the registered sharp field and charge anchor. Rotate the charge port and all thirty oriented field heads together to obtain two further sharp sources. Enumerate the original accessible charged component using the archived constructor. Rotate every configuration in it to give proposed copies. Independently reconstruct every transition of all copies using the material and face rules, including the actual selected cell's degree potential. Count cross-copy moves and destinations outside the proposed union. Check the full Gauss vector and pairwise intersections. Compare integer Hamiltonian blocks in corresponding rotated bases, the commutator with the actual rotation, and the associated three Fourier character restrictions. Measure their common lowest energy if the whole blocks coincide. A transition between distinct orientations, a mismatch of integer blocks, or unequal character energies would change the conclusion. Compute the dimensionless balance of the three positive energies only after the physical test.

Repeat with the same sharp configurations, rotation and contact rules in the actual control cell's degree potential. Report whether the orientations continue to be conserved, the full rotation still commutes with the Hamiltonian and the energy of each oriented component.
