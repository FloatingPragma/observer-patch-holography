The geometric constraint removes one of the two independent ratios. It supplies a rejection test without any energy calibration. The exclusive read on the specified 432-state register also gives a phase of \(2/9\). Its squared character amplitudes give **1 : 206.77031597 : 3477.47283710**. The first ratio exceeds the register's frozen direct-mass comparison by 9.83 ppm, or 442 quoted experimental standard uncertainties. The 432-state register, its cycle, and the physical reading of its effect as a mass are distinct inputs to a proposed identification. A separate conditional calculation uses measured electron and muon masses plus ordering to give a tau mass of 1776.969027 MeV. ## Coupling a cyclic label to the moving charge

Two concrete implementations test the proposed connection to the lepton register. First permit coherent superpositions of a fully written charged cell and its two properly rotated copies. With a degree-preserving threefold rotation these copies have different conserved integer head-count backgrounds. Every allowed hop and ring flip stays within its original background. The three Fourier characters of the rotation have identical Hamiltonians and give **1 : 1 : 1**, or \(Q=1/3\), for every intrinsic level. Breaking the degree symmetry splits the orientation energies but also removes the rotation characters as stationary labels. [Complete native transition test and changed-cell control](cyclic_charge/README.md)

Alternatively, supply a three-state label on the 48-cell route and allow every forward contact to advance it by an arbitrary cyclic shift, with its inverse on the return hop. After local relabellings only the total shift around the route matters. The three possible totals exhaust every arrangement of these couplings. Two totals give lowest thresholds in the ratio **1 : 1.00041112 : 1.00041112**, and one gives exact equality. The splitting vanishes as the inverse square of the ring length. Adding a separately balanced register energy to the same positive native formation energy instead gives \(1/3<Q<2/3\) at every finite positive coupling. In both constructions the character is conserved. A viable combined excitation needs a field interaction which splits its internal states in free flight and independently accounts for conversion and spin.
