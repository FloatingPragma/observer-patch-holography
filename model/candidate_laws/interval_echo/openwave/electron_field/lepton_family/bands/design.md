# Three-port guide search design

Use the longest archived simple three-port ring on the 320-cell carrier. Verify the listed seams and their degrees against the independent carrier tables. Use the written-field extension with positive contact Laplacian, its existing load-one neutral reference and its fixed contact and local port energies. The guide is a preparation of active seams. Its empty state supplies the energy zero.

Enumerate every asymptotic one-end Bloch band in energy order at each cell momentum, for both load signs. Record each band's minimum over the full zone, its gap at zero cell momentum, the curvature at the minimum, its whole-zone width and the ratios in both conventions. Select no band or momentum by an observed particle mass. State the units. Use the three-port homogeneous hopping chain with its original cell folding as a geometric control.

Prepare one localized pulse for every band with both momentum directions, using the three amplitudes on the route's physical ports at the central momentum. Also test bandwidth-resolved eigenpackets, whose independent group velocity can be predicted from dispersion. The other charge starts half a ring away in a stationary low-band wavepacket. Run the full ordered two-end Hamiltonian with the intervening seam string through times zero, two and four. Measure the launch and exit band weights, finiteness of encounter probability and each end's inclusive signed cell displacement. Repeat with both charge signs as projectile, using exchange symmetry verified on the Hamiltonian, and close the intercell seams as a cell-transport control. These wavefunctions specify ideal coherent initial conditions. Autonomous formation from a neutral vacuum requires a separate experiment.

Bring a middle or upper mode toward a low-band spectator from ten cells away. Sample the outgoing reduced band populations, encounters, norm and total energy against a matched run launched apart, over twelve time units. Compute the isolated-end off-band matrix elements over the whole momentum grid. Distinguish autonomous decay of a separated parent from population transfer in a two-end encounter.
