## Distinct fan records on paths

Use one retained four-face written fan per cell and the native material bridges. A fan contains its hub and the five rim addresses, with one rim bond of the full pentagon omitted. The geometry census checks every cell of each 20-, 80- and 320-cell federation. Every cell has three external contacts and at most one adjacent pair of external ports. A fan centered at an external port can therefore enroll at most two bridges. A cell with two such bridges uses its unique adjacent pair. The graph connecting those available pairs is a forest on each board. A fan with only one bridge can add a leaf. On the 320-cell board the transit graph has 68 links, with twelve five-cell paths, eight three-cell paths, four two-cell paths and twelve isolated cells.

Start with one charge per occupied fan and define the guarded sector by requiring the two charges to occupy different fans. This is a necessary condition for the two established local readers to register independent whole cores at every stage. It still allows all off-hub positions within each fan. In a path ordered from one end to the other, a hop from one fan to its next neighbor cannot invert the material origins while the guard holds. Ring flips and local material moves do not change the cell ordering. Hence an exchange amplitude on this sector vanishes at every exposure. This argument assumes the guard throughout flight. A start and end coincidence read by itself admits paths that temporarily put both charges into one fan.

For the hard-core Pauli comparison, assign the usual scalar sign to a hop that crosses the other particle in the numerical ordering of ports. Rephase each configuration by the sign that relates this numerical order to the order of its occupied fans along the path. The relative sign of each guarded hop is then the product of the rephases at its endpoints. The signed generator is a diagonal conjugate of the native one, including all position potentials and head dynamics supported there. The replay checks the identity on 672 and 1,368 directed guarded moves on the actual three- and four-fan 320-cell paths. ## Bridge records on cycles

The 80- and 320-cell boards have no four-face fan around **any** hub that can enroll all three bridges of its cell. Even moving the fan hub to an ordinary port gives degree at most two in a fixed one-fan-per-cell network. Cycles then require a separate check.

For a cycle of $L$ fans, lift the two ordered positions to the infinite sequence of fan indices. With distinct occupied fans, continuous tracking through single hops keeps $x<y<x+L$. For a return to the initial pair of sites without exchange, the sum of displacements is an even multiple of $L$. For a crossed return the end lift is $(y_0+kL,\ x_0+(k+1)L)$, and the sum is an odd multiple. Since the occupation of every site returns, continuity makes the signed net crossing count equal on every bridge. Its parity is therefore even for the first record and odd for the second. Native material moves flip the crossed intercell head, and the local ring terms cannot do so. Starting from a sharp bridge word, the two kinds of return end with disjoint bridge words.

Observers who require the original bridge record reject the crossed branch exactly. Observers who discard the bridge bits cannot interfere the two returns. This follows from the vanishing cross term between orthogonal bridge records. A phase comparison that reads a coherent superposition of the two bridge words, or changes their initial preparation, falls outside this test.

At fixed cell charges, independent local Gauss-preserving measurements cannot erase that distinction. The complete control compares bare bridge reads with a supplied coherent read of the physical circuit including its internal heads.
