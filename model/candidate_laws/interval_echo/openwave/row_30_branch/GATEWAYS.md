# Gateway geometry and the two energy groups

The first full-generator solve returned two separated groups within the lowest 20 energies. This follow-up is exploratory. Test whether the three gateway locations in a cell distinguish those groups. The 20-cell fixture contains two geometries distinguished by the three pairwise graph distances between its gateway ports inside the isolated cell. These distances use only incidence.

Project the uniform internal-seam orbital at each cell into the low band. Orthonormalize the projected cell orbitals by the positive inverse square root of their overlap matrix. Report the effective Hamiltonian in those coordinates with only its common energy removed. This is an exact change of basis inside the computed band. For each gateway geometry compute the diagonal range and its overlap with the lower and upper clusters. Split the clusters at the largest consecutive energy gap in the 20-state band. Retain every off-diagonal matrix element. A strong geometry association means that the two diagonal ranges are disjoint and at least 95% of each local orbital belongs to the cluster its range predicts. These are numerical spectral diagnostics. The terminal pair detector accepts adjacent loads regardless of the branch.
