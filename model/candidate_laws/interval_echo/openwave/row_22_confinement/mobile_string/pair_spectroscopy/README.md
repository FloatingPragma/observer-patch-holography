# Two signed wall excitations and a tagged source for a conjugate colour pair

OpenWave lists M10 as untested for this row. Its simplest test asks for pion and kaon states and a strange decay. The two-family construction listed in the upstream cell calibrates the charged kaon and Lambda and gives no decay amplitude. On Interval Echo we tested two concrete preparations. A static mobile strand produces two bound bands with the same signed constituents. Independently calibrated conjugate orbital colours on a different stock discriminate a coherent singlet from a classical mixture. A local memory mark releases a pair under an inverse write, but none of its unassisted flight enters that colour sector. The **physical OW-18 row stays Open**. The material state of each dimer is its own orientation, and an observer locates an upward or downward wall by comparing neighboring rail readings. ## A local stored-pair label and an independent singlet detector

The previous colour apparatus supplies three independent compact orbital modes for a vacancy and three conjugately tested modes for an excess. Each triplet is supported on non-gateway ports of its own owner. The two-owner product space has ordinary excitation energy 102 with all native contacts active. Its single-particle energies are 35 and 67. Here the two stations use owners 5 and 6 of the 80-owner federation. Four distinct pairs of conjugate local colour bases distinguish coherence within the joint sector. All nine joint colour outcomes and the common refusal enter each setting. The observer recovers singlet fractions 1 on the prepared singlet, 0.33338 on the incoherent mixture of matching colours and 0 when one reader alone undergoes a cyclic colour permutation. [Original compact triplets](../../../quark_winding/belt_walls/orbital_singlet.py) · [Local gates and joint colour receiver](colour.py) · [Detector tapes](colour.json)

A write on an oriented 02 puts both material ports at one and records signed contrast −2 in its memory. With no write, ordinary 11 gives the same two material readings and a blank memory. Reversing the write releases 02 with certainty only on the tagged preparation. A passive native contact preserves the blank/written projection at its own seam. All other contacts act on different memories. The recorded mark therefore cannot fade to a clean sector under passive full-graph evolution. Even after an addressed inverse write releases the pair, its overlap with each of the nine calibrated colour products is zero for **every one of the 5,040 possible oriented single seams** on the board. Within an owner both defects would occupy the same owner. Between owners both would occupy gateway ports on which the orbitals vanish. Since the complete colour space is stationary, the overlap stays zero for all time. [Full-graph derivation](PROOF.md#what-a-local-tag-can-release)

In a positive control five addressed swaps route the released excess to port 80 while the vacancy stays at port 64. The two receivers give a singlet fraction **0.046956 ± 0.000652** at release, 0.046782 at exposure one and 0.046853 at exposure sixteen. The calculated fraction is 3/64. Routing only as far as the receiving gateway gives zero. These are complete measured fractions of an initially sharp ordered pair, and the positive control supplies the route. Separately attaching the contrast-two mark on ports 0 and 1 to the *same prepared singlet* leaves its colour fraction at one and its free energy at 102. Reading the remote tag distinguishes the copies at identical local loads 11. The inverse write on the marked copy gives an additional material 02 while the colour fraction stays one. The conserved vacancy count plus its compensating written mark permits this conversion. A final state that omits the compensator has a different inventory. Hence this label and the measured singlet coexist, but the label acts as a stored pair on this test. Autonomous emission into this colour sector and a flavour-selective strange decay remain unobserved.
