# Can the distant owner recover an unknown polarization?

On an intact 80-cell federation an observer launches one unit with one of six transverse dipole polarizations. A second observer three owner steps away can receive the polarization as a coherent qubit. At 64 exchange exposures the measured average fidelity among arrivals in the transverse plane is **0.770216**, with **1.6354%** of original launches accepted. A source that measures the input and sends a newly prepared qubit can reach at most 2/3 under this protocol, even if its success probability varies with the input. A phase-randomized control gives **0.661590** on almost the same accepted fraction. [Design](PROTOCOL.md) · [Simulated counts](records.json)

## What the owners do

The two-dimensional space at each end is the geometric transverse pair x, y from the earlier dipole experiment. The emitter uses each eigenstate of the three Pauli axes equally often. The receiving owner first distinguishes arrival in the specified two-mode plane, arrival in its axial dipole, other local arrivals and remote quiet. The successful mode pair is analyzed in one of three bases. Each basis always uses the same two complementary sign effects. Only the scorer knows which detector sign agrees with the input. The simulated bank stores its two sign bins relative to the recorded input sign. Reversing that relabeling gives the local detector labels for every launch class. The test assumes a trusted source and receiver, a supplied port frame, and no classical side channel carrying the state label. [Executable preparation, receiver and score](measure.py)

Applying the lossless two-mode rotation $VU^*$ removes an estimated mixture of the two polarizations. The analyzer passes both modes at unit efficiency. Calibration is separate for each exposure and station. The challenge reads both signs, the axial mode, the rest of the receiving cell, and a miss. ## The classical bound including losses

Let $C$ and $W$ be the probabilities per original launch of the matching and opposite signs, averaged over the six inputs. In a probabilistic measure-and-prepare channel each classical outcome has a positive input effect $E$ and prepares an output state $\sigma$ in the accepted plane. Loss can enter either operator. The output is fixed before its verification basis is chosen. For the six Pauli projectors $\rho$, $\langle\rho\otimes\rho\rangle=(I+\mathrm{SWAP})/6$. This outcome therefore contributes $(\mathrm{tr}(E)\mathrm{tr}(\sigma)+\mathrm{tr}(E\sigma))/6$ to $C$ and $\mathrm{tr}(E)\mathrm{tr}(\sigma)/2$ to $C+W$. Positivity gives $\mathrm{tr}(E\sigma)\leq\mathrm{tr}(E)\mathrm{tr}(\sigma)$. Summing over outcomes gives $C-2W\leq0$. Every simulated trial has an outcome and the score uses the entire launch denominator.

At the last receiver and exposure 64, the native score has simultaneous 99% interval **[0.004853, 0.005307]**. Randomizing the relative input x/y phase leaves the full x and y basis distributions invariant; its score interval is **[−0.000490, −0.0000087]**. Halving every exchange strength while preserving the board and flight duration gives **[−0.002065, −0.001596]** with the native receiver calibration. The changed law could be calibrated separately for its own flight time. This control tests the registered schedule-dependent response. The bound uses fixed-enrollment binomial intervals with simultaneous coverage of all enrolled matching, opposite and sharp-arrival probabilities. The sampled input signs and recorded reference calibrations are independent.
