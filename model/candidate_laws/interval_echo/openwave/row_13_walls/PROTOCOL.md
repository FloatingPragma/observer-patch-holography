# Energy of three resolved walls

Use the three ten-port belts and both connecting seams of the [native three-wall experiment](../quark_winding/belt_walls/README.md). The material is supplied on this gated guide. A wall is recognized by the measured occupation gaps around one belt: (4,3,3) for three loads or (1,3,3,3) for four, up to translation. The phase shifts and material polarizations of these two words were known when selecting this test. Three such records have net material excess zero or one. OpenWave's static test asks for a stable three-quark composite. This experiment asks a narrower energy question: can a state that is energetically competitive with a separated preparation in the same conserved binary-load sector have most of its probability on these three wall records? [OpenWave row](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/blob/d4fc3e4046c472f4ad38468df8dddfa1f97bac97/MODELS.md#L115)

For the energy comparison put a consecutive cluster of three or four occupied ports on each belt with no bridge endpoint occupied. Use the first such segment around each belt in the established native address chart. For the wall comparison take the first translated word of the same belt loading also avoiding bridge endpoints. Keep all 62 contacts active in the energy operator. On independent copies read both-occupied and exchange-odd at each seam; these projections commute. Record all outcomes including the complement, with 16,384 copies per seam from seed 131305. Compare the two preparations at the registered contrast, at zero contrast and at reversed contrast with the same routing and detector.

Find the exact maximum of the number of occupied-occupied seams in the whole sector, and separately among the recognized wall words. Enumerate the possible words of each belt grouped by load number and bridge-endpoint bits, then combine all boundary groups at known total load. The exchange part of the generator is positive. This gives an upper bound on the three-wall read probability of any state with mean energy at or below the separated trial energy. A bound below one half excludes a wall-majority state in that energy range. A larger bound is inconclusive. Report the full sector size, maximizing words, and the ceiling in each sector. The separated trial is a variational energy comparison on a finite guide; an asymptotic fragment threshold or a colour measurement would require additional observations.

The mean-energy ceilings from this protocol exceed one half. A follow-up distinguishes a state certified below the comparison energy by an energy filter from a mixture having only a low mean. Keep the geometry, sources, contact data, wall projectors and both thresholds fixed. Enumerate every edge from a wall word to its complement in the full connected guide and bound the norm of the off-diagonal Hamiltonian block by half the geometric mean of its maximum row and column counts. Apply the separated-spectrum projection bound to the *entire subspace* below the original threshold. This can fail if the wall block overlaps the threshold or its coupling to the complement is too large. Record both the original ceiling and this bound. The latter is a property of the native generator; no spectral filter is needed to calculate it.
