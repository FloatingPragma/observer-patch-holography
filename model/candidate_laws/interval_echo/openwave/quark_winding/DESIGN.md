# Winding and charge at the same field endpoint

Use the one-cell positive written-field Laplacian and the fixed field component of the [parity-string experiment](../electron_field/flux_string/DESIGN.md). The prepared face pair has a known winding response. The new question is whether the same preparation carries regional signed charge. OpenWave asks for fractional electric charge from partial winding. The two target ratios and the parent winding result were known in advance. A unit here is a transported signed material load. Its correspondence to laboratory electric charge is open.

Select the first and second unordered antipodal face pairs in lexicographic order in the dual graph. Use the first as the primary setting and reserve the second to test the prediction without changing the apparatus. The two regions are the three material ports of each respective face. Begin with the parent's coherent, uniform field and with its parity string between the two faces on fresh preparations. Retain the original background throughout. The source, clock, interferometers, protected pulses and ideal readout are supplied.

1. For each region read the signed change of incoming heads across the cut relative to the preloaded background. Sum the head changes coherently into one pointer and read only the sum, preserving coherence within each outcome. In the winding experiment preload the tour charge at its known starting port in both reference and string arms. This subtracts the probe's own initial charge. Read both regional sums before the tour and retain them with the final interference port. On fresh copies a signed voltage pulse on the three material ports reads the characteristic function of their excess load relative to the same preloaded material. Compare its two interference quadratures at pulse angles π/2 and π, including the magnitude as well as the phase.
2. Recompute the two signed tours from the addressed material swaps. Use the boundary of the ten faces touching the near face, then enlarge that disk by the lowest-index face sharing one boundary edge, excluding the far endpoint. For an exterior control take the boundary of the lowest-index single face containing neither endpoint in its interior. Use the parent's canonical cycle convention and sweep. Include all returns and departures in the interference probabilities. Report any contour with zero winding weight as unresolved. Compare the disk and its deformation to the exterior, and to the same contours on the bare preparation. The known parent result sets the winding prediction on the primary contour. Enclosure predicts the other results.
3. For each sign prepare an ordinary material load coherently on three ports with equal weights, first with one port inside the measured region and then with two. Start outside, with the compensator fixed. Use the lexicographically first enabled two-edge fork in a sharp word of the component, ordered by word then port addresses. Use two partial native hops with conditional transfer probabilities 1/3 and 1/2. Include sharp unit transfers of both signs across the same cut as calibrations. Compare the full head-sum distributions and material-voltage responses with ideal sharp charges of the same mean. Repeat with a changed transport law that swaps material on the same addresses and leaves the head unwritten. In a separate symmetry-preserving coherence control, dephase every head in the sharp-word basis immediately before the tour.

For the endpoint test, a winding-sensitive interference port together with unchanged regional flux and unit material coherence at both angles rejects signed fractional charge for these two meters and preparations. A change of the regional records must be reproducible on the second pair and distinguishable from the delocalized unit controls before interpreting it as an endpoint charge. Exact probabilities of each observer outcome suffice on this finite fixture. Keep identically dark outcomes and unsuccessful tours. Global eigenstates, a continuum string tension and quark flavour lie outside this measurement.
