# A static interaction across a polarizable material

Two stationary qutrits can influence each other's energy across a chain of quantum dipoles on registered Interval Echo. In the longest even guide, the source-dependent energy measured by reversing the end probe is **31.502402 exchange units**. Adding one dimer changes it to **−31.502300** with both material signs defined by the same local reference. A programmed critical medium gives an interaction of inverse-length magnitude on the same seam law. It retains the sign alternation. The measurements identify a boundary interaction in polarizable material. An elementary electric source with a far-field Coulomb potential and a mechanism for α remain unresolved. ### What the observers can read

Each endpoint holds record 0, 1 or 2. An adjacent unit standard and the native contrast echo read the signed material deviations −1, 0 and +1. Between endpoints each freely polarizing dimer shares one amount-two load between two ports. A guard fixes that amount and enables both orientations. Every between-dimer contact still acts diagonally, while its exchange would leave the guarded sector. Other contacts are shuttered. The route follows a shortest chain of owners on each 20-, 80- or 320-owner federation. Each dimer's two ports have identical native degree, so the complete federation's degree correction contributes only a common scalar there. Shuttered full-swap ranging, with the degree clock suspended in this independent instrument, measures the shortest native route. At the longest even placement it uses 62 hops; the open polarization guide uses 89 links. All registered-law spectra use the original degree-reference service.

An energy selector supplies the lowest state at each enrolled boundary condition. The Ramsey observer compares matched two-copy branches so all endpoint-only energies cancel. Geometric clock exposures start at 0.05 and double to 102.4; each quadrature uses 8,388,608 simulated shots. The first phase magnitude is below π by the native bandwidth. Nearest-phase unwrapping across successive doublings is unique within the simultaneous counting bound. Binomial Hoeffding bounds with a union bound over 30,000 quadratures give ±0.000027 exchange units per static read with 99% coverage. A guide without polarizable material gives zero connected phase. ### Static spectrum on the registered law

Write h for the magnitude of the intra-dimer hopping element and J for half the unlike-minus-equal diagonal energy on a guarded inter-dimer contact. The native 99-state operator gives h=1/2 and J=(4κ+1/2)/2. Thus κ=8 gives J=16.25. Label the orientation of the two loads of dimer i by a Pauli Z and use an alternating orientation convention along the guide. Up to additive constants the Hamiltonian in this convention is a transverse-field Ising chain with field h and ferromagnetic bond J. A neutral end has no orientation preference. For two charged ends, the parity of the n+1 intervening domain walls is the product of their signs times (−1)ⁿ. A transverse flip on a dimer flips both adjacent wall bits. This gives an open dual chain with field J and hopping/pairing h. Its two parity floors differ by twice the least singular value of the (n+1)-square bidiagonal matrix with diagonal J and subdiagonal −h. Diagonalization of the microscopic qutrit matrices with the guards and physical endpoints checks the result for both parities and every pair of end signs. The parity dependence is essential to the measured sign.

### How an inverse-length signal can arise

At J=h the static dual chain gives the exact splitting magnitude 4h sin(π/(4n+6)). The changed-contrast law realizes this control at κ=1/8. Even there a pure two-parameter fit from the short guides misses the finite-length offsets. The registered seam also reaches a critical *programmed* medium: pulse intra-dimer contacts for δ=1/8 and inter-dimer contacts for fδ. Independent contact and dimer readings give f=0.03076925, compared with the selected h/J=2/65. One period in the physical exchange clock is δ(1+f). Interferometry between parity-resolved Floquet modes measures a signed energy +0.0334061 at 89 links, −0.0326854 at 91. The product of magnitude and guide length is 2.9731 and 2.9744. Halving the inter-dimer duration makes the corresponding late signal consistent with zero; doubling it gives magnitudes above 0.94. The relative pulse durations, coherent reference and preparation of Floquet modes are supplied. [Calibrations, changed schedules and signed phase counts](schedule.json)

The critical inverse-length order can be proved for the exact pulses. In a Majorana basis they are real orthogonal rotations A and B on successive matchings with common angle θ=2hδ. Their skew Cayley generators X and Y satisfy (I−X)(AB−I)(I−Y)=2(X+Y). X+Y is the open skew tridiagonal chain with bond magnitude tan(θ/2) and least singular value v=2 tan(θ/2)sin(π/(4m+2)). The singular values of I−X equal sec(θ/2). Those of I−Y lie between one and sec(θ/2). For the least positive Floquet phase ω this bounds sin(ω/2) between v cos²(θ/2) and v cos(θ/2). Both bounds scale as 1/m. This critical boundary spectrum can give a Coulomb-shaped magnitude without an electromagnetic mode. An electric interpretation needs the same independently calibrated sign on different routes, a physical three-dimensional range, and a charged field source outside the polarization guide.

### Does the same medium carry a traveling signal?

An independent launch prepares a smooth packet in the 45-dimer guide. A polarized packet is a pair of orthogonal width-three Majorana envelopes in the material domain-wall algebra. Their even product is an observable in the fixed-endpoint material sector. The coherent prepared and translated detectors are supplied. A maximally mixed parity sector projected onto the positive source observable retains all its other degrees of freedom. The receiving observer translates a matched detector in one-eighth wall-site steps. No static boundary gap enters the launch, detector or timing. An independent finite-word evolution checks the detector law as an explicit trace on a fixed material parity sector. Two mirrored launches pass the visibility and distance cuts. From the 32nd to the 96th pulse period each signal peak moves **7.875 wall spacings**, one to the left and the other to the right. The peak contrasts stay above 0.98 at those appointments. Single-sublattice launches give split, low-visibility returns. The measured speed is 0.95499 wall spacings per physical exchange-clock unit. Critical boundary reflection gives k of order π/(2m) for m=n+1 wall sites. Since the guide length in links is R=2n+1, the independent speed forecast for R times the boundary energy magnitude is **πv=3.00019**. Counting uncertainty alone gives the conservative simultaneous 99% interval 2.714–3.286 for the two receivers' grid-peak result. Corresponding static reads give 2.9731 and 2.9744 at 89 and 91 links. The exact all-size pulse bounds give limiting coefficient bounds of 3.0399 and 3.0459. Finite packet width and finite guide length affect the two measurements separately. This links a propagating polarization excitation to the boundary-energy magnitude in this one-dimensional material. An electric far field or a second transverse Maxwell polarization would require distinct observables. [Packet tracks and speed calculation](flight.json)
