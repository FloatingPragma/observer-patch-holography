# Three equal material standards coupled to a closed polarizable ring

The reference is the open dipole guide and the separately measured blank host. Test a different medium on the same seam law with identical source and receiver signs. In each dimer call its two physical ports A and B, with one amount-two load shared between them. Join B to A of the next dimer by a native seam and close the last bond. Couple each sharp endpoint to the A port of its own dimer. Positive means a double relative to an adjacent unit, negative a vacancy and neutral a unit. The charge reader and terminal contact orientation stay fixed across all settings. Other bonds are shuttered. Use equal-degree pairs so degree completion contributes only a common dimer shift. Pin the dimers' total loads and the endpoint records in the measured evolution, as in the open guide. An odd ring has competing preferred orientations around the circuit.

Add a fourth terminal at site 2 as an even-arc control. Each of the four terminals has its own sharp unit comparator. The geometry search uses adjacency and degree only; among admissible layouts pick deterministic representatives. Retain endpoint distances on the unshuttered board. Across the three designated sources acquire all 27 simultaneous charge combinations. Set the fourth terminal to neutral. In an independent bank use the zero and two sites alone. Test three-source additivity by predicting the fully charged configurations from the one- and two-source measurements. Repeat with one bond cut on either route between a chosen pair, and with exchange removed from every seam. An orientation reversal in the held data, a nonzero residual of the three-source forecast, or a failure of the independently ranged reciprocal-distance test distinguishes this medium from the corresponding electric interpretation. Preserve those outcomes regardless of their sign.
