# Three local measurements certify a connected orbital singlet after free flight

One exchange time after an energy-selected triple was released, three local observers could certify a connected orbital singlet with four settings of their position detectors. On the 20-cell federation the joint weight was at least **99.56% of the admitted triples**, or **32.05 to 36.49 per million original simulated launches**. The original denominator includes every source and aperture refusal. Two alternative apertures on these very same preparations gave high singlet fidelity while their connected-singlet weight was exactly zero. The occupied addresses therefore matter to the spatial interpretation of the colour record. [Protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Complete simulated banks](records.json)

## What the observers measure

The guarded home-cell energy filter prepares the parent trimer from the three ordinary loads on face (1,8,9). It accepts about 8.36% of original launches. During flight all native contacts and private references are open. At a terminal appointment, isolate the material and route three sets of home addresses to local qutrit detectors. Each detector applies one basis rotation and reads the position of its one load. Source refusal, absence of exactly one load on each detector, and all 27 admitted label triples are possible records. A group of three input addresses maps to its three receiver addresses by a permutation of physical ports. If all three receivers admit then the conserved total of three loads puts every input load in these groups, even when the flight took material through neighbouring cells. Source isolation, the address routes, the local basis words, the coherent shutters and the sharp Born readout are supplied controls. The data concern terminal cohorts from the same preparation. Each frame and each free-flight appointment has fresh launches. Let C be the sharp position event that the three occupied ports induce a connected subgraph at the time of arrest. For three ports it means at least two occupied seams. Let S project onto the determinant in the three accepted qutrits, with zero action on refusals. Its only nonzero coefficients are the six permutations of three distinct labels. All six have **two** occupied seams for omissions 2 and 3 and **one** for omissions 0 and 1. This gives CS=SC=S in the first two apertures and CS=SC=0 in the other two, for every possible input state, even one entangled with an unobserved reference. In each connected aperture 19/27 sharp words are connected. In each disconnected aperture 8/27 are connected. Geometry fixes which joint projector the colour witness measures; dynamics and preparation determine its weight. The identity-basis reading returns the actual terminal positions. None of the other bases requires recording those positions before its rotation. [Exact geometry check](test_product.py)

For one pair of observers sum the equal-label projectors over the four common orthonormal qutrit bases. The sum equals the identity plus the pair swap. Summing the three pair results gives D=3I+swap₁₂+swap₁₃+swap₂₃. The symmetric, mixed and antisymmetric permutation sectors have eigenvalues 6, 3 and 0. Only the determinant spans the zero eigenspace. The mean d inferred from the local counts therefore bounds the admitted singlet weight F for every mixed state,

    1 − d/3 ≤ F ≤ 1 − d/6.

The same d can be estimated by counting how many pairs agree on each triple. It equals one point for any repeated label and two extra points when all three labels agree. Using both ways of summing the same records narrows numerical ambiguity. This second grouping was added after the first analysis of the changed-order control. The original pairwise counts alone separated it from the native case but its upper fidelity bound exceeded the planned threshold of 0.95. The final family allowance covers both ways of scoring for all 19 cases, so the data used to choose the grouping remain in the stated uncertainty.

The second connected aperture, omission 2, gave at least 0.99586 on 20 cells at time one. At one quarter unit the lower bounds for both connected apertures on that board exceeded 0.99969. Conversely the two disconnected apertures gave at least 0.98257 and 0.98330 in conditional orbital singlet weight at time one, with zero connected-singlet weight in each. This is consistent with the parent all-time lower bound of 98.349% on connected triples after the energy filter. Aperture admission selects a small part of that ensemble.

Dephasing the 20-cell terminal position words before the main read gave an upper fidelity bound of 0.62917. Using AB on one arm and BA on the other two gave an upper bound of 0.91438. Turning off the contrast **during flight** after preparing the native source retained high singlet fidelity and raised the connected-singlet yield to 3,814.78–4,137.93 per million. The free contrast controls the detector admission strongly here. The colour of the prepared source survives the alternate flight too. The separate control that removes contrast throughout preparation has vanishingly small filter acceptance and supplies no conditional colour reading. ## How finite-host uncertainty enters the records

The full 20-, 80- and 320-cell hosts supply the geometries. Their home cell plus three neighbours admit a 48-port principal compression with 17,296 sharp three-load words. All missing outward half-diagonal energies are retained. The parent Duhamel estimate bounds the difference between the full-host and compressed **home amplitudes** at each chosen time. This study recomputes the complex amplitudes by a fixed 32-substep Taylor propagator of degree 24 in the centered compressed generator. All 19 cases agree with the preceding calculated aperture admission to 10⁻¹⁰. The propagated component and its signs, rather than a supplied determinant or an inferred fidelity, determine the new product-bank probabilities. The parent's bound at time one is 0.000524 in home-vector norm or less on the native hosts. The exchanged-flight bound is larger. [Endpoint calculation](measure.py) · [Full-host bound](../measure.py)

On each substep the maximum absolute row and column sums of the dimensionless generator are below 2.1. Taylor's remainder summed over 32 steps is below 10⁻¹¹. Under ordinary binary64 arithmetic a sparse row has at most 19 nonzeros. Its dot-product norm error is bounded by the entrywise absolute generator norm times the usual dot-product rounding factor. Including additions and complex scaling, a coarse bound of 10⁻¹² per substep follows by summing the term weights exp(2.1). The sum over 32 steps, the independently recomputed 220-word source eigenspace residual and basis-word rounding fit within the extra 5×10⁻⁸ vector allowance added to the parent's bound. Each terminal prefix is a positive contraction E. Applying its square root changes a projected amplitude norm by no more than this combined error. Squaring the interval and multiplying by the source-acceptance interval bounds the absolute cumulative probability, even after the dephasing control.

A single seeded inverse-CDF cohort per setting is coupled to all complete response tables consistent with these cumulative bounds. Records store [first possible label, last possible label, count], in the explicitly archived order, followed by aperture refusal (27) and source refusal (28). A singleton is a definite outcome. For any accepted event the least and greatest possible counts give conservative Clopper–Pearson intervals for its true binomial probability. For an event conditional on admission, the least event count and largest admission count bound the lower limit; the reverse pair bounds the upper. We allocate 1% failure probability across at most 399 two-sided intervals (three pair frequencies, two grouping frequencies per setting, plus one admission frequency per panel). Combining bounds requires no independence between the scores formed from a common record.
