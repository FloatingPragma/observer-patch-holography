# Can three identified moving orbitals have lepton masses?

A material receiver can distinguish three freely transferred charged orbitals and recover an accompanying two-state signal for each. In a second apparatus, both charge signs travel in three periodic bands. The first two bands gain energy in the wrong order for a common relativistic mass assignment. Both experiments use registered Interval Echo, a supplied finite apparatus and simulated terminal counts. [Two-path measurements](paired.json) · [Ring measurements](results.json) · [Independent checks](test_bands.py)

## Three orbitals and a two-state signal on the same charge

On the twenty-cell federation take two paths of thirteen ports from a common source owner to a common receiving owner. Give three consecutive ports on each path and their three distant counterparts one written side contact apiece. Each mark adds 32 when the single charge occupies its site. The rest of the path supplies ordinary blank exchange. Each of the two spatial regions now supports three distinguishable local standing waves. Prepare a single vacancy on the left in one of the three local waves. Its path address carries a second amplitude that the source can rotate and the receiver can read in three bases. The entire right region initially has its filled reference load. For the negative charge, the three pairs are centered at about 48.29688, 49.00781 and 49.71094 native units. Each member occurs twice, once per path. The independently read mean energies of the sharp regional preparations are 48.2935, 49.0014 and 49.7082. A terminal charge reading and an exchange-parity bank give these means. Energy weight outside the selected pair contributes. A fit to 257 independent receiver counts over a uniform grid finds the splitting of each pair from its oscillation. A training bank chose the read time from this grid. On fresh copies, three adjacent native exchanges and marked-port phases rotate the complete receiving orbital basis into the three site addresses. The decoder keeps all four outcomes, including a miss. For qubit tomography both paths undergo the same inverse transform before the matched addresses are brought to the common receiver, preserving their relative phase. Reversing the charge gives at least 99.95% delivery for each wave. The near-resonant path levels characterize a finite double well. A rest mass requires a momentum experiment.

The three waves preserve the two-state path amplitude. For every prepared qubit the ideal accepted channel is the same scalar transfer times the identity, since both paths have the identical tridiagonal generator. Independent six-state tomography gives unnormalized partial-transpose eigenvalues from −0.49989 to −0.49976 for all six sign and orbital settings. After decoding the occupied receiving wave by inverse local pulses, addressed swaps bring its two possible ports to the common receiving cell. Two physical half-turns of that load in perpendicular orders give the positive interference sign. A path swap and local phase reversal give the negative sign on the same input. These measurements rotate the material in the laboratory frame; a spatial rotation of a mobile spin dressing requires its own detector. Removing one middle side mark on one receiving path suppresses one transmission singular probability below 0.00001 in the upper and lower waves. The middle wave has a node at that mark and retains nearly equal transmission. ## Three bands on a periodic material route

The second apparatus uses 144 ports in 48 actual cells on the 320-cell federation. The three-port motif has two gateways and a five-seam port. One native magnitude-two side mark on the latter adds 32 for either charge. All three on-route ordinary bonds remain blank. At cell quasimomentum $q$ the diagonal of the three-port Hamiltonian is $(17,49,17)$, with hopping $-1/2$ and boundary phase $e^{iq}$. Ordering the three lines in each momentum sector before comparison to any laboratory value selects the bands. Their minima lie at $0,\pi,0$. The determinant has a single momentum-dependent term proportional to $\cos q$ and its three separated roots alternate between increasing and decreasing. The first numerical column is the eigenenergy independently checked by terminal energy banks. The second comes from the phase of the observed charge density. Both signs give the same ordering. In the first two bands the packets travel for 256 exchange units and in the third for 12,288. Comparing an energy minimum and its neighbor uses the same momentum increment $2\pi/48$ relative to that band minimum. Near the zone edge the middle band consequently receives the same envelope momentum as the other two. An observer prepares a coherent sum of two waves and reads the spatial density harmonic at successively doubled delays. A separate exchange dimer calibrates one scheduled clock tick, repeated at the same rate. The first phase is unambiguous by the spectral-norm bound $|\Delta E|\le |e^{i\Delta q}-1|/2$. The measured Fourier errors permit unwrapping at every doubling. The simultaneous 99% intervals include both signs, clock calibration, energy and Fourier sampling. They put the lower-band gain below 0.00006480 and the middle-band gain above 0.00006886.

For positive rest energy $E$, a common relativistic dispersion at a fixed nonzero momentum requires $2E\,\delta+\delta^2$ to be the same for each energy gain $\delta$. Increasing $E$ therefore strictly decreases $\delta$. The measured middle band starts higher and gains more. An arbitrary positive change of units or common energy offset keeping rest energies positive preserves these two orderings. This rejects a relativistic identification of the three scalar thresholds under that momentum assignment. [Lean recoil-order theorem](../../../../../../Lean/SourceLaw/ChargedRegisterPole.lean)

## Native preparation and a bound for other memory backgrounds

For each side mark, temporarily stock its endpoints with 0 and 2. A blank half exchange and an addressed private phase prepare the equal superposition of both orders. The native echo leaves both loads at 1 with an even written contrast of magnitude two. This costs one supplied vacancy–excess pair per programmed mark. The head stays in the same state when either charge enters or leaves the active port. Its exact local energy is zero at joint filling and 32 on a singly occupied defect configuration. Each side contact has a private-reference-capable endpoint for the programming phase. In the double-well geometry the second endpoint provides this phase with the orientation exchanged. In both experiments the compensator is confined to a remote two-port store by equal-load writes. The outer endpoints of all side marks remain filled. A vacancy erases the degree potentials on the 48-cell ring with zero-contrast private writes. On the paired paths the two outer buffers of each rail retain their ordinary private references for preparation and detection.

An isolated marked side contact now supplies a position phase. Reversible adjacent swaps bring any required address to it and back. Partial exchanges and these phases emit the stated normalized port amplitudes from a sharp charge. For the paired test the common owner's two-buffer pulse initializes the path qubit; equal pulse sequences shift it to the two left regions and prepare the selected wave. The ring energy reader counts sharp ternary positions and uses independent copies for the three-port antisymmetric bond analyzer. It estimates $16+32 P_{\rm middle}+144P_{\rm odd\ random\ bond}$. For the local left or right trimer the corresponding expression is $48+12 P_{\rm odd\ random\ path\ bond}$. Both count every start. With the ring preparation pulse times fixed, changing contrast from 8 to 40 preserves the oriented side write and reduces overlap with each intended emitted rest wave to below 0.00171. A separate finite bound covers arbitrary physical seam memories in a filled material background, including entangled heads. Extend the generator on a filled physical pair $(1,1)$ as a head operator $B_e$ tensored with the identity on the loads. The integer 99-column check gives $[h_e,B_e]=0$. Each $B_e$ commutes with the other seams since it acts only on its own head. The operator $h_e-B_e$ vanishes on two filled loads, preserves defect number, and has spectrum in $[-9,33]$. This yields a joint energy difference against the same background, also when a head is still dynamic. On a five-seam port the ordinary private reference contributes −8 for a vacancy or +24 for an excess, and a zero-written private reference contributes zero. Counting each nonunit port at most six times gives
$$
-54D\ \leq H-H_{\rm filled\ heads}\ \leq 198D
$$
on the sector with $D$ nonunit ports. The inequalities apply to a single charge superposed across arbitrarily many sites and to every finite board with the stated incidence and private references. Comparing different background excitations on the two arms changes the energy convention. With either a 16-unit or a 35-unit light anchor, even the muon target exceeds the entire one-defect spectrum under this matching. The bound also allows small positive light lines if a different physical state sets the scale.
