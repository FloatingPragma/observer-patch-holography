# Spin on the same resolved charged line

For both charge signs, every energy of the measured 96- and 144-port guide has a one-dimensional eigenspace. Two operations preserving this guide Hamiltonian therefore commute on its entire reachable charged sector. This excludes a pair of anticommuting spin half-turns acting within the resolved same-energy excitation. The conclusion concerns precisely the sources and detector ports of the dispersion measurement.

**Endpoint certificate.** In physical port order, $J=2H$ is an integer tridiagonal matrix. Every neighboring hopping entry $b_j$ is either $-1$ or $-8$. Starting with the left endpoint $e_0$, column $J^k e_0$ vanishes strictly after port $k$ and has value $b_0\cdots b_{k-1}$ at port $k$. The endpoint matrix with columns $e_0,J e_0,\ldots,J^{N-1}e_0$ is triangular with nonzero diagonal. The executable checks its actual integer columns and the native sign-specific chart at each length. This bypasses numerical eigenvalue resolution, even for an extremely close pair of end modes.

For each vector $x$ there is a polynomial $p$ with $x=p(J)e_0$. If a linear map $A$ commutes with $J$, write $A e_0=q(J)e_0$. Then $Ax=p(J)q(J)e_0=q(J)x$. Every such $A$ is polynomial in $J$ and commutes with every other such map. Since $J$ is real symmetric, an eigenvalue of multiplicity greater than one would admit noncommuting maps on its eigenspace. Equivalently, the nonzero first hopping determines the second coordinate of any eigenvector from the first; induction determines every coordinate. **Reading the same pole.** Use the two original foreign detectors 1832 and 2078 on 96 ports, or 1832 and 3686 on 144. The original narrow two-mode emitter and the first reciprocal sharp emitter span the two source rails. Orthogonalizing this span gives $V$. For an ideal energy selection $P_E=|u\rangle\langle u|$ and the two-site receiving map $D$, the amplitude matrix is $(Du)(u^*V)$. Its second singular value vanishes for every possible encoding, including one without these emitter restrictions. A single receiving owner has the same upper bound. A fully resolving energy measurement leaves a fixed outgoing state conditioned on its outcome.

Both detector ports have nonzero weight on **every** finite eigenstate, so an insensitive aperture cannot explain this result. The monic characteristic polynomial of the path and the characteristic polynomial of the leading $r$-port prefix have greatest common divisor one modulo 65537 at each receiver index $r$. A common root over the complex numbers would give a common monic integer factor before reduction. The endpoint recurrence makes the eigenvector amplitude at $r$ proportional to the prefix polynomial there. A sharp source at that same port consequently overlaps every pole as well. On the lowest interior 144-port mode, the two weights are 0.004447774 and 0.004447658; the singular values for the measured source span and the joint aperture are 0.06699723 and zero. The receipt gives all 4 and 7 measured modes of both signs.

**Spin-sensitive comparison.** Give the same orbital path an explicit two-state spin with Hamiltonian $H\otimes I_2$. The orbital energy, narrow emitter and two local apertures coincide with the measured case. Local half-turns $-iX$ and $-iZ$ preserve that energy and anticommute. On either sharp receiving port the spin-resolved amplitude is $u(r)\langle u|\mathrm{source}\rangle I_2$ of rank two. The $Z$- and $X$-analyzing count banks recover both input spin eigenstates. Ordinary charge readings give identical probabilities for either spin, as expected. This comparison declares the spin and its analyzer. The registered guide has neither of those added coordinates.

A second comparison adds an explicit splitting of 64 native units between the two spin labels. It exceeds twice the orbital operator-norm bound, making the two spectra disjoint. Each resolved line has one spin state even though the larger comparison system still carries spin. Likewise, a material trap or applied field can split a real multiplet.
