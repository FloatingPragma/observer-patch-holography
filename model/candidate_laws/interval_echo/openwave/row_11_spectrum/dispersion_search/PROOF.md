# Native preparation, spatial phase and free dispersion

**Path sector.** For a charge at $x$, every marked edge strictly to its left has flipped once and every other mark has its prepared direction. This prefix convention supplies one native charge-head state per port. The integer unit-contrast columns move the charge one step and flip that head together. Reversing a stored endpoint convention complements the direction bit and reverses the written contrast sign; the 99-column orientation check preserves the generator. A blank swaps just the charge. The code checks every nonzero local column for both material signs on both actual paths and verifies that exterior shutters isolate exactly the given ports. The charged conjugate also reverses the initially prepared direction bits. The blocked direction has zero relative cost. On an enabled pair the matched-host contact is $8.5I-0.5X$ on a blank and $-4I-4X$ on an even mark, within the two active path states and zero on the other coordinates. The unused blank bypass is biconnected, allowing a borrowed phase reference during stock preparation. The same bypass accommodates dark private writes and both unit-contrast write heralds. The program verifies the reachability and native local source columns. It does not simulate a preparation-time global scheduling algorithm.

**Native phase gates.** A blank pulse of duration $\pi$ and a marked pulse of duration $\pi/8$ act as $X$ and $-X$ respectively on their selected pair, with identity on the other coordinates. A word of these swaps brings either native contact onto any desired pair; complementing an axis sign in this process is tracked as $s_B,s_M\in\{-1,1\}$. The resulting generators are $8.5I-0.5s_BX$ and $-4I-4s_MX$. Choose durations with $t_M=-t_Bs_B/(8s_M)$ modulo the marked period $\pi/4$. The hopping cancels exactly. The surviving common phase on this pair runs at rate $8.5+0.5s_B/s_M$, while every other coordinate stays fixed. The blank period $2\pi$ permits positive durations. Three such pair phases implement one coordinate phase by $2P_a=P_{ab}+P_{ac}-P_{bc}$. Adjacent physical splitters and these phases sequentially prepare any specified vector on the finite path. Transporting a blank half-swap to two chosen positions and applying a native phase to the second implements a real interference measurement. The regression test multiplies full physical pulse matrices to compare a nonadjacent phase and both output amplitudes against this compilation.

**A momentum instrument independent of frequency.** Each interior spatial coordinate of a fixed energy obeys the second-order difference equation of a $P$-periodic Jacobi matrix. A unit-determinant transfer over one period, followed by its inverse, gives $y_{j-P}+y_{j+P}=2\cos\theta\,y_j$, valid whenever the three coordinates are inside the physical interval. This follows from the Cayley-Hamilton equation for the two-by-two period transfer. Its trace is invariant under a cyclic shift of the period. No momentum label from diagonalizing the Hamiltonian enters the reader. The position counter measures $I=\sum_{j=P}^{N-P-1}|y_j|^2$. Two banks mix disjoint pairs $(j,j+P)$ with opposite supercell parity and retain every output. An edge joining two interior periods has multiplicity two, an end edge has multiplicity one. The weighted sum of differences in the two output probabilities is $4I\cos\theta$. For a complex wave, take the real part of the recurrence before summing. This instrument measures the standing-wave phase magnitude. The two opposite phases in the separate current experiment determine signed motion.

**Energy and free current.** For negative real hopping $-w_j$, the native energy is $\sum_j w_j |y_{j+1}-y_j|^2+\sum_j V_j|y_j|^2$, where $V=H_{jj}-w_{j-1}-w_j$. On the selected guide $V$ takes values $0,8,16$. Two half-splitter banks read the gradient term and a sharp load bank reads the potential. To read a recoil, prepare two independently synthesized modes in equal superposition and let all contacts run freely. Project against the two quadrature superpositions with the inverse native preparation. The complex fringe is $e^{i\Delta t}$ in the detector convention. The eight waits are the powers $8^0,\ldots,8^7$. Successive phase estimates lift the argument without a predicted dispersion. A separate $2^{20}$-launch local-energy bank on each mode resolves the first $2\pi$ ambiguity. Its coarse recoil interval initializes the phase lift, and the code verifies uniqueness at every subsequent wait. The reported per-frequency radius uses both quadratures and the final wait.

For the opposite quadrature preparations, let $P_+(T)$ and $P_-(T)$ be the probability on the right side of one fixed cut. Reality of $H$ gives $P_-(T)=P_+(-T)$. Since only two modes contribute, the signed initial coordinate current is $[P_+(T)-P_-(T)]/[2T\,\mathrm{sinc}(\Delta T)]$, where $\mathrm{sinc}(u)=\sin(u)/u$. Compare that independently sampled result to a local native splitter measuring $2w_j\operatorname{Im}(\overline y_jy_{j+1})$. Multiplying by the signed material deficit or excess gives the signed material current. At a localized receiving port the marked incoming head has a definite direction, distinguished from the opposite native control by a reverse transfer after the load herald.

**Uncertainty and controls.** Binomial Clopper-Pearson intervals bound each absolute-energy category and both cut outcomes. Other bounded counts use Hoeffding intervals with tail budget $0.01/10{,}000$ per appointment or category. The two weighted momentum banks have outcome range four. Interval arithmetic propagates the momentum and frequency errors and the training uncertainties; repeated use of one count can widen a bound. The squared relativistic model is $E^2=M^2+C^2\theta^2$, and the comparison laws are $E=M+A\theta^2$ and $E=M+B(1-\cos\theta)$. A fourth law replaces $\theta^2$ by the operator symbol $2(1-\cos\theta)$ in the square root, with exactly the same calibration procedure. The finite cosine and discrete-momentum square root are unresolved at the reported precision. Only the shorter negative-charge calibration fixes the shared predictions. The narrow emitting pair has opposite real phases at the reflection of the first interior detector address; this fixes the arbitrary signs of numerical standing modes. Reciprocal narrow and sharp emitters additionally test the same two-mode beat on two physical material detectors, with their zero-time fringe and incoherent offset measured directly.
