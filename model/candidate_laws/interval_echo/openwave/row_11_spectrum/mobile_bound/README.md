# How do three mobile negative charges compare when observers weigh them in flight?

On one fifteen-port route through five cells, an observer can release one vacancy, a vacancy–excess–vacancy triple, or an alternating group of five defects. Each carries one unit of negative load. The three measured zero-momentum formation energies above the same filled material are **16.00000000, 82.04665460 and 147.05535129**. All three propagate. Their first momentum recoils decrease by roughly three orders of magnitude at each step. In particular the five-defect charge costs 9.19 times the formation energy of the vacancy and has about 920,000 times its inferred finite-momentum inertia. These three preparations distinguish the cost of forming a bound charge from its response to motion. ## Binding and energy resolution

Write d for the odd number of defects and r for the number of runs of neighboring defects in a sharp load word. There are two exterior unit-contrast contacts per run and at most d − r internal contacts of squared contrast four. The blank exchange gives one half on the diagonal and one half in absolute off-diagonal amplitude per unequal contact, with at most d + r such contacts. Thus the absolute row-sum bound for the Hamiltonian is κ(4d − 2r) + d + r. For two or more separated runs this is at most B = κ(4d − 4) + d + 2. The same bound holds on a tensor sum of two or more independent outgoing rings carrying the original split inventory. A single connected run with the wrong ordering contains at least one like adjacent pair and gives a smaller bound. This controls all relative momenta and energies of that separated channel. Additional degrees of freedom or changes of inventory require a different energy budget.

Let P select every translation of the sharp alternating d = 3 or 5 core. PHP = A P with A = κ(4d − 2) + (d + 1)/2. Each core has exactly d + 1 legal swaps into its complement Q. A noncore word is at most one swap from one such core. The complete word enumeration and independent uncompressed Hamiltonian confirm that PHQ QHP = (d + 1)P/4. Consequently the off-diagonal block has norm C = √(d + 1)/2. At registered κ = 8 the gaps A − B are **13 and 12** units. The two upper bands therefore sit above the full independently separated spectra. Even the sharp configuration has a conserved mean energy A. For p equal to its intact probability, the expectation bound gives (A − B)(1 − p) ≤ 2C√(p(1 − p)). Squaring gives p ≥ (A − B)²/((A − B)² + 4C²) uniformly in free time.
