# What can fluctuating elapsed time explain?

The two clocks separately admit a shared distribution of elapsed times at all 2,123 appointments. Recording where the material source arrives tests a stronger hypothesis. With the source launched one link from the clock, changing the clock species changed how often the source reached it. The data require clock-dependent source dynamics in any account that describes the clock readings by fluctuating elapsed time. [Clock and source counts](counts.npz) · [Deterministic time test](COMMON_TIME.md)

For a given elapsed time, the two mean readings (same minus flipped) are cos(32τ) and cos(33τ). A classical distribution over time gives a weighted average of points on this curve. For each appointment, the two flip counts and the two independent timing counts provide simultaneous binomial intervals. The time distributions can vary freely between appointments. The calculation takes the common interval of the two timing references and permits elapsed times from zero to its upper end. The construction admits at least one reference duration consistent with both timing counts. Its unit is the calibrated blank reference dimer.

The calculation constructs a polygon whose vertices are actual curve points, intersects it with the clock intervals and recovers a positive combination of up to three vertices. It checks the two probabilities by evaluating their cosines directly at the recovered times. All 193 appointments in each of eleven settings admit such a combination. The binomial intervals jointly cover the four native readings at every appointment with probability at least 99% under the enrolled detector model. At appointment index 108 for a freely launched source one link away, elapsed times 3.14159, 3.04342 and 0.09817 with probabilities 0.01957, 0.01977 and 0.96066 give flip fractions 0.98043 and 0.97796. This is the same appointment at which a single elapsed time fails. These constructions use separately prepared cohorts. They supply neither a common trajectory across appointments nor a distribution selected by the dynamics. [Construct and check all witnesses](mixture.py)

A passive clock that merely reads a common random time and leaves the source trajectory independent of clock species also predicts equal arrival probabilities for the source. This prediction holds even if the elapsed time and the arrival event are correlated. At index 27 in the one-link setting, the endpoint detector recorded **1,681** arrivals with the even clock and **1,280** with the odd clock in **1,048,576 launches each**. Both initial coins and all final coin outcomes count. A two-sided conditional Fisher test, corrected for all 2,123 comparisons, gives a probability under equal arrival rates of at most **3.71 × 10⁻¹⁰**. The endpoint launch also rejects equal arrival rates. Blank and parked controls give identical endpoint counts for both species. A model allowing the physical clock to disturb the source is outside this passive-clock hypothesis. These records establish reciprocal material influence. Inferring a source-induced gravitational lapse requires an independent geometric measurement and a quantitative prediction for it.
