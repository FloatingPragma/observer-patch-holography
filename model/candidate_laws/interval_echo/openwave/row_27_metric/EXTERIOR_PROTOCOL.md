# Exterior clock and relay test

This follow-up was designed after the original static-source counts. The signed size of the new exterior timing effect is unknown. The native written contact is already known to shift the coincident unit-unit energy, and the double-tag control is predicted to be quiet. The construction below was fixed before generating the exterior count bank.

Use the first external seam of the 20-cell board. Open a full ten-port belt on each of its two cells. In the source cell put the external gate at belt position 0 and select the pole outside its nearest neighbors. Station one unit or a vacancy at that pole, with the same magnitude-two even memory on its contact to antipodal belt position 5. In the radar cell put the external gate at position 2, the emitter at 0 and the relay at 5. Prepare the external contact in the same even memory. All other contacts and degree-reference services are shuttered. Verify the native operator on all local source, mediator and probe occupations, and measure the physical graph distance from the pole to every radar port. The two belts are dynamically connected by the external contact. The probe can never occupy a source-cell port.

Launch a sharp unit mediator on the source belt, either at position 5 facing the source or at position 0 facing the receiving cell. Let it evolve freely for 4, 8, 12 or 16 exchange units. The alternative launches distinguish emitted and reflected signals. Use copies with a vacancy in the mediator belt as a control. Interrogate the same source independently with unit and double clocks on the outside radar bond 1–2 and with sharp unit and double radar packets.

1. In the held-storage exposure, shutter only mediator-belt exchange after the free flight. Keep the prepared exterior contact active. Read the probability of finding the mediator at the external seam on an independent branch. Independently calibrate the receiver with a mediator parked at or away from the external seam. Use those two clock and timing records, weighted by the independently measured arrival fraction, to forecast the full held clock-transfer curve and the probability of returning by 8, 16, 32 and 64 ticks. The same stored mediator persists across both legs and all failed looks. The two native standards and the reference-clock exposure are those used in the original assay.
2. In the freely mobile exposure leave both belts active throughout the radar measurement. Record all four deadlines at the held 12-unit release for both launch directions. Retain the mediator state after a relay click and after each miss. Compare occupied versus empty pole, a double probe, an empty mediator, and a contrast-free law at the same cadence. Distinguish a delay (fewer timely returns) from an advance, and report all frozen outcomes and signs. A receiver-only change that survives these controls would warrant further range and clock-universality tests.
3. The primary probability denominator is every prepared trial. Use 2^25 trials per bank, simultaneous 99% Hoeffding errors for the declared probabilities and propagate these bounds to the mixture forecast and the occupied-minus-empty differences. This experiment supplies a mediator and its release, the protected contacts, shutters, and relay. Any motion generated by the mediator is part of the causal signal.
