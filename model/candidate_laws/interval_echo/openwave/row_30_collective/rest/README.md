## Which boundary condition does the observer prepare?

On a ring each link contributes a conditional cost, including the link between the last and first dimer. The linear end fields cancel. Up to a constant the physical generator in dimer orientations is
\[
H=J\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}Z_{j-1}Z_j-h\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}X_j.
\]
The wall sign on bond \(j\) is \(\tau_j^z=-Z_{j-1}Z_j\). Their product is \(P=(-1)^n\), fixed by the closed geometry. The independent symmetry \(F=\prod_jX_j\) is a product of physical dimer flips. Prepare a uniform mixture in the sector \(F=-P\) by drawing the first \(n-1\) local X-eigenvalues fairly, choosing the last one to fix their product, and preparing the corresponding local orientations in the X basis. In the dual Ising variables an internal dimer flip reverses two adjacent wall signs. The product of the dimer flips fixes the closing dual link to sign \(F\). The Jordan–Wigner string around the cycle adds \(-P\), leaving physical boundary twist \(-PF\). This sign is positive for the selected preparations. A constant orbital is then a periodic spatial mode of \(C=2(JI-hS)\), with \(S\) the cyclic one-site shift. Its singular frequency is \(2|J-h|\). An independent full orientation-space calculation on 5-, 6- and 7-dimer rings compares local physical string correlations against the reduction in **both** flip sectors. It includes each closing link and both signs of the twist. [Finite physical checks](physics.py)

## What the observers read

The four spatial launches are the constant profile and the first three real cosine waves. For each wave pair measure a product of physical dimer Pauli strings at preparation and release. The products represent an even Majorana bilinear. On the real periodic modes their return trace is the product of two cosines. The Fourier transform therefore contains a sum line and a difference line. Each source and terminal setting is importance sampled, retaining both signs. A controlled dimer hand supplies independent elapsed-time counts. The simulator uses the singular decomposition of the freely evolving native orientation chain for the record probabilities. The decoder receives only the integer records, the geometry, the exposure budgets and the chosen phase templates. It searches the full sampled positive frequency band for the two lines and refines their positions with independent fitted sine and cosine amplitudes. The native two-constituent frequency has upper bound 34 from the seam restriction. The measured clock step is approximately 0.08 and gives a Nyquist frequency of 39.27, so a native pair line cannot fold into the measured band. Its upper-line frequencies \(s_{01},s_{02},s_{12}\) give \(\omega_0=(s_{01}+s_{02}-s_{12})/2\). The fourth wave permits a further sum prediction. All five measured difference lines supply tests outside the three-line inversion.

Two independently demodulated spatial scans for each of the first three pairs span its two-mode plane. The common profile of the two planes sharing the claimed rest mode determines its overlap with a constant, and a nearest-neighbor difference includes the closing link. This tests the spatial response on fresh launches. Reducing the flight contrast by half leaves the source, timing and terminal detector settings fixed and changes the measured frequencies.
