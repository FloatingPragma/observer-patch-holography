# Single-branch recoil and charged transfer

A single vacancy moving on the upper winding band has a measurable recoil spectrum, independent of the zero assigned to energy. On the 144-port stock all **72** recoil energies relative to a stationary vacancy on that same band agree with the native prediction within **0.000085**. Freely fitted nearest-neighbour scalar hopping misses **31 of 36 held modes**. On a separate 82-port geometry the third, fourth and fifth harmonics of that single branch all differ from zero and agree with forecasts from two isolated native contact measurements. The second experiment distinguishes the branch even from reciprocal scalar hopping confined to four cell distances. It makes no claim against all multiorbital hopping models. Alternating nearest-neighbour transfers between two native degrees of freedom generate the observed band. ### Single-band recoil with a stationary vacancy

The independent charged-composition task compares one charge at rest with the same charge carrying a spatial wave and tests ordinary cosine hopping. Its published rule selects composition minima on the belt. Here the actual finite winding stock has an upper band separated from the lower one by a large gap, and a stationary charge state B at its upper-band minimum of 8. The lower formation band and both sides of the frequency spectrum are kept. The selection of the upper band in this experiment is explicit. At each spatial index the emitter occupies the two geometric blank-pair components in equal amplitudes with phase $i$. Every real eigenvector in their invariant plane has weight one half. A four-phase read against B returns the individual frequencies above and below it. Spatial phase is inferred from cells 3–34. Odd holds at cells 35–69 give a maximum RMS probability error of 0.000244 against 0.0005. The observed upper-branch wave numbers range from 0.04316 to 3.09866 radians per two-port cell. For the lower-side lines the maximum error against their own signed forecast is 0.000079. These are computed quantum outcome laws and simulated multinomial samples. Writing $q$ for phase measured from B, $a=0.5$ and $b=4$ for the native hopping magnitudes, the positive recoil is

$$\delta(q)=\sqrt{(b-a)^2+4ab\sin^2(q/2)}-(b-a).$$

It requires neither the band centre nor an assigned formation energy for comparison with data. The alternative scalar nearest-neighbour curve has the form $A+B(1-\cos q)$. Fitting $A,B$ on the 36 even modes gives a maximum error of 0.01618 on the 36 odd modes against the preregistered tolerance of 0.003. Adding independent second-neighbour hopping reduces that maximum to 0.001043 and **passes** this test. On the ten waves with $q<0.45$, the continuum upper-envelope recoil, host-relative continuum with rest 8, and curvature-matched ordinary hopping all meet the same tolerance, with errors at most 0.000771, 0.000991 and 0.000353. The remaining wavelengths discriminate the measured recoil shape. The global lower band about its own bulk minimum instead gives $a+b-\sqrt{(a+b)^2-2ab(1-\cos k)}$. Its curvature versus $1-\cos k$ is positive, opposite to that of positive massive square-root recoil. The prepared upper band is the object of the positive result.
