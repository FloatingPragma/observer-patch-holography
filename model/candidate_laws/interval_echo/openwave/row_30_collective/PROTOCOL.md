## Local reduction and exact dispersion

Let $Z_i=+1$ if the left port of the i-th enrolled dimer holds its unit and $Z_i=-1$ otherwise. Exchange on that dimer supplies $-hX_i$ and a scalar with $h=1/2$. Equal physical endpoint degree makes the sum of its two private reference phases a scalar. In all four binary endpoint words the prepared conditional collar gives exactly $4\kappa(1-n_a n_b)$ and retains its memory. The idle zero write gives zero. Substituting $n_R=(1-Z_i)/2$ and $n_L=(1+Z_{i+1})/2$ into the conditional term and summing leaves the physical open-chain generator, up to a scalar,

$$
 H=-h\sum_i X_i + J\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_iZ_{i+1}+J(Z_1-Z_n),\qquad J=\kappa.
$$

Include fixed virtual end orientations $+1,-1$ to represent the two end fields. These are bookkeeping for actual terminal energies. The $n+1$ wall variables have $\tau^z_j=-Z_{j-1}Z_j$, with virtual spins used for the first and last factors. A load comparison gives the wall indicator $d_j=(1-\tau^z_j)/2$. Its total parity is fixed to $n\bmod 2$. A dimer flip reverses its two adjacent wall signs. Therefore the dual Hamiltonian is exactly $-J\sum\tau^z_j-h\sum\tau^x_j\tau^x_{j+1}$, restricted to that physical parity.

Define $A_j=(\prod_{r<j}\tau^z_r)\tau^x_j$, $B_j=(\prod_{r<j}\tau^z_r)\tau^y_j$. Their linear Heisenberg evolution uses the open bidiagonal matrix $C=2(JI-hS)$ with $S_{j+1,j}=1$. The square of the single-mode frequency is an eigenvalue of $CC^T$. Every interior standing-wave component obeys the same three-site recurrence. This gives

$$
 \omega^2=4(J-h)^2+4Jh[2-2\cos(k)] = m^2+c^2 k^2+O(k^4),\qquad
 m=2|J-h|,\quad c^2=4Jh .
$$

The endpoints select the discrete wave numbers. The observer recovers them independently. The intrinsic spatial unit is one dimer step. The coupling-meter observations give $m=15.000511$ and $c^2=16.001559$ in dimer and local clock units. The native values independently derived from the restriction are 15 and 16. ## Recovering the line from physical records

A local dimer's left/right load gives $Z$. A blank exchange pulse gives rotations about $X$. On a connector between occupied-endpoint projectors $P,Q$, the term is $4J(1-PQ)$. Flip the unknown neighboring dimer between equal connector exposures and flip it back. The two diagonal generators commute and sum to $8J-4JP$, giving an addressed $Z$ rotation even on a degree-six dimer. At exposure π/$8J$ per half, this is a quarter turn up to a global phase. These pulses give the three physical measurement bases and any single-dimer source rotation. They are used only in preparation or terminal measurement.

Each $iA_jA_l$, $j<l$, is a tensor product of physical dimer Pauli operators. In increasing wall order it equals $i(\prod_{i=j}^{l-1}X_i)(\prod_{r=j}^{l-1}\tau^z_r)$. The factors reduce to single-site Paulis and the end signs. To estimate a two-time correlation, randomize the initial physical dimer orientations uniformly. Measure the first product in its local bases, evolve freely, measure the second product and multiply both signs. All outcomes count. The expectation is the normalized physical trace of the two-time product. For sine-shaped test vectors $f,g$ on the observed bond addresses, expand $iA(f)A(g)$ in these physical products. Its antisymmetric coefficients are $f_jg_l-f_lg_j$. Choose a term with probability proportional to its absolute coefficient, include its sign and multiply by the sum of absolute coefficients. A location scan uses the exterior product of $f$ and a single address, which also works when the two vectors overlap. Source and terminal choices are independent. Thus a normalized sum or a scan reduces to one simulated binomial counter, with $2^{32}$ preparations per hold/setting. Across the nine initial boards these timing, changed-law and spatial arrays represent 390,546,530,186,035,200 binary trials, excluding the separate clock and transport runs. The simulator draws aggregate binomial counts with the same probabilities as independent repeated preparations. It does not require a coherent observer measuring a nonlocal odd operator.

For a physical four-Majorana trace on these strands, projection to the fixed parity gives the same answer as the full normalized trace because the extra trace contains the parity product of all $2(n+1)$ Majoranas and cannot reduce to the identity. Thus the two-time correlation of $iA_jA_l$ with $iA_aA_b$ is the two-by-two determinant of the corresponding entries of $U\cos(\Sigma t)U^T$. This identity supplies the efficient simulator for the actual randomized measurement outcomes.

Each launch uses sine templates based only on the strand length and address order. A separate isolated blank dimer gives cosine and sine terminal orientation readings (the second analyzer advances by a quarter turn and exchanges the two terminal outcome labels) at 16,384 consecutive holds. Unwrap the observed phase and use its fitted increment in the Fourier frequency scale. Sample ordering and an upper bound of a half-turn between holds are apparatus assumptions. Temporal spectra of the physical products contain lines at pair sums and differences. Search for the largest peak in the upper half of the positive unaliased frequency interval. Use the same Hann taper and log-parabolic three-bin interpolation for every pair. On independent spatial-read trials demodulate the two scanned response profiles at the observed line. Two independent pair planes sharing a mode select its standing-wave profile. The interior three-site recurrence of that measured profile yields $\cos k$, including its lattice-gradient normalization. The launch template index is excluded from this estimator.

For measured pair sums $s_{ij}$, recover $\omega_1=(s_{12}+s_{13}-s_{23})/2$ and $\omega_i=s_{1i}-\omega_1$. Withhold $s_{2i}$ for $i\ge4$ from this inversion and predict them from the inferred frequencies. Each small physical spin Hamiltonian is diagonalized independently as a cross-check on the reduction, product-Pauli correlator and finite wall motion. ## Single-wall motion

On each odd open strand a sharp orientation word can have exactly one wall. Prepare a finite superposition of such words. A dimer flip conditioned on different values at its two flanking wall locations rotates their two one-wall basis states. In the bulk the conditioned generator is $X_i(1-Z_{i-1}Z_{i+1})/2$. For each source angle, conjugating a half-angle $X_i$ rotation by the two adjacent controlled-Z gates and composing it with the opposite half-angle rotation implements this condition. At an end only one controlled-Z and the known end sign are needed. Each native connector supplies controlled-Z up to local port conventions. The code checks the compiled gate on every local input at every preparation angle. A sequential set of these rotations fixes the moduli. A diagonal phase conditional on each wall fixes the phases. The packet simulator verifies the resultant one-wall amplitudes and uses the exact unrestricted native Hamiltonian thereafter. A wall-selective phase is a nearest-neighbor $ZZ$ phase up to a scalar. The two terminal walls use an addressed $Z$ rotation. The phase-difference reader selects the two flanking wall locations and reads their strip-flip quadratures. Outcomes are plus, minus and quiet. The recorded sine and cosine components of nearest-neighbor coherence give the initial momentum for each independently prepared sign.

After release, read every physical wall density at local clock exposures near 0, 1, 2 and 3. Fit the slope of half the signed difference between the unnormalized first moments of wall density about the strand midpoint for opposite momenta. The normalized centroid uses the measured wall total for each preparation and exposure. [Comparison of both readings](../submission/row_30_cautions/detector.json) The group forecast integrates $c^2\sin k/\omega(k)$ over the declared source envelope and uses the measured carrier phase. A sharp-domain packet need not have exactly one dressed quasiparticle. For $C=U\Sigma V^T$, put $D=(U-V)/2$. In a normalized one-bare-wall orbital $f$ the excess expected quasiparticle number above one is $\|D\|_F^2-2\|D^T f\|^2$. Fixed odd parity bounds the probability of three or more by half this excess. A seven-dimer physical Hilbert-space replay independently checks the predicted real-space density and this bound. In the exchange-only comparator short endpoint and orientation transients give a small opposite displacement over the same interval.
