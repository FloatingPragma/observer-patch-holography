# One outgoing observer can distinguish the phase of two incoming arms

Cell 6 reads the loads on its own ports 82 and 74. Both incoming sources start with one wall, with equal probabilities of finding it on either of two other arms. They differ only by the relative sign of those two amplitudes. At six exchange-clock radians the probability of unequal loads in cell 6 is **0.592011549424–0.592011549425** for the positive sign and **0.113831304201–0.113831304202** for the negative sign, on one of the two enrolled orientations. The receiver detects the phase through a change of 47.818 percentage points without conditioning on a read in any other cell. An integer propagation independently encloses both probabilities. ## Why the exclusive channel is dark

Exchange the entire B and C arms. They have identical connectors to port 6 of the central dimer. This permutation on the restricted dimer words commutes with the full free generator for every uniform contrast. A word with its only wall on A has identical B and C orientations, so it is fixed by the exchange. The negative source changes sign under this permutation. Its amplitude on each such word is exactly zero at every time. The positive source can reach these words. At 44 radians and central orientation one the far A wall was the only wall on 57,837,293 positive-phase starts and zero negative-phase starts. The unconditional local detector also fires on configurations containing other walls. With the exchange-only law the free evolution factorizes over dimers, and so the outgoing reduced state is independent of the incoming relative sign. These two results distinguish a phase visible through a distant local interaction from phase-dependent joint postselection. A scalar continuation of the strand assigns one particle to one connector, with positive-frequency generator √(m²I + bL) for the wall-graph Laplacian L. It shares the dark channel and predicts a bright exclusive near-A turn probability of at least **0.0015609** at the first nonzero appointment. The bound includes the independent hand error and ±1% variation of each strand calibration. The two material orientations each gave zero such turns in 268,435,456 starts, with a simultaneous upper bound of 0.000000192. There is also a direct deterministic comparison. For either incoming word an exclusive near-A turn requires three or four dimer reversals. The amplitude bound for a specified set of d reversals is (t/2)ᵈ, by integrating the driven Schrödinger equation on that set. Cauchy–Schwarz for the two incoming amplitudes then bounds the bright probability by 2[(t/2)⁶+(t/2)⁸], below 1.87×10⁻⁹ at t=1/16. ## Reproducing the calculation

Run from the repository root with NumPy, SciPy and pytest.

```sh
export OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=1
P=model.candidate_laws.interval_echo.openwave.row_30_collective.fork.interference
nice -n 15 .venv/bin/python -B -W error -m "$P.experiment"
nice -n 15 .venv/bin/python -B -W error -m "$P.certificate"
nice -n 15 .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q model/candidate_laws/interval_echo/openwave/row_30_collective/fork/interference
```

Archive axes in order are contrast (8, 4, 0), central orientation (0, 1), source (b, c, sum, difference), appointment and categorical outcome for the full and remote-only banks. The source calibration uses orientation, source, basis (X, Y) and sign. The hand uses appointment, basis (cosine, sine) and sign. Arrays in laws.npz are cumulative probabilities in units of 2⁻⁴⁰. The summed Bernoulli tails bound the reported marginal intervals jointly. The ±1% parameter ranges are separate sensitivity allowances.
