# Can a single traveling strand wall turn at a junction?

The published OW-30 test asks for emergent massive matter-wave dispersion. Its wording specifies no spatial dimension. The existing [strand](../README.md) and the [platform decision](../../submission/ROW_30_DECISION.md) distinguish the locally measured dispersion from admission of the source and a public acquisition. This separate experiment tests extrapolation of that one-dimensional wall to a branch. OpenWave revision 0df73dc6 and RER revision 9b9f41b2 identify the upstream clause and baseline code. Each preparation below is chosen before generating the counts.

On the registered 20-, 80- and 320-cell carriers choose the same central dimer, ports 6 and 7 of cell zero. Each of the three gateways of this cell has a neighboring central port. Use the lower address on a tie. In ascending gateway order call the arms A, B, C. Each first dimer is the true intercell seam from that gateway to the next owner. Extend each arm using the lowest available private port neighboring the far end and its lowest unused private neighbor. Continue through the far endpoint of each private dimer, staying in its neighbor cell. Compare arm lengths two and three. The arm ruler counts actual connector links. This is a supplied fork with three distinct receiver owners.

Use the coherent connector and idle collar of the strand, one unit of load in each blank equal-degree dimer, and the registered law during flight. All other seams have idle collars. Keep the fork material in every control. Orient a product word with the load on port 6 at its center; recursively require the two exposed connector occupancies to agree. This is the zero-wall reference. A wall is a mismatch on one named connector. Flip every dimer on arm A to prepare exactly one incident wall at its first connector. Also run the global complementary orientation and both matching zero-wall controls. Neither orientation is selected by a pilot. Repeat with flight contrast zero at fixed preparation and exchange. During flight no shutter, pulse or read acts on the fork.

On separate copies obtain all dimer orientations at appointments 0, 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4. Encode the entire wall word and include the complementary orientation. Use 2^28 trials for each joint setting. Read the independently prepared isolated exchange hand over one quarter of each exposure; its first-branch swap probability inverts to exposure in exchange radians. Take two further copies with opposite quarter-turn readout of the central dimer before reading its orientations, with the terminal pulse isolated from the other contacts. Their population difference on the incoming orientation reads its coherence with the two-wall word obtained by flipping the center. Keep all other outputs. Reference starts, sampled orientations, widths, clock and held zero outcomes are included in the record.

Look for exclusive two-wall arrivals on the B and C connectors, first at the junction and then one step out along both arms in the two separate neighbor cells. Compare their fractions against the zero-wall reference over the same material and require nonoverlap of familywise 99% binomial intervals for a resolved source effect. Record the largest difference over the three boards, all times and both complementary sources with simultaneous intervals; separately report both signs and the zero-contrast control. Test the sharper scalar-walk extrapolation of the strand: a wall has direct single-step transfer to either neighboring edge with exchange amplitude one half. It predicts the same leading order for an intraleg step and for a turn. Derive the power of exposure for each exclusive event from the native Hamiltonian. This tests a continuation with the calibrated local hop; derivative junction conditions, other junction couplings, composite excitations and general interacting field theories are different hypotheses.

## Independent short-exposure comparison

The first complete wall banks and source controls are available when this follow-up is specified. Use fresh seed 790302 on the 20-cell fork at both arm lengths, both incoming orientations and the first nonzero appointment. Obtain 2^28 joint starts and 2^28 independent timer starts per setting. Use a single-particle extension of the measured strand dispersion: the positive square root of m²I + bL, with L the ordinary graph Laplacian on the wall sites (connectors are neighbors when they share a dimer). Prepare exactly one site at the incident connector, and predict the exclusive probabilities of finding it one site along A or one site into B or C. Include every run in the denominator. The reference m and b come from the separate strand coupling meter. Allow independent ±1% changes of both parameters, in excess of their reported sampling errors. Bound the effect of both parameters and the independent timer's confidence interval on each forecast using the operator norm. Reject this graph continuation if a fresh observed probability interval misses its forecast interval. This scalar assignment identifies the sharp material wall with a pure single excitation; other source maps, dressing, and junction conditions give different predictions. Also compute from the complete first banks the events of the B and C observers alone, marginalizing over every other connector, so that central postselection cannot produce a remote effect. All reported comparisons use the same conservative allocation across at most 50,000 individual probabilities.
