# Which frequency can the observer read?

## The mobile band

Let N be the number of dimers. In the verified physical orientation space the free generator, up to a common scalar, is
`H = κ(2 × number of walls + number of upper sites) − (1/2) Σ X_j`.
The one-upper words form one translation orbit. At zero exchange the N momentum characters each occur once in this eigenspace of energy 5κ. The all-lower word has energy zero and every other nonconstant word has at least two walls and two upper sites. The all-upper word costs Nκ on the tested rings. Choose the second ordered eigenvalue in momentum zero and the first ordered eigenvalue at every other momentum, exactly as in the independent material experiment. Each moves by at most N/2 under the exchange term by the Hermitian min-max principle. The ground remains at or below zero by the sharp all-lower trial word. Every within-band Bohr frequency is therefore at most N and the rest gap is at least 5κ−N/2. The inequality 3N < 10κ holds on every enrolled ring, so every within-band frequency is strictly below rest even without floating-point eigenvalues. [Material derivation](../../../../../experiments/material_inertia/pair_spectrum/neutral_guide/polar_ring/PROOF.md) · [Formal endpoint bound](PassiveRead.lean)

For an arbitrary fixed effect the terminal probability is a finite sum of phases with differences of occupied energies. The uniform address average commutes with translation. In the selected band the translation characters are all distinct, so each off-diagonal matrix element of this average vanishes. Every joint outcome probability is constant in free time for *every* input in the band. The effect can include an upper-site tag, and the source may be localized or have any momentum coherence. A detector fixed at one address can observe motion at frequencies bounded by the bandwidth. An externally time-dependent detector could supply another frequency. For the enclosure take F to be the stored translation isometries assembled side by side, V the block diagonal matrix of stored mode columns, and D the real diagonal matrix of stored energies. Regard the stored binary64 values as exact numbers. Set Q = FV. The verifier checks both Gram matrices against identity. F is a permuted direct sum of orbit matrices of size at most N. Each V block has dimension d at most 352. For a product with at most m terms per entry and multiplicands of magnitude at most 1.01, the usual binary64 dot-product estimate gives entry error below 32m²u with u = 2⁻⁵³. This follows by expanding real and imaginary products and bounding the accumulated rounding factors by 1/(1−8mu). At most m entries per row contribute. The verifier adds 64m³u to twice the measured maximum absolute row sum for the Gram deviation, covering subtraction and the positive norm summation. Both certified deviations are below 0.01. In particular the least singular value of Q exceeds 1/2. The result requires IEEE binary64 arithmetic with ordinary rounding. [Enclosure code](exclusion.py)

The generator H is assembled directly in the full word basis with exactly representable entries. Write L = 3κN+N/2, bounding the maximum absolute row sum of H and its transpose. Compute the Frobenius norm of R = HQ−QD. Forming Q requires one complex multiplication for each nonzero entry because each word belongs to a single orbit column within each sector. A row of H has at most N+1 terms. Elementwise multiplication and summation therefore give the following conservative allowance on the Frobenius norm, using the positive matrix |H| with operator norm at most L and the Gram bounds above to bound ‖Q‖F by 1.01√(2ᴺ). With M the largest absolute stored energy, the allowance is `64(N+4)u (L+M)√(2ᴺ)`. The squared norm accumulates fewer than 2⁴⁸ terms on these inputs (in fact fewer than 2²⁵). Doubling the computed norm covers its own rounding; a further 10⁻¹⁰⁰ covers negligible underflow errors. [Numerical residuals and allowances](enclosures.json)

Write Q = UP with U unitary and P positive. For A = U*HU−D and S = U*R one has AP+PA = S+S*. The unique solution is the integral of exp(−tP)(S+S*)exp(−tP) over positive t. Thus ‖A‖ ≤ ‖R‖/σmin(Q) < 2‖R‖F. Weyl's inequality encloses every ordered eigenvalue of H around its sorted D entry with this final radius. This includes degeneracies and supplies a complete-spectrum count.

The reference entries for the ground and zero-momentum island are sorted levels zero and one and both are isolated by more than four radii. The zero-momentum assignments also follow from the corresponding column residuals and their nondegenerate spectral neighborhoods, or by applying the eigenvalue residual bound in that invariant character. For each sorted excited energy find the two insertion neighbors of this energy plus the rest gap in the sorted excited array. The smallest separation covers all excited pairs. Subtract four eigenvalue radii and a further 10⁻¹⁰ for the search arithmetic. All four lower bounds stay positive. Therefore every exact rest-frequency term in any passive single-terminal record requires coherence between these two specific levels. The finite data admit spectral leakage from other levels within the apparatus resolution. This classification is restricted to the stated closed orientation sectors and static effects.
