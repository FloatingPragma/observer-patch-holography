# An ordering clock on a mobile occupied bond

An isolated seam with ordered unequal port loads has an exchange-even and an exchange-odd mode. A sharp initial order gives a clock hand, the sign of the final order. The question is whether the same hand crosses a cell boundary with both loads and runs at the isolated rate during free flight.

Use the blank, empty 20-cell sphere and the fixed bridge fixtures 0, 10 and 20 from the neutral-pair flight study. At either endpoint choose the lowest-numbered degree-five neighbour in its own cell. Each emitter and receiver is the seam between this neighbour and the degree-six gateway. Launch from the first endpoint only. Load the regular port with amount one and the gateway with amount two. Before release run just this input bond through one of four free exchange angles 0, π/2, π, 3π/2. The two exchange parity amplitudes then have a known relative phase. The phase resource is local preparation. After release every contact and degree reference is active. No band selection or moving trap is permitted.

At appointments jπ/4 for j from 0 through 48 read the amount on both named ports of the different receiving cell. Count the prescribed order, reversed order, and every other outcome. A fresh cohort measures the same two orders on an isolated bond left ringing through the same exposure; its phase supplies the independent forecast. A separate exchange timer runs through one thirty-second of each exposure and supplies a swap-count time. Use 2³² simulated copies per phase setting and 2²⁶ per timer appointment. Repeat the free flight with zero contrast and every swap path retained. Also erase the source phase by mixing opposite preparations: the four-phase hand must then vanish.

Let A be the mean fraction of all enrollments arriving in either order. For each source phase let D be the prescribed-order fraction minus the reversed fraction over the full enrollment. The complex hand is z = (D₀−Dπ + i(Dπ/₂−D₃π/₂))/2. All misses enter this linear statistic. An arrival hand is resolved if some appointment has a component of z more than seven counting standard errors from zero. An unperturbed transported clock requires z/A to follow the isolated-bond hand up to one constant complex factor per route. On the first 24 nonzero appointments estimate that factor by unweighted least squares on z = C A z_bench, where z_bench is the independent measured phase. Test the remaining 24. Also test the more permissive phase-only law, allowing visibility to vary at every appointment and an arbitrary fixed phase offset. For this test compare pairs of held appointments using the imaginary part of the product of the two bench-corrected complex hands. A seven-standard-error separation from zero rejects a fixed ray even allowing either sign. Retain every route and the maximum discrepancy; use a conservative simultaneous counting allowance in the implementation. Record late arrival rates and visibility without imposing a minimum acceptance during decoding.

The spatial observable is same-shot, joint occupancy at the two receiver addresses. This shows travel of both loads; the histories between the appointments are unobserved. A negative relative energy alone does not establish persistent binding on any individual flight. Compare the full spectral width with the mean energy of the same source referred to the empty host, using the independently calibrated single-load storage energies and the native encounter cost. A claim about inertial mass additionally needs a kinetic response or dispersion in an operational length and time convention. No laboratory mass input enters this study.
