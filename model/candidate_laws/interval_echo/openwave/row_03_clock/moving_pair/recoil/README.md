# How does the moving pair respond to a spatial impulse?

The same two loads that carry an ordering phase across a cell boundary also have a measurable recoil. After one exchange-clock unit of free expansion, opposite phase steps give half-differences of **0.00828 to 0.00838** in outward current across the three launch placements. A separate swap measurement predicts that current without fitting a mass. Each preparation retains at least **84.31% probability of adjacent loads** at every freely chosen time after either impulse. This lower bound and the flow use the same state, law and energy reference. [Protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Full simulated counts](records.json) · [Unperturbed pair and energy](../README.md)

## What the local observers read

An amount-one and an amount-two load start on neighboring ports of one owner. A locally controlled exchange sets the ordering phase. The preparation and 20-cell screen are those of the [three-bridge phase experiment](../README.md). Let the pair expand, apply a spatial phase step to the original owner's twelve ports, and reopen all contacts. Both amounts acquire the same phase when inside that owner. The private references act as exp(−iκt n²), and 1² and 2² agree modulo three. A full native swap, a private phase service and the reverse swap address ports without their own reference. The phase is set at ±2π/3. Split-and-recombine dimer readings of both materials calibrate its sign and sine relative to a supplied quadrature analyzer. A separate exchange dimer reads the scheduled waits. Pulse shutters, path addresses and analyzer phases are laboratory controls.

The first apparatus picks one of the launch owner's three outgoing bridges uniformly. It asks whether exactly one endpoint is occupied. On that subspace a controlled full swap against idle reads exchange parity. A second apparatus on fresh copies, after the impulse, makes the same coarse read and runs the selected bridge until quarter exchange. It then reads which side holds the load. Both pointers retain their complement outcomes. Multiplying their mean signed scores by 3/2 accounts for random bridge choice and converts them to exchange units. The second pointer reads the summed instantaneous flow out of the owner. A third cohort flies freely after the kick. Its terminal address read gives the number of loads outside the owner, including those on separated ports. A detector in the enrolled neighboring cell distinguishes the two loads arriving together on adjacent ports.

At the first placement and age four, the two current pointers returned 7,862,196,774 outward and 7,499,170,462 inward records with the positive kick, and 7,486,903,398 outward and 7,874,329,292 inward records with the negative kick. Each sample had 68,719,476,736 starts. Half their difference gives an outward current in **[0.008172, 0.008209]** to the shown rounding. By one eighth of an exchange unit after the pulse, the difference in mean centre position between the positive and negative kicks was **[−0.000189, −0.000186]**. By half a unit, that difference was **[−0.000091, −0.000087]**, and the intact receiver clicks were **5,737 versus 11,007**. The differential flow therefore changes sign during the flight. Each centre coordinate is half the number of loads outside the emitter. These numbers describe a boundary crossing, in units set by the phase step and the exchange clock. The smallest enclosing intervals at full numerical precision and the zero-age, opposite-order and changed-interaction controls are in the [receipts](records.json).

## The independent prediction

Write N for the number of occupied ports outside the emitting owner. For each outgoing bridge and each fixed other load, let L and R be the amplitudes with the mobile amount immediately inside and outside. Include only states with exactly one occupied endpoint. All other swaps preserve N. The cross-boundary Hamiltonian element is −1/2. With z the sum of L*R over the enrolled configurations and bridges, the parity pointer reads C = Re z and the quarter-swap pointer reads J = Im z. The latter equals the free derivative of the expectation of N by the continuity equation. The addressed pulse multiplies the outside branch by exp(iθ) relative to the inside branch. Thus

    J(θ) = J(0) cos θ + C sin θ,
    [J(θ) − J(−θ)] / 2 = C sin θ.

A second acquisition uses the sine obtained from the independent dimer counts. The two-instrument forecast agrees with the opposite-kick current across all 54 combinations of source age, placement, order preparation and flight law. The direct law check also tests the cosine relation against the sham on all of them. For the native quarter-turn source at age one the three placements give intervals [0.008283, 0.008321], [0.008282, 0.008320] and [0.008341, 0.008379]. At age zero a spatial impulse supplies a common phase to the complete localized preparation and the checks admit zero response. Each interval uses both exact binomial tails, with simultaneous coverage of at least 99% across the registered scalar comparisons. These are finite synthetic measurement banks.

The count intervals come from direct binomial tail inversion at the recorded launch count. The checker verifies both sides of every interval used in the recoil and phase experiments. On the native age-four first placement, the independent dimer and parity pointers predict [0.00817399, 0.00820638] for the opposite-kick current. [Tail inversion and full count checks](../test_counting.py)

The change in energy is (1−cos θ)C + sin θ J(0). Removing the known 240-unit reference, the registered generator is K−32P, where K is positive and P projects onto two occupied neighboring ports. Before the kick its mean is −27. After the kick its mean is −27 plus the measured work and is conserved during free flight. Therefore the adjacency probability at every subsequent appointment is at least (27−work)/32. The lowest simultaneous bound for all native settings and either sign is **0.843198**. This is a probability bound for a single read at any time. Monitoring adjacency continuously would change the dynamics.

Swapping the identities of the two amounts everywhere commutes with the free generator, the species-blind kick and every terminal motion pointer. The three input order preparations have the same weights on the two exchange parities and differ only in the relative phase between them. Their complete motion records therefore have identical probability laws. The ordering detector in the earlier flight resolves that relative phase. A species-sensitive π/2 reference pulse also distinguishes the two quarter-turn preparations in the current meter at age four on all three placements. ## Can the 213-unit formation energy predict the flow?

Keep the screen and all instruments fixed. Remove the contrast interaction during flight and add a uniform material energy (208/3) times the total load. This control is deliberately calibrated to the observed formation energy. It gives zero on the empty host, 208 in the three-load sector and the independent five-unit mean exchange energy on the source. The total is **213** on both laws. The added term supplies only a common phase during flight and in each material timing dimer, so the archived exchange-only flight gives its exact motion probabilities. At the first placement, age four, its opposite-kick current is **[0.04320, 0.04324]**, disjoint from the registered **[0.00817, 0.00821]**. A rest-energy anchor and the timing unit alone leave this recoil undetermined. Assigning a Lorentzian inertial mass calls for a dispersion or long-wavelength force response in an independently calibrated spatial geometry and signal-speed convention. The OpenWave electron criterion also calls for a dynamically selected localized state and a second physical quantity agreeing with measurement after one anchor.
