# A freely ringing record at unit filling

Use the first internal seam of cell zero with an available vacancy reference at its second port (allow either orientation), on each 20-, 80- and 320-cell screen. Place a sharp excess and vacancy in a filled host, split them by a native half exchange, set their relative sign with one vacancy-reference phase, then write them onto their common collar by the native echo. Record the coin without resolving the contrast sign. Both coin values are admitted. The resulting loads occupy the uniform filled configuration and the written record has contrast magnitude two. Prepare both relative signs. Also test the sharp unsplit pair with a full sign record, a dephased coin-energy preparation, and a uniformly filled blank sham. Supplying the initial excess and vacancy, the addressed preparation, the coarse coin pointer and the release appointment are laboratory operations. These instruments and the known separate parity gaps of the evacuated memory were inspected before this experiment.

Open every seam after release. At 193 appointments from 0.02 to 12.02 native exposure read the coin once, keeping same, flipped and blank. No other intervention touches the source during the flight. A separate native dimer travels for one eighth of the unknown interval; infer durations from its swaps (2^26 independent starts). Count 2^20 signal preparations per sign and setting. Fit a free single frequency from 0.4 to 46 radians per dimer-gap unit on the first 97 appointments and forecast the last 96. Test for a frequency agreement within 0.15 with the independently measured excited energy above the zero-control reference, and a held probability RMS error below 0.035. Separately compare the energy mean on the very same coin-localized source against this beat. Repeat with contrast halved during the release and readout, and with contrast removed. Keep the prepared sea and reference convention fixed. Record every arm, including an unresolved constant and the sharp sign case.

Use a separate phase-estimation detector on fresh copies of each source with 512 running-time arms at increments of 1/16. A coherent switch adds the chosen contact time against a parked seam. All other paths share their exposure. A terminal Fourier read reports all 512 outcomes; compare the two modal energy bands using the supplied instrument resolution. Calibrate the increment from a distinct dimer after sixteen service increments. The raw output bins are independent of the target energies. Classify an excitation window by bins above 6 and below 46 in calibrated energy units, plus the exact parked zero control. For the integral mean-energy check use an independent local decomposition read: undo the echo, read contrast magnitude squared and exchange parity, and report all occupied/blank counts. Access to an ideal coherent pulse switch, parity analyzer and the digitized energy detector is supplied. Changing the energy of the whole charge sector adds the same scalar to the initial host and packet.

The two-branch frequency assignment fails if the fixed observer effect leaves the reduced invariant sector, if its signed read is constant, if the held counts miss the frequency/forecast gate, or if the two independent measurements differ beyond the resolution. A free single-frequency law on a sharp-sign input could fail through a second resolved line. Laboratory mass, kinetic inertia and emission of a mobile particle require separate measurements. Upstream OW-03 calls for a self-starting frequency-rigid internal oscillation at rest energy divided by Planck's reduced constant. The move from the mathematics of an isolated collar to a physical rest-mass assignment requires an interpretation of the independently measured energy.
