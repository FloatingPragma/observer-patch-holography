# Free density spectra on two prepared record backgrounds

A sharply released unit reached other cells in both freely evolving preparations. Its spatial density oscillated, but none of the ten tested responses had one frequency that explained 90% of the variation in both fitting and held exposures. An equal-degree, two-position preparation on the same sphere supplied an isolated line. The same instrument recovered angular frequency **7.999982** against the native value **8**, with held explained variance **0.999983**. [Protocol](SPECTRUM_PROTOCOL.md) · [Complete simulated detector histograms and fits](spectrum.json)

At a hop, the departing port loses an arrowhead on the repaired seam and the arriving port gains one. A patch measures the coins on its occupied port's incident seams or on those of an empty port. Initial occupation plus the change in head count gives that port's terminal occupation. The native six-column interaction commutes with this difference at either endpoint even as both terms change during a hop. Shared seam coins can be read consistently by overlapping patches at the terminal appointment. Across the patches each preparation gives a single occupied address. The histograms contain every address with nonzero support and all 65,536 trials at every exposure. The geometry supplies five independent spatial weight profiles through projections of the source port onto five eigenspaces of the screen Laplacian.

Each entry uses the 384 terminal exposures after exposure 16. The preceding 385 settings fit a single angular frequency, its two quadratures and a constant. A negative value means the curve predicts held data worse than the constant mean of that held interval. The noiseless probabilities give the same decisions, with maximum held fractions 0.8199 and 0.3558. A dominant periodic contribution can therefore be visible without supplying an isolated dispersion line across these scales. The positive control prepares a different coin order with just two accessible joint configurations. All 630 seams remain active, and the carrier and free generator are shared across the three preparations.

These measurements constrain the local **density** description on two specific backgrounds in radians per unit of supplied native exposure. Occupation is quadratic in the joint wave, so a sharp launch can contain several transition frequencies even when individual amplitude branches have well-defined energies. A Klein–Gordon mass and propagation speed could not be calibrated from this measurement. The experiment leaves open what a phase-sensitive joint load–coin reader on a suitably prepared mode would find.
