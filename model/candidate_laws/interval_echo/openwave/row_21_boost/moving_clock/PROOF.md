## The measured comoving phase

Write $a=sπ/2$, $b=s(π-0.4)/2$. Removing the global scalar phase and choosing the branch about the measured rest splitting gives two eigenphases $\mp E(k)$ with
\[
 \cos E(k)=\sin a\sin b\cos k-\cos a\cos b .
\]
For nominal exchange this reduces to $\cos E=\cos(0.2)\cos k$. On the positive-energy branch at k and the negative-energy branch at −k the group velocities coincide,
\[
 v=E'(k)=\frac{\sin a\sin b\sin k}{\sin E(k)} .
\]
On either nonzero observed rail the interferometric cross term of the plane-wave arms accumulates phase $2Et-2kx$ up to its fixed launch phase. Along the central ray $x=vt$ its ratio to the rest-clock phase is $(E-kv)/E(0)$. This follows algebraically from two dispersive waves coupling to the same laboratory detector. It assumes no time dilation. For the five-component packets the full native gates propagate all sidebands. Their direct quadrature measurement includes spreading and changes of phase relative to their own envelope.

For four input phases $0,π/2,π,3π/2$ on two normalized orthogonal arms A,B, the measured probabilities at port j are $p_θ=|A_j+e^{iθ}B_j|^2/2$. The two differences $p_0-p_π$ and $p_{3π/2}-p_{π/2}$ give the complex quantity $2\bar A_j B_j$. A phase-erased mixture gives zero at every port for both differences. Summing all address counts separately verifies the denominator at each time. The paper's elapsed phase uses the principal increments between adjacent stroboscopic samples. Data and statistical errors keep each increment within a single resolved branch. As for every discrete clock, phase evolution between those appointments belongs to the specified gate model.

## Radar times and observed current

An outgoing native sharp transfer is launched at time $e=T-|d|$. At the first reachable station the occupied rail is read, and on success a full swap turns it onto the return rail. The two histories are distinguished explicitly. The ideal return is first possible at $r=T+|d|$. A disjoint material ring with two full native swaps per period provides a sharp count hand. The selected times are shorter than its translation period. Its read addresses identify the three appointments, including missed echoes in the separately reported exits. In an exact flat chart for this common signal, the time and position of the terminal event are $(r+e)/2$ and $\pm(r-e)/2$. Its proper-time interval from the common initial event is therefore $\sqrt{er}$. This forecast is evaluated on the observed integer-address event. It does not assume that two preparations choosing this event had identical center velocities.

The neighbor interferometer moves the second selected rail adjacent to the first, corrects the known exchange phase, then applies an equal native mixer. In the native band the small-k expansion gives $E(0)=δ=0.2$ and $E''(0)=\cot δ$. If an ideal clock from this band obeyed a Lorentz law with the measured unit front speed in a whole neighborhood of zero, its quadratic dispersion coefficient would require $E(0)E''(0)=1$. Instead $δ\cot δ<1$ for $0<δ<π/2$. More generally, differentiating $E-kE'=E(0)\sqrt{1-(E')^2/c^2}$ on an interval with changing group velocity yields $k=E(0)E'/(c^2\sqrt{1-(E')^2/c^2})$, the ordinary relativistic energy and momentum relation. The periodic lattice band does not obey it on the full measured branch. A continuum approximation with k and δ small has different quantifiers.

## Counting uncertainty and scope

At most 30,000 individual binomial probabilities enter the two reported families of bounds together. Each is a marginal of its full multinomial bank. Two-sided Clopper–Pearson intervals with tail budget $0.01/(2\cdot30{,}000)$ therefore cover all used probabilities simultaneously at 99% without assuming independence between marginals. The independent pilot selects stations before the phase banks are read. Coverage can be conditioned on these selected addresses. For each cross term, sum the relevant absolute marginal errors along each axis and enclose its rectangle by a disk of radius r. When the measured modulus z exceeds r the angle error is at most $\arcsin(r/z)$. Check adjacent angular intervals before unwrapping. The elapsed-phase error is the sum of its endpoints' angular radii. The ratios use outward endpoint arithmetic on positive denominator bounds. For a shared rest sample its own plotted interval is conservative. The current and wave-number bounds are propagated over all interval corners, with the maximum Lorentz fraction at zero included when required. The confidence describes simulated counting error for the declared preparations and readout. It assigns no probability to the architecture or to an unknown physical continuum.
