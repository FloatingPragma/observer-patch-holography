# Interval Echo evidence for OpenWave rows 4 and 30

Interval Echo is OpenWave’s pinned M10 candidate. OpenWave awarded **partial credit on row 4** after independently reproducing the persistence of a prepared pair at the supplied coupling. The RER scorecard grades that finite persistence test **Demonstrated** and the full row 30 matter-wave criterion **Partial**. A freely propagating mode on a prepared channel passes a finite low-energy dispersion test. The pair’s identification as an electron requires separate measurements. ## Submission decisions

**Row 4.** A second source passed the frozen binding gates. The original one-cell patch registered 4,028 of 4,096 pairs after exposure 37. Under exchange alone it registered 824. At every exposure from 0 to 37 the computed probability of a home-cell pair is at least 94%. On the 320-cell sphere at exposure one the full-screen local probability is bounded between 98.88% and 99.32%, versus 44.78% to 45.22% with exchange alone. [Largest-board size check](row_04_large/README.md)

The independent platform recomputation gives probability 0.980272 for a home-cell pair at exposure 37, versus 0.202906 under exchange alone. The reported counts of 4,028 and 824 are seeded draws. OpenWave’s partial grade reflects the supplied strength of the interaction that binds the pair. [Independent review](https://github.com/openwave-labs/openwave/pull/745#pullrequestreview-6005365373) · [Reproducible filing packet](submission/README.md)

**Row 30.** Selectively preparing individual energy states on the ascending-arrow background resolved five temporal beats. Its zero-wave-number preparation split over several frequencies. In a second preparation, the repeated path through four native ports per cell produced a rest gap. A local material reference read individual frequencies, and two wave packets moved in opposite directions with the gates off. At the highest held wavelength index the continuum prediction missed by 1.18% using packet speed, and a parabola given its own speed calibration missed by 6.68%. The shorter-wavelength challenge missed the continuum prediction by 18.03%. The corresponding shortest path between the same gateways supports 62 modes inside the periodic channel’s gap. [Selective preparation](row_30_selective/README.md) · [Static channel](row_30_autonomous/README.md) · [Scope of the claim](submission/ROW_30_DECISION.md)

## What the measurements distinguish

* **Stability.** At the final 20-cell exposure, the calculated probability of finding the pair on any seam in its starting cell was 98.39%, including 2.83% on its birth seam. With 4% of the starting probability on a separated configuration, the same patch reading had probability 94.43%. On 80 cells the sharp release gave 98.35% at exposure 32. The terminal detector records a seam address or quiet, and the counts include every preparation. The all-time spectral bound concerns adjacency anywhere on the screen. [Pair measurements](row_04/results.json) · [Release protocol](row_04/PROTOCOL.md)
* **A second mechanism for persistence.** On every one of the 420 cells across the three boards, a four-port state has exactly zero outgoing amplitude under the full free generator. Some gateway arrangements also admit an internally oscillating mode with period 2.809926 native time units. Its internal movement was visible at a single port while all links remained open. The compact mode and the two-load bound pair have different preparations and mechanisms. [Compact-mode archive](row_04_compact/results.json)
* **Wave propagation.** Spatial interference gives the momentum. On separate trials, local occupation counts give the frequency as half the beat between two bands. A gapless packet sets the speed and a zero-momentum mode sets the rest frequency. A finite patch timed two held modes with its own repair clock. At higher momentum and larger detuning the continuum formula misses by more than 9%. Removing the contrast interaction preserves the guided dispersion. [Wave and packet records](row_30/results.json) · [Clock-triggered records](row_30/patch_time.json)
* **Free-wave control on the same mobile double.** On a 20-cell screen with all 630 seams active, a three-seam local reader tracked five independently prepared waves. The free generator gives beat frequency λ/2, where λ is squared wave number from the unweighted graph. All five measured ratios lay within 0.00014 of one half. An exact Klein–Gordon dispersion with any one finite rest gap and one common speed is incompatible with this dependence. The finite counts alone cannot bound arbitrarily large rest gaps in the absence of an independent calibration. [Free source, raw clicks and temporal fits](row_30_free/README.md)

## Can the released pair be identified as an electron?

Use the same source that passed free release. A candidate electron must have a unit negative electromagnetic response relative to a neutral control and an opposite-charge partner. A coherent comparison of a physical spatial rotation with a reference can test the minus sign of a full turn and the return after two. A colour-sensitive probe must leave a singlet invariant while resolving a coloured control. Weak response and a stable light branch distinguish it from the other charged leptons. These comparisons must refer to the same preparation or a measured dressing of it. [Species requirements](../../../evaluation/particles.json)

The finite representation table gives search targets before any fit. In its hypercharge convention, the left-handed electron is the $T_3=-1/2$ member of a colour-singlet weak doublet with $Y=-1/2$. Its right-handed partner is a colour and weak singlet with $Y=-1$. Both give $Q=T_3+Y=-1$. To apply these labels, reconstruct the response to independent currents on a persisting excitation and distinguish its branch from the muon and tau. Measuring one centre phase leaves multiple assignments possible. [Finite defect class table and conventions](../../../../code/defect_particle_classes/PROOF.md)

The bare pair responds to a centre turn of the specified local SU(3) current after exposure 150. The turn changes the 31 detector probabilities by 0.196 in total variation. The proposed singlet assignment fails for this current. On the same screen, the patch also distinguishes all five nonidentity relabelings of the three port loads from the original pair, with the degree references fixed. Neither experiment identifies the physical electromagnetic current. [Colour probe and inverse control](row_04_identity/README.md) · [Port relabeling experiment](row_04_conjugates/README.md)

## From free propagation to a matter-wave dispersion

A finite patch needs a reproducible frequency at rest and a measured spatial phase for the same propagating excitation. Determine the propagation speed independently, then use the rest reading and speed to predict frequencies at held-out wavelengths in a momentum range that distinguishes Klein–Gordon dispersion from a parabola. Report local clock records, the spread of each frequency and signals from receiving patches. A second direction or board tests the scope of the result. In the guided experiment the two service durations set the gap. The free experiments keep every seam active during propagation.

* **Bound pair.** A neighbouring patch detected an adjacent pair 387 times in 262,144 preparations at exposure 256. On 20 cells the twenty lowest modes separate according to the arrangement of their three gateways. On 80 cells, 56 cells with the same gateway geometry form a connected region. In both spatial metrics tested there, the first shift from rest is negative and the second positive. This violates the monotonic increase of the proposed positive-frequency Klein–Gordon branch. [Bound-pair transport and dispersion](row_30_branch/README.md) · [Larger-screen protocol and precision check](row_30_branch/REFINEMENT.md)
* **Repair memory.** A single unit at a written gateway changed the repair coin with probability up to 74.49% during free flight. Moving the unit far away suppressed the response. For the mobile double the full 99-state interaction gives a different result: the collar evolves independently of the double's position on that marked seam. The single unit supplies an interaction to measure on a larger set of retained records. [Free matter and memory](row_30_memory/README.md)
* **Phase at a receiving patch.** On a sphere prepared with a sharp coin on every seam, a unit reached another cell through free evolution. Two complementary terminal contacts at one receiving link yielded 57,693 and 16,972 clicks in 1,048,576 simulated preparations per setting. Erasing the joint phase between the load and the seam record suppressed the difference. This gives a local observable for tracking a coherent disturbance even when the port counts alone lose the phase. [Joint phase experiment and controls](row_30_arrows/README.md)
* **Local timing of the same signal.** Two material hands occupy unused seams of the receiving cell and precess during free flight. Each trial records both hand bits and the matter-detector bit. Across ensembles at three independently scheduled appointments, the inferred clock angle agreed within 0.003 radians, including the reversed-hand controls. A hand observed once gives a phase modulo a turn. [Local clock and joint records](row_30_arrows/CLOCK.md)
* **Frequency from positions and joint phase.** For each of two freely evolving load-and-memory preparations, five spatial patterns from the graph were selected before the frequency analysis. Across 769 exposure settings, none of the ten density signals or twenty joint-phase signals passed the single-frequency test on held-out times. A two-state control recovered its native angular frequency of 8 within 0.00002 from density and 0.00019 from the joint current. A sharp source can excite several modes at once. The selective-preparation experiment resolves individual energies on this background and tests their wave numbers separately. [Density spectra](row_30_arrows/SPECTRUM.md) · [Joint-phase spectra](row_30_arrows/PHASE_SPECTRUM.md)

## Replay

In a checkout, run from the RER repository root. In the standalone archive, run from the extracted directory containing `model/`. Use Python 3.12 with the pinned dependencies. The replay checks the saved detector results against fresh calculations.

```sh
python -m pip install -r model/requirements.txt
python -B -m model.candidate_laws.interval_echo.openwave.replay
python -B -m pytest -q model/candidate_laws/interval_echo/openwave
```

The standalone archive carries the required generator code, native boards, run protocols, detector receipts, tests and repository license notices. It can be replayed offline once the Python dependencies are installed. The bundle's root manifest identifies each included file by SHA-256. Broader theory references in the supporting notes use the full RER checkout.
