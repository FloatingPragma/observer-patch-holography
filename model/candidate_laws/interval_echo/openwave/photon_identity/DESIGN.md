# Can independent charge probes resolve two components of the same field oscillation?

Use the charge-sourced, positive ring-Laplacian field on the two complete spherical fixtures already used for written-field flight. Keep every spherical face active during free flight. Each run prepares a signed material source, parks it after intervention, and prepares a separate signed material probe. Keep both source outcomes and both probe outcomes. The input is the uniform ground on the previously verified reachable Gauss words. Time is the exponent of the archived finite field generator in its own units. No extra moving register measures that time.

Choose four source faces and six receiver faces by greedy maximum separation in the spherical face graph, beginning at the original source. Break ties by face index. Assign the first, fourth, seventh and tenth stations to sources. Select each next face only if all required cross-station combinations admit physically disjoint source, parking and readout arms on different originating cells. Keep a count of skipped proposals. Project each source and probe onto the actual oriented private seam it touches. Verify the admissible microscopic ring reversals both before the source and after each selected seam intervention.

Read the signed-current contrast at field times 0, 2, 8, 32 and 128. Record exact instrument probabilities and jointly sampled source and receiver outcomes over 65,536 independent repetitions per setting. Use source signs +1, 0 and −1, separately prepared probe signs +1 and −1, and a fixed sampling seed. Compare the matrix of complete-record contrasts with an independent reconstruction from zero-time head effects and the field propagator. Switching off free flight supplies a matched control.

For the smaller board resolve the entire spectrum of the reachable sector. For the larger board resolve the lowest 24 eigenvalues including the zero ground with one guard eigenvalue. Group a frequency multiplet within absolute tolerance 1e-8 and verify the individual eigenpair residuals. For each isolated nonzero frequency record the singular values of the source-to-receiver contrast residue. Count components with singular value greater than 1e-10 relative to the largest value and greater than 1e-12 absolute. A rank of at least two means the selected electrodes distinguish two source-to-probe patterns at the same finite frequency. Helicity additionally requires a direction of motion, a rotation action and circular-polarization analyzers on that mode. It cannot be inferred from the rank on a finite sphere. A simple finite-volume pole need not remain simple when size and symmetry change. Report the apparatus sensitivity with a deliberately supplied two-copy positive control and a readout that sums those copies.

Replay the archived guided face chains as a separate finite-momentum check. Compare the measured first three frequencies with the published record. A linear small-momentum phase law would give a second-to-first frequency ratio tending to two on these equal-phase harmonics. Retain the complete-sphere and masked-chain scopes separately.

## Follow-up after the two-sphere spectrum

The initial survey found a simple, visible lowest pole on the 80-cell sphere. Freeze this pole as the target for a count-only fit; the spectrum-informed choice and calibration are part of the test. Use the existing 24 station pairs and the positive material probe. Use 256 arrival times sampled uniformly from 0 to 4,096 field time units with a separate fixed seed. This observation window spans about 2.6 periods of the target. Record 65,536 independent four-outcome trials per source sign, station pair and time. No endpoint selection, pulse heralding or rank constraint enters the fit.

Measure the two signed-source crossing rates and subtract. Fit the complete small-board frequency inventory as simultaneous nuisance components. At the calibrated lowest frequency fit its entire six-by-four amplitude matrix. Bound its two largest singular values with simultaneous 99% coverage using the worst-case binomial concentration bound and the actual fitted time weights. Also estimate the lowest frequency from the counts over the interval from 0.001 to 0.008 with the higher frequencies held at their calibration. Record the ideal-data reconstruction as a numerical control. The frequency calibration uses the finite generator; the two fitted amplitudes and the frequency estimate use complete detector counts.
