Two independent probe dimers interact with opposite polarization walls after one load was added to or removed from the alternating material. A sensing rail sits next to both endpoints of its wall. The other rail touches neither endpoint. An excess and a vacancy prepared in independent copies acquire opposite local phases. ## Sharp standards and signed material response

The source evolves for three exchange times on each of the four published circuits. Two observers select a definite pair of distant bond addresses using geometry alone. The terminal herald requires two equal-neighbour bonds with the enrolled sign, exactly at those addresses, and restored alternating order elsewhere. This is a narrower event than the original three-bond apertures. The carrier ports, sensing tips, reference rails and distant controls for every circuit are in the records.

Each probe starts in a coherent superposition of the two paths of a mobile excess on locally empty stock or a vacancy on locally double stock. With ring exchanges stopped and its loads unread, both probe dimers hop and interact with the ring through the native squared contrast. The observer either reads the occupied path or applies a calibrated half swap and reads the path. In the latter case the two-terminal click measures the signed phase quadrature. The eight original-start bins keep the wall herald, its failures and both terminal clicks. All probabilities reported here come from synthetic count banks drawn in aggregate. For a probe path label $s=+1$ (excess) or $-1$ (vacancy), the physical hopping is $-1/2$ in exchange units. Subtracting the two path energies gives $D=-2\kappa s(n_j+n_{j+1})$. The squared-contrast energies of the sensing and reference rails have identical zero-stock and private-reference baselines. The two remaining source terms give the displayed detuning. Thus $(1,1)$ beside an ordinary occupied wall has the same spectrum as a two-unit point stock $(2,0)$ or $(0,2)$ on the sensing bond, and differs by one full point-unit response from alternating $(0,1)$. An empty wall $(0,0)$ gives the full negative difference. The same electrical-unit interpretation for all these source preparations does not follow. A sum of these bond readings around the ring counts each load twice; using a global one-load normalization recovers the half-unit contribution of each wall. ## Simultaneous propagation and the physical cut

Both walls and both probes continue to move after the three-time-unit release in a second experiment. The two original-start terminal rail reads and the wall herald are taken after one common dwell. The combined Hamiltonian retains the native source exchange and all actual tip contacts. Compared with relocating the probes to two distant zero-stock dimers, the wall rate changed in 4 of 8 native sign/time comparisons on the direct-rail banks. In 17 of 32 conditional terminal frequencies either site's moving-source response was resolved from the stopped-source read. On positive source and positive probes, at dwell 1/4 the source and two nearby dimers gave 413,634 joint wall events in 268,435,456 original simulated starts. The relocated probes gave 651,705 and the stopped ring gave 316,595. Source and probe thus measurably affect one another on this finite material. The effects can depend on the probe placement, ring dwell and local self energies. [Live experiment](LIVE.md) · [Joint generator](live.py) · [Held moving-source counts](live_counts.json)

A literal in-place cut removes $\kappa(n_j-n_a)^2$ for each of the four wall-to-tip contacts, including both endpoint self energies. Relocating the probes keeps those self energies on equivalent zero-stock neighbours. In a fresh three-arm acquisition, positive source and probes at dwell 1/4 gave 414,191 joint wall events for the nearby open contacts, 651,335 for relocated probes, and 419,006 for the literal cut, each in 268,435,456 starts. The connected and in-place-cut samples separated in 2 of 8 native wall-rate comparisons and 30 of 32 conditional rail comparisons. Agreement on one wall count can thus coexist with distinguishable probe statistics. Neither detached setup carries an independently propagating field between the ring and a distant probe.
