# Conditional coefficients and observation comparisons

The boundary packet declares the pixel coordinate, unified-coupling proxy,
electroweak scale and renormalization scale. No proof in this package selects
those values from finite repair. The charged-response architecture has
target-informed ancestry. Separation from experimental files during execution
does not establish prospective provenance.

## Charged coefficients

For the declared coordinates P and u, put

\[
 \kappa={u/50\over1+u/31+u^2/310},\quad
 \chi={u^2/512\over1-u/77},\quad
 \zeta={(P-\varphi)^2/(21\pi)\over1-u/27-u^2/135}.
\]

Let rho = sqrt(2) exp(-chi), delta = 2/9 + zeta and
q_j = [1 + rho cos(delta + 2 pi j/3)]². Choose the common positive multiplier
so that the product of the three masses divided by v³ equals
exp(3 kappa)/(2·6¹⁴). Ordering j = (1,2,0) gives the charged triple.
At this packet every square root is positive. Summing the three cosines and
their squares proves Q = sum(m)/sum(sqrt(m))² = (1 + exp(-2 chi))/3.
The code checks the determinant and this identity to 70 decimal places.

The comparison with charged pole masses is an adopted physical premise.
Similarity of dimensionless circulant coordinates cannot derive that premise.
Under it the absolute discrepancies are approximately -70.5 ppm, and the
muon/electron ratio differs by +0.0725 ppm. A common scale cannot place the
electron and muon simultaneously in their printed intervals. Setting
rho = sqrt(2) and delta = 2/9 exactly gives a muon/electron discrepancy of
about +9.8 ppm. Individual tau-driven coordinate uncertainties cannot be
treated as independent uncertainties of that joint two-coordinate test.

Declaring the same native numbers to be running coefficients at Q0 and
applying the retained one-loop relation gives different comparison values:

\[
 M=m(Q_0)\{1+\delta_{EW}+(\alpha/\pi)[1+\tfrac32\log(Q_0/m(Q_0))]\}.
\]

This alternative attachment is a convention control. A pole-shaped numerical
triple does not select a physical scheme by itself.

The frozen boson/top packet feeds these native lepton coefficients to SMDR
as running Yukawas. Adopting the pole premise for the displayed lepton rows
does not also convert those packet entries. Thus the combined table is a
set of conditional comparisons, with no single scheme-complete physical
attachment proved for the whole branch.

## Quark inversion and scale-free checks

For fixed positive A and base b > 1, the equation m = A b^-n gives

\[
 n_{\rm target}=n+\log_b(m_{\rm output}/m_{\rm target}).
\]

Thus an output above the target requires a larger exponent. Substitution
into m_output b^[-(n_target-n)] returns m_target. Applied with all transport
factors frozen, this yields the diagnostics
(u,d,s,c,b) = (6.63023,6.19375,4.52837,3.11653,2.25334).
Changing a source Yukawa also changes thresholds and the charm/bottom
self-scale solutions. These diagnostics are not exact inversions of that
coupled calculation.

At the common 2 GeV convention, the calculated s/d ratio is 35.9999258,
where the rectangle from the two quoted PDG intervals is approximately
[19.3291,20.2160]. The calculated u/d ratio is 1.0046294, outside the directly
quoted [0.442,0.482]. A common multiplier cancels in each ratio, so neither
unit conversion nor a common light-sector normalization repairs this
declared texture. Pure same-charge, same-scale mass-independent running
preserves ratios; the full matching includes small non-universal finite-mass
terms. A finite search over other bases is not a theorem excluding every
integer texture. The QCD+QED source convention and the PDG QCD convention
need an explicit electromagnetic matching prescription for precision claims.

## Common factors and uncertainty

For positive computed values x_i and reference intervals [l_i,u_i], one
common positive factor f is feasible exactly when

\[
 \max_i l_i/x_i\leq\min_i u_i/x_i.
\]

The intersection is empty for all twelve values, for the charged triple
alone, and for W/Z alone. This is an exact statement about the printed
interval rectangle. No covariance or joint confidence level is inferred.
The source calculation supplies no complete truncation-error bound.
Agreement to every decimal of an estimated experimental central value is
neither a measured fact nor the criterion for physical identity.

## Electroweak and neutral scope

The W and Z outputs use Breit-Wigner masses; the top uses the cross-section
pole comparator. Substituting an experimentally constrained reference VEV or
gauge packet diagnoses sensitivity. It cannot prove that a source law chooses
that attachment. The numerical equality of the source VEV with a reference
VEV at 172.4 GeV likewise supplies no scale-selection theorem.

The strong-coupling dictionary requires decoupling before comparison:
the six-flavour source alpha_s(Q0) is approximately 0.11833589; corrected
matching and running give five-flavour alpha_s(91.1879 GeV) = 0.11944066.
Multiplying by a reference-model conversion ratio gives 0.11945742, a proxy
with a distinct meaning.

The [finite neutral theorem](../particle_finite_response/PROOF.md) bounds
the adjacent squared gap ratio by 0.00498720 for the unshifted common-scale
phase-to-mass attachment on its stated domain. The measured normal-ordering
ratio is 0.0310838, with printed rectangle [0.0301053,0.0320809]. This excludes
that attachment. The theorem does not exclude arbitrary neutral response
operators, offsets or other mass readouts. The minimal massless action yields
zero squared gaps and does not reproduce oscillations.

Determinism specifies an outcome after a complete model, initial state and
observation map are supplied. Equality of finite and physical record laws
requires a map preserving the relevant joint records, including their
probabilities and observational conventions. Neither determinism nor a
capacity change alone supplies that map. No particle abundance distribution
is computed by this package.
