# One-link optical timing for the three-bit Interval Echo clock

One home patch timed a reciprocal mirror pulse. On a separate trip, a traveller’s arrival at the far end triggered another pulse. At reunion the same home patch read the traveller’s elapsed word and its own. These records give two independent leg-speed readings for the clock comparison. The apparatus supplies the guide, reciprocal optics, arrival response, movement opportunities and read windows. For each cohort the six means are calibration emission, calibration return, trip start, far-arrival pulse receipt, reunion and traveller elapsed count. Half the calibration round trip gives the one-way light time $d$ under the reciprocity assumption. The outward duration is the time from trip start to pulse receipt minus $d$. The return duration is the rest of the trip. Divide $d$ by each duration to get the respective leg speed. If $T_1,T_2$ are the durations, the predicted ratio is $(\sqrt{T_1^2-d^2}+\sqrt{T_2^2-d^2})/(T_1+T_2)$. The residual subtracts this forecast from the ratio of elapsed cohort means. Uncertainties are one paired block standard error. The first uniform control was 3.08 standard errors low. Its larger repeat changed sign.

Each of the three motion patterns ran on 1,280 and 5,120 cells. The two embeddings produced the same sequence of material handoffs, remote receipt and reunion for each pattern. The statistical cohorts resampled those three sequences with fresh home and travelling primaries. Both embeddings therefore share one cohort per pattern and group. All service intervals in this test fit the three-bit hand. The historical experiment used the same closing-event rule, twelve-step walk and twelve-attempt address cap as the registered clock. The current implementation has its own register and cohort replays on 20, 80 and 320 cells.
