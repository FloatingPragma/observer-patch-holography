# Chiral mass protection and a domain-wall operator

The fractional Wilson source is audited at zero bare mass. The controls
distinguish its energy-reflection grading from a conserved axial charge,
and test a spatial-overlap charge against a quantum electric rotor. A
Euclidean Ginsparg-Wilson action is a separate comparison component.

Declare an even fifth-coordinate length S in {2,4,8,16}, with every length
retained. On each slice use the massless fractional Wilson spatial
operator, h=1/8, r=1. Adjacent slices have hopping
T=−(beta+i alpha4)/2, alpha4=Y tensor Ispin. There is no fifth-coordinate
gauge field: the same physical U3 transformation and the same spatial
link act on every slice. The boundary mass is the actual432 register
coefficient times h, coupled only through P+ beta P− between the two
ends, P±=(I±gamma5)/2. No critical mass or wall length is selected using
measured masses. All bulk modes and both boundary chiralities are kept.

Verify the zero-momentum boundary isometry, the inherited vector/axial
probe and charge map, the spatial derivative and common clock, full U3
covariance, and the exact finite-wall axial Ward identity. Compute all
free bands and the complete boundary spectral measure, including bulk
leakage. A scalar wall recursion provides an exact finite-S residual
bound; it is not a bound on the interacting gauge vacuum. Execute a
small full-CAR quantum-rotor control to separate axial failure through
the wall from the spatial-overlap electric commutator.

The construction is a vectorlike Hamiltonian service. It supplies no
chiral Standard Model gauge theory, physical pole-mass correction, or
transfer of a Euclidean measure theorem to real-time source dynamics.
