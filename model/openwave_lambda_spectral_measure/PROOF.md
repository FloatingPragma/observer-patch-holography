## Finite-resolution spectral certificates

Let mu be the normalized energy measure of the current-success state, m its
mean and v its variance. For a window whose upper endpoint b is below m,
Cantelli's inequality gives

    mu([E-w,E+w]) <= v/[v+(m-b) squared].

The executable uses an outward variance upper bound and mean lower bound.
It reports complete source-window upper bounds at half-widths one, ten and
one hundred MeV around the midpoint of the inherited Lambda rest enclosure.
The midpoint is a declared energy-readout setting; it is not substituted for
the exact unknown rest pole. These bounds quantify possible low-energy
weight, without asserting that such weight is positive.

## What is required for an energy-shell statement

The exact first two normalized moments also admit a two-atom measure at
m plus or minus sqrt(v). Both atoms lie outside the narrow Lambda window.
Thus those moments alone cannot force a positive shell weight. A positive
finite-eta Lorentzian response is equally insufficient: a measure supported
away from E gives such a response for every eta>0. The code retains this
exact countercontrol, the zero-current control and the exact wrong-charge
projection of the literal native current.

The full-source separated-cluster Weyl theorem places the Lambda rest
energy in essential spectrum. It does not identify the spectral measure
of this current. A density statement requires a controlled shrinking-window
limit mu([E-w,E+w])/(2w), or the appropriate outgoing wave-operator overlap.
No unnormalized positive fixed-window weight is called a density. Weak
on-shell transfer, a decay lifetime and physical three-dimensional particle
identification do not follow from the calculations here. All strong,
strange, Cabibbo and GeV calibration inputs are inherited unchanged.
