# What an active exterior probe reads

A patch measuring a circulating load can recover its signed energy current from two exterior click rates. A separate experiment launches test material into a written field and measures how endpoint clouds approach one another. At short exposures the leading approach is the same for every source. The first term sensitive to the source distinguishes two preparations with identical energy and current records. These instruments establish which material information reaches an observer. Relating their readings to null curvature on a macroscopic ball requires a common physical metric. [Exterior current calculation](circulation.py) · [Written-field calculation](measure.py)

## A signed exterior current measurement

Use the pinned one-load generator, the home cell's energy-three stationary sector above the constant offset, and states supported there with zero amplitude on every gateway. On separate copies open seam $(4,5)$ for $\pi/3$ or $5\pi/3$, restore free evolution for $t$, then count the three exterior receivers and the complementary outcome. Let $\Delta P_g$ denote the first click probability minus the second at the receiver across gateway $g$. For every density matrix in this sector,

$$
\lim_{t\downarrow0} t^{-4}(\Delta P_1,\Delta P_3,\Delta P_{10})
=\frac{\sqrt3}{96}(J_{45},-J_{45},0).
$$

The energy current above the offset is $J_{45}=3\,\mathrm{Im}(\psi_4^*\psi_5)$ on a pure state and extends linearly to mixtures. Separate internal quadrature analyzers measure it. The exterior calculation uses only the two pulse schedules and final port flags. The two forward pulses act as mutual inverses because the seam energies are $0,8,9$. If $D=U_{\pi/3}-I$, the two nonzero local amplitudes change by $D(\psi_4,\psi_5)^T$. In the local symmetric and antisymmetric basis $D$ has eigenvalues $e^{-2\pi i/3}-1$ and $-2$. The dark stationary state contributes zero exterior amplitude throughout free flight. For a receiver separated by two hops, the leading amplitude from the change is $-t^2/8$ times its incident marked-port component. The reversed exposure replaces $D$ by its conjugate. Subtracting the two squared-amplitude effects cancels both populations and the real coherence entry by entry. The script verifies the resulting two-by-two operator identity and the local paths on the 20-, 80- and 320-cell boards. [Source, internal quadratures and exterior pulses](../README.md#an-exterior-detector-for-the-sign-of-internal-current)

The error after division by $t^4$ is at most $10368t$ for each receiver, uniformly in board size with these paths. After removal of the homogeneous energy offset the free generator is $L/2$ with norm at most 6. The local state change has norm at most 2. At the receiver its constant and linear amplitudes vanish. The second-order Taylor integral bounds its amplitude by $36t^2$, and the remainder after the quadratic term by $72t^3$. Apply the difference-of-squares bound once per pulse. The exposure bound is deliberately coarse. At exposure $1/8$ on 320 cells a mixed source gives an independent internal reading of $-0.1443376$ and exterior signed coefficients $(-0.002913,+0.002913,0)$. The limiting first coefficient is $-0.0026042$. Switching off the squared contrast on the same graph gives zero paired exterior response at every exposure on this sector. For exchange alone $U-I$ is a scalar times the same real rank-one projector for both settings and the scalar magnitudes agree. The detector counts source material emitted by the interrogation. **Spatial averaging.** A signed seam current can cancel when measured across a whole cell. Let $x_a$ be arbitrary chart positions in a cell of diameter $h$. Stationarity gives $\sum_b J_{ab}=0$, and antisymmetry implies $\sum_{a<b}J_{ab}(x_b-x_a)=0$ identically, coordinate by coordinate. Test the oriented current on straight chart segments against a Lipschitz covector $\omega$ by integrating $\omega$ along each segment. Subtract its constant value at one cell port. The absolute result is bounded by $h^2\operatorname{Lip}(\omega)\sum_{a<b}|J_{ab}|$. In native energy units this sum is at most $15m/2$ for a cell of probability mass $m$, since $3|\psi_a\psi_b|\le3(|\psi_a|^2+|\psi_b|^2)/2$ and every internal port has degree five. Convexity extends the bound to density matrices. Summed over disjoint localized cells, the estimate uses their total mass. It is conditional on the spatial chart, bounded mass and normalization in native energy units. A physical generator or a density normalization growing as $h^{-2}$ would require a separate bound.

## Independent charged probes on a written field

Use the 458-word neutral component of the written-field protocol. The calibrated vacuum word superposition is $g$, uniform and coherent. For head $e=(4,11)$ and ring $f=(2,4,11)$ prepare $\psi_\pm=U_f\exp(\pm i\pi Z_e/4)g$, with $U_f=I-(1+i)h_f$. Here $Z_e$ is the written orientation and $h_f$ the addressed ring projection. These are controlled preparations on the written-field extension. Independent charged probes launch at each of twelve ports. Average the vacancy and excess arrival probabilities equally, including all endpoints and the launch port. In a separate vacuum survey with exposure tending to zero, take $d_t$ as the total variation distance between the two vacuum arrival clouds. For neighboring launches $i,j$, the discrete optical observable is $K_t=1-W_{d_t}(P_i,P_j)/d_t(i,j)$. The cost and generator stay common to all source preparations. [Source generators and calibrated transport observable](../../interval_echo_einstein_station/field_stress_curvature/PROTOCOL.md)

**All ring histories agree.** Each $h_f$ annihilates $g$ and preserves its orthogonal complement. Write $v=U_f Z_e g$. Then $\psi_\pm=(g\pm iv)/\sqrt2$ with $\langle g,v\rangle$ real. Any ring-history effect, including adaptive ring pulses and free ring flights, preserves the vacuum line and has a real expectation there. Its sign-dependent interference term vanishes. This applies to all ring energies, to their transfer currents and to a stress defined by varying only the real coefficients multiplying these rings. Both signs have $\langle i[h_{(2,4,11)},h_{(2,10,11)}]\rangle=53/916$. Independent pulses and dark-port counts read this commutator. The signs may be distinguished with other head-sensitive instruments. [Physical current readout](../../interval_echo_einstein_station/field_stress_curvature/README.md#counting-current-with-an-addressed-ring-interferometer)

**Quadratic and cubic orders.** For $i\ne j$ and a source state $\sigma$ write $P_{ij}(t)=a_{ij}t^2+b_{ij}t^3+c_{ij}(\sigma)t^4+O(t^5)$. At each charge sign use the integer generator $N=2H$, the injection isometry $J_i$ and endpoint projector $Q_j$. Set $A=Q_jNJ_i$, $B=Q_jN^2J_i$, $C=Q_jN^3J_i$. The pooled quadratic and cubic effects are $\sum A^TA/8$ and $\sum(A^TB-B^TA)/(32i)$. Taylor expansion of the finite unitary gives the quartic effect

$$
C_{ij}=\frac1{384}\sum_{\mathrm{charge}}
\big[3 B^TB-2(A^TC+C^TA)\big],
\qquad c_{ij}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}(C_{ij}\sigma).
$$

Integer sparse products verify $a_{ij}=8$ on each edge, zero on all other pairs, and $b_{ij}=0$ on all 132 directed off-diagonal channels **for every source state**. At quadratic order each possible hop has magnitude four and one of the two charges can make it for each written orientation. Each neighboring pair has two common neighbors. Their endpoint overlap $O_t=\sum_k\min(P_{ik},P_{jk})$ therefore starts at $32t^2$ on every ray. Write $q(\sigma)$ for its quartic coefficient. At the two launch ports use $c_{ji}$ and $c_{ij}$. At each other port choose the quartic coefficient from the row with the smaller quadratic coefficient. In case of a tie use the smaller quartic coefficient.

**Transfer to the surveyed distance.** For two endpoint distributions write $\alpha=(P_i-P_j)_+$ and $\beta=(P_j-P_i)_+$. Since $d_t$ is a metric, matching common mass at the diagonal leaves a minimum-cost coupling of these residual marginals. For the discrete unit distance the residual cost equals their common mass $1-O_t$. For the surveyed distance the cost is $1-O_t+F_t(\alpha,\beta)$ where $F_t$ minimizes the residual transport cost with off-diagonal costs $d_t-1$ and zero diagonal. Put $\epsilon_t=\max_{a\ne b}|d_t(a,b)-1|$. For two pairs of residual marginals the difference of optimal correction costs is bounded by $3\epsilon_t(\|\alpha-\alpha'\|_1+\|\beta-\beta'\|_1)$. From any coupling trim excess rows and columns and fill the resulting deficits. The trimmed mass is at most the displayed sum of marginal differences, and the filled mass is at most twice that sum. Interchanging the two pairs yields the bound. The two residual supports within each pair are disjoint. The vacuum launch gives $\epsilon_t\to0$ as its exposure tends to zero, with any exposure schedule common to the sources.

Comparing with the unmoved point masses first gives $K_t/t^2\to32$ on every neighboring ray. Comparing any two source preparations makes each residual marginal difference $O(t^4)$ because the lower arrival coefficients are identical. Consequently

$$
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{K_t(\sigma)-K_t(\sigma')}{t^4}
=q(\sigma)-q(\sigma').
$$

**The source-sensitive term.** On ray $(2,4)$ the difference $q(\psi_+)-q(\psi_-)$ is $123793/1374$. At the equal incoherent mixture the excess above the endpoint mean is $16873/916$. The smaller arrival term at receiver 11 switches at positive-sign mixture weight $54839/71712$, producing a downward slope change of $35856/229$. A fixed one-copy source expectation with any coupling and intercept is affine on mixtures. On these three preparations its maximum residual against the differential optical coefficient is at least $16873/1832$. The inequality follows from the triangle inequality on the midpoint error minus the mean endpoint error. An independent vacuum subtraction cancels from this inequality. Suppressing the addressed ring pulse makes all quartic source differences vanish. The complete census has 21 of 30 rays with a pure-source difference and 11 with positive midpoint gaps. A separate matrix-exponential replay at $t=1/256$ gives endpoint-overlap contrast $90.548\,t^4$ and gap $18.563\,t^4$, compared to the limits $90.097$ and $18.420$. An additional vacuum survey at exposure $1/384$ supplies the full transport cost matrix. Solving the resulting transport programs on those same preparations gives curvature contrast $90.542\,t^4$ and midpoint gap $18.561\,t^4$. [Exact rational coefficients and finite replay](results.json)

**A signed perturbation at a faithful state.** Mix a common $1/16$ uniform lottery over all 458 sharp legal words into each source arm. Every resulting mixture has minimum eigenvalue $1/7328$. Each ring-history probability stays constant with the positive-arm mixture weight $p$. The limiting differential optical coefficient on ray $(2,4)$ is piecewise affine, with slope $218015/1832$ below $p=1901/2430$ and $-50905/1832$ above that value. The midpoint gap is $43907/2748$. In particular $p=1/2$ admits both signs of variation. For each finite exposure the calibrated curvature is concave on this mixture since the positive transportation cost is convex and the vacuum distance is fixed. Bound the two one-sided slopes of the normalized differential curvature at $1/2$ between its finite secants to $1/2\pm1/8$. Both secants converge to $218015/1832$ by the metric-transfer calculation. Thus both finite-exposure slopes converge to the displayed positive value while every ring-history variation is zero. Faithfulness alone does not select a stationary thermodynamic reference.

These are finite gate-exposure limits for specified source and detector choices. The curvature inequalities test one-copy stress on a common source-independent calibrated frame. Restricting the source family, changing the optical construction, reconstructing a state-dependent frame, or taking a joint spatial refinement gives different hypotheses. Null balance on proper balls needs its own operational convergence statement for the proposed source family.
