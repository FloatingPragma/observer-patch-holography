# Finite-bank accuracy for long intervals

For an accepted-record clock on a unit reciprocal transfer, use interval q ≥ 1 and the same local closing-event rule with its twelve signed steps and twelve address attempts. Compare three-, four- and five-bit age, closing and address fields. The five-bit profile adds one moving bank cell to the four-bit layout. The first-return decision has no optical or target-rate input. Adopt an analytic design tolerance of 1/2000 in the ratio of expected moving to rest elapsed counts. The external calibration target per completed interval is √(1−1/q²). The tolerance measures the finite implementation's approximation to that curve on the specified unit relay. Resolving physical equivalence from observations is a separate experiment.

For each profile, compare the exact finite-walk probability from an independent rational recurrence with the registered response for every in-range interval. Use rational inequalities against the squared target bounds. Beyond capacity the local rule posts a positive closing mark as ordinary; use monotonicity of the target to test the whole tail at its first interval. Report the worst interval and all violating interval values, or an exact tail boundary, for each profile. Make no changes to the walk or the tolerance based on the results. The weighted combination over unequal completed intervals uses their durations. The bound refers to ratios of ensemble means and assumes equal fair primary opportunities in the two apparatuses.

For a material check use the same reciprocal sequence 8, 16, 31, 32, 2, 1 on the 20-, 80- and 320-cell spheres, seeds 31 and 47, fixed before the measurement. Require home and traveller to agree on the registered words at reunion, all field ages to saturate/reset correctly, and moving and home hands to equal the sum of their own marks. Check the conditional decisions at 8, 16, 31 and 32 with one prespecified direct fair-bit tape giving an immediate first return when the interval is representable. Qualify finite elapsed readings by the nine-bit word capacity. Large service counts in an analytic rate comparison do not extend that memory.
