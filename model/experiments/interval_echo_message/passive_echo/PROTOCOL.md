# Predicting a clocked bit from two passive echoes

Use the intact 20-cell native Interval Echo screen, blank memories and the established one-double/one-single free register. Keep the three sender stations and both legal native local encodings of the ranged-deadline test: (82,74), (72,77), (182,184). The receiver's signal gateway is port 1. Its hand is the double initially at port 2. Each supplied unit of open-board exposure ends with a sharp binary local reading: is the amount at port 2 still two? A yes advances the home count. The first no expires that launch. These successive local records time the service. This clock uses a sharp one-port hand rather than the earlier two-port sum clock. Every survivor, miss, quiet and default decision enters its original preparation denominator.

Use n = 1,2,3,4 successful ticks at this same cadence. In the message arm, choose the balanced bit after setup by the registered sender-local exchange. Read the single when the clock reaches n. Guess zero only on a single at gateway 1 and guess one otherwise, including on expiration. Also retain all twelve local address or quiet outcomes. Use 2^30 independent multinomial launches per bit and setting from a fixed stream.

On separate range launches start the single at home gateway 1 and the same double at port 2. After n service opportunities a remote local observer makes a sharp yes/no reading for the single at its enrolled port. Flight continues without a return pulse or feedback. After a further n opportunities the receiver reads its gateway if its hand has survived. Count as a marked return only a remote yes bracketed by a launch at home and a later hit at home with 2n positive ticks. Both observers' logs are joined for scoring. Trials abandoned by the home hand before the remote appointment enter as expirations; the prebooked remote outcome on such a trial is immaterial to the scored record. All nonreturned outcomes are retained in the clock/mark/gateway alphabet. Range both bit release ports separately in independent banks of 2^30 launches each.

Forecast the held-out far station's one-way accuracy at n ticks from the square roots of its two independently measured round-trip rates at 2n. Submit the same forecast for every station and tick. Use simultaneous 99% binomial intervals for all one-way gateway and echo probabilities (two port releases, three stations, four deadlines, native and half-strength flight, both services) before propagating the bounds. A disjoint directly measured and echo-inferred interval rejects transfer at that setting; a shared interval wider than one percentage point is inconclusive at that resolution. Use the exposed 52% practical single-bit criterion; classify only when its joint lower or upper endpoint clears that threshold. Assess the changed law by halving every seam's free exchange at fixed contrast, timing and readout, leaving the encoder unchanged. A no-encoding control uses the zero-bit port for both labels; an unmarked return control compares remote misses with remote hits. The original clock's .5-interval pilot and its failures are known. These one-port clock outcomes, cadences, and echo-forecast values have not been evaluated in this protocol.

The input calibration, interrogation cadence, initial ports, stationary observer, mark and threshold are supplied.
