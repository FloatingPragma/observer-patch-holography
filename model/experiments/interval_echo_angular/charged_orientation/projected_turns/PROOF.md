# Rotation signs and the cost of projection

## A bound for every coherent code

Let R and S be Hermitian unitary operators representing two commuting physical spatial half-turns on the complete microscopic Hilbert space. Let V be any isometry from a logical space into that space, P=VV*, and Q=I−P. Define the accepted maps A=V*RV and B=V*SV. Since RS=SR,

[A,B] = −V*R Q S V + V*S Q R V.

Writing ℓR=||QRV|| and ℓS=||QSV|| gives

||[A,B]|| ≤ 2 ℓR ℓS.

The proof permits arbitrary finite material, auxiliary and memory registers, coherent dressing and an untouched quantum reference. The assumption concerns the full operators, not reduced states or classical marginals. Tensoring the whole construction with the reference identity preserves it. If a dressing changes the full actions so that they fail to commute, this theorem does not apply.

Suppose the two accepted maps are scaled unitaries, A=√pR U and B=√pS W, and UW=−WU. Their success probabilities are input-independent. Then ℓR=√(1−pR), ℓS=√(1−pS), and ||[A,B]||=2√(pR pS). Consequently

pR pS ≤ (1−pR)(1−pS), hence pR+pS≤1.

In particular, exact anticommuting accepted half-turns cannot each have success above one half when the full physical half-turns commute. A unit-success intrinsic spinor action is excluded throughout this class. The general commutator bound remains available when a measured map is not an input-independent scaled unitary; assigning it the scalar success formula without that condition is invalid.
