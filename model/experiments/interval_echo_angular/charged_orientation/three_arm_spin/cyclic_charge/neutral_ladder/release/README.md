After the charged winding arrives at port 160, the [registered local ladder](../README.md) puts the opposite stock charge on adjacent port 167. Two remote owners can record both signs arriving together after all addressed operations stop. On a five-owner ring, reading and retiring two original winding heads before release makes that joint arrival frequent. Leaving the heads in place makes it rare. Both runs use the same Interval Echo law, the same two signed loads, and the same receiving ports.

## Why this composite stays adjacent

The free generator comes from the registered 99-state seam, with the filled material energy subtracted. There is one vacancy and one excess. On the blank circle a neighboring 0/2 pair costs more contrast energy than separated loads. A written side exchanges a load together with its directional bit. Once that leaf's initial unit occupation and direction are fixed, its boundary current fixes its subsequent direction given its occupancy. The four sharp initial head sectors have disjoint native supports on both signs. Consequently all charge-position probabilities use the corresponding incoherent mixture even for a globally entangled received head state. The weights are obtained by freely propagating the original three character sources for 32 time units and tracing their unobserved records. [Finite generator](flight.py) · [Acquisition](measure.py)

In each connected component, 28 basis words have the two signs adjacent on the 14-site circle. The integer native matrix gives a row-disk upper edge 36 on all remaining words and a lower edge 40 on adjacent ones (48 on the clean circle). Scaling the off-diagonal entries continuously from zero to full strength cannot move an eigenvalue through 38. Thus there are exactly 28 upper-band eigenstates. Diagonalization puts 99.791–99.861% of the adjacent source in this isolated upper band. The experiment supports a **repulsively bound, mobile pair on this finite guide**. Its free-space mass, decay products and weak-flavor response remain unmeasured.

There is also an all-time adjacency bound. Let $P$ project onto adjacent positions, $Q=I-P$, $A=QHQ$, and $C=QHP$ on their respective spaces. The row-disk bounds give $A≤36$, $PHP≥45$, and $‖C‖²≤20$ (clean case 36, 49, 1). The min-max principle bounds the high-band restriction $B$ below by 45 (49). Applying the Sylvester equation to $Q$ restricted to this band and integrating $e^{tA}CY e^{-tB}$, where $Y$ is the restriction of $P$, bounds the separated-position norm of a unit high-band state by $‖C‖/(λ_{min}(B)-36)$. The sharp source has mean 49.5 and variance 3/4. Its low-band norm is at most $√{3/4}/(49.5-36)$. Squaring the sum of these two bounds gives a time-uniform upper limit on separation. Adjacency therefore exceeds 68.52% with retained heads and 98.00% with retired heads. Using the numerically diagonalized projectors gives tighter floors of 96.81% and 98.73%. The nine sampled appointments have expected adjacency at least 99.56% and 99.49%, respectively.

Retiring two of the three original receiver winding bits distinguishes the entire one-hot basis there. The strong clean-ring signal therefore supplies no evidence that the winding qutrit travels with the molecule. The intact-head counters measure occupation and signed contrast; testing coherent winding transport with the pair requires a joint remote phase analyzer. The experiment identifies the neutral regional product as two separately resolvable opposite loads.
