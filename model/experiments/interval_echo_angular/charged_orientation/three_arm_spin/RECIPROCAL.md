# Independent launches from both common ports

Two owners can exchange their roles on the same three-arm charge apparatus. At native time 32, free evolution carries a small coherent component from hub 4 to hub 160 and from hub 160 to hub 4. A charge measurement alone reports 399,744 opposite-port arrivals in the forward $X+$ bank and 398,956 in the return bank, per $2^{39}$ simulated starts. This bare read requires the local source preparation. It precedes the receiver head decoder.

**Source and detector.** Each emitter starts with the charge at its own hub and its three heads in one of the six Pauli-axis states of the locally one-hot, sum-zero code. All three heads at the opposite hub start unwound. The source recorder erases the path label with probability $1/3$. At the receiving appointment the opposite observer first reads the vacancy at its own hub. Its controlled reverse lap unloads the local head word onto a three-path pointer. It checks both return to the hub and zero local winding before selecting the pointer's sum-zero component and measuring a local basis. The matched decoder contributes a second factor $1/3$ to the accepted code probability. Each controlled lap uses an addressed native sequence of four blank swaps and one written swap. The code checks all six triangles, the common phase, the successful inverse and the blocked-direction control. Coherent control of the three paths and the local pointer analyses are apparatus services. All guide contacts run autonomously during the wait.

The emitter holds a local three-head degree of freedom; the other end's initial head state matters. For each direction the code therefore launches two fresh source basis columns at its own hub and applies the full sparse registered Hamiltonian. It forms the receiver channel from its own three heads. The unequal source-head results are discarded as orthogonal alternatives before evaluating any coherence. No source or intervening record is passed to the receiving analyzer.

**Retaining every launch.** Conditional on source preparation, the law gives own-hub return 0.983886216 and opposite-hub arrival $2.17754524\times10^{-6}$. After both local erasures, successful transmission of the receiving code occurs with probability $1.55220981\times 10^{-7}$ per original start. Each tomography table records positive and negative outputs, the symmetric pointer refusal, failure of the matched head/return test, absence of the vacancy at the receiving hub, and source refusal. A separate bank directly counts the home hub, receiving hub, elsewhere and source refusal.

With $2^{39}$ independent simulated launches per setting, the measured minimum eigenvalue of the partially transposed Choi matrix on original starts is $-7.196\times10^{-8}$ forward and $-7.188\times10^{-8}$ in reverse. The simultaneous 99% upper bounds are $-6.291\times10^{-8}$ and $-6.284\times10^{-8}$. These use marginal Clopper–Pearson intervals for the 72 accepted-bin probabilities across both directions, followed by a Frobenius error bound on the reconstructed matrix. Both directions can preserve entanglement conditional on successful local readout. The low total yield is part of the result.

**Why the two measurements agree.** Interchanging the two hub addresses, reversing the order of every complete arm and swapping the two three-bit head registers preserves every entry of the 3,584-state native Hamiltonian. It maps the two directional source bases and their receiving apertures onto each other. Forward and reverse calculations start at opposite ends and give a largest Choi-entry discrepancy of $8.3\times10^{-19}$ or less. This is a symmetry of the prepared charge-and-head guide. It makes no identification of the code with physical spin. The reciprocal runs use a fixed free waiting time and do not apply an energy filter.
