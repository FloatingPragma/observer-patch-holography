# OW-07 pre-acquisition exchange test on a ternary junction

Use four physical stations joined as a Y, with a fixed aperture admitting its three contacts and their blank native pair blocks. Prepare two sharp unit loads on two leaves, an empty centre and a double load on the spare leaf. Interrogate the centre and spare leaf for one native swap time in a balanced comparison arm, retaining both measurement outputs with their Born frequencies. No outcome is selected by measuring a later exchange. The subsequent pair exposures use identical contrast, clock, and aperture in both material branches.

Compare three-step paths A–O, O–C, B–O and B–O, O–C, A–O. Both end with units on O,C. Check identity, closed once and closed twice, the complete permutations of mobile unit tags and material tags, and a direct swap of the two background ports with both unit cores stationary. Check the effect of changing the source branch alone. Predict every closed path from the cover of the six two-core positions.

Independently release all three contacts simultaneously for times 1, 8, 32, 64 and 128. Use the full twelve-word native pair Hamiltonian, and read the two unit locations on all ports. Compare the opposite-site probability in both source branches with an independent six-position reduction and its reflection selection rule. Repeat at contrast 4 and test absence of core transfer with the swap generator removed. Report the raw probabilities and independent simulated binomial cohorts (2^24 trials per repetition, two fixed seeds 701, 1701). Small finite apertures and simulations are part of the apparatus description.

Give the exchange paths an additional accessible spectator with the same two non-unit load levels. Test the two available exchanges through different spare leaves, and maximize the mean negative exit over all allowed joint host preparations, including entangled preparations. A phase attributable to the host branch, or lost upon this physically available enlargement, restricts any inference of intrinsic spin or statistics for a portable excitation. No continuum or rotation claim follows from a finite junction.
