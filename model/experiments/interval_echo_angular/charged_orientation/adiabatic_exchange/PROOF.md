Use $(|c,d\rangle+s|c,-d\rangle)/\sqrt2$ for $d=1,2$ and $s=\pm1$, and the two original zero-direction labels. This basis choice is independent of time, preparation, graph, dwell or well position.

## Ground transport preserves positivity

Let $H(u)$ be any piecewise twice continuously differentiable cyclic path of such Hamiltonians, with a lowest spectral space of constant finite rank separated by a positive gap. For a large enough scalar $c$, $cI-H(u)$ is entrywise nonnegative. Its exponential power series shows that $e^{-tH(u)}$ is entrywise nonnegative for $t\ge0$. Removing the scalar ground energy and taking $t\to\infty$ gives the same entrywise bound for the entire ground projection $P(u)$.

Parallel transport in this space is the limit of ordered products of the successive projections acting on $P(0)$. To see this in finite dimensions, differentiate $P^2=P$ to obtain $P\dot P P=0$. One projection step takes a vector in the current range to $v+\delta\dot P v+O(\delta^2)$. The unitary parallel transport generated by $[\dot P,P]$ has the same increment on that range. The finite product converges by summing the local errors: projections are contractions and the derivative bounds are uniform on each smooth segment. Every approximating product has nonnegative entries. Its limit on the initial range, extended as zero on the orthogonal complement, shares this property.

At the base point, decompose the graph of negative off-diagonal entries of $H(0)$ into connected components. Within each component attaining the common ground energy the Perron–Frobenius theorem gives one positive ground vector. These vectors have disjoint supports and form an orthonormal basis of the complete ground space. The cyclic geometric holonomy is an orthogonal matrix with nonnegative entries in that basis. It is a permutation matrix, since orthogonal nonnegative columns have disjoint supports. A simple ground therefore returns with geometric phase **+1**, and a whole nonzero ground space cannot acquire central $-I$. An antisymmetric superposition of two permuted degenerate components can acquire a minus sign. The theorem concerns the geometric phase after dynamical phases are matched. It gives no restriction of this kind on finite-speed interference, excited multiplets, negative or complex hopping, discrete echo events, selected readouts, or an independently enacted rotation that changes the basis.
