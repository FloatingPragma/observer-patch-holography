# Can the radius between two material tags supply a third spatial coordinate?

The anchor address and the distance to a second tag give nearly cubic growth in the number of distinct recorded outcomes on the finest three enrolled native boards. The count with uniformly prepared distinct tag addresses grows near the fourth power of radius. At a fixed anchor and measured radius, different unrecorded bearings give different radial flight probabilities. An equal-phase superposition over a shell also transfers amplitude outside the corresponding uniform radial modes.

## Counting the same window in two ways

Prepare an excess at x and a vacancy at y in the uniformly filled Interval Echo host. Both species have independent address records and cannot share a port. Its calibrated routed hand reads d(x,y). The proposed coordinate is (x,d(x,y)). Enroll the window d(0,x)≤r and 1≤d(x,y)≤r. This rectangular condition can be observed without a prescribed three-dimensional metric. On every enrolled anchor the route instrument realizes every integer distance from 1 to r. Consequently there are r times as many coarse labels as anchors. Each allowed tag address at a particular radius contributes to the preparation measure. Selecting distinct labels uniformly would reweight that measured distribution by the inverse bearing multiplicity.

## Does the observer coordinate determine transport?

Fix a sharp excess at home and a sharp vacancy at the surveyed radius. On the three finest boards, pairs of degree-five vacancy addresses give identical anchor, radial read, degree energies and vanishing initial current on every seam, yet different inward acceleration. For the outcome that the excess stays home and the vacancy moves inward, the exact coefficient of t² in the native probability is one quarter of the number of the vacancy site's inward neighbours. On the finest board one bearing has a single inward seam and the other has five. At exposure 1/64 the measured inward frequencies are 0.00006042 and 0.00030529. Their simultaneous exact binomial 99% intervals are [0.00006004, 0.00006080] and [0.00030443, 0.00030615], respectively. A ninth-order native flight bounds the normalized probability error by 5.65 × 10⁻¹⁰, independent of host size. All other outcomes, including departures of the anchor, are enrolled. The coarsest shell has identical inward multiplicities within each fixed degree, and its extremal-source convention selects the same address twice.

There is a further test of an angularly uniform quantum source. Prepare all ports at relative distance r−1 with equal amplitudes, then release both tags. With the anchor still home, the first derivative of the outgoing amplitude at each site of radius r is proportional to that site's inward multiplicity. On the finest host, 23.41% of the squared transferred amplitude within the degree-five shell lies orthogonal to the uniform receiving orbital. For degree-six ports the fraction is 11.84%. These fractions include the variation even within a given port degree. [Shell test](SHELL_PROTOCOL.md) · [Exact coefficients](shell.json)

This demonstrates a relational coordinate with an observed cubic label count. Its natural sharp-pair population has a different volume scaling, and hiding the bearing erases a variable that predicts the next coarse read. The uniform angular state also fails to furnish an invariant radial sector. A different preparation that equilibrates hidden bearings, a different stationary measure or a new observer orbital could yield a coarse radial dynamics under separate hypotheses. Proper three-dimensional volume, a Lorentzian metric and gravity would require independent instruments.
