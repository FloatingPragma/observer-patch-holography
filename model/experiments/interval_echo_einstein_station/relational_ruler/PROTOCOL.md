# Does an anchor and the radius to a second tag give three operational dimensions?

Use the deposited blank routed clock surveys with 80, 1,280, 20,480 and 327,680 cells. Recompute the hop range on their pinned seam tables. Enroll two distinguishable sharp native tags, an excess at x and a vacancy at y above uniform filling. They occupy different ports on one common host. The recorded coordinate retains x and only the radial outcome d(x,y); suppress the second port's bearing. At reference radius r=3,6,12,24 and at its double, examine the rectangular window d(0,x)≤r, 1≤d(x,y)≤r. Report the number of distinct (x,d) labels as well as the number of ordered source pairs (x,y) in the same window, which is the measure obtained from independent uniform-address preparations conditioned on distinct ports. Compare their doubling exponents. This test declares the rectangular window and reads actual graph ranges. It assigns no Euclidean metric, physical volume density, or angular equivalence to the second source.

Within each degree class, choose y with the largest and smallest counts of neighbours at distance r−1, breaking ties by port address; use the degree class with the largest difference, breaking ties by smaller degree. The two preparations have the same measured coordinate, same two source degrees and launch energy, and zero current on every seam. Compute the amplitude polynomial on the reachable configuration graph through order nine and give an explicit norm remainder for the whole untruncated federation. Require their difference to exceed the simultaneous 99% sampling interval and both deterministic propagation allowances.
