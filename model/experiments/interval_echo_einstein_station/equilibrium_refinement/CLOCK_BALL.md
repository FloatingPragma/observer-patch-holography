# A ball selected by round-trip reports

In the one-cell monitored two-load experiment, a central observer times binary messages to twelve named stations, including home. A ball of two outgoing exchange appointments contains six inner stations and five on the rim. On two held preparations **every individually addressed vacancy comparison has the same probability**, at the center, throughout the ball and on its rim. Nevertheless the observed change in boundary completion information is **−0.45815 nats** per unit probability transferred. A pair of rim receivers measures that change independently. ## Choosing and surveying the ball

The observer enrolls the original monitored one-cell patch on the 20-cell board. Each terminal word reports two double loads. A local authenticated exchange transports the central binary reading to the next receiver in one appointment. A separately calibrated material hand times the round trip. On the return the observers reverse the swaps, including any displacement of the other resident. A routed terminal comparison against a blank gateway is read and undone. Its local reading service is accounted separately from the timed message legs. The source is parked for this interrogation; its preceding monitoring and redistribution use the original sharp-word experiment. Both binary messages are tested over all 66 original terminal words.

The clock radius is half the length of the shortest successful out-and-back scheduled relay. A chain of exchanges must cross at least that many adjacent links to carry a changed input. The reported distance multiplicities at zero, one, two and three appointments are 1, 5, 5 and 1. Heralding both residents within radius two admits 55 of the 66 equiprobable words. The remaining eleven are counted as outside the selected cohort. The uniform conditional law follows from the monitored equilibrium. None of the two held preparations has an outside resident.

The volume instrument queries each named receiver for a vacant marker slot. A second acquisition repeats the query on the rim, giving the shell increment of the marker volume per one clock step. The readings can be taken on independent copies of the same source. Each held preparation gives nine available slots in the ball and four on its rim in expectation. Each individual receiver's complete binary law also agrees. Consequently every assignment of fixed weights to the individual readings gives the same profile, including directional weights. This is a finite, clock-defined shell measurement. Continuum proper volume and proper area would require a three-dimensional metric and convergence of the marker measure and shell thickness on those observer records.

## What two receivers see

The held comparison exchanges the equally weighted words (0,2) and (1,9) for the equally weighted words (0,1) and (2,9). The central observer and its clock remain fixed. Each named receiver appears equally often on both sides. The archived permutation carries one mixture to the other and commutes exactly with the full monitored two-load Hamiltonian. The two incoming preparations therefore have identical energy distributions for every spectral energy measurement.

An interior report with k residents on the five-station rim has exactly binom(5,k) compatible rim reports. At the uniform reference its interior probability is that count divided by 55. Every small signed displacement along the difference of the held preparations is feasible in both directions. Going from the split to the spread mixture changes expected log multiplicity by

$$
\tfrac12\log\binom50+\tfrac12\log\binom52-\log\binom51
=\tfrac12\log(2/5).
$$

The interior Shannon first variation is its negative. Independent samples give **+0.46092 nats** from the baseline interior frequencies and two held lotteries. This is a derivative at the faithful reference, measured with differences of reference scores. The mean shell signal has exactly zero change, so calibration on any other matter transfer cannot turn it into this capacity response.

On fresh preparations, two distinct randomly selected rim receivers read their sharp flags. The probability that both are occupied changes from zero to 1/20. With m rim receivers and at most two residents, the expected capacity is exactly

$$
 m P(\text{occupied})\log m+
 \binom m2 P(\text{both occupied})\log\!\left[\binom m2/m^2\right].
$$

The formula follows by evaluating it on the three allowed occupancies. It holds for arbitrary correlations or directional asymmetries when receivers are sampled uniformly. Independent single and simultaneous comparisons give **−0.45676 nats** for the capacity contrast across 524,288 simulated preparations per meter and arm. The joint record identifies the missing correlation. To interpret it as an area change on this ball one would need a geometric response to that correlation that predicts other observers' range or volume records. The individual marker and relay readings tested here leave it unresolved.
