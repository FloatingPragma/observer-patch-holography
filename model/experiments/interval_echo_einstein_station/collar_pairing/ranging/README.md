# Radar clocks on the formed 80-cell collar

An excess crosses the 80-cell federation and leaves two correlated seam memories. A fresh material probe can visit a second station and return to its sender. We measured both legs with a separate exchange clock and retained every missed detection. The memories change finite return times while leaving every detectable propagation front intact. The measured ranges also fail a static radar reciprocity test, even if each station can rescale its clock. ## Ranges with all missed events retained

For each of the twelve ordered pairs of stations, a fresh excess or vacancy starts at the first port. At tick intervals of 1/4 or 1/2, the second port reads occupation until its first hit; then the first port reads until the first return. The clock is an isolated blank dimer initially prepared either on one side or with relative phase $i$. Its two occupation probabilities are $\sin^2(t/2)$ and $(1+\sin t)/2$. Together they recover the unwrapped elapsed exchange phase. Preparation, isolation and the successive inspection appointments are supplied. The two-way range estimator is one half of the mean time to return, capped at a common clock horizon $T$ so that a miss contributes $T/2$. For each copy the two outcomes of every actual inspection are included.

Let $U_{qp}(n)$ be the four-by-four head block of freely moving from $p$ to $q$ in $n$ ticks. The first-detection amplitude is $F_{qp}(n)=U_{qp}(n)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}U_{qq}(n-j)F_{qp}(j)$. A relay detected at $j$ followed by return $k$ ticks later contributes the squared amplitude of $F_{pq}(k)F_{qp}(j)$, traced against the freshly prepared heads. Different detection histories add as probabilities. The full federation Hamiltonian acts on all 960 possible probe positions. No fit enters the clock or distance calculation.

There is an exact reason pooling removes part of the signal. On each unique shortest path the transmitting coin direction reverses when the charge reverses. For both left-head parities the two leading forward and reverse return effects swap as four-by-four operators. Consequently their equal mixture has identical leading forward and reverse probabilities on all six station pairs for any initial coin density. This is a shortest-path statement. At finite exposure the full charge-reversed generators, after flipping both coins, differ by $4\kappa(6-\deg v)$ on port $v$, up to a scalar. This position-dependent term spans 32 native energy units, and the formed coin state need not be invariant under both flips.

For a direct analytic test use a tick of length $t$ and censor after three ticks. A return at the third tick contributes the full capped duration. On the right marked seam the fourth-order charge-pooled differences cancel. There is also a dimension test independent of the particular charge or source. Add any fifth distinct station and take the tick interval to zero at fixed finite tick count. Off-diagonal transition amplitudes vanish at release on a fixed finite board. Thus every round-trip completion probability tends to zero and every directed range divided by half its cap tends to one. Any reciprocal station gains fitting these ranges with vanishing relative error must approach common ratios of one. The limiting five-point squared distances have ones off the diagonal, up to an overall scale. With $J=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/5$, the centered Gram matrix is $J/2$, of rank four. If every off-diagonal squared distance in this normalization differs from one by at most $\delta<1/4$, the centered Gram still has rank four: the error matrix has row norm at most $4\delta$ and each centered eigenvalue is at least $1/2-2\delta>0$. Five such stations cannot lie in a three-dimensional Euclidean tangent space. Thus repeatedly reducing the tick interval with a fixed number of inspections cannot supply a three-dimensional shrinking ruler, even though the cycle ratios tend to one. Application along a varying family of boards requires uniform decay of the completion probabilities; uniformly bounded generators and a fixed number of inspections suffice. ## What the leading arrivals prove

The timing distribution and the first possible power of exposure are distinct. For one inspection on each leg at spacing $t$, the leading probability along a unique shortest path with $d$ hops is $t^{4d}\operatorname{Tr}[(B^\dagger B)^2\rho]/(d!)^4$, where $B$ is the product of the $d$ hopping blocks. The six station pairs give $d=1,2,3,1,2,1$ in lexical order. Both faithful head preparations and both types of probe give the same exponents. In the even sector at the registered contrast strength, a hop on a marked seam has magnitude 4 times a rank-one coin map and the middle blank hop has magnitude 1/2. Moving the left mark to the odd sector changes its hopping magnitude to 4.5. The four local blocks are checked directly against the 99-state law.

Across just the left seam the leading coefficient changes under the ordinary energy calibration by the ratio $(9/8)^4$, while a fixed-marginal pairing change is exactly invisible. Across both marks the coefficient responds to their joint transmission population. It changes by $(256/81)$ times the signed diagonal coin correlation. Its magnitude exceeds $(256/81)(17/1000)$ by the integer certificate below. The two measurements therefore have independent source responses even at their leading powers, irrespective of how modular nats are converted to energy. The effect measures probe transmission through the heads. The shortest-hop readings on the same path retain the graph distance in both arms.

There is a front result for **all** fixed protocols acting on one such collar. Exact rational bounds on the generated state and the product of its true marginals give $10^{-7}\pi\leq\rho\leq8\pi$. Any joint history of a common protocol, with arbitrary supplied ancillas independent of the source and with adaptive quantum measurements, defines an effect $0\leq E\leq I$ on these four initial head states. Its probabilities consequently obey the same inequalities. A history has zero probability on one preparation precisely when it has zero on the other. For a family of common instruments with positive history probabilities, the difference of their negative logarithms divided by an increasing scale $L$ is at most $\log(10^7)/L$. This equality of leading exponential rates holds for a fixed formed pair, or families satisfying the same uniform bound. Multiple independent pairs can compound the constants. Neither finite time delays nor their subleading long-range effects are fixed by the support result.

The [rational certificate](certificate.py) implements the formation on all 3,840 probe-position and head states. Twice the native real generator, after subtracting a scalar, has integer entries and absolute row sums at most 41. Each of 128 unitary steps at $1/32$ is approximated by its degree-18 Taylor polynomial, with 54-bit fractional amplitudes and a checked integer overflow bound at every multiplication. A rounded Taylor term costs at most $\sqrt{2\cdot3840}/2^{54}$ in vector norm and its later terms in the step amplify this by less than three. The Taylor remainder uses unitarity of the exact generator and the checked input norm bound two. Summing local errors gives a final state-vector error below $3.377\times10^{-11}$. The trace-norm errors of the head density and its product of marginals are bounded by three and seven times this error. Exact Hermitian principal minors establish the comparison above and a lower bound $10^{-9}$ on the least populated head eigenstate. The numerically estimated extremal probability ratios are $4.06\times10^{-7}$ and 7.43776.

These experiments establish material clock readings on one board. Equal-volume proper balls, their spatial curvature, the complete stress tensor and a universal gravitational coupling require further source identification.
