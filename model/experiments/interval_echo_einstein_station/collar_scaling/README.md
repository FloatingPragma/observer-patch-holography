# Does a measured native collar charge scale as the fourth power of radius?

Two correlated written words give a nonzero fourth-order charge on a family of shrinking native clock regions. The spatial extent of their preparation controls where that charge can be detected. With independent preparation bits in the 20 ancestor cells, distant regions have zero mixed charge, while two nearby, disjoint regions sharing an ancestor retain a measurable mixed charge. The two word-energy sums keep their original joint distribution. [Primary protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Finite correlation range protocol](ANCESTOR_PROTOCOL.md)

## A ruler and a collar on one material preparation

A protected word consists of two of the registered Interval Echo seams at one local port. The three-level writer gives each seam its measured native alternatives of 0, 32 and 33 energy units. The same proper cell rotation moves the word centre to port 4. A parked load at port 4 stays outside every routed clock path. All four enrolled boards use the same internal template, and port 4 has no external link on the enrolled boards. The addressed swaps avoid port 4 in every cell. Thus the whole ruler survey and the two source memories can coexist. A separate one-swap pulse calibration establishes the hand time before the regional experiment. Terminal sharp word measurements occur after the route schedule. Every written mode, including the zero-energy word, lies orthogonal to the native blank memory. The blank-memory projector commutes with the local generator. This supplies the word-presence outcome. The prescribed protected route, writer, cell and slot addresses, tag assignment and comparison reference are parts of the apparatus.

At each scale, enrol one station (port 0) per cell. Distances are the shortest numbers of clocked blank swaps joining two stations while bypassing the stored memories. The farthest station defines the reference range. An observer reads the regional algebra consisting of both local word slots whose station ranges lie in the annulus (r/2,r]. The enrolled hosts have 80, 1,280, 20,480 and 327,680 cells. Their radii in swap units are 6, 12, 24 and 48. The corresponding maximum station ranges are 31, 127, 511 and 2,047. An independent shortest-path comparison on the full, unreduced 80-cell board checks three launch surveys.

For distinct uniformly selected source cells, a common or a reversed fair tag changes only the joint word law. If the observer sees one word, its law is identical to the fresh-tag case. If both arrive, the observer uses the connected modular log score at an independently prepared reversed-tag reference. The calibrated shared-minus-fresh conditional charge is 0.003305124 nats, against 0.003305128 from the source law. Reversal changes the sign of this contrast. Choosing the product and shared microscopic references would give zero and −0.002948370 nats, respectively. These alternatives quantify the dependence on reference choice. A variational stress needs a reference selected by physical criteria independently of the response.

## Scaling and spatial extent

Let N be the number of source stations and n the population of an annulus. For distinct uniformly placed words with a global tag, the population charge is the calibrated conditional contrast multiplied by n(n−1)/(N(N−1)). For a pair of separately written words in the same cell the multiplier is n/N. Selecting independent fair tags on different level-zero ancestors replaces the numerator of the first expression by Σᵍ nᵍ(nᵍ−1), where nᵍ counts the admitted stations belonging to ancestor g. An ancestor-conditional reversed reference has a zero connected modular score between words with different ancestors. Separate calibration and acquisition records include every absent, single and joint outcome. ## Can these same records set the acceleration scale without rotation data?

The per-cut theory uses a number n of independent gravitating cuts per proper length, a variance of anomalous mass per cut V(Mᵦ)=c Mᵦ, and the Newton coupling G. In SI units n has units of m⁻¹ and c has units of kg, so G n² c has units of acceleration. The native collar read supplies dimensionless log probabilities, whose variances are quoted in nats². The native energy meter supplies seam exchange units. An independent ordinary baryonic mass calibration and a conversion from the modular defect to gravitating mass are both necessary to measure c.

A retrospective sharp-energy comparison tests whether these particular native fields can determine a per-cut variance slope. Use the reversed-tag microscopic modular reference calibrated in this experiment and all eligible outcomes in independent archived word cohorts. Restrict the two regional word energy sums to (32,32) or (65,65) in exchange units. Within either bin, the two allowed orientations for each word have identical relabelled probabilities and connected modular scores at the population reference. The exact variances of the connected defect are **0.00690305, 0.00800701 and 0.00900036 nats²** for reversed, fresh and shared histories, respectively. Thus changing the tags changes the variance at exactly fixed measured energy. Changing the matched total stored energy from 64 to 130 leaves the three population variances unchanged. Counts give 0.00690293, 0.00800780 and 0.00900175 nats² at (32,32), with single-cohort sampling standard errors of 0.00000060–0.00000069 conditional on the frozen calibration. These cuts give no positive linear slope of this variance against this energy that transfers between the two sharp bins and tag histories. Local word energy and ordinary baryonic rest mass are distinct readouts.

The radial cut count also admits a direct native proxy check. Count distinct routed radial bins containing an actual written word. In an annulus of nₛ out of N stations the expected number of admitted marks is 2nₛ/N. The count of their distinct radii can fall below it by at most nₛ(nₛ−1)/(N(N−1)), the probability that both distinct marks enter. On the finest board the resulting density is between **0.15194 and 0.15201** occupied radii per routed home-range unit in (r/2,r]; in the adjacent doubled annulus it is between **0.30140 and 0.30194**. These ranges describe the actual two-word population under the specified sampling law. They do not identify the nested gravitating cuts or their density per proper metre. No physical acceleration scale is recoverable from these inputs alone. A fit to rotation data can calibrate a conditional external prediction, with the fitted galaxies excluded from the independent test.
