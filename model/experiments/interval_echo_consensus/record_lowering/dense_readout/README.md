# Which publications can a dense observer actually read?

Sixty-four Interval Echo observers each receive every independent bit in their assigned finite ball. They retain the preceding version and recover new publications after a change at the boundary. Two consecutive layers each contain 1,192 binary readings on both the 80- and 320-owner federations. The desired ball cannot be recovered from a common arrival deadline on either mount, even allowing all 64! simultaneous reassignments of the chart labels. **PR-174 is Partial.** The local intervention and lossless readout clauses hold on these finite mounts. The physical dense radius, layer duration and horizon remain open. [Frozen design](PROTOCOL.md) · [Complete simulated transcripts](records.json.gz)

## How a receiver obtains its tuple

The site labels form a 4 by 4 by 4 cube with spacing L/4. On each federation the labels occupy owners zero through 63 in lexicographic order. Within each site two native dimers encode the published bit and its cache by 20 or 02 on their rails. A separate receiving head and zero standard occupy a third native seam. The six ports avoid gateways. The unreserved ports remain connected. All binary variations preserve the total load on each dimer.

Before the first layer the actual head reads the prior bit and its outcome controls the full swap on the cache dimer initialized to zero. At each publication boundary a local π exchange or equal-length closed slot sets the desired public word. Each of the 64 choices touches only its own dimer. Previously received copies, cache dimers and transit loads retain their values. The local editing knob is an intervention input. During a survey the sender's primary rail travels along a shortest permitted route of blank contact swaps. The receiver writes the echo on the head and zero standard, reads the balanced outcome, undoes the write and reverses the route. On each allowed sharp input the calibrated projection returns the bit exactly. The inverse restores all ports and the blank collar. Disjoint readings can overlap. The source, guides, closed slots, pointer and read calendar are supplied equipment. [Native calibration](../hardware.py) · [Physical compiler](apparatus.py)

A receiver's complete record includes its own publication knob, the retained prior copy, local acknowledgments, addressed swaps, pulse times, all projected bits and quiet outcomes. The path, timing and metadata of the receiving survey are fixed before the chosen word. Its final projection sequence alone decodes its requested tuple. A bit does not have to leave a trace in remote source-side logs for the receiver to recover it. The machine receipts retain both epochs, intervention masks (separate from receiver lamps), every history of projected outcomes, the complete paths and their start and finish slots. Expanding a path yields its forward swaps, the three local read operations and its reverse swaps. Independent bitplane simulation executes the sharp boundary swaps and both entire surveys; symbolic source labels cover all 2⁶⁴ words per layer. These are deterministic simulated native records. [Physical and symbolic replays](measure.py) · [Independent checks](test_dense.py)

A site requests squared grid distance at most four, including 192 directed pairs exactly on the radius. Eight corner sites have 11 inputs apiece, 24 sites have 15, 24 have 21, and eight have 30. At fixed values of all other bits and caches, any one receiver with k inputs therefore distinguishes 2ᵏ possible publications from its own reply. Each source enters its own ball, so the joint set of replies distinguishes the entire word. Keeping a common prior first layer and varying one second-layer remote publication changes exactly the called-for projection at each receiving owner. Across a layer there are 1,128 such remote source–receiver pairs. Routing the stale rail instead, or parking the transport while the pointer reads its original head, produces a collision on every one of those pairs. The receiver's own intervention setting, both local times and its retained prior history also coincide on each collision. [Formal local intervention and capacity bounds](DenseRead.lean)

## An independent arrival clock

The radius rule above is a requested menu. To test it as a prediction, release every one of the 4,096 site pairs on separate enrolments and read the earliest projection along the calibrated protected shortest path. For d blank exchanges that appointment is native slot d+2. A common positive duration for each swap and a fixed write/read overhead preserve this ordering for any native calibration. The corresponding serial and parallel costs below include inverse routing and restored references. Each is measured in appointments on the supplied calendar. The horizon L/c would require an independently calibrated metric and time.

All 64 individual receivers also have an outsider that arrives no later than one requested insider, on both mounts. The endpoint records can be assembled by the scheduler even though these physically timed arrivals reject the proposed radius. Each allowed path's distance follows from ordinary native adjacency and the protected ports, and its single-cohort clock uses the same sequence of operations as its census read.

After observing those results we froze a test of the placement itself. The sorted receiver degrees of the prescribed graph are invariant under relabeling. For a fixed cutoff the sum of absolute differences between the two sorted degree lists lower-bounds the number of wrong ordered edges. Sorting minimizes that sum over all pairings. Each unordered pair with a signal timely in just one direction also forces at least one error against a symmetric ball. Even the best deadline gives lower bounds of **130 and 138 wrong ordered reads** respectively, under every common permutation of the 64 enrolled stations. The specific best cutoffs for this lower bound are 15 and 16. New hardware or different transport can give different outcomes. [Predesign for the relabeling test](PLACEMENT.md) · [All cutoff certificates](placement.py)

There is also a structural restriction on building the cube from independent owner corridors. Both registered owner graphs are planar. A three-by-three-by-two subgrid has three disjoint horizontal rows on one floor and three disjoint columns on the other. Nine unit vertical edges join every row to every column. Contraction yields the nonplanar graph K₃,₃. Even with a new placement, realizing all nearest grid adjacencies by internally owner-disjoint corridors that avoid the other terminals would give this forbidden minor. Shared owners, separate head channels within an owner and serial routing can realize other experiments. This argument concerns independent corridors on the owner graph.
