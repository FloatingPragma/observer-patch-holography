# What survives an unknown number of continuations?

The one-swap decoder was frozen for a specific continuation menu. In this follow-up leave every one of the seven installed tree exchanges available after each use, and accept an arbitrary finite native word with no label or length disclosed to the terminal observer. Test this extension against the measured single-step tables. Show finite closure, including the identity, under each admitted repeat. For each of 256 physically prepared source words, deposit a receiver-certified route to each word of its own boundary sector. Test the entire 128-element group of reachable permutations by composing the measured single-step maps; a proof by the labeled edge group must establish that this accounts for unbounded word length. Under one fixed public source-parity preparation, predict one zero-error message. When both sectors are prepared, predict two (one invariant bit); the result must include all inputs in both fibers.

Then admit repeated transfer on the eighth, exterior edge, withholding the exterior rail from the decoder as before. Independently acquire its full-swap transfer on all 256 source words using both receiving stations and both possible outer-rail loads; the native unitary acts in both orientations. Predict merger of the two complete confusability classes. As a control let the receiving stations retain copies of all exchange labels; the terminal word plus that label sequence must recover the source exactly. Produce the quotient counts, distinguish the enacted one-swap menu from the closed-under-composition menu, and do not evaluate a trial-universe capacity or cosmological selection formula.
