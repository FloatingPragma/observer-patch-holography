# Compatible measurements of one load

On one twelve-port Interval Echo cell, encode a four-dimensional input as one mobile unit on the first four sites of a path through the cell. All seam memories are blank. Open individual contacts and degree-reference terms for prescribed real durations during protected measurement windows. The terminal sharp address is exhaustive. Under these controls every pair of complex positive matrices \(E,F\) with \(E+F\le I_4\) has disjoint events in a single native instrument. This includes noncommuting pairs and singular effects. The word *compatible* here means that the two effects can label disjoint outcomes of one measurement. ## Physical construction

Stack the three matrices \(\sqrt E,\sqrt F,\sqrt{I-E-F}\) into a twelve-by-four column isometry \(V\). Its three four-port blocks have the requested Gram matrices. Adjacent complex row elimination reduces any such isometry to four occupied coordinate columns. Eliminate below the diagonal from the bottom of each column. Phase the two entries to be real and nonnegative, and rotate them by a real two-port rotation so the lower one vanishes. Each step preserves the zeros in earlier columns. Orthonormality makes the four resulting diagonal entries unit phases. Reversing the steps implements the entire isometry simultaneously on every input and on inputs entangled with untouched registers. A zero pair needs no rotation. At most 38 adjacent contacts suffice because the four columns require at most 11, 10, 9 and 8 eliminations.

The selected physical path is 0, 1, 5, 4, 2, 3, 9, 8, 6, 7, 10, 11. All adjacent pairs are native seams. On an addressed blank seam the one-position Hamiltonian is \(8.5 I-X/2\), and the empty word is fixed. An isolated degree-five reference contributes \(8n^2\) and supplies any single-port phase modulo a full turn. Conjugating a partial exchange by two such phases gives the real rotation, with its common endpoint phase also removed. Pulse durations are circuit settings. With finite pulse precision a telescope bounds the isometry error by the sum of the primitive operator errors, and the effect error by twice that sum.

## Why equal effects have equal record weights

In this encoded experiment an implemented event is a set of terminal ports. If \(P\) selects it, its matrix is \(V^*PV\). Suppose two circuits have the same event matrix. Their selected row blocks have the same Gram matrix, as do their complementary row blocks. Sending corresponding columns to one another defines an isometry on their spans, even at deficient rank, because the squared norm of every column combination is fixed by the Gram matrix. Extend it to an output-basis rotation within each block. Blocks of different sizes can first concentrate their occupied span on at most four rails each, omit certain-empty outcomes and permute the output addresses. A hardware swap at exposure π relabels the address exactly in this sector.

A rotation inside one output block leaves the sharp address law of the other block invariant. One load is certainly recorded somewhere, so the probability of each block is invariant as well. These are properties of the committed sharp position reader, local unitary actions and complete event probabilities. The unitary action on prepared states also makes different implementations of the same terminal isometry equivalent. In the declared quantum model one verifies these properties directly by commuting a disjoint pointer with the output unitary. The argument needs only these properties of the readout for the quotient step. It gives a single probability for each effect across all the above coherent terminal-read circuits. Disjoint event unions are additive by finite counting. Together with the three-block construction, the hypotheses of the finite operational Busch–Gleason theorem hold on the entire encoded complex effect space. Each preparation consequently has one representing density matrix there. The model supplies the complex register, coherent operations and local sharp readout semantics. Finite synthetic counts provide a check of those operations. The conclusion concerns this prepared sector and measurement grammar.
