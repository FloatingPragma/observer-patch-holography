# A common cooling threshold for measured material energies

A guarded two-port seam has two measurable stationary modes separated by one native energy unit. At the next spatial subdivision an observer can distinguish four times as many locations for the same mobile excitation. The entire canonical distribution still projects exactly to the preceding one. An observer with finer address resolution therefore uses the same inverse-temperature threshold to bound the excited fraction. The ground-space entropy grows with address resolution, so the uniform entropy limit concerns the excess above each stage's own zero-temperature Gibbs entropy. ## What the observer prepares and measures

On each cell reserve data ports A,B and a vacant neighbouring helper C with a vacant reference D. All three links AB, BC and CD are native internal contacts. An exact finite layout check supplies such a path avoiding any three gateway ports. During free flight open AB at every owner and park the other terms. In the enrolled sector one double is on a data port somewhere on the board, and all other ports are vacant. On each pair of address states the actual blank-seam Hamiltonian is 32 I + (I−X)/2. Its symmetric and antisymmetric modes thus have energies 32 and 33. Separate sharp A launches read out B at native times 0, π/3, π/2, π and 3π/2. The predicted transfer probabilities are 0, 1/4, 1/2, 1 and 1/2. On 320 cells the measured transfers were 0, 16,390, 32,932, 65,536 and 32,573 per 65,536 simulated launches. The clock reports native exposure units.

The energy reader applies a quarter phase to B and a half-exchange on AB. This maps the two stationary states to A and B up to phases. Apply a half-exchange between B and C and check C against vacancy D with the registered balanced-pair pointer, then undo the pointer echo. A C click rejects the launch. After a dark reading C is again vacant. The ground state always survives and the excited state survives with probability 1/2. Repeating this fixed instrument b times and undoing the decoder gives, on a uniform mixture of input rails and addresses, a state proportional to the ground projector plus 2⁻ᵇ times the excited projector. The test reconstructs the full conditional density, including its coherences. Reading the energy again and counting every leak time on the original denominator tests the energy selection. Counts were simulated from the native gates on each sharp input stratum. No Gibbs weights enter the sampler. [Density and detector tests](test_band.py)

At b=6 the ground/excited accepted counts were 20,492/343, 81,929/1,275 and 327,634/5,044 on 20, 80 and 320 cells, respectively. Original launch counts were 40,960, 163,840 and 655,360. The excited fraction among survivors is predicted to be 1/65 at every size. With the filter parked the 320-cell terminal detector gave 327,217 upper-label outcomes. With the exchange term removed throughout it gave 327,680, and the native free-flight oscillation disappeared. In the latter control the energy levels coincide. All filter depths also pass a joint 99% test using the independently sampled one-appointment calibration. [Complete per-owner counts](records.json)

## Why one threshold works at every refinement

At level l there are K=20·4ˡ owners and 2K energy-and-address records. A finer record maps to its parent address while preserving its energy label. These maps compose and preserve the designated ground record at address zero. On the measured boards grouping the four actual owners recovers every coarse intercell adjacency. The mathematical extension uses the same four-child triangular subdivision at each later level. A path exists at each child for every allowed gateway layout. The map from levels to integer owner capacities l ↦ 20·4ˡ is an order embedding, cofinal among finite requested capacities, with no maximal capacity. The carrier cardinal grows strictly. The simulations cover the first three levels.

Each mode has multiplicity K. For β ≥ 0 in inverse native energy units, the excited fraction is p=1/(1+exp β). A parent receives four children of weight one fourth its own for either mode, at every β. Hence p ≤ exp(−β) independently of K. The canonical entropy in nats is log K + h(p), with h(p) the binary entropy. Its excess is log(1+exp(−β))+βp ≤ (1+β)exp(−β), again uniformly in K. The experiment realizes the cofinal sequence β=b log 2. This sequence suffices for the cooling limit. Both analytic bounds apply to all real β ≥ 0. These data instantiate the cofinal family, energy-compatible restriction, designated ground and uniform tail named in PR-08 on this particular material sector.
