# Heat flow through a chain of guarded dimers

Use one, two and four guarded energy dimers and two independently prepared boundary dimers on distinct owners of the 20-cell board. The finite thermometer assigns energy gap one to its two sharp signs. Every counted appointment draws one uniform tag from the smallest power of two at least three and at least the number of bonds. Give one tag to each end and each interior bond, and park the rest. On an interior offer exchange both rails. At either boundary make one fresh native filter proposal. Record a refusal as an idle appointment, or swap the accepted dimer with the endpoint and read both signs. Record the signed energy lost by the left source. In a control park the right swap while retaining its tags and filter readouts.

Before reading the transport samples, fix the leak-rail pulse durations by cosine-squared survival odds of 1/2, 5/8, 2/5, 9/16 and 4/9. Measure each supply with a separate thermometer bank. Each chain register initially uses an independent baseline proposal retried to its first acceptance. Count all refusals. Use equal sources for fluctuations; in the driven banks exchange the hotter and colder sources, once at each of the two spacings about the baseline log odds. Start every driven bank from the baseline, run 1024 appointments, and read the next appointment. Also test the disconnected right end at the larger imbalance.

At equilibrium retain the joint histograms of the initial left current and the left current at each of the following 64 appointments. Compare their Green–Kubo sum, including half the zero-lag fluctuation, to a finite-chain calculation with an explicit discarded-tail bound. Use an independent forward and reverse driven bank for each signed slope, and independent calibrations to infer the thermal bias. Give simultaneous 99% uncertainty, keeping dependent lags together. Nonconservation in a completed transfer, absence of the predicted negative first-lag correlation, a wrong signed response, or a measured conductance incompatible with the finite-bias and finite-time bounds rejects the corresponding identification. A one-sided contact must retain short-time noise but have zero steady conductance. Tag probabilities, contact timing and reservoir preparations are apparatus. Chain distance counts intervening register bonds.

Use one fixed generator seed, 2^26 calibration proposals per setting and 2^37 independent paths per transport bank, drawn by multinomial counts of identical finite records. Save raw calibration counts, source preparation failures, total offers including refusals, and all joint current counts used by the tests. This compressed experiment uses at most sixteen simultaneous chain words per initial current label.
