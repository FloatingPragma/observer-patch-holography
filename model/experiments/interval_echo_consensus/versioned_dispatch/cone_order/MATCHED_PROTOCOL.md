# A matched pair of causal comparisons

The four-contact midpoint census motivates a separate experiment in which the geometric comparison itself receives identical inputs in two different native histories. Choose distinct disjoint seams A and C in lexicographic order, with a seam B joining one endpoint of A to one endpoint of C. Choose X disjoint from A, B and C. Choose E disjoint from A meeting C at exactly the same end as B. Choose D disjoint from A and C meeting E. Within each word require four distinct seams; choose the first admissible tuple in (A,C,B,X,E,D) order.

Run the equal-length schedules (A,X,B,C) and (A,D,E,C), first on a uniformly empty work cell, then with a double prepared at both ends of A and no other input change. The two station heads, allocated register widths, total event count, native law, addresses A and C, source height convention and clock calibration stay fixed. Enumerate all 16 hidden coins; the listener has only the two physical stations' load and stamp traces. Use a third arm that parks only the third echo of the transmitting word while both writes remain stamped. No conditional selection on successful deliveries.

Before seeing the acquired counts, predict that the two empty-host runs give equal input and output loads and equal endpoint-version reports on both A and C, at serial slots one and four and heights zero and two. The full read-from chains predict opposite ancestry decisions at this same pair. The localized intervention should deliver total load one to C for all coins on the bridge word and zero on every independent and parked-bridge branch. Check both ports on every retained run. A common chart assigning coordinates from just the fixed seam, its height and appointment cannot distinguish the matched comparisons, irrespective of its dimension or its clock calibration. A version-dependent chart is a different input. Record which class of placements is excluded and the scope of the conditional finite order theorem.
