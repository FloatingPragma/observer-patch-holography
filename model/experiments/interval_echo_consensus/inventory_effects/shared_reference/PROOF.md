# Phase discrimination with a finite shared load

Alice and Bob receive the two signs of a split unit with equal priors. In their local occupation basis the alternatives are (|10⟩ ± |01⟩)/√2, up to the common calibrated phase of the splitter. An independently prepared shared reference has fixed total load L. Write its normalized pure state as Σₙ Rₙ, where Rₙ has local loads n and L−n, and let rₙ=‖Rₙ‖. Components may be entangled and may have any multiplicity. Additional local ancillas begin with definite local load or a mixture of such preparations. Any other shared source of load coherence must be included in the reference budget.

A local material unitary preserves the station's total load, and the sharp record effects commute with it. Finite classical feedback preserves this property branch by branch. Conditioned on a previously recorded classical bit, transferring its sharp pointer shifts the sector labels by known integers. The two prescribed routes move a total of one sharp unit for either bit and restore the pointer. The intermediate load on a route can reveal the already measured message. The routes avoid the coherent data rails, so conditioning on that classical history preserves the pinching argument. No unmeasured quantum rail is exchanged during the comparison. Thus replacing the joint input by its pinching in Alice's total load preserves every complete transcript probability. The restriction also holds with arbitrary classically coordinated load-preserving instruments; it does not apply if a new coherent load-bearing reference can be supplied during the read.

In the block where Alice holds k units the two unnormalized sign states have vectors (uₖ ± vₖ)/√2, where uₖ is the signal on Alice with Rₖ₋₁, and vₖ the signal on Bob with Rₖ. Their signal rails are orthogonal. The difference of the two phase density matrices on this block has eigenvalues ±rₖ₋₁rₖ and is zero on the orthogonal complement. The trace distance after pinching is therefore Σₙ rₙrₙ₊₁. The best success probability for any subsequent read is bounded by half of one plus that distance, by resolving the positive and negative parts of the difference. For mixed references, convexity and Cauchy--Schwarz give the same inequality using rₙ² equal to the unconditional weight in sector n. This proof uses the declared quantum probabilities.

For normalized r, the sum of adjacent products is the Rayleigh quotient of the (L+1)-site path matrix with each nearest-neighbor entry 1/2. The sine vectors with coordinates sin((n+1)jπ/(L+2)), j=1,…,L+1, diagonalize it, as follows by adding the two adjacent sine values with zero boundary extensions. Hence D ≤ cos(π/(L+2)). Every finite L leaves some error. Squaring the two-load bound gives 2(r₀r₁+r₁r₂)² ≤ 1. A sum of squares and its equality case are checked in [Lean](Bound.lean). For L=0,1,2 the sector norms of independent equal splits attain the upper bound. Entangled or pre-correlated signal/reference sources would address a different discrimination task.

## Attainment by the native receiver

For m=L+1 initially separated split pairs, write x for Alice's m-bit pattern and its complement for Bob's. Each term has amplitude of modulus 2^(−m/2), with probability 2⁻ᵐ. At fixed Alice weight all splitter phases coincide apart from the signal sign. Alice's balanced m-mode Fourier analyzer acts by its exterior powers. Each adjacent refocused contact implements a pure two-mode rotation, which fixes both the empty and the fully occupied pair; a private one-port phase acts once on each occupied address. In occupation order those adjacent operations are their exterior lifts. For m up to three, all Fourier minors in a given nonempty proper weight have equal modulus, since the two-particle minors of a three-mode unitary are its signed conjugate entries times its determinant. Alice's terminal word is consequently sign blind. Tracing out Bob initially leaves the maximally mixed state on Alice's m rails, which stays maximally mixed under her Fourier unitary. Each word has probability 2⁻ᵐ for either sign.

After this word is sent, Bob's possible vectors are proportional to u+v and u−v, with orthogonal components from the two initial signal positions. Interference between their normalized directions resolves the eigenvectors (û ± v̂)/√2 of the conditional difference. The remaining orthogonal exits are retained. The two extreme weights carry zero distinguishing information and receive opposite fixed guesses. This realizes the optimal decision on every block. For m=3 the two informative weights each occur with probability 3/8; each has conditional trace distance 2√2/3. They yield D=1/√2 over **all** starts. The setting-dependent Bob decision uses Alice's public word. This is a phase discrimination experiment with classical communication.

For a particular Alice word of weight k, the equal-modulus minors give squared branch weights k/m and (m−k)/m according to whether the original signal was on Alice or Bob. Thus every informative word has conditional visibility 2√(k(m−k))/m. Native discrimination succeeds equally often on both signs after averaging the two oppositely guessed extreme weights. A sharp path read removes the relative phase and gives success one half on each sign. For exchange only, the phase writer vanishes and both refocused analyzers act as identity. Alice and Bob receive bitwise complementary words for either setting. Of the eight equally likely Alice words, the enrolled rule guesses plus for five. Thus the success fractions conditional on the plus and minus preparations are 5/8 and 3/8, with equal-prior average one half. This control disables both source encoding and detector contrast.

The archived transcripts retain all starts. An invalid full-width detector code or an explicitly registered erasure produces a refusal, which counts as incorrect. Missing fields, missing banks, and incomplete enrollment prevent a score. A valid classical word delivered with changed bits selects Bob's setting using the bits he actually receives. All ten individual sign accuracies and every Alice word frequency are tested together at 99% simultaneous coverage under the independent-draw sampling model. The paired means and marginal differences inherit that coverage.

The prior OPH proof gives common refinements of effects on fixed blank inventories. Here the global inventory stays fixed while each station's local load varies. The experiment establishes the cost in shared reference loads of a specific additional phase-sensitive read. It leaves the PR-03 quantifier over arbitrary coexistent effects on the native public algebra open.
