# Independent unitary control in every blank material inventory

Freeze a connected region of m ports with blank seam memories. Individually time its native contacts or a private reference, parking the other services. At least one degree-five reference must be reachable in the region. A word lists port loads from {0,1,2}. Words with the same three multiplicities span one inventory block. The operations below preserve each such block, including its coherent superpositions. Pulse durations can be arbitrary reals.

**Theorem.** The available control group contains the independent product of the special unitary groups of all inventory blocks. Conversely no scheduled combination can mix the blocks. This leaves possible constraints on their relative scalar phases. The conclusion uses no probability rule.

1. On a blank contact the matched words have energy zero. For different labels a,b the two orderings have common diagonal
   \(h(a,b)=8(a-b)^2+1/2\) and off-diagonal −1/2. The spectrum is integral and a π exposure is exactly a material SWAP on all nine words. It extends any port permutation on the connected region. Thus the private diagonal \(Q_j=n_j^2\) can be addressed anywhere, and any two-site contact can be conjugated to the desired addresses. All primitive flows are periodic, so the construction has both signs of every duration. [Native column and integer checks](certificate.py)
2. On the two ordering matrix units the squared commutator with \(Q_j\) has eigenvalue \((b^2-a^2)^2\), with the sign reversed in the skew-Hermitian Lie algebra. The three unordered species pairs have distinct values 1, 16, 9. Diagonals have value zero. Lagrange interpolation of this commutator gives each species-resolved symmetric exchange generator by itself. Subtracting these exchanges from the contact gives the diagonal \(h(n_i,n_j)\), including its scalar part.
3. Fix a pair a<b and sites i,j. The exchange for these labels acts on all possible words of the m−2 spectator ports. For any remaining site l, the doubled diagonal \(2h(n_j,n_l)\) contributes the gap digit \(2h(b,c)-2h(a,c)\) if spectator l has label c. For pairs (0,1), (0,2), (1,2), the three respective digit lists are (17,−17,−48), (65,0,−65), (48,17,−17). Each list separates the three spectator values. Form the diagonal \(D=CQ_j+\sum_l 256^l\,2h(n_j,n_l)\), numbering the spectator ports from zero and taking \(C=1+131\sum_l256^l\). Every oriented gap is positive. For two distinct spectator words, divide the gap difference by the power of 256 of their first differing digit. Modulo 256 the result is nonzero because the digit difference has magnitude between 1 and 130. Thus every squared gap differs. Interpolation in the squared commutator with D isolates precisely one *individual configuration exchange*, acting by zero on all other words. One more commutator with D gives its antisymmetric partner. Their bracket supplies the corresponding diagonal difference.
4. The configuration swaps connect exactly the words of a fixed inventory, since all permutations of ports are generated. Bracketing along paths gives all off-diagonal units and diagonal differences of that block. The configuration-selective generators annihilate all other blocks. Exact finite-dimensional group reachability follows as well. The real span of conjugates of the primitive generators by finite control words is invariant under each primitive adjoint flow. Differentiation gives the Lie algebra generated by the primitives. A basis of this span gives a product of conjugated flows with full differential at the identity of the connected analytic subgroup. The inverse function theorem supplies an open neighborhood in that subgroup. The subgroup generated by finite products therefore contains each special unitary factor. This is an existence result with no uniform pulse-depth or finite-precision cost guarantee. The displayed 248-exposure reader is compiled explicitly for the six-word triangle. The pulse count includes private services.

## Which operational probability premise follows?

Consider a source supported on a d-dimensional inventory block. A nearby register of enough initialized ports may be supplied. Adding three distinct marker labels already provides six different marker words with unchanged added inventory. In the enlarged connected inventory, the theorem realizes any isometry with three d-row outcome blocks. For each coexistent pair of effects A,B stack the square roots of A, B and I−A−B. Complete the isometry to a special unitary by choosing its action on an unused column. Group the output words by block. This constructs the missing joint event realization for **every** coexistent pair on a many-load inventory, including noncommuting or singular pairs. All controls, dimension requirements and the selected inventory are part of the construction.

There is a second realization when the board can accommodate at least 3d initialized single-marker detector ports. Encode the three d-row output blocks in the position of the marker, resetting the original data to one common word. The input subspace and these output addresses have the same total inventory, so the preceding control theorem implements this isometry on their connected union. A complete read then has just one marked address. For the probability argument the detector must allow arbitrary rotations of the remaining rails with any selected rail held physically untouched, for instance by a connected punctured register with an available private reference. The argument extends the [single-defect terminal probability theorem](../signed_effects/README.md) to arbitrary many-load input blocks **with this available workspace**.

Suppose each complete marker reading has a single outcome, is certain on sharp preparations, is invariant under equivalent prepared rays, changes labels covariantly, and a control confined to other marker ports cannot alter the marginal at the held port. Complementary unitaries are transitive on vectors of a fixed norm, so the held probability has the form f(x) where x is its squared component. On three positions, compare squared components (x,y,1−x−y) and (x+y,0,1−x−y). Normalization yields f(x)+f(y)=f(x+y). Positivity gives monotonicity. Integer partitions and rational bounds then imply f(x)=x on the whole interval, without continuity or a trace-pairing axiom. Applied to the compiled marker isometry, the same argument gives the Born form for all these coexistent effects. Sampling genuinely randomized repetitions of different preparations gives the corresponding density mixtures. The representation, access and record locality are hypotheses. Simulating probabilities with the native quantum instrument tests its implications and cannot prove its locality assumption as a new fact about classical deterministic record generation.

The proof covers arbitrary protected blank inventories with a suitable register. A finite board has finite capacity. Unread written memories, coherent superpositions between inventories, free histories lacking these controls, and the relation of records to sampling across all physical contexts require their own coverage. A finite sample of compatible events cannot discharge that remaining physical quantifier.
