### Persistence and its observables

For every initially adjacent pair, **adjacency somewhere has probability at least $5/6$ at every exposure** on a federation of complete cells with three distinct gateways per cell, a matching of external ports, and at most one bridge per pair of cells. This holds for either aperture. Let $P$ project onto adjacent pairs, $Q=I-P$, and $F$ be unsigned triangular face-edge incidence. On $P$, twice the exchange generator equals $\operatorname{diag}(d_a+d_b-2+(F^TF)_{ee})-F^TF$, with maximum diagonal in the first term at most 12. Internal edges belong to two triangles and bridges to none. Thus $PHP\le -10P$ and $QHQ\ge0$. For $B=QKP$, an internal edge has four in-cell departures to separated pairs, each reached from four adjacent states, and up to two intercell departures each reached from two. A bridge has ten departures, each reached from two. Each hop has magnitude $1/2$. The maximum row sum of $4B^*B$ is at most 20. Integer comparisons check the face identity and the Gram bound on all three boards. [Block certificates](adjacency_bound.py)

Here is why these bounds control every exposure. Let $g$ be the diagonal block gap, $\beta=\|B\|$, and $L$ the lower spectral band. Min-max keeps the bands separated while $B$ increases continuously from zero. At the largest principal angle $\theta$ between $L$ and $P$, pick principal vectors $x\in P$, $y\in Q$. The rotated vectors $\cos\theta\,x+\sin\theta\,y$ and $-\sin\theta\,x+\cos\theta\,y$ lie in opposite spectral bands. Their $H$ matrix element vanishes. Its real part gives $g\sin\theta\cos\theta\le\beta|\cos 2\theta|$. The angle starts at zero and cannot reach $\pi/4$. Unitary evolution preserves $L$. A state starting in $P$ stays within projective angle $2\theta$ of $P$, giving a probability at least $g^2/(g^2+4\beta^2)$. Mixtures follow by linearity. The isolated cell gives $121/137$. This theorem concerns distinct unit loads and an initialized record bank.
