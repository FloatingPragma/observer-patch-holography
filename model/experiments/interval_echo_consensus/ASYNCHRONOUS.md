# Consensus for sharp repairs across an entire screen

Starting from the same sharp port loads and signed seam memories, local repair attempts on the 20-, 80- and 320-cell spheres reached identical material endpoints in forward, reverse and shuffled orders. Each controller inspects at most three adjacent echosahedra, independently of when its neighbours request service. The observer reads which incident collars are occupied and which are ready to repair. These answers define a closed operational quotient. The service uses the native strict write and a supplied priority rule fixed for that installation. [Protocol and readout audit](ASYNC_PROTOCOL.md) · [Complete receipt](asynchronous.json)

## Why the endpoint is unique in this sector

A local inconsistency is a blank edge whose endpoints differ by two. Each controller orders its incident seams using the same cell chart, with an external seam ahead of internal seams. External seams form a matching, and internal seams at the same port all have different chart addresses. An enabled write repairs a discrepancy with no higher-priority discrepant edge touching either endpoint. The probe is deterministic on a sharp input. On the accepted branch the registered echo sends 2,0 or 0,2 to 1,1 and retains the oriented difference on the collar. The private coin is in the same fixed state for either sign. A completed guard and write is one local transaction. Its admission and material payload can be recomputed at commit. [Native collar check and local controller](asynchronous.py)

1. **Protected data and descent.** On the public quotient keep the two edge sets O (occupied) and R (ready). A commit at edge e adds e to O and deletes from R exactly the edges incident to its endpoints, including e. Thus the public update is independent of the hidden loads and signs. For the theorem's boundary map use the occupation flags of any fixed set of initially marked collars. They are invariant. The public integer |R| strictly decreases. The stronger material calculation also conserves total load, preserves every existing collar and decreases the sum of squared loads by exactly two per write.
2. **Joining competing schedules.** Each write removes all discrepancies at its two endpoints because a load of one differs by at most one from any allowed load. It creates no new discrepancy anywhere. Any two simultaneously enabled edges must have disjoint endpoints. After either write the other edge stays discrepant and cannot acquire a higher-priority blocker. The two commits give exactly the same port loads and signed collars in either order.
3. **Completeness.** Every inconsistent configuration has a highest-priority discrepant edge. It can commit. A consistent configuration enables no write. Strict descent and the local joining result therefore give one consistent normal form per initial configuration, including for classical mixtures evolved branch by branch. A scheduler that ignores an enabled edge forever can stall; a weakly fair scheduler reaches the normal form.

This establishes the sharp-sector hypotheses of the paper's finite quotient normal-form theorem directly. This proof uses the monotonicity of discrepancies under this service. The paper's stronger, implementation-specific hypotheses about conflict-component reclosure are a separate route. Free propagation, coherent source states and clock transactions change the set of admissible moves. Applying the proof to those operations would require a new descent and joining argument. The chart orders the internal addresses and the supplied order can affect physical results. [Formal theorem and hypotheses](../../../paper/reality_as_consensus_protocol.tex)

## Coherent repairs with local precedence

A protected sweep can settle the whole screen and preserve coherence within a common record of outcomes. Give each seam a completion bit initially clear. A seam becomes eligible once every higher-priority seam touching either endpoint has completed. It then performs the native strict instrument once, retains its outcome at its own address, and raises its bit. The three outcomes are occupied, quiet blank and accepted write. All measurements belong to finite local patches. Each controller reads at most nine predecessor bits on the three equipped boards. Each board admits a schedule of 12 parallel visit layers, with service order inside and across unrelated layers unrestricted.

The complete material endpoint and its address-labelled record define the same quantum instrument for every fair schedule under these dependencies. Incident visits are comparable. Incomparable visits act on disjoint ports and seam memories, so their branch operators commute for every pair of local outcomes. Any two linear extensions of a finite partial order are related by exchanges of consecutive incomparable visits. Thus the product for a fixed address-labelled outcome agrees branch by branch, including on a state entangled with an external reference. The argument also allows a scheduler to select the next eligible visit using completed outcomes when it records no additional material observable. The unvisited edge count strictly decreases. A commit maps the two extreme loads to (1,1); these endpoints cannot become discrepant with any allowed neighbouring load. For any sharp input column, a visited quiet blank seam therefore stays quiet, and every active branch ends consistent. Linearity places the quantum output on the span of these consistent configurations. Within a fixed transcript the branch acts isometrically on its compatible input columns: the quiet and occupied projectors are identities there, and each accepted echo is an isometry on both discrepant signs. The supplied chart and protected service window fix the precedence relation. Concurrent free evolution or clock transactions are outside these operations.

## What the observer can read after a strict repair

Put two load units at one port of the 20-cell screen, with every other port vacant and every seam memory blank. A strict repair produces unit loads at both ends of the selected seam $e=(a,b)$. Its memory contains the signed input difference $+2$ or $-2$ with the coin in equal superposition. This follows by applying the native write to $|2,0\rangle$ or $|0,2\rangle$. The priority service selects the seam. Over all 240 input ports it selects an internal seam 180 times and an intercell seam 60 times, reaching 202 distinct seams. Forward, reverse and shuffled visits agree with the serial priority rule on this domain. Starting at port 156 selects $(156,157)$ with positive sign. After release the generator acts freely on all 630 seams.

The usual detector echoes a seam, copies the flag for two unit loads, and undoes the echo. On the signed memory this flag is silent throughout the released sector. On a blank seam it detects the two occupied endpoints exactly. To read the repaired address, each endpoint probes three distinct internal neighbours other than its partner, using the first three in port order. The native contrast-one flag on a blank binary seam reads the exclusive OR of its occupations and the inverse echo restores its memory. An empty endpoint cannot yield three yes flags with only two units in the entire system. Both occupied endpoints give six yes flags. Thus their conjunction detects precisely the repaired address. For an intercell seam the two endpoint patches must compare their three records. In the home cell at port 156 the terminal menu has 30 seam addresses and a quiet outcome. It uses this conjunction at the repaired seam and the usual flags elsewhere. These effects are diagonal on the binary sector, so their terminal probabilities apply also to coherent load states.

### Probability of detecting both loads at any exposure

In each sign-reversal parity $\delta=0,1$, the signed seam contributes zero at the launch word $|e\rangle$ and $4\kappa+\delta$ on every other binary word. The exact local columns also give $(I-\mathrm{SWAP})/2$ and $\kappa(n_i-n_j)^2$ on each blank seam. The degree references $6-d_i$ use the full board. The written memory occupies both parities with equal weights, and the terminal occupation effects preserve parity. [Local columns, observer effects and full-sector replay](strict_pair.py)

Write $m=d_a+d_b-2$. At launch the energy expectation is $E=m/2+10\kappa$, with $m$ outgoing moves of amplitude $-1/2$. The squared norm of the off-diagonal column is $v^2=m/4$. For any other pair word the all-blank contrast including degree references is $12-2\mathbf{1}_{\rm adjacent}\ge10$. Replacing the blank seam subtracts at most one unit of contrast there and adds the signed contribution. Since blank exchange is positive, the complementary compression satisfies $H_\perp\ge(13\kappa+\delta)I$ for $\kappa\ge0$.

Set $g=3\kappa+\delta-m/2$. For $g>0$, the variational principle puts the lowest eigenvalue at or below $E$, while interlacing puts the second at or above $E+g$. The projected eigenvector equation bounds the norm of the complementary component by $v/g$ times the launch component. The launch weight in the lowest eigenstate is therefore at least $w=g^2/(g^2+v^2)$. For $g>v$, the triangle inequality bounds the return amplitude at every exposure by $w-(1-w)$ from below. Squaring and taking the weaker parity gives a bound on the observer's six-flag probability.

At $\kappa=8$ the single-seam probability is at least $(717/727)^2$ across all 630 seams, and at least $(84/85)^2$ on $(156,157)$. More generally $m\le10$ gives a floor of $(333/343)^2>0.94$ for every $\kappa\ge6$. The guarantee is for one terminal read after any chosen free-flight exposure. A sequence of intermediate measurements changes the experiment. The guarantee applies whenever the strict output is prepared on the specified seam, including the 202 selected by the whole-board service. Free evolution preserves the written memory subspace on that seam and can mix its two signs. The bound and the six-flag read establish localized material persistence at the written seam. Translation, a physical lifetime and particle identity require other observables.
