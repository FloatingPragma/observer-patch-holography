# Recoil of a separate load in the three-guide current

A prepared one-load receiver moves toward a localized source in the three-guide address chart. A source release gives mean signed displacements of −0.00515, −0.00212, −0.00106 and −0.00065 receiver links on four successive outward faces. The reflected face gives +0.00516. At each face we read the **unconditional** change in right-port population of a fresh receiver at every live appointment. A second mobile load has exactly the same bare propagation on all 240 ports and gives zero mean response to the same interrogation clock. The physical result is an apparatus-dependent coupling to the guide current. [Protocol](protocol.json) · [All counts and controls](results.json) · [Guide definitions and Green theorem](../README.md)

## What acts on the receiver

Each of three separately operated twelve-port guides holds a sharp load two. The corner source launches their three addresses together. A uniformly chosen one of eight labels moves a single coordinate by ±1 or idles; the two idle labels are distinct. A walk ends when any coordinate reaches its outer drain. Each interior face is specified by its two adjacent **joint** addresses \(x,x+e_i\). The current per source release is \(J_i=q_i[u(x)-u(x+e_i)]\), where \(u\) is the mean number of visits and \(q_i=1/8\) is measured on the guide contacts.

The test load \(Q=1\) or \(2\) starts in the equal, real superposition of two adjacent material ports 49 and 53. Position is the right-port occupation indicator, in receiver-link units. At each appointment an independent fair choice of arm selects one face endpoint. Three independent fair switches enable or skip interrogation of its three guide ports. For each enabled contact a reversible route swaps the selected guide port with sensing pad 55, performs the phase interaction, and restores the route. The route exchanges the two endpoints and restores every borrowed port. The native full-swap pulse on sharp loads has duration \(\pi\) at both tested stiffnesses. The sensed occupation is two if this *coordinate* matches its selected address and zero otherwise. The guides are sharp and their occupations are restored. No controller reads a three-coordinate match, removes an unsuccessful shot or commands the receiver to recoil.

Interrogation holds the receiver coordinate and pad loads stationary, allowing the native diagonal contact energy to accumulate a phase. On release the two receiver ports exchange freely for a fixed duration while the exterior stays clamped. Pad 52 supplies the reference on the opposite leg. The two mirror arms present opposite pad polarities on release. The near arm reverses the sensing phase by conjugating it with a swap of the two receiver legs. Face routing, clamping, coherent test preparation and time allocation are supplied parts of the apparatus. The source, guides and receiver have separate records; the apparatus maps a face of the guide chart onto a physical receiver link.

## How the sign and magnitude follow

The blank native seam has diagonal energy
\[
 e(l,s)=\kappa(l-s)^2+\tfrac12{\bf1}_{l\ne s}
\]
and exchange amplitude \(-1/2\) between the two different orderings. The sensing leg sees load \(s\) and the reference leg sees two. The relative energy is
\[
 d(s)=e(Q,s)-e(0,s)-e(Q,2)+e(0,2).
\]
It vanishes for a coordinate match. For a miss it is \(d=Q(4\kappa+1/2)\) for both supported test loads. At the fixed interrogation time \(\tau=\pi/(65/2)\) a miss for the ordinary load at \(\kappa=8\) reverses the sign of the coherence if its switch was enabled. After averaging the independent switches, each missed coordinate contributes a factor \(z=(1+\exp(i d\tau))/2\). The product over the three coordinates has mean one on a full match and zero otherwise. The camera reads one material load after a free flight in every arm, including ones with zero mean coherence. This argument uses a signed coherence generated by the native phases and a positive mixture of physical trials.

Up to a scalar shift, the far-arm flight Hamiltonian in the left/right basis is \((d Z-X)/2\). The near arm reflects it. For real unit coherence and zero starting velocity the right-port probability changes by
\[
 a(d,t)=\frac{d}{2(1+d^2)}[1-\cos(t\sqrt{1+d^2})].
\]
The sign reverses on reflecting the apparatus. Thus \(\partial_t^2 a(d,0)=d/2\). On a matched active one-load face the complete mean signed displacement **per source release**, summed over fresh receivers, is
\[
 B_i(x,t)=\frac{a(d,t)}2 [u(x+e_i)-u(x)]
         =-\frac{a(d,t)}{2q_i}J_i(x).
\]
Every receiver arm starts with right-port probability \(1/2\); at the registered phase the initial current vanishes even conditional on the switches. The measured flight time \(t=\pi/\sqrt{1+(65/2)^2}\) gives \(B_i=-(520/4229)J_i\), and its release-time second derivative is \(-65J_i\). The source activity would multiply these expressions for continuously supplied independent releases. On enlarging these guides, their three-dimensional Green theorem gives \(J_i=x_i/(4\pi r^3)+o(r^{-2})\) for \(r\to\infty,\ r^2/L\to0\) with the drains at distance of order \(L\). This is an inward inverse-square *receiver response in material address coordinates*. The finite experiment measures the Dirichlet current with its boundary effects; the four axial values are not a fit to a continuum power law.

## Bare propagation and controls

With a single mobile load \(Q\) on an otherwise blank board, summing the native onsite term and every incident seam gives
\[
 H_Q=6\kappa Q^2I+\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(\deg)-\tfrac12 A .
\]
The load multiset is invariant. Hence **every** position transition probability is the same for \(Q=1,2\), at every time and on any board with the stated degree correction. On the 240-port board the two native matrices differ by exactly \(144I\); independent \(2^{22}\)-start flights read their position distributions at dwell three. Bare hopping and this dispersion test alone do not measure a rest mass.

For the second load the same address pulse gives \(d\tau=2\pi\). Its coherence survives every address, including the absent source. The two equally probable mirrored arms then cancel exactly, for **any** release time and source distribution. Half the release time tests this cancellation away from the near return of the two-load flight. Without the two transverse contacts the same apparatus instead responds to the gradient of a one-coordinate marginal. A stiffness-four control changes the phase per miss and hence the current weights at fixed timing. Parking the receiver exchange eliminates every position change. All these cases use the same ports and count all trials. The relative universal acceleration and Newton coupling required by OW-26 remain open. A test would also need an independently ranging body, a mass law for the emitter, and transfer of its coupling to different bodies without retuning the interrogator.

## Acquisition and uncertainty

An integer multinomial propagation batches \(2^{34}\) independent source walks. At each step it counts the surviving cohorts at the actual guide addresses. For each face the receiver bank samples both arms and all eight switch patterns with their stipulated frequencies, then draws its two-outcome event using an independent exponentiation of the compressed native Hamiltonian. Rounding probabilities to a dyadic grid of \(2^{-32}\) fixes the binomial sampling convention at exact fair outcomes. The analytic Bloch and Green forecast above is evaluated separately. The fourteen panels share the same source walks and have independent conditional receiver draws. The saved run reached its last drain at appointment 796 and recorded 906,369,662,542 right-or-left reads per panel. These are simulated cohorts.

For a per-walk sum \(S_H\) of centered binary reads up to \(H=2048\), let \(M\) bound the absolute conditional mean at a single live appointment, including rounding. The source lifetime has mean at most 120 from every address because even the exit time of one coordinate has mean \(j(11-j)/(2q_i)\le120\). Each mark has square \(1/4\). Conditional independence along the path and the Markov property give \(\operatorname{Var}(S_H)\le120/4+2(120M)^2\) by summing off-diagonal time pairs. The centered walk sum is bounded in magnitude by \(H\). The symmetric killed transition matrix has spectral norm \(s=1-\tfrac34(1-\cos(\pi/11))\); the mean absolute discarded tail is at most \(\sqrt{1000}s^H/[2(1-s)]\). Bernstein’s inequality for independent source releases, followed by a union bound over the fourteen panels, gives the simultaneous 99% half-widths in the record, including a further \(120/2^{33}\) for probability rounding. This counts uncertainty in the walk occupations as well as in the detector. A shared source trajectory across panels is allowed by the union bound.
