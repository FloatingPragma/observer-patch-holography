# Free release and unrestricted round-trip ranging

On the 240-port carrier use every available contact, the enrolled lexicographic source and four dispersed disjoint sources. On each clock tick sample one of the six local contact slots uniformly. Communicate by the two-slot native bit protocol on fresh stationary material. Count missing slots and erasures as stays. Each successful hop moves the message one station. Divide the mean clock ticks to a landmark and back by six times the board size. Record the resulting range before preparing the material release. A single or double sharp probe at an exterior station has unit blank-star release coefficients in ordinary squared-clock units. Ask what a fixed landmark infers about the initial acceleration of its mean range. Also difference the readings of any two independent landmarks and project the launches onto the line joining two reference landmarks using the square root of the round-trip range as distance.

Enroll all stations with at least two blank hops of clearance from every written endpoint and all native landmarks. Retain zero and reversed signals. Include the same-charge alternative on the single source, one uniformly added ordinary single per chain, the matching fractional ordinary population, and a preparation-blind relay on the same contacts. Measure which source endpoint a random message from each neighbor of stations 25 and 104 reaches first. Measure the bypass resistance by an excursion starting at its first endpoint. Include unresolved trips in the count bounds. Combine these independent readings with the local source gate counts to bound the initial raw-range acceleration change for every landmark. A measured potential difference across an exterior star and an increased measured gate probability predict its sign. Opposite signs of the source change or an unresolved potential gradient would defeat that sign inference. An independent direction requires a change that two landmarks can distinguish on subtracting their ranges.

For a conditional radial comparison, interpret the single-landmark acceleration as a central vector toward each source. Group exterior launches by their original round-trip range from the native bypass center or from either end of the active transmitter. A station at exact zero radius retains its observed scalar reading and has no direction for a central vector. Use ten equal-width radial bins, zero central vector at the origin, linear interpolation between populated shells and a taper to zero one bin beyond the last measured shell. Compare every populated positive-radius shell with a nonzero ordinary calibration. Use the existing stellar-disk length and image-based closed-source populations in all ten enrolled galaxies. Record the dependence on that choice and the effect of angular quadrature refinement. For the differential-ranging prescription, evaluate the zero-vector prediction at the fixed SPARC baryonic calibration.
