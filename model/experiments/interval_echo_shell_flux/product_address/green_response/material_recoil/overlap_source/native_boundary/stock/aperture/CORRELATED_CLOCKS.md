# Can one fluctuating gravitational potential predict the clock and the flight?

Use the occupied 240-port chart, written heads, accepted 2200 source, independently re-paired source and same one-defect material generators as in the occupied release. Keep all one-head distributions fixed. Test an extra scalar potential that is allowed to vary between trials and to be arbitrarily correlated between its two locations. Calibrate each material clock's response by adding one ordinary load to the same neighboring clamp. Select the first live ordinary neighbor shared by the two written contacts and the first incident endpoint on each. Use the same port choices for both species. The scalar comparison assumes that a head-induced potential shifts each static clock linearly with the gain it showed on this ordinary source. It permits different gains and constant clock offsets. The shared field must give the same joint law of potential differences for either pair of instruments.

Interrogate each written head with a defect at its endpoint on one arm and a defect stored outside the written pair on the other. Record the real or imaginary fringe at dwell 2π/65, choosing both phases with equal frequency. Use one clock after the other on the same frozen pair, including one vacancy clock and one excess clock. Keep idle trials in the signed measurement. The generator check must verify that the monitored conserved head sectors survive the two reads. Compare a zero wait and 64 ticks between the clocks. Independently measure both single-clock detector tables on the three sharp ordinary-frequency standards. Reconstruct the joint clock-frequency law on the two unsorted arms. Use it to predict the change in another clock's reading and the change in freely evolving material. Also predict the cross-frequency covariance directly from the previously measured source displacement and the sharp frequency calibrations. Keep simulated trial errors throughout.

For the ordinary calibration use the actual blank contact pulse on the neighboring port at dwell 1/64 with clamp loads one and two. Record both quadratures of the conditional return, including the exchange loss. The small unwrapped phase divided by the dwell gives the change in the local frequency. Check the return on the full native word matrix for both defect loads. With the vacancy clock as the standard, predict the vacancy–excess and excess–excess covariance changes from the ratio of the two ordinary gains. A field-independent additive noise with unchanged inter-port covariance cancels. Also test the following extension to the moving probe. Give the vacancy's measured head frequencies to a common trial-wise potential on their four measured endpoints, zero elsewhere on the chart. Transfer it to the excess with the ordinary gain ratio. Zero both head terms in inactive sectors. Use the independently measured native kinetic generator of each probe and the same reserved clamp with one load throughout the flights. Compare this with the native clock law in which either defect removes the same occupied contact energy. This specifies two physical source responses before testing flight counts.

Release a uniform ordinary wave and measure the probability of finding the defect at the source and inside one, two, four or six live hops at 1, 4, 16 and 64 ticks. Retain all the cuts and ages and the uniformly sampled age on [0,64]. Predict held readings from the local clock banks and the ordinary generator without a calibration on sharp-head flights. A second prediction uses the original source displacement and the two individually calibrated ordinary head clocks. Compare with fresh unsorted terminal counts. Before evaluating those counts, enroll a frequency-domain check on the same flight. On a uniform launch, reference an exterior return to the uniform mode on each measured cut and its complement, separately normalized. Tune the ordinary reference oscillator to the mean launch energy on the inactive chart. Sample waiting times exponentially with mean 4, 16 or 64 ticks. The two signed Ramsey outcomes read the real and imaginary part of the averaged amplitude. Use the inactive chart and the four source endpoints to predict the entire frequency response from a four-port linear system. Check on independent held counts obtained by diagonalizing the full native chart. Also compare a vacancy and excess clock on the same retained head. This directly tests the part of the two readings along the ordinary gain when a second local frequency shift is allowed to add equally to both clocks. A positive clock covariance alone establishes a local frequency correlation. Identifying it as a gravitational source further requires the ordinary coupling to transfer and an extended geometry and spatial abundance appropriate to the astronomical observations.
