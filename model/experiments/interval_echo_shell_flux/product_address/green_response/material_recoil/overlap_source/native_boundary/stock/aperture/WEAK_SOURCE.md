# Can ordinary source records predict the response to a different weak write?

On the 240-port open chart, reserve the farthest port from the registered seam (lowest address in a tie) as an ordinary source station. At that station test a projectively clamped double load against the same empty clamped port. In the held arm the clamped port remains empty and the registered native seam is written. The small-duty stationary linear-response calculations are known. Test actual finite, coherent alternation of the two native generators. Select the blank lowest band from a common uniform launch by an energy filter calibrated only on the blank chart. Archive its pass rate and rejected starts. Measure independent blank-reference position counts. With the spatial coefficients fixed from those counts and the native hopping calibration, predict the change of the finite ordinary occupation measure from the independently calibrated ordinary interaction and the held seam map.

Use symmetric blank/active/blank cycles of total width 1/8 and duty 1/10000. On each accepted copy choose uniformly among the first 2^21 completed cycle counts, including zero. Fresh copies supply terminal positions of the blank, loaded ordinary standard and written contact. Use 2^48 accepted copies per setting; keep unsuccessful spectral preparations in the accounting. Average ordinary and written evolutions separately; each cycle coherently visits the two physical contacts. Score all ports before radial binning. Report measured and predicted changes for balls of radii 0, 1, 3 and 6 about each source, counting graph hops on the original chart. Include counting uncertainty and state any finite-duty or timing remainder rather than treating a first derivative as an exact finite-exposure prediction. Spectral selection and source clamping are experimental preparations.
