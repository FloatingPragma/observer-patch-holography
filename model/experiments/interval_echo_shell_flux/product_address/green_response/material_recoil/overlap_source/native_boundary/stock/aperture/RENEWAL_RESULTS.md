## The galaxy test measures clocks and orbits independently

A clock that is slow because of rate fluctuations makes an additional prediction about the spread in the rate. For positive local rate \(u\), let the event distribution be \(\pi\), the renewal rate be \(R=1/\mathbb E_\pi(1/u)\), and the random-time distribution be \(\mu_i=\pi_i R/u_i\). Then
\[
  R=\mathbb E_\mu u=\frac{\mathbb E_\pi u}{1+\operatorname{Var}_\mu(u)/R^2}.
\]
If the event-mean rate follows the ordinary lapse, the anomalous potential difference between two radii is bounded by \(c^2\log(1+s_{\rm in}^2)\), with \(s\) the fractional width at the inner radius. This grants zero outer width. A proper-time model fixing the mean squared lapse to the ordinary reference gives half this potential at the same width. Both tests use \(v_\mathrm{circ}^2=c^2\,d\log R/d\log r\) in the weak, static orbital reduction. [Finite clock identities](../../../../../../../../../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/SourceApertures.lean)

Use each galaxy's orbital interval from its measured radius nearest half the final radius through its last point. After moving every measured speed down by two quoted errors at the fixed baryonic reduction, the ten observed potential differences require time-sampled renewal widths of 33.8–199.6 km/s. The flux-weighted RMS of the co-spatial detected 21 cm widths ranges from 8.65 to 34.50 km/s. Each galaxy's width is below its own required width. For NGC 2841 the figures are 199.54 and 33.83 km/s. The test assumes that the emitting gas samples the lapse distribution, the frequency offset persists through each emission, and other Doppler scatter is uncorrelated with the clock. It gives no constraint on undetected wings or fluctuations shared by every emitter in one resolution element. [THINGS observations](https://www2.mpia-hd.mpg.de/THINGS/Data.html) · [Measured widths and orbital integrals](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/clock_width.json)

Counting emitted ticks can change the observation weights. For the calibrated three-rate clock define \(a=p_{t1}, d=(p_{t0}+p_{t2})/2-a\), \(b=[(p_{t0}-a)^2+(p_{t2}-a)^2]/2\), and \(k\) as the coefficient of \(f\) in \(T_t\). Both \(d,k\) are positive. The ratio of \(\mathbb E_\pi u/R-1\) to the event-sampled fractional variance is
\[
 \frac{(ak+d/a+dkf)(a+df)^2}{b-d^2 f}.
\]
It increases on \(0\le f\le1\) and at \(f=1\) equals \((p_{t0}+p_{t2})^2/(4p_{t0}p_{t2})\). Thus tick sampling can relax the required width by at most the square root of 4.220 for the single and 1.210 for the double within this ternary family. Both lower bounds exceed the corresponding detected width on all ten galaxies. If emission averages many independent rates, the time-sampled comparison requires roughly 11–108 independent samples per emission across the ten objects. Native service has temporal correlations. For the single receiver advancing at every slot, 32,768 is the first tested power-of-two exposure reaching 108 effective samples. Advancing at ticks takes 262,144 slots.
