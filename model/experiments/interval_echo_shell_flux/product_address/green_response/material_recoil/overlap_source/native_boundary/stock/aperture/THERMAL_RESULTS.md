## What the equilibrium experiment measures

Choose two written contacts on the 240-port board. Keep their measured conserved-parity frequencies, subtract their empty-head clock on each arm, and use the ordinary inverse temperature $\beta=\log(8/7)$. In an energy measurement at a specified pair address, a filter accepts with probability $\exp[-\beta(E-E_{\min})]$. Holding the source sector fixed and repeating until acceptance prepares a normalized ordinary equilibrium for that sector. Reading one uniformly chosen ordinary unit gives the shell histogram. Comparing its probabilities per site between shells gives the change in the tagged-position potential of mean force. Every rejected attempt remains in the single-attempt filter record. [Enrolled preparations and meter definitions](THERMAL_PAIR.md)

Let $K=CI-H_0$ be the shifted ordinary matrix with entrywise nonnegative elements. Two stored heads add positive diagonal matrices $A$ and $B$ in the active sectors. In the expansion of $(K+A+B)^n-(K+A)^n-(K+B)^n+K^n$, only words visiting both insertions contribute. Every entry is nonnegative. Here the two marked pair addresses are 13 ordinary hops apart. A diagonal contribution requires at least 26 hops, one insertion at each mark, and thus at least order 28. Summing the hopping series with the maximum diagonal dwell gives an upper bound of $2.86\times10^{-27}$ on each mixed heat diagonal. There is a positive return word at the first mark. A single ordinary unit cannot occupy both ends of either seam and has zero source response. The enrolled filter's positive excess success probability per attempt is below $2.53\times10^{-32}$.

Separate equilibration has an additional effect. Write $z$ for the reference trace, $x,y$ for the two single-mark trace increases, and $w,u,v$ for their respective contributions to an aperture. Up to the bounded joint-return contribution, the normalized mixed reading is exactly
\[
\frac{xy}{(z+x)(z+x+y)}\left(\frac wz-\frac ux\right)
+\frac{xy}{(z+y)(z+x+y)}\left(\frac wz-\frac vy\right).
\]
It becomes negative at an aperture in which both single-mark increases are more concentrated than the original thermal material. With the positive measured modular increment, the ordinary fraction on shell zero drops by $1.04\times10^{-5}$. Source and thermostat predict a negative change of $3.17$–$3.18\times10^{-10}$ in the work from two to six hops. Direct shell counts resolve the zero-to-one change and include either sign at two to six hops. The latter sign comes from the finite transfer and the source readings. Sparse spectral moments bound the unperturbed diagonal. Positive single-mark power sums, a joint-return bound and interval division bound each spatially varying term before the common thermal normalization cancels. ## What does the source predict for clocks and areas?

On the same two written contacts, compare the original pairing of the head readings with a pairing drawn independently at the same one-head frequencies. Separate simulated counts give 0.09274–0.09287 nats. The following two implications concern that measured quantity and its measured reference.

### An ordinary message that reads both contacts

A single at one end of a written contact shifts the phase of an ordinary receiver split between the other end and a local rail. A second half swap turns the phase into a port receipt. A dark-port count signals a present sender. With the sender absent or a magnitude-zero written head, its probability is zero. With a positive head the native interaction gives \(c=(1+\cos(\pi/65))/4\). Independent ordinary spectroscopy gives \(0.499685<c<0.499731\). This is an instrument in the executable model. [Two-route protocol and apparatus schedule](PHASE_RECEIPTS.md)

Record the travel time on a blank bypass and on two routes that each use a different written contact. Interrogate each contact once, in separate timed slots on the same source copy. A missed receipt leaves the bypass available. Write \(x,y\) for the nonnegative time each assisted route can save over that bypass. If both work, the earlier receipt saves \(\max(x,y)\). At fixed marginal receipt probabilities, re-pairing changes the probability of two successes by \(Dc^2\). The same amount moves into the two-miss cell. The mixed difference of the four earliest receipt times is \(\min(x,y)\). The change in the mean earliest receipt is therefore
\[
\Delta T=Dc^2\min(x,y).
\]
This sign follows from measured contact counts and ordinary clocks. A single assisted route gives zero connected change. The calculation predicts later receipts whenever both routes can beat the bypass. On the 240-station board, this holds for 1,776 of 57,360 ordered pairs. The independent simulated banks resolve the delay for every such pair, using one bank for each distinct triple of route times. Between stations 20 and 41 the two assisted routes each save 8.9692 blank-hop times when successful. The prediction is 0.15982–0.16013 hop times and the receipt interval is 0.15323–0.16523. The bounds for the receipt banks are simultaneous at 99% under the specified independent sampling model.
