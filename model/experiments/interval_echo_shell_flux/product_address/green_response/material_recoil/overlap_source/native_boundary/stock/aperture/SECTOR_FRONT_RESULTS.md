# An overlap measured from distant arrival times

Two observers can read both home occupations from outside the source. A signal travels from station 72 to 97 along one of two prescribed routes. Both take twelve protected acknowledgement periods on the empty chart. One passes home 8 and avoids the other three homes. The other passes home 44 and avoids the other three. The measured times are exactly \(12+2X\) and \(12+2Y\), where \(X,Y\) are the respective binary occupancy reports. The sender can use either ordinary probe load. Native swap, parking and restoration give these times on every one of the 448 returned words. ## Does the signal grow when many records are compared together?

Let \(S_X,S_Y\) be the sums of the two home readings over a register of \(N=896\) distinct members. Exterior observers can reconstruct them from the two summed arrival times \(12N+2S_X\) and \(12N+2S_Y\). Consider the change relative to \(N\) independent ordinary returns in \(\operatorname{Cov}(S_X,S_Y)/N\). At probability \(1/8\) this gain is **0.0279454** with finite error below 0.0000055. The one-member gain is 0.0026520. Comparing the whole register therefore gives a factor of **10.54** per member. The ordinary reference covariance per member is \(-13/392\). The gain makes the summed readings less anticorrelated. The absolute covariance in the peer preparation is also negative. Independent native acquisitions of 4,096 peer and 1,170 ordinary registers gave a gain of **0.02681**, with estimated sampling standard error 0.00466. The detector used the two time totals for each register. The standard error describes that sample. The finite error bound comes from the native return equations. [Simulated time totals and finite forecasts](sector_fronts_collective.json)

The finite calculation conditions on the free type counts \(V\) in an accepted register. Protected words have fixed quotas. Given \(V\), distinct members draw independent native returns within their types and report on independent clocks. Let \(h_x,h_y,h_j\) be their conditional single-home and joint probabilities. The law of total covariance gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(S_X,S_Y)
 =\sum_c E[V_c]\,(h_{j,c}-h_{x,c}h_{y,c})
  +h_x^\top \operatorname{Cov}(V)h_y .
\]
The type-count covariance was evaluated with integer multiplicities over 31,603 accepted families. Within each family the split of blank labels has the multivariate hypergeometric law. The protected words enter the sum with zero count variance. A dyadic residual of the killed return equation bounds the conditional probabilities at \(1/8\). [Count moments and finite return calculation](sector_fronts_counts.py) · [Independent acquisition](sector_fronts_collective.py)

At every positive reporting probability \(p\), the complete gain per member is at least \(0.02884p\). The contribution from distinct members is at least \(0.02705p^2\) per member of the full register. To obtain the all-rate result, subtract the uniform means before summing the native return-age series. Each conditional response then has the form \(h_x=x_0+pX(p)\). With \(\pi=E[V]/N\) and \(M=\operatorname{Cov}(V)/N-\operatorname{diag}(\pi)+\pi\pi^\top\), the excess over the single-member gain is exactly \(p^2X(p)^\top M Y(p)\). Integer first-return enclosures, convolution of 2,048 age coefficients, and an oscillation contraction below 0.142 per 256 returns bound the infinite series across 193 rate intervals. For a uniform subset of \(m\) distinct members the cross contribution per member is \((m-1)/(N-1)\) times that for the whole register. This is a consequence of counting ordered pairs in the subset. ## How much gravity would the collective reading buy?

Grant each unit of the enlarged covariance the ordinary mass equivalent measured by the mean exterior commute clock. Use the double standard, the more generous of the two calibrated ordinary masses. Each member costs its share of the ordinary reference mass. Free radial placement, any positive reporting rate and any subgroup up to 896 then permit at most **0.000634** units of extra mass per unit of ordinary mass. The full photometric forward calculation assigns each mapped tile its baryonic filling as its reporting probability and uses the enrolled native availability and area. It covers **26 of 369** measured rotation speeds within twice their quoted errors and **zero of ten** complete imaged galaxy curves. Even allocating the galaxy's entire ordinary budget to successful preparations at arbitrary radii leaves 334 speeds above the mass allowance.
