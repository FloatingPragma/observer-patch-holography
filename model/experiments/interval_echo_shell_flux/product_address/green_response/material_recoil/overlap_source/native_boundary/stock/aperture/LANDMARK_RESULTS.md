# What an overlap repair changes when every signal route is available

A local source can change where two ordinary material probes arrive, by exactly the same amount, with four ordinary energy readings held fixed. On the 240-port carrier the experiment measures a change in the variance of a signed overlap record and a change in the fraction of contacts that pass both probes. The second measurement, together with calibrated propagation through the surrounding network, fixes the change at the receiving stations. Preparations with the same modular table, ordinary energies and selective-contact frequencies also give zero or reversed changes. The additional source measurement matters. ## Why the source sign survives every positive entropy weight

The marginal head frequencies fix a line of joint two-head tables. Write \(s\) for the fraction of the displacement from the measured original table to the product of those marginals. The latter is feasible under the original home, energy and selective-word constraints. It uniquely maximizes the two-head entropy by the nonnegativity of mutual information. At \(s=1\) the two-head entropy gains 0.04539 nats. The entropy of all sharp words instead falls from 6.10479 to 6.02793 nats. If the observer maximizes word entropy alone, the uniform original is its unique solution and the change is zero.

At fixed \(s\), maximize the word entropy under these same constraints. Throughout \(0<s\le1\), **the extra blank frequency exceeds \(s/512\)**. The inequality also holds for the extra local successful-bit probability in a disjoint pooled preparation, for every physical packing \(0\le(m-1)/V\le1/48\). The latter score adds \(b_0(m-1)N/(4V)\) to the blank flag, with \(N\) the initial single count. The selective frequency and the partial-head/single coincidence are fixed. Linearity reduces the packing check to the two endpoints. This bound concerns expectation. It supplies no finite sample size uniform down to arbitrarily weak source strengths. For \(s\le1/1024\), the least-squares feasible path is the original times \(1+sh\), with \(|h|\le1\), zero mean and \(h\) in the span of the constrained feature rows on the free histories. Consequently any positive coefficient \(\eta\) in the observer rule that maximizes word entropy plus \(\eta\) times two-head entropy gives the common transport sign. On the one-dimensional table interval, the word-entropy envelope is strictly concave and maximal at \(s=0\). The two-head entropy is strictly concave and maximal at \(s=1\). ## What the two probes can distinguish

For the canonical unconstrained modular repair, the blank fraction changes by \(1/231\) and the selective fraction by \(-3/539\). Double probes use the blank bypass with a unit transmitting edge present or absent. For a single on a selective head, the 24 native tickets per site collapse exactly to two copies of the same bypass. One edge of conductance \(1/2\) joins port \(u\) in the first copy to \(v\) in the second. The four selective labels give equal starting weight to the two copies. This reduction is checked at every port and label.

Ground the two absorbing beacons and let \(G\) be the Dirichlet inverse of the bypass Laplacian. Write \(S=G_{uu}+G_{vv}\) and \(r=S-2G_{uv}\). If \(h\) is the bypass probability of reaching beacon B first, opening the unit link changes it at \(x\) by \((h(v)-h(u))(G_{xu}-G_{xv})/(1+r)\). The two-sheet calculation has the same numerator and denominator \(2(2+S)\). This follows by injecting the exchanged current at the two ends and solving for its voltage drop. In the two-sheet case the terminals occupy different copies before connection, so their two grounded self-resistances add. The extra factor two is the equal initial sheet mixture. The killed Green matrix has nonnegative entries by the maximum principle, and \(r\ge0\). The partial response is therefore \(\lambda=(1+r)/(2(2+S))\) times the open response, with \(0<\lambda<1/2\). The single's repair response is \(1-9\lambda/7\) times the double's. They always agree in sign when a signal exists. ## What a single landmark calls inward

In a separate protocol each of six contact slots receives equal sampling weight and each message hop uses fresh stationary material. Missing contacts and erasures cost a tick. The two observers divide their mean round-trip ticks by \(6V\), where \(V\) is the number of ports. On this symmetric walk the resulting raw range \(R(A,x)\) equals the effective resistance. The commute-time identity follows from the two first-step equations on a connected finite graph. At a clean launch the native coherent material acceleration in ordinary squared-clock units is twice the sum of the neighboring changes in the measured coordinate. [Ranging protocol](LANDMARK_RELEASE.md) · [Random walks and electric networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0001057)

Let \(z\) solve the bypass unit-current problem for the transmitting edge \(uv\) and put \(r=z_u-z_v\). Raising its measured success from \(p\) to \(q\) changes \(R(A,x)\) by \(-t(z_A-z_x)^2\), where \(t=(q-p)/[(1+pr)(1+qr)]\). This is the rank-one grounded inverse update. At an exterior launch \(x\) the voltage is harmonic. Expanding the square therefore gives the change of initial material range acceleration
\[
        \Delta\ddot R(A,x)=-2t\sum_{y\sim x}(z_y-z_x)^2 .
\]
The result is independent of \(A\). The same identity adds over sequential increases at several relays, using the unit-current voltage in the network just before each increase. Two independently placed landmarks see exactly zero change on subtracting their ranges. With \(d=\sqrt R\), the two-beacon projected coordinate is their first-hit probability up to a constant and choice of orientation. Its material acceleration vanishes at clean launches away from the beacons in each arm.
