# A joint modular reading predicts the geometry measured by exterior clocks

Two model observers record whether their respective homes at stations 8 and 44 are occupied. A second set of observers times journeys between exterior stations. For one service protocol, four single-home occupancy frequencies and one joint modular reading determine the mean duration of **every** exterior round trip. The joint contribution has the sign of an ordinary deposit when averaged over 19,701 exterior station pairs. Its sign depends on the pair. This gives the mean separation for two choices of ruler, the normalized round-trip time \(d=\langle R\rangle\) and \(d=\sqrt{\langle R\rangle}\). On a clean launch the native contact has hopping amplitude \(-1/2\) for either probe load. In native time \(t\) each neighboring address therefore acquires probability \(t^2/4+O(t^4)\). Both probes moving has probability \(O(t^4)\). If \(L_0\) is the ordinary unweighted graph Laplacian, the initial acceleration of their measured separation is exactly
\[
 a_d(i,j)=-\tfrac12\big[(L_0d)_{ij}+(L_0d)_{ji}\big].
\]

**Why an inward reading on this ruler has a different signature from an ordinary deposit.** Let \(H\) be the difference of the two network Green matrices, grounded at the same station. At a launch whose incident contacts are unmodified, each column of \(H\) is harmonic because the two Green equations have the same row and right-hand side. Substituting \(\Delta R_{ij}=H_{ii}+H_{jj}-2H_{ij}\) and using harmonicity gives
\[
 \Delta a_R(i,j)=\tfrac12\sum_{k\sim i}\Delta R_{ik}
                  +\tfrac12\sum_{k\sim j}\Delta R_{jk}.
\]
Thus the round-trip ruler measures the sum of two local changes at the endpoints. Adding positive ordinary loads away from the two launch neighborhoods increases or preserves every incident resistance by the unit-current minimum-dissipation principle. Such a delivery can only make this acceleration more outward or leave it fixed, even for many simultaneous deposits and after averaging matched preparations.
