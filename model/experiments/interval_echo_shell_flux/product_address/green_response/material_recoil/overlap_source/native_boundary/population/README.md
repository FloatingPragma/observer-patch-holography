# How much energy can heterogeneous writes store in one service?

A coherent native write on an initially blank contact can gain at most the energy incident on that contact when its memory and outgoing loads are physically re-paired. This holds for every density matrix of the nine ternary inputs. On the tested all-blank federation, only three of each owner's twelve ports serve external edges. One simultaneous service on those external edges can gain at most **3/7 of the ordinary energy of the entire board**. A later externally powered service can add more. ## The accounting identity

Let $W$ be the native write isometry and $B=(I-S)/2+8D^2$ the blank-contact energy, where $S$ exchanges the loads and $D$ is their difference. Define the memory observable $G=(I-R)/2+8d^2$ where $R$ reflects the recorded difference $d$. On the written space the actual native generator obeys

$$GW=WB,\qquad H_{\rm written}=G-WBW^\dagger,\qquad H_{\rm written}W=0.$$

The deposit of an incident density $\rho$ into the coherent write image therefore has zero local energy and memory expectation $\operatorname{tr}(B\rho)$. The memory observable commutes with the free generator on its own seam. Other seam generators act as the identity on this memory. Any new joint state $\sigma$ with the same memory marginal has energy between zero and $\operatorname{tr}(B\rho)$, because $B$ and $H_{\rm written}$ are positive. This argument also applies to a reduction of an entangled input. The computed integer matrices verify the identities on the full native contact at stiffness 0, 1 and 8. The operator inequality and the native branch reflection and contrast identities are formalized in Lean. A sharp read dephases the joint load and memory in their pointer basis. It has its own work cost and leaves half the incident pointer-diagonal energy on the contact. The diagonal incident ruler is at most $17/16$ times $B$ as a quadratic form. For nonzero difference the added diagonal term is $1/2$ and the contrast term is at least 8, while the exchange energy in $B$ is positive. Memory exchange can add at most the remaining half. For diagonal inputs the sharp bound is $1/2$ of the actual incident energy and for arbitrary inputs it is $17/32$. These gains use the retained write as the starting state. On two independent copies an actual swap of entire addressed memories realizes the product of the output marginals. A second swap reverses it. Other local seam energies are insensitive to permuting these memories alone.

For a concrete limiting family, mix a symmetric one-load input with probability $p$ into otherwise empty writes, then re-pair between copies without sorting by input. The energy ratios are $1-p/4$ for coherent writes and $(17/32)(1-p/2)$ after the sharp read. At $p=1/2$ the coherent gain is 3.5 on 4 units incident. The simulated native energy measurements also cover $p=1/16$, with $2^{22}$ copies per bank. The near-unit fraction at low $p$ concerns a correspondingly small absolute supply. ## From one contact to a federation

The sum of blank-contact energies over a matching is bounded by the board energy because the remaining terms are positive. For external servicing write $S_g=\sum_{\rm gateway} n_i^2$ over the three gateways per cell. The external blank energy is at most $9S_g$ since the exchange term is bounded by the squared difference and $(n_i-n_j)^2\le n_i^2+n_j^2$ for nonnegative loads. The ordinary complement is at least $12S_g$. To see this, drop the positive exchanges on the cell and count internal squared differences plus squared loads at the nine non-gateways, in units of stiffness eight. Their sum is at least $3S_g/2$. The calculation checks all $3^{12}=531441$ words, taking the three largest squared occupations as the worst possible gateways. The minimizing nonzero word attains equality in the cell inequality. These diagonal inequalities apply to coherent and mixed inputs as well.

Dividing the energy inequalities gives $3/7$ for coherent writes. For a sharp read let $M$ sum the incident squared differences on external edges, so $M\le S_g$. Gains are at most $17M/4$, with incident external energy at least $8M$ and complement at least $12S_g$. This gives $17/80$ for arbitrary quantum inputs. For classical inputs external energy is at most $8.5S_g$, and at most half of it can be gained, giving $17/82$. The earlier $17/260$ bound assumes a uniform binary word within each owner. None of these fractions constrains distinct incident supplies or total work received over many rounds.
