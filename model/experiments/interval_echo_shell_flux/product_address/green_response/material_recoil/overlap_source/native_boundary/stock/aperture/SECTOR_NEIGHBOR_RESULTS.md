## Why extending this clock to a larger exterior has a limit

The change at a remote link can be predicted using ordinary transfer measurements. Let \(B_S\) have the incidence columns of all source links. Put \(X=B_S^\top G_0 B_S\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1-c_f)\), and \(w=B_S^\top G_0 b_e\). The entries of \(w\) are obtainable by polarizing four ordinary terminal resistances. On the space orthogonal to constants the finite inverse update gives
\[
 R_e-R_e^0=w^\top D^{1/2}(I-D^{1/2}XD^{1/2})^{-1}D^{1/2}w .
\]
Here \(X\) is a principal block of the blank current projection, and \(0\le D\le I/2\) in the tested apparatus. This predicts every changed edge to within \(5.2\times10^{-15}\) of the separate full-graph solve. It also explains how an ordinary receiving edge with \(w=0\) can be completely silent. [Independent triangle and articulation examples](test_sector_neighbor_ticks.py)

There is a size-independent bound for any connected finite graph with passive link slowdowns, even with a different exterior topology. From \(L=\sum_e c_e b_e b_e^\top\) one obtains \(\sum_e c_e R_e=\operatorname{tr}(L^+L)=n-1\). Decreasing conductance increases each terminal resistance by the minimum-energy principle. Direct passage along \(f\) bounds \(R_f\le1/c_f\), giving the exact budget and its upper bound:
\[
 \sum_e|R_e-R_e^0|=\sum_f(1-c_f)R_f\ \le\ \sum_f(c_f^{-1}-1).
\]
Any unit flow to a neighbor exits \(i\) with total current one. Cauchy–Schwarz on the exit currents and \(c_e\le1\) give \(R_{iv}\ge1/d_i\). Each incident sum is therefore at least one in each preparation. Since \(\log\) is 1-Lipschitz on that range, for a normalized source/control contrast \(D_z\) the sitewise epoch log-rate changes obey
\[
 \sum_i|\Delta\log\nu_i|\le
  2\sum_z |D_z|\sum_f(1-c_f(z))R_f(z)
 \le 2\sum_z |D_z|\sum_f(c_f(z)^{-1}-1).
\]
The factor two counts both ends of every link. The native peer contrast on the board spends \(3.421\times10^{-5}\) of the first upper bound \(0.008708\). [Finite network calculation](sector_neighbor_ticks.py) · [Random walks and electrical networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0001057)

This also bounds a comparison of clocks that needs no absolute time normalization. Choose one station at each proper shell \(r_k=k\ell\), or average the log rates over distinct shells. Each shell enters at most twice in a sum of absolute octave differences between \(k\) and \(2k\). That sum is at most twice the preceding bound. A nonzero compact Newtonian mass would require \(GM/(2c^2\ell k)\) per octave at large \(k\). For fixed hop length, fixed positive mass and a uniformly bounded source budget, its harmonic sum eventually violates the finite bound on a growing family. The obstruction belongs to compact passive service changes and this choice of clock. A source extending over additional shells spends an additional budget.

## Ten galaxies at the ordinary calibration

We used the mapped baryons and atomic gas separately to place sources on the ten imaged SPARC disks. For each active four-home sample the laboratory reporting probability was fixed at \(1/8\). The adopted disk transfer uses \(4p\) ordinary load opportunities per tile and half the four-site pair availability \(6p^2(1-p)^2(448/2110)\) for the joint contrast. Here \(p\) is the imaged filling divided by the tile capacity. With a common hop length, each source contributes the measured shell-mean log frequency out to the last half-hop. The ordinary source is centered on its deposition site 20. The joint source is centered on the first station minimizing the greatest distance to the four homes, station 178. Azimuthal averaging uses the exact intersection angle of each map ring with each laboratory shell. Finite radial continuation, tile weights, and linear superposition in the abundance of sources are assumptions of this comparison.

For each held galaxy we chose the magnitude and hop scale by minimizing the mean squared fractional-to-RMS baryonic potential errors on the other nine. The eight scale options were one 0.8 kpc pixel and \(1/16,1/8,1/4,1/2,1,2,4\) times the imaged disk scale. Orbital comparisons use the weak-field relation \(d\log N/d\log r=v^2/c^2\), interpolate squared speeds linearly in log radius, take two quoted velocity errors at each endpoint, and hold the published baryon model fixed. The lower and upper values are sensitivity bands under these choices. At each of 14 tracer/capacity settings, **30 of 359 annuli and zero of ten complete galaxies** agree at the ordinary calibration.
