# Measuring the modular source and testing its geometric consequence

A model observer can establish a source locally even if a laboratory force measurement is beyond reach. To infer extra gravity, the finite implication has to take that same record, at its measured reference and normalization, to a common change in the geometry inferred from ordinary clocks and trajectories. Here the first step succeeds. Pooling local clocks produces a measurable modular contribution. A fixed-volume survey supplies a counterexample to identifying that contribution with the entropy term balanced by its boundary timing. ## What can a local observer establish?

Four timing links read the occupancies of homes 8, 20, 32 and 44. On each of two members the observer times a protected ordinary round trip and adds the readings link by link. Both ordinary probe loads give the same record. A controlled experiment uses two equally weighted preparations in each arm. Every individual member has exactly the same complete record histogram in both arms. The joint four-clock records are as follows.

| Arm | First recorded tuple | Second recorded tuple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Together | (2, 4, 4, 2) | (3, 4, 4, 3) |
| Crossed | (3, 4, 4, 2) | (2, 4, 4, 3) |

For an observed tuple \(u\) define its relative modular reading as \(Q(u)=\log[P_{\rm exchanged}(u)/P_{\rm retained}(u)]\). The two reference laws come from an ordinary thermal clock calibration. The labels refer to whether the reference bath exchanges the sector label. Keeping the change of sign explicit, **crossed minus together increases the measured mean \(Q\) by 0.02250–0.05111 nats**. The tuple records and calibration suffice to infer this interval without accessing the preparation labels at readout. The control uses repeatable simulated ordinary exchanges. The probabilities of the two preparations are set by a balanced design. The finite native prediction for the pooled four-home charge change is **+0.0001036 to +0.0001254 nats per pair**, rounded outward from the region-specific intervals. This number is calculated from measured reference prices, native return probabilities and exact population counts. It is distinct from the controlled measurement above. ## Which geometric premise does that close?

Center the shortest-time survey on marker 231. Fix the volume at 197 of the 239 markers and order ties by public address. Bound every mean link time between its all-empty and fully occupied values. The worst inside distance and address are (12, 44). The best outside pair is (12, 46). Monotonicity of shortest times certifies the same membership for every number of reports and every supported preparation. All four homes lie inside. All 36 crossing links avoid the homes and return exactly one acknowledgement period per member. Thus *every function of this complete crossing-time record* has zero change between matched preparations. This is an exact instrument result. Its volume is measured in markers. Identifying those markers with small proper spacetime balls on a refinement family would require additional evidence. [Distance certificate and list of links](sector_register_balls.json)

With ordered member records on a fixed ball, the independent reference prices add, \(K(u,v)=K_1(u)+K_2(v)\). The difference of joint laws has zero row and column sums, so its entropy first-law response \(\sum_{uv}\Delta P(u,v)K(u,v)\) vanishes for *every* faithful independent reference of this kind. Pooling changes what the observers retain. Its price is the negative logarithm of the summed reference probability of each pooled outcome. At the ordinary reference the native pair's pooled entropy derivative on the 197-marker ball is \(-0.000361216\) nats. With the retained thermal reference it is between \(-0.0003670\) and \(-0.0003551\), and with the exchanging reference between \(-0.0004806\) and \(-0.0004706\). Their difference gives the positive modular response above. These are first variations about the respective reference laws, and the modular-price expectation is linear in the intervention. Across 794 balls at 199 exterior centers, 26 admit a resolved positive area coefficient from an ordinary double delivered to an empty home. All 26 fail the resulting entropy balance for the distinct-member source, for either choice of record. Five admit a resolved positive coefficient for boundary service capacity, and all five fail the corresponding test. Geometry does change elsewhere. At center 178 and volume 197 the mean crossing count increases by 0.0015852 for the native pair. The ordinary calibration there still misses the source's required change. The experiment closes a premise about a modular charge on an identified observer algebra. The implication from that charge to ordinary-normalized gravitating stress remains open. ## Can a probe arriving at the boundary still see the source?

Yes, when it travels through the interior. Launch a probe uniformly among the 197 interior markers. At each tick choose one of six slots. A link with measured mean period \(t\) passes with probability \(1/t\). The first exit is an ordinary arrival anywhere outside. The calibrated single and double give the same walk. Raising the local charge by the controlled crossing above increases the 256-tick exit probability by about \(5.6154\times10^{-9}\). The native peer intervention also raises the charge and decreases that probability by about \(2.5158\times10^{-8}\). These are finite path probabilities on the effective walk reconstructed from the measured link periods. For launches outside, the entire transition matrix up to first entry is identical on all preparations. The first-entry distribution is identical at **every** deadline. [First-exit protocol](SECTOR_REGISTER_ESCAPE.md) · [Finite path calculation](sector_register_escape.py)

The different signs persist at **exactly the same local charge and from the same initial ensemble**. Prepare a baseline of half the together arm and half the independent native arm. Within the first half one can switch a fraction to crossed records. Alternatively, within the second half one can switch a fraction to distinct native members. Each switch preserves the full single-member histograms. If a full switch changes the calibrated charge by \(q_i>0\), switch the fraction \(2(0.00005)/q_i\) in its own half. Both fractions lie strictly between zero and one over the measured calibrations. Each preparation then raises the pooled charge by exactly \(0.00005\) nats. The two 256-tick exit responses have opposite signs. This is a finite inference about tunable mixtures, not a claim to have prepared an exact physical sample at that value. Other joint statistics differ. A source law for this propagation observable must retain some further information. [Equal-charge mixture and signed intervals](sector_register_escape.json)

## Does adding more reports change the price?

Two exterior routes from 72 to 97 give \(12N+2S\) and \(12N+2T\), where \(S,T\) are the counts of occupied homes 8 and 44. For a one-member binary reference table \(p\), let \(\omega=p_{00}p_{11}/(p_{01}p_{10})\). In the independent product reference, the modular price of \((s,t)\), up to separate functions of \(s\) and \(t\), is
\[
 -\log\sum_k {s\choose k}{N-s\choose t-k}\omega^k .
\]
This follows by grouping the possible joint counts \(k\) at fixed margins. In a four-corner intervention the marginal prices cancel. At \(N=896\), two native preparations both increase \(\operatorname{Cov}(S,T)\) by exactly \(1/2\). Yet the changes in their relative modular readings lie in disjoint intervals, \([-0.000161,-0.000156]\) and \([-0.006485,-0.006379]\) nats, with the same four-word switch applied at different aggregate counts. The covariance alone cannot specify this charge. On the archived acquisition of 4,096 peer and 1,170 ordinary registers, subtracting the independent-channel comparison within each arm gives a peer-minus-ordinary change of \(-0.00772\) nats per whole register, with jackknife sampling standard error \(0.00134\) at the nominal thermal calibration. This is a different record and intervention from the local four-home comparison. [Native clock control and pooled price](sector_register_price.json)

## What does that charge predict for galaxies?

Use a common thermal conversion \(\Delta E=\Delta Q/\beta\), with \(\beta=\log(8/7)\), ordinary insertion-energy standards, disjoint paid members and a Newtonian spherical transfer. These are hypotheses about how to count and gravitate the measured charge. For each imaged tile use its baryonic filling as the native reporting probability \(p\) and its previously enrolled return availability. Two members pay for each local reading. No orbital speed enters the source assignment. At all 14 tracer and capacity settings these radial forecasts cover 26 of 369 SPARC speeds within two quoted errors and zero complete curves among ten galaxies. Even allowing arbitrary radial placement and reporting rate, 334 speeds need more than the whole galaxy's allowed pair budget. [Radial predictions and budgets](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/sector_register_sky.json) · [SPARC catalogue](https://astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/)

For this pooled charge a finite return calculation also explains what happens in sparsely observed tiles. Write the conditional returned-word table \(H(p)={\bf1}u^\top+pX(p)\), and \(M=\operatorname{Cov}(V)/N-\operatorname{diag}(\pi)+\pi\pi^\top\), with \(\pi=EV/N\) and protected word quotas included. Two distinct draws have excess joint law \(p^2 X^\top M X/(N-1)\). Grouping them into the measured home sums and multiplying by their thermal prices gives, for \(0<p\le2^{-24}\), between \(0.4961p^2\) and \(1.0430p^2\) nats per pair. The computation encloses 1,024 return ages, the remaining oscillation of the return operator and both reference calibrations on 193 rate intervals. This specific sparse-report mechanism suppresses the measured charge per pair. [All-rate calculation](sector_register_source.py) · [Enclosures and one-eighth check](sector_register_source.json)

There is a stronger limit for these two thermal references. In each of the 59 native sectors, the retained and exchanging baths have exactly the same normalized within-sector operator. Write \(r_j\) for the ratio of their sector weights. For any positive outcome effect on \(N\) members, its probability ratio at the two product references is a weighted mean of the products \(\prod_i r_{j_i}\). The weights are nonnegative because each tensor sector has a positive operator and the outcome effect is positive. Consequently every reported log ratio lies between \(N\min_j\log r_j\) and \(N\max_j\log r_j\). The difference between its means on *any* two supported preparations is at most \(N(\max_j\log r_j-\min_j\log r_j)\). This includes joint instruments and arbitrary correlations in the prepared source. It assumes independent product **reference** baths and the same observation map on both arms.

The ordinary calibration bounds the one-member span by 8.0001 nats and the four-home ordinary cost from below by 191.879 native energy units per member. At the tested conversion the extra-to-ordinary mass ratio is therefore below **0.313 for every register size**. The difference of two separately connected comparisons is below 0.625. Even this latter allowance excludes 184 speeds across all ten imaged curves when the entire galaxy budget is available to each radius. All four isolated weak-lensing stacks at 291.07 kpc need at least 5.41 under the published spherical deprojection and the doubled baryonic allowance used here. Increasing the number of reports, regrouping the same records or moving this paid source between radii cannot supply the missing amplitude at this conversion. An OPH gravitational mechanism with a different physical stress must derive its coupling and radial distribution from shared records.
