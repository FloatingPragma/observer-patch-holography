# A measured source for the entropy of moving records

On the returned Interval Echo board, a laboratory observer can identify a local interaction that produces more shared records than a binary signal can convey. The measured abundance of this interaction enters an exact relation between interior history entropy, boundary communication and volume measured by communication. At matched volume the relation predicts a decrease in boundary communication in one held region. Other regions containing the same positive source gain boundary communication. A universal attractive response and a common astronomical source profile remain open. [Protocol](MOVING_RECORDS.md) · [Raw counts, matched preparations and finite certificates](moving_records.json)

## A source that can be measured without a force detector

Calibrate sharp inputs on one native seam. At a full exchange pulse, 21 of the 99 input columns return the original word with certainty, 66 change it deterministically, and 12 give four equally probable words including the original. Endpoint and shared-head records distinguish the outcomes. Prepare two fresh copies with the same measured input. Two **different noninitial** outputs identify a four-way interaction. The probability of that witness is \(3/8\) on a four-way input and zero on the other 87 inputs. Ten independent pairs on a specified four-way input have over 99% probability of producing a witness with ideal readers. This test detects the interaction. Quantifying a small change of its prevalence is a separate experiment. [Column audit and paired acquisitions](branch_balance.py)

Use all 630 seams on the 240-port board. After the closed preparation, apply native half-exchanges with sharp reads to moving material. Each nonzero column of the full native Hamiltonian preserves the unordered pair of incident loads. The composition is therefore conserved even in free coherent propagation. The stationary probabilities below refer specifically to the monitored exchange. Blank edges span a connected graph, so nearest-neighbor transpositions generate all permutations of a given material composition. To change a written head, the material can bring any available ordered pair to its ends and move the loads back. Thus each communicating class is the product of the material permutations and the reachable label sets of the three heads. Symmetric transition probabilities and a positive chance of holding give a uniform stationary law in each class under random servicing of all seams. Stationarity also holds at every appointment of a fixed internal sweep begun in this law. A simulation from a returned sharp word records 10,080 successive appointments. The equilibrium calculation uses the exact class law. [Stationary-class derivation and native matrices](moving_service.py) · [Small-graph exhaustive replay](test_moving_service.py)

On an ordinary blank contact the probability of seeing unequal loads at stationarity is
\[
 U=1-\frac{\sum_{j=0}^2 N_j(N_j-1)}{240\cdot239}.
\]
The four home readings suffice to predict this distant quantity. For four load units and \(m\) doubles, \(N_0=236+m,\ N_1=4-2m,\ N_2=m\). The minimal two-head repair changes the two moments of \(m\) by \(-23/1617\) and \(18/539\). Consequently \(\Delta U=11/100380\) at every one of the 627 blank seams. The independent source census predicts 0.00010801–0.00011126 at 99% confidence. A conservative two-arm design requires 408,459 independent accepted four-home readings per arm to distinguish this increase from zero at a one-sided 1% bound under either ideal prediction. Other reachable repairs preserve the same head table, all four mean loads, the compact-path energies and the original repair's returned-energy target while giving either sign of \(\Delta U\). One of these also decreases the total four-way frequency. The extra composition and head readings matter to the implication.

## How the regional relation works

The sender chooses a full native pulse or a wait on an existing contact. The receiver reads its target load and the shared head before and after the common slot. A change identifies the pulse. Since the unordered pair of loads is conserved, the receiver can detect every change of the full contact word from these readings. With equal bit priors, twice the best decoding probability minus one equals the full-pulse change probability \(p\).

In independent trials an observer registers the initial sharp regional configuration and the result after each half pulse inside the region. Let \(S\) be the extra joint history entropy in bits per sweep. The chain rule adds the conditional entropy of each new result, including when consecutive pulses share a port. For the deterministic full-pulse columns, half-pulse entropy equals \(p\). On a four-way column the half-pulse probabilities are \(5/8,1/8,1/8,1/8\), while \(p=3/4\). Write \(\gamma=9/4-(5/8)\log_2 5\) for their difference. Let \(F\) be the sum of the four-way input frequencies on interior seams. Count each crossing channel's \(p\) once for boundary \(A\), and each incident channel at every enrolled marker for volume \(V\). Each interior channel occurs twice in \(V\). This gives the exact finite identity
\[
             S+\tfrac12 A-\tfrac12 V=\gamma F .
\]
Two independent ordinary aperture equations have coefficients \((12,-32)\) and \((15,-37)\) and right sides \(-10,-11\) for a blank ordinary double, after cancelling its measured change rate. They uniquely give both coefficients \(1/2\). The same ordinary double inserted on the written source has exactly zero change in \(F\). An added ordinary single gives a four-way term of 0.00128020 bits per sweep. [Finite implications](../../../../../../../../../../Lean/ObserverPatchHolography/EinsteinBranch/SourceApertures.lean)

The measured repair raises the four-way frequency on all three eligible written seams, by \(5/562128,\ 3/294448,\ 373/1325016\), respectively. The net term is 0.000240109 bits per sweep. At the third seam the paired-output detector predicts a residual of 0.0001803–0.0002711 bits. Independent history and return measurements give 0.0001761–0.0002720 bits, rounded outward. A direct comparison of the rare paired witness against a zero-change control needs up to 1,652,980,468 accepted pairs per arm under a conservative Hoeffding design for two simple hypotheses. These are ideal design counts and simulated acquisitions. Laboratory fidelity, physical preparation and access to the shared head require measurement in hardware.

## What a local material reading predicts at a distance

Two observers at the ends of the third written contact measure how much material arrives at the receiving end, minus how much was there before the exchange. Call this signed reading \(J\), positive toward port 46. On each blank contact the monitored half pulse averages the two mean loads exactly. The first two written contacts preserve each incident load even when their heads change. Only the third contact can change the material readings beyond those predicted by blank transfer. Its two readers can therefore predict a complete remote material census. [Release protocol](MATERIAL_RELEASE.md) · [Exact native computation and separate whole-board simulation](material_release.py)

Write \(b=e_{46}-e_{32}\). Let \(B_1,B_2\) propagate the expected material readings through the measured blank exchanges in each round. For any initial distribution, including correlated loads and resident heads, linearity of expectation and pair conservation give
\[
x_2=B_2B_1x_0+B_2B_1b\,J_1+B_2b\,J_2 .
\]
Here \(J_i\) denotes the mean of the signed readings at that appointment. No assumption of independent visits enters the formula. All three written contacts stay active through both rounds. The acquisition parks the initial endpoint loads, launches a single or double in either direction and subtracts an otherwise identical empty release. At equal interior history and communication volume, the previously measured positive branching contrast shifts the single's mean radius by \(+0.00008487\) hops from one launch and \(-0.00008487\) from the other. For the double the shifts per unit load are \(+0.00012579\) and \(-0.00012579\). Reversing the service order reverses these signs. A changed law preserving the outgoing head frequencies but holding the two loads gives zero signed current at both visits.

**Equal weighting of the two launch directions gives exactly zero excess at every terminal station**, for both probes and both schedules. This is an exact equality of means; an individual arrival is random. Both local currents change sign under reversal of the launch contrast. A separate calculation propagating the three possible terminal load values gives the same mean at all 240 stations. Direct simulations of 8,192 complete boards per schedule, each servicing all 630 contacts twice, reproduce the predicted local and terminal distributions within their simultaneous 99% bounds. The much smaller matched shifts are resolved by ideal histogram draws at \(2^{46}\) trials per histogram. Their counts describe a simulation and give no physical laboratory exposure estimate. There is also a statement about the long-time limit, for either launch direction separately. Each half pulse is doubly stochastic and has a positive holding probability. The repeated sweep can enact any one allowed exchange while every other visit holds. The connected blank graph allows every permutation of the material at fixed head labels. It follows that within each reachable class the stationary material placement is uniform at fixed inventory. Replacing the empty launch by a load \(t\) therefore changes every station's late categorical probabilities by exactly \((e_t-e_0)/240\). This holds for any of the tested preparations and for every repeated order that services all seams. Subtracting the ordinary reference leaves zero excess in every late mean. ## What observers measure when they reconstruct the chart

At a full pulse, send a random bit in two copies of an agreed sharp start. Send a positive load on the first or the second copy according to the bit. The receiving observer keeps a load pair only if it identifies the bit uniquely. Each fresh attempt, including an erasure, costs one pair of pulses. Every blank contact accepts immediately. The first two written contacts convey no bit on either material alphabet. Let \(p\) be the acceptance probability on the third contact. Independent renewal gives mean cost \(1/p\). Both ends of that contact already lie seven blank hops from station 219. A shortcut between them, even with zero cost, leaves **all 240 radial ranges** from that station unchanged by the triangle inequality. Distances between stations *on* that shell can still change. [Relay protocol and cost convention](RELAY_RANGE.md) · [Relay kernel and independent graph calculation](relay_range.py)

There is a local experimental premise for those transverse distances. Let \(u_t\) be the mean fraction of a load \(t\) that reaches an initially empty receiver on a half pulse. On each of 99 sharp starts the full relay acceptance is \(2u_t+C_t\), using the corresponding head on the empty-target trial. The correction can be measured before transmission. For the double it is half the frequency of a target single together with head label 2, 4, 7 or 9. For the single it is a signed half frequency of occupied targets on those heads, with opposite signs for the two head pairs and the reverse direction. Its average vanishes in all six stationary preparations tested. These equalities follow by counting the two full-pulse outcomes and the empty-target half-pulse outcome separately. No orbital record sets a coefficient.

The canonical repair **shortens the mean transverse range** over all 378 unordered pairs of the 28 shell stations, by 0.005095 attempt units on the single receiver and 0.011151 on the double. Source-side acceptance counts predict the independently drawn terminal renewal times. When volume and interior history are both matched, the arm with greater four-way frequency **lengthens** the same two ranges by 0.002923 and 0.005593. Adding an ordinary double changes neither transverse mean. Thus the experiment closes the implication from local transfer and occupied-target records to this perceived change in geometry. The ordinary control supplies no nonzero range response with which to identify this as ordinary-coupled gravity. [Local readings, held transverse times and ordinary controls](relay_range.json)

The dark-matter paper also asks whether a **fluctuation amplitude** could source gravity. On the first half pulse, normalize the signed transfer by the released load. Its only outcomes are \(-1,0,1\). With the two equally likely launch directions its mean vanishes and its variance is exactly the probability of a transfer. A separate acquisition of \(2^{28}\) sharp trials per arm and alphabet measures this variance. The canonical repair *increases* it by \(19/12936\) for the single and \(1/462\) for the double. In the same preparation the direction-averaged terminal material excess is zero throughout the board. Adding the ordinary double leaves both normalized variances unchanged. A positive change in this particular local fluctuation therefore coexists with zero excess in the specified mean motion. The paper's premise that the amplitude of a collar fluctuation contributes as gravitating mass would need a physical identification beyond the existence of a measurable variance. ## What a balanced release would predict in galaxies

If the galaxy source is a linear sum of this two-round excess material response, with equally likely launch directions at each patch, its extra term vanishes at every radius under any common linear mass conversion. Any radial abundance of such patches gives the same result. This continuation tests a particular source law. A stress derived from other joint readings, a preferred orientation measured independently, or a different clock would require its own implication.

With this zero extra term, the deposited SPARC gas, disk and bulge curves at the reference mass-to-light ratios 0.5 (disk) and 0.7 (bulge) reproduce 452 of 3,391 observed speeds within two quoted errors, and four of the 175 complete curves. The previously enrolled ten disks give 26 of 369 and no complete curve. As a sensitivity test, give each galaxy its own gas multiplier from 0.7 to 1.5, disk ratio from 0.3 to 0.8, bulge ratio from 0.4 to 1.0, and distance and inclination within two quoted errors. Allow one common choice over all its radii. Just 29 of the 175 galaxies then admit a complete curve. Within the 153 quality 1–2 galaxies at inclinations of at least 30 degrees the corresponding count is 20. These are pointwise error-band comparisons, with a more permissive nuisance-parameter test; they do not represent an independent new survey or a joint cosmological likelihood.
