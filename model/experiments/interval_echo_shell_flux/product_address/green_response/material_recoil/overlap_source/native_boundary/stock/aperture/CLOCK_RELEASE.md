# Does the local clock delay transfer to freely released ordinary material?

Use original home 20, the monitored clock site 13, and the first adjacent port two transparent hops from 20 with the same degree as 13. Require both placements to touch only blank contacts and the farther site to border no other home. Release a single or double in the even standing combination of the two ports. Keep the odd parity as a control. Both have the same initial load counts and zero initial current. Open the full native board, including all three original written contacts and all degree references. On matched copies release the identical original word without the added probe. Measure the near-minus-far ordinary load contrast and the total ordinary load weighted by transparent hop distance to home 20. Subtract the release without the probe and divide by the injected load. Calibrate the same experiment with an ordinary donor at home 20 on an otherwise empty blank board. Derive the initial acceleration of both measured quantities from full native columns. Compare the signs and ordinary-calibrated responses to the two clocks.

For a source serviced repeatedly, also test whether the changed clock rate survives autonomous sharp writes and half exchanges on all three original service contacts. Use the entire connected two-double inventory. Admit all intermediate outcomes. Compare advancing the source service at every attempt and advancing only at a recorded tick. Resolve the distribution seen at arbitrary slots and at ticks, and check whether two different initial accepted preparations lead to different stationary clock rates. These scheduler comparisons are part of the apparatus.
