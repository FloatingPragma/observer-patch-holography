# A measured overlap effect forces an exterior tide on the finite board

The canonical two-head repair changes a local communication experiment. Its measured sign forces a more inward projected relative acceleration on 25 of 991 registered exterior chords. Every other chord in that cohort has exactly zero response. Both native probe loads give the same acceleration on the common ruler and ordinary clock. At the four-hop pair the initial separation is eight in both preparations. Matching the scalar modular-charge and energy readings alone also admits a preparation with the opposite tidal change on that very pair. The exterior effect has a measured radial distribution that can be compared with galaxies. [Source, remote witness and full survey](exterior_tide.json) · [Exterior protocol](EXTERIOR_TIDE.md)

The corresponding many-source experiment finds an inward change that requires three repaired overlaps on a selected native message circuit. An independent local calibration gives the ordinary material population producing the same perceived tide. On the full communication graph that particular pair is silent. ## What the laboratory observers record

On the third written contact use two native full pulses on fresh copies of the same sharp input. Send a bit by two different two-slot words of loads from \(\{0,1,2\}\). A shared random mask exchanges the two codewords with probability one half. The receiving observer decodes only a pair of endpoint readings possible for a single bit value. For each agreed start choose the best of the 36 codeword pairs using their measured conditional probabilities. This gives one fastest ruler within this protocol for both material probes. The mask, code and renewal convention are explicit apparatus choices. The time unit is a two-pulse attempt and omits the time to prepare the copies and exchange acknowledgments. [Optimal relay and separate receiver tests](tidal_release.py)

On the stationary preparation let \(a\) be the observed fraction of blank heads, \(b\) the fraction with one of the four partially transmitting labels, and \(c\) the fraction with such a label and a single load at the target. The native transition table and the uniform accessible head labels give the same acceptance in both directions,
\[
p=a+(b+c)/4 .
\]
At most four singles are present on 240 addresses, so \(0\le c\le b/60\), even allowing arbitrary correlations between the accessible label class and the conserved composition. The two head fractions alone put the repair's \(p\) in 0.306509–0.307974 and the original in 0.303571–0.305060 (endpoints rounded). These ranges are disjoint. Independent finite counts still separate them. A two-sample Hoeffding bound gives 8,768,053 ideal independent preparations per arm as sufficient for a one-sided 1% test with at least 99% power at this gap. This estimate counts accepted, stationary preparations; their physical production rate is unknown. The expected attempts per successful relay are \(1/p\), decreasing from 3.282853 to 3.250721. When the full two-head modular score, the four mean home loads and the two energy readings are held at the repair values, an alternative returned-word population has \(p=0.264952\). At the four-hop witness its initial length stays eight, while its acceleration becomes more outward relative to the original. The blank and partially transmitting head readings supply physical information beyond the modular score. [Exact source comparison, matched readings and held times](exterior_source.py)

## Why those local readings imply perceived relative acceleration

Calibrate a local clock by the probability for a single to reach an adjacent blank port on sharp release. In the squared clock reading \(s^2\) use that probability. The native Hamiltonian gives \(s^2=\tau^2/4+O(\tau^4)\) at small native exposure \(\tau\). A release from a sharp position has zero initial first derivative for each position read. For a recorded local input let \(q\) be its full-pulse material-swap probability and \(D\) the probability that two copies disagree about a swap. The coherent swap probability divided by \(s^2\) tends to \(q+144D\). All 99 columns establish this identity. Their normalized rates are 0, 1 and \(145/2\). A separate acquisition at finite dwell tests the spectral remainder. The ordinary blank rate is one for each distinct incident load pair. Owing to the unique edge on which the loads change, its transition amplitude has no contribution from another contact. Diagonal energies have zero initial position curvature on a sharp input. Launch at two stations \(U,V\) outside every written endpoint. Both have the same measured radius from station 219. For separation \(d\) and initial chord \(L=d(U,V)\), read each landing through \(\phi(x)=[d(U,x)^2-d(V,x)^2]/(2L^2)\). The difference between the mean coordinates of the two opposite releases begins at one. Subtract the empty release in each arm. On a blank contact the acceleration of any load-occupancy mean is twice the sum of its neighboring differences in ordinary clock units. A single and a double therefore obey the same exterior equation. Writing \(K\) for the second derivative of the normalized projected separation gives
\[
L^2 K=\sum_{i\sim U}d(V,i)^2+\sum_{j\sim V}d(U,j)^2
-(\deg U+\deg V)(1+L^2).
\]
Here each incident link has unit range. All ranges are shortest times of the calibrated message protocol. A cheaper third link can only shorten those times. If the initial chord length is equal in both preparations, every changed term on the right is nonpositive. **One strictly shorter cross-range forces a strictly more inward relative acceleration.** No remote gravitational force reading enters this inference. The mathematical conclusion concerns this operational coordinate and initial acceleration. A Euclidean metric at astronomical scales and its identification with free fall require further evidence. A direct interaction also moves material across the third written contact. Two complete monitored sweeps give a different finite experiment. Compare four interventions, empty, each single launch and both launches. The joint response is the double launch minus the two single launches plus the empty one. Catch a signed transfer on the written contact together with an occupied second station. Its contribution to a weighted remote pair census is the coincident current times the difference in the two transported receiver weights. Summing over both visits reconstructs the terminal pair census. When the initial background contains none of the probe load, the final two positive probe readings give the pair directly. ## Which radial distribution reaches a galaxy observer

On a circle in an axisymmetric field the acceleration at each position is \(-v^2\mathbf x/r^2\). Two equal-radius positions therefore have relative acceleration \(-v^2(\mathbf x_V-\mathbf x_U)/r^2\), regardless of their angular separation. The observed circular speed and baryonic mass model specify the required excess chord tide. At 5.92 kpc in DDO 154 the nominal two-error interval is 44.97–58.48 \((\mathrm{km/s})^2/\mathrm{kpc}^2\) inward. These are rotation-curve inferences. [SPARC catalogue and mass models](https://astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/) · [Data comparison](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/tidal_rotation.json)

The local exterior experiment predicts a radial profile without using rotation speeds. The signal is largest at six hops, weakens by seven, and vanishes on shells 9 through 15. On the five active registered shells (4 through 8) its mean is equivalent to 8.01–8.02 ordinary single insertions relative to their own measured tides. The ordinary double gives zero change. The ordinary single's inferred \(r^3 K(r)\) also varies across shells, leaving the absolute Newtonian mass conversion open.

For a conditional astronomical forecast, assign each eligible overlap patch the measured exterior profile and distribute the patches in annuli using the independent radio and light maps. Adopt one stellar disk scale length per hop, interpolate the native tide between its measured shells, and truncate its range outside the finite chart. Sum the inward and outward radial force projections from each annulus, including annuli outside the orbit. Use five possible active calibration shells, the measured ordinary single-to-double energy ratio as a mass ratio, two imaging tracers and seven capacities. The most favorable combination covers 28 of 369 SPARC speeds within two quoted errors. No complete galaxy curve is recovered. Even granting a free positive mass normalization, one common gain covers at most 128 of 369 speeds. Giving each galaxy its own positive gain still recovers no complete curve at this length mapping. The reference six-hop calibration with the first mapped-baryon capacity gives 26 of 369. Doubling the radial and angular quadrature resolutions changes no nominal acceptance classifications. The galaxy comparison tests this choice of spatial and mass conversion and the imaged, axisymmetric patch distribution. ## Can the measured source predict the galaxy profile at a different size?

A change in patch size can move the peak of the perceived attraction to another radius. It also changes the force per unit mass at that radius. The same patch acts on every orbit. Giving each target its own source distribution would discard that last physical condition. The following comparisons keep the imaged patch inventory, the ordinary energy-to-mass identification, linear addition of the finite compact field and an axisymmetric disk. They grant each source annulus every positive physical patch size and every nonnegative mixture at its given total mass. The baryonic mass models and two-error speed bands are those of the ten enrolled SPARC galaxies. Their quoted speed errors set pointwise comparison bands. Distance, inclination and stellar mass-to-light systematics are fixed at the catalogue choices. [Comparison protocol](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/TIDAL_SCALES.md) · [SPARC data](https://astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/)

**The bound.** Write \(L\) for kpc per hop, \(x>0\) for the orbital radius, \(K(u)\) for the inward native tide at \(u\) hops, \(D=s^3K_{\mathrm{ord}}(s)>0\) for the ordinary calibration and \(\mu=405/1557\) for the assumed ordinary mass ratio. The mass model assigns surface density \(\Sigma_j\) to each imaged annulus between \(R_{j-1}\) and \(R_j\). Polar integration about the observer cancels opposite radial projections. A disk edge \(R\) contributes the angular first moment
\[
h_R(x,\rho)=\frac{\sqrt{((x+R)^2-\rho^2)(\rho^2-(x-R)^2)}}{x\rho}
\quad (|x-R|<\rho<x+R).
\]
Outside this interval the contribution is zero, with one-sided value 2 at \(\rho=0\) when \(R=x>0\). Substituting \(\rho=Lu\) in the area integral gives the radial acceleration
\[
a_x(L)=\frac{G\mu}{D}\int u^2K(u)
       \sum_j\Sigma_j\big[h_{R_j}(x,Lu)-h_{R_{j-1}}(x,Lu)\big]\,du .
\]
The formula includes exterior annuli and the small-\(L\) limit at an annular boundary. It retains the sign of each radial projection. For finite \(x,R>0\) the peak of \(h_R\) is \(2\min(1,R/x)\), attained at \(\rho=\sqrt{|x^2-R^2|}\) or approached at zero. The program encloses each arc with integer square roots at the endpoints and the peak when present. It integrates the piecewise-linear kernel weights exactly before outward dyadic rounding. Intervals cover all positive lengths, including the zero-field tail. A nonnegative mass mixture is bounded by the largest response in its support. [Annular force and interval arithmetic](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/tidal_scale_kernel.py) · [Scale bounds for every disk and calibration](../../../../../../../../../../code/cosmology/paid_trail_bao/tidal_scales.json)

The most favorable annular length mixtures supply at most 99.08. At 44.08 kpc in NGC 3198 the corresponding values are a required minimum above 368.90 and an upper bound below 52.69. **An extended range alone does not fix the normalization.** For a point source in the interior of a band of lengths, effective enclosed mass is the mass integral of \((\mu/D)(r/L)^3K(r/L)\).
