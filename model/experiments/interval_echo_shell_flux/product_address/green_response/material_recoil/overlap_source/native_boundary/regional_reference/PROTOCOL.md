# Does an equilibrium modular charge predict the matched probe?

Use the same native closed 24-port, two-owner assembly, complete one-load/four-memory Hamiltonian, and original 01 input as the matched-probe experiment. The matched finite-load probe results and their opposite signs at time four are known at design. Test an independently defined modular reference: the equilibrium state of the entire freely coupled assembly, reduced by tracing out all ports outside a ball about the two endpoints. Use equal graph-distance cuts of radius 0, 1, 2 and the whole board, conserving total load one and absolute written difference one, at inverse temperature log(8/7) fixed by the ordinary shutter standard; include half and double this value. No target read determines the reference. Compare three sources at identical ordinary and memory marginals: the retained native sharp write, the independently re-paired state, and an alternative physical preparation that transfers bright to dark native phase on the same retained input and costs the same energy. Evaluate their actual full regional logarithm on its support, calibrate the scalar gravity prediction on the alternative preparation and test it on the re-paired one. Address the connected modular component as well as the full charge. Where symmetry forces equality, give an exact matrix argument, and identify the sector inaccessible to the single-load experiment. Do not identify graph distance, canonical reference selection or a directional transient with physical gravity by definition.

Include asymmetric comparison cuts centered on either individual endpoint at every graph radius containing both source ends. Compare the full modular measurement law of the final two alternatives as well as the difference of means. Their reduced load and memory distributions coincide, so subtraction of any separate memory or load logarithm has zero effect on the difference. Record the observable that does distinguish these sources even on the original contact.
