## The same source on the same board

On each of the 448 accepted sharp words, let \(n\) be the load at port 20. Put an ordinary unit at its empty neighbor 13 and write their original blank contact once. In both allowed native write outcomes its stored contrast, oriented toward the middle, equals \(X=n-1\). The original controlled preparation and its minimally reweighted repair have \(\mathbb E n=1\). The variance of this signed contrast is therefore the nonunit frequency \(f\). The exact zero-zero identity gives \(f_0=3/28,\ f_1=5/28\). The increase in variance is precisely \(1/14\), and multiplying it by the measured reference log odds \(\log(11/3)\) gives the connected modular increment. The reference load, the additional write and the readout are parts of the measurement. The original two source memories are retained.

Park the two ordinary outputs on blank apparatus rails. A new single attempts one full native transfer on the written contact. Launch it from a fair side. On every magnitude-one head the chance of passage is \(1/4\); every written-zero head blocks it. After a failure, the sharp head preserves its magnitude. Two attempts with independently chosen sides thus give probability \(7/16\) on magnitude one. Both gains can be calibrated by writing one ordinary single against a vacancy, then comparing with an equal-load write. The double probe stays on its side for both standards. These statements follow from the full native pulse columns before averaging over source trials.

A separate clock walks on a ring of \(2L\) material ports. Each pair of fair nearest-neighbor hops supplies one appointment. Score a successful single transit if it occurs by the clock's first return; retain returns without transit. Compare the two material records by appointment number. When each appointment samples the source independently, its hazard (conditional chance of crossing) is \(h=\alpha f\), where \(\alpha\) is the calibrated single-contact gain. The terminal probability is exactly
\[
R_L(h)=\sqrt h\,\tanh\!\big(L\operatorname{atanh}\sqrt h\big).
\]
The walk and rendezvous are supplied instruments. Neither observer has to compute a square root to produce the transit record.

## Why the square root appears

In two-step time the walk on the \(L\) even stations stays put with probability one half and steps each way with probability one quarter. Write \(U_j\) for the probability of surviving to a return when the next crossing test is at station \(j\). Let \(q=1-h\). At arrival \(U_0=q\); elsewhere \(4U_j=q(U_{j-1}+2U_j+U_{j+1})\). Starting just before the first appointment gives \(R_L=1-(U_{L-1}+2q+U_1)/4\). Solving the two-root recurrence, whose characteristic roots are \((1\pm\sqrt h)/(1\mp\sqrt h)\), gives the displayed response. Equivalently \(R_0=0\) and \(R_{L+1}=(R_L+h)/(1+R_L)\). At finite \(L\) this is a rational function. Its leading term at \(h=0\) is \(Lh\). At fixed positive \(h<1\) it approaches \(\sqrt h\). Each step before stopping has crossing probability \(h\); summing over attempted steps gives mean appointments \(R_L/h\). The code independently solves the finite first-step system over the rationals. Give \(K\) of the \(L\) source contacts one unit of ordinary material each. Native writes record their independently prepared signs. The signed sum has variance \(K\). Uniformly addressing the resident contacts gives conditional hazard \(K/(4L)\) on every visit, including after past failures. Thus an ordinary source standard relates the large-ring probability to the RMS divided by \(2\sqrt L\). The signs must be checked. Four jointly aligned donors have second moment 16 and two opposite pairs have zero; both give the same receiver probability as four independent donors, whose second moment is four. The receiver is sensitive to the number of active contrasts. Identifying that reading with the variance of a sum requires the measured covariances.

Access can be strictly local. Give each even vertex a distinct physical siding, keep its mark there and take the launch uniformly. A walker querying only its own arrival point must reach a live siding before it returns. In a gap of \(g\) stations bounded by marks, an interior launch at distance \(j\) from the first mark has probability at most \((1/j+1/(g-j))/4\) of reaching either one: the first lazy step must leave on that side, then gambler's ruin gives the inverse distance. Counting marked launches generously as certain successes bounds the average by \((K+\frac12\sum H_{g-1})/L\), where \(H_j\) is the harmonic sum. Concavity maximizes this bound at equally spaced gaps to within one station. It grows at most as \(p[1+O(\log(1/p))]\) for sparse fraction \(p=K/L\). An indefinitely sparse square-root response cannot come from this particular local returning survey.

A different local instrument sends a courier forward one station per appointment on a synchronized survey carrier. Compare its first material transit with the independent walker's first-return time. If the largest gap to a marked siding is \(g\), the probability is at least \(\frac14 {2g-2\choose g-1}/4^{g-1}\): the courier reaches a mark by \(g\), while the last factor is the walker's survival through \(g-1\) appointments. No trajectory that short can wind around the entire ring. This bound scales as the square root of the marked fraction for uniformly bounded relative gaps. For any layout and uniform launch the chance at appointment \(j\) is at most \(K/(4L)\) before conditioning on earlier failures; a union bound up to return time gives \(\mathbb E[\min(1,KT/(4L))]\), with \(T\) measured in appointments. The inverse-square-root survival tail bounds this by a constant times \(\sqrt{K/L}\). On the 496-port ring with one active source, the forward courier gives 3.309%, the uniformly addressed probe 3.175%, and the walker inspecting its current siding 1.157%. Even the all-placement upper bound for the last survey is only 1.631%. The courier's forward motion, synchronization and selected ring are apparatus conditions. ## Do all observers infer the same extra mass?

The middle read also identifies its compensating partner. Whenever \(n_{20}-1\) is nonzero, \(n_8-1\) has the opposite sign on every accepted word. The four centered home loads sum to zero. Their measured covariance matrices \(C\) have zero row sums and nonpositive off-diagonal entries in both tested preparations. Consequently the variance of the signed load in *any* region \(B\) is exactly
\[
 V(B)=-\sum_{i\in B,j\notin B} C_{ij}.
\]
Only correlations crossing the aperture contribute. In particular \(-C_{8,20}=f\) supplies the entire measured middle variance. Internal correlation can survive even when the signed total fluctuation vanishes.

Use the public echo distances on the common bypass chart. Observer 178 sees all four homes at 11 hops. Every nested aperture consequently contains either none or all of their signed load. Each has exactly zero fluctuation. Observer 20 sees the four homes at 0, 11, 14 and 17 hops and resolves a nonzero fluctuation between successive arrivals. At diameter 20 both observers have surveyed the identical whole board. Assigning a common positive mass per unit of accumulated RMS would give a repair increment of zero to observer 178 and \(+0.73669\) in load-RMS times hop units to observer 20. Independent records give 0.6963 to 0.7792 for the latter. Across all 240 centers, simultaneous intervals resolve a positive increment at 91 and a negative increment at 142. Thus this sum cannot assign a shared total mass to the whole board. A rule applying only about a designated barycentric center has a different domain. Once a cut encloses every correlated endpoint, generating further mass increments by this rule requires additional correlations extending across later cuts. ## What happens on measured galaxies?

Grant the direct receiver one positive gravitating contribution per cut, constant cut density, pooled imaged patches, spherical extra orbital force, and one common gain. Count eligible source patches from image pixels before using any orbital speed. The original and repaired reads each admit at most 157 of 369 measured speeds within two quoted errors, even fitting the gain on the comparison itself. The same ceiling holds for their difference. Fitting the gain on the other nine galaxies gives at best 109 of 369 held speeds for that difference across the tested settings, and 91 if restricted to the three finite receiver sizes.
