# What the local clock correlation forces at exterior stations

Two observers in the occupied 240-port model can measure the correlation of two ordinary clock frequencies and use it to predict another observer's free-material records. The same source measurement implies a positive dispersive response at **every** connected material station when the reference oscillator is tuned below the material band. In an explicitly coupled detector this lowers a local level and gives positive work from one to two hops at finite exchange. The ordinary source and the astronomical profile provide separate tests of the gravitational interpretation. ## A sign theorem for a below-band ordinary clock

For a reference frequency \(\omega\) below the inactive spectrum put \(R_{ab}=(H_{ab}-\omega I)^{-1}\). At station \(x\) the diagonal \(R_{ab}(x,x)\) is a measurable frequency response. For example, launch the defect at \(x\), wait an exponentially distributed time with rate \(\lambda\), and compare its return with the reference phase. The signed quadrature divided by \(\lambda\) averages \(d/(d^2+\lambda^2)\) over the local spectral lines \(d=E-\omega>0\). It tends to the desired diagonal. Five finite linewidths give a bound on that limit. For interpolation in \(\lambda^2\), the spectral remainder is \(d^{-1}\prod_j\lambda_j^2/(d^2+\lambda_j^2)\). Its sign and size follow directly by partial fractions.

On a connected finite chart with real nonpositive hopping, every entry of \(R_{ab}\) is strictly positive. To see this, choose \(m\) above the largest eigenvalue of \(H_{ab}-\omega I\). Then \(T=I-(H_{ab}-\omega I)/m\) has nonnegative entries, spectral radius below one and a positive walk between any two sites. The inverse is the sum of these powers divided by \(m\). For \(A=aP_A,B=bP_B\) the ordinary resolvent identity gives
\[
 R_{00}-R_{a0}-R_{0b}+R_{ab}
 = R_{00}B R_{0b}A R_{a0} + R_{0b}A R_{a0}B R_{ab}.
\]
Both terms have positive entries for \(a,b>0\) and nonempty supports. The change from re-pairing is \(D/16\) times the sum of this expression over \(a,b\in\{32,33\}\). Thus a **local measurement establishing \(D>0\)** entails a positive change in the exterior clock response on this construction. The argument works at finite size and throughout the below-band interval. It needs neither a remote detection at each station nor a fit to a remote clock. ## Work at a finite detector coupling

A specified additional rail with level \(\omega\) and exchange \(g\) to \(x\) has a lowest level \(\omega-s_x\), where
\[
 s_x=g^2\langle x|(H_{ab}-\omega I+s_x I)^{-1}|x\rangle .
\]
With a gap of at least \(d\), \(0\le g^2R_{ab}(x,x)-s_x\le g^4/d^3\). In frequency-sector space the signed re-pairing change has total variation \(D/2\). Consequently the change in the rail work from one shell to another has error at most \(Dg^4/d^3\) around \(g^2\) times the difference of their inferred clock responses. At the common exchange \(g=1/16384\) the re-pairing lowers the mean rail level more at one hop than at two for both species and all three primary tunings. At detuning \(1/16\) the additional work to move a vacancy detector from the nearer to the farther shell is \(8.57\)–\(9.41\times10^{-14}\) native energy units. Independent roots of the finite rail equation confirm the bound. The weak exchange was chosen from the measured clock bound to certify the sign in all six settings. This is a quasistatic energy difference of a coupled detector. The free trajectory of an ordinary body requires its own response law. The vacancy's mean shift peaks outside the source set for these tunings, so positivity alone does not guarantee attraction on every radial step.
