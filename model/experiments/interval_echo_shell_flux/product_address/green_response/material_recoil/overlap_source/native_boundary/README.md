## Energy and modular charge on the same contact

The treatment is a physical permutation between two banks. An independent pair of sharp native writes produces two memory labels and their respective outgoing load pairs. Crossing only the memories keeps the original total load on each shot and preserves both marginals exactly. Each contact in the second ensemble has the product of its own marginals. Repeating the swap restores the original joint state and returns the work. A change of observer access without changing the state has zero work and zero change of energy. At positive stiffness the paired preparation has a binding-energy deficit relative to the independent preparation. The gain reported below uses the paired preparation as its reference.

## Strength and shape of a material boundary

Put the same binary load on all twelve ports of each occupied owner. The three inter-owner links per owner form a matching of individual ports. Apply a sharp write on every external link. Only unequal links carry the native energy gain. Local work to write and hold the other edges is included in the whole-board comparison. For $N>0$ occupied owners and $b$ boundary links, the initial energy is $72N+17b/2$. The increment upon losing pairing is $17b/8$. The three available links per owner give $b\leq3N$, so the largest ratio is $17/260$. One isolated owner attains it. The 64-owner descendant face has 24 boundary links. Its initial, paired and decorrelated energies are 4812, 5730 and 5781. The gain from decorrelation is **51**, or **1.06%** of the initial energy. A common integer threshold makes the sum over all layer boundaries equal to the sum of the absolute height differences on the edges. Independently sampling the two endpoints preserves each local mean while changing that prediction. These are physical choices about the preparation. Finite sharp servicing can protect a compact boundary; including ordinary load exchange can erase the memory of its placement. ## What reaches a remote receiver with finite stock?

Two independent guide records leave each actual boundary edge. Each stops once or is lost at a drain. Conditional on a single stop the courier stays there. At address $x$ the connected part of the joint state survives with probability $p_s(x)^2$ for a write at $s$. Replacing a missing component with an independent marginal gives zero connected contribution. Coupling both ensembles to the same ordinary energy ticket gives the deposited source $(17/8)\sum_s p_s(x)^2$. The sum over all addresses per write cannot exceed $17/8$. A first-passage experiment that restarts a fresh source for each target uses a different inventory. On the ten-site-per-axis chart, the sum of squared first-passage probabilities is 2.988 per source. The one-stop joint probability is 0.001721 at stop probability $1/64$. [All origins, stopping settings and shells](transport.json)

For the six nearest-neighbour directions with probability $q=1/8$ each and two idle tags, define $\ell^2=(1-\alpha)q/\alpha$ with stop probability $\alpha$. Killing at the boundary can only reduce each destination probability. On the whole lattice Parseval gives the squared probability sum as the integral of
$$
[1+2\ell^2\textstyle\sum_j(1-\cos k_j)]^{-2}\,d^3k/(2\pi)^3 .
$$
Using $1-\cos k \ge 2k^2/\pi^2$ for $|k|\le\pi$ and extending the positive integral to all space bounds this sum by $\pi^2/(64\ell^3)$. The same bound applies to every finite chart. At side 82 and triangle side eight, increasing the lifetime through $\alpha=1/64,1/512,1/4096$ reduces the measured coincidence fraction from 0.001910 to 0.00008043 and 0.000003095. With the same two sources and the same middle lifetime, moving the origins sixteen sites apart changes the central eight-site cube's share from 70.76% to 2.83%. The assigned chart and length of the guide are part of the apparatus.

The raw signed probe counts were +181.5 and +332.5 against a prediction of −33.85 each. This pilot did not resolve inward recoil. These counts allow perfect delivery and disregard the reservoir work.
