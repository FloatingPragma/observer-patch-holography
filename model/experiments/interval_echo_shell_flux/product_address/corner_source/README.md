# Can the three guide records supply a proper ball and its source?

On the three guides one can change the ordinary seam energy or change a correlation among the three launch addresses while keeping every surveyed distance, volume and aperture fixed. The correlation changes the entropy of the released guide registers and is independently visible at the collector. The same experiment admits an ordinary-energy calibration on a single native seam. Its measured area response is zero on both changes. Four direct clock comparisons identify why the parallel-arrival balls cannot approach smooth spatial geodesic balls with vanishing relative error. ## Two controls on the same preparation

The original flag fires and all four doubles remain present. On each axis choose one of the two original central sites, writing its sign as −1 for index 5 and +1 for index 6. Prepare the classical lottery on the eight possible released corners with weights

$$
 p(x,y,z)=\frac{1+sxyz+bx}{8},\qquad |s|+|b|<1 .
$$

The diagnostic source changes the initial corner lottery; the subsequent signal and transport contacts are those of the original apparatus. All recorded arms use fair bits. An equally weighted bank of 32 settings realizes each ordinary calibration. After preparation trace away the source tags and retain the occupied guide addresses. The flag is in the same fired state at every corner. The address alternatives are orthogonal, so the entropy of the released material registers is the Shannon entropy of these eight probabilities. Every positive-radius ball in the original survey contains them all.

At $b=0$, summing over any missing sign gives the uniform two-guide distribution, regardless of $s$. Thus every measurement confined to any two owners at release has the same distribution along this correlation variation. This includes every single native Hamiltonian seam. The relevant two-site input columns of the 99-state generator have energy zero for equal sharp occupations. For unlike sharp loads 0 and 2 the symmetric and antisymmetric states have energies 32 and 33 with equal spectral weight. The measured address asymmetry fixes a native mean energy of 65/2 per unlike edge. The entire 630-seam census on fresh copies has 22 such edges when the first guide is at site 5 and 21 at site 6. A local calibration uses the first guide's link from site 5 to 4. Site 4 is initially empty. A half pulse on the link transfers the double with probability 1/2 given site 5 and with probability zero given site 6. The reader measures the new occupation of site 4. The calibrated mean seam energy is 65 times the arrival probability. Cutting exchange gives zero arrivals. At $s=-1/2$, changing $b$ from −1/4 to +1/4 gives 20,602 and 12,218 arrivals from 65,536 independent launches per arm. The theoretical probabilities are 5/16 and 3/16. ## What reaches a distant collector?

Keep the original six signed coordinate offers and the two idle tags at each active address. On any of the available drains let the terminal address be $j$, let $u_i=2j_i-11$ and define the signed score $M(j)=u_1u_2u_3$. In each coordinate the two opposite moves have scores summing to twice the starting score. With the idle tags this makes $M$ harmonic for the actual walk. Absorption occurs almost surely: from any of the finitely many interior vertices there is a finite allowed word of positive probability reaching the drain, giving a common positive escape probability in a bounded number of steps. The stopped score is bounded. Its terminal mean therefore equals its source mean, which is $s$, on every drain. This proof uses the actual terminals, including all signs and paths. Taking each allowed transfer checks the harmonic identity at all 1,000 interior positions of the largest drain.

The reflections of the guides exchange the eight release corners and preserve every walk appointment's transition probabilities, every concentric shell and the drain. The entire exit-time law is independent of the launch mixture. More strongly, its signed change in $s$ is odd under *each* coordinate reflection. Every collector statistic invariant under at least one such reflection has zero response, including all time-resolved polynomial moments of total degree at most two in its address. The single changed-axis direction at final exit has six possible values. The probability of every fixed exit direction is invariant under either of the other two reflections. Its whole distribution therefore stays fixed along the $s$ direction. An energy-flux or null projection using only these six directions with common fixed weights misses the correlation. Each full launch crosses every enclosing cut once net. The total shell current per release and the common renewal factor also agree in the arms if the reinjection overhead is the same.

On the nearest exterior, the score is always ±3. At any interior corner the next appointment exits with probability 3/8 and otherwise has conditional mean parity multiplier −1/5, because two idle offers preserve the sign and three internal hops reverse it. At exit time $t$, the conditional mean is $3s(-1/5)^{t-1}$. Unconditionally the plus probability is $(1+s/3)/2$. Independent 65,536-launch banks gave 27,146, 32,550 and 38,160 positive terminal readings at $s=-1/2,0,+1/2$. All launches reached a collector. Their observed means were −0.51471, −0.01996 and +0.49365. The deposited counts retain every final vertex at every exit time. This is a rank-three angular correlation. ### Reading the overlap charge on the same preparation

At the full-support reference $s_0=-1/2,b=0$, the positive-parity corners have probabilities $1/16$ and the negative-parity corners $3/16$. On the eight occupied address states the reference modular Hamiltonian is $K_0=-\log\rho_0=C\mathbf 1+\lambda XYZ$, where $\lambda=\log(3)/2$ and $C$ is a common scalar. Harmonic stopping therefore gives a terminal estimator for its **mean** on a fresh preparation, $C+\lambda\mathbb E[M_{\rm exit}]$. This determines the signed modular response without reading the new preparation's entropy. In particular $d\langle K_0\rangle=\lambda\,ds=dH$ at the reference, including variations in $b$. The specified aperture is stationary through those same runs even though the entropy changes.

There is also an exact conditional mutual information for the three physical guide registers considered as the consecutive algebras $X,Y,Z$. At $b=0$ it is $I(X:Z\mid Y)=H(XY)+H(YZ)-H(Y)-H(XYZ)=3\log 2-H(s,0)$. The corresponding fixed-reference combination of modular Hamiltonians $K_{XY}+K_{YZ}-K_Y-K_{XYZ}=\log(8)\mathbf1-K_0$ has the opposite signed response and is readable from the same terminal scores. This is a three-owner overlap statistic at release. Its relation to an extended spatial collar in a vacuum remains unspecified.

A fresh bank of 65,536 simulated native releases gave a parity coefficient of 0.54537 nats from the measured reference address frequencies. Two independent terminal banks at $s=-1/2$ and $+1/2$ gave a fixed-reference modular difference of **0.54993 nats**, against the exact **0.54931**. The entropy difference between these two endpoints is zero by sign symmetry. Its finite departure from the modular difference is the relative entropy $D(\rho_{+1/2}\Vert\rho_{-1/2})=\log(3)/2$, consistent with the first law being a derivative at the reference. ## A clock that can see the correlation

There is a source-dependent geometric observable in the original timing panel. Select a target, forget which corner fired, and average the actual arrival times using the release frequencies. Write $a_i$ for the target's distance on axis $i$ to its nearer central site, $A=\max_i a_i$, and $I$ for the axes attaining $A$. Let $\tau_i$ be the sign of the nearer central site on axis $i$. For every launch, including dispatch, arrival takes $1+A$ appointments when all coordinates in $I$ start at their near site, and $2+A$ otherwise. The mean is $2+A-\Pr(\text{all near on }I)$.

All one- and two-guide marginals are independent of $s$. The mean arrival therefore changes along $s$ only where all three displacements tie. On those eight body diagonals its slope is $-\tau_1\tau_2\tau_3/8$ appointments per unit $s$. The sign changes at the opposite target. On the finite board exactly 48 of the 1,728 targets respond, with zero mean around each complete shell. Independently weighting the eight complete archived clock tapes verifies the predicted $s$ and $b$ derivatives at all targets. This establishes an angular travel-time signature of the same correlation that the distant collector reads. The emitter here is a lottery over eight starting positions. Interpreting its mean time as the proper distance from one physical event requires an independently identified event and return clock. [Source-weighted survey](records.json)

Could the eight diagonal shifts be eight contractions of one local rank-two tensor? At an assumed orthonormal frame take the eight nominal null vectors $k=(1,\tau/\sqrt3)$. For any symmetric $Q$, the values of $Q(k,k)$ on these directions are linear combinations of $1,\tau_x,\tau_y,\tau_z,\tau_x\tau_y,\tau_x\tau_z,\tau_y\tau_z$. Their signed average against $\tau_x\tau_y\tau_z$ vanishes. The measured correlation slopes on each shell have signed average $-1/8$. The maximum error in appointments per unit $s$ is therefore at least $1/8$ for every choice of $Q$, since the absolute signed average bounds the maximum from below. The zero tensor attains that error. The independently calibrated $b$ slopes on those directions equal $-\tau_x/8$, which a mixed time-space component represents exactly. This excludes identifying these correlated first-arrival shifts directly with one local quadratic form on the specified rays. The shifts are absolute appointments. Their size relative to the travel time tends to zero on longer guides. Paths from several emitters through different regions of a varying metric can carry higher angular moments. The eight-direction test alone cannot determine the metric or a curvature coefficient after a continuum rescaling.

## Which distance does the parallel clock measure?

After release, start at any active address $a$ and route to another address $b$ by the original parallel guide services. A single appointment advances at most one site per guide. Addressing the three disjoint native paths simultaneously and parking completed axes attains this bound. The elapsed appointments are $d(a,b)=\max_i|a_i-b_i|$. For example use the four positions at $\pm k e_1$ and $\pm k e_2$ about $(5,5,5)$ in cyclic order. The code uses the actual sharp transfers to measure all six shortest legs at $k=1,2,3,4,5$, with the returns checked separately. The four sides take $k$ appointments and each diagonal takes $2k$. Suppressing native exchange prevents the same paths from reaching their targets.

Every Euclidean metric space obeys Ptolemy's inequality, $d_{13}d_{24}\le d_{12}d_{34}+d_{14}d_{23}$. This follows, for example, by inverting three points in the unit sphere about the fourth and applying the triangle inequality. The measured ratio of the left side to the right is exactly two. If Euclidean distances lie between $l$ and $u$ times the corresponding ranges, Ptolemy requires $4l^2\le2u^2$. Thus $u/l\ge\sqrt{2}$ under every common positive clock calibration. On any longer three-guide family with the same parallel protocol, small copies have the same obstruction. On a smooth Riemannian spatial slice, distances in a shrinking regular convex normal neighbourhood agree with Euclidean normal coordinates to relative error tending to zero. Such a limit cannot identify this clock with the proper distance of that slice on all these positions. The result concerns a metric read from three simultaneous marker registers. Other rulers could reconstruct a different effective space.
