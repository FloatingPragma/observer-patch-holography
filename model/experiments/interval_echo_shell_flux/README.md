# Does a central material source spread its current evenly over a shell?

A source of sharp doubles on Interval Echo sends a constant total current through six successive graph-distance shells. On the 320-cell federation an independent source meter read **0.00417390 doubles per appointment**. The six aperture readings ranged from **0.00416412 to 0.00418026**. Individual edges at the same radius carry different currents, and some even run inward. On a separately shuttered icosahedral cell the same source shares its current exactly equally between the edges of each measured shell. The distinction matters for a Gauss-law argument: conservation fixes the total; a local radial strength also requires equal sharing in the appropriate area measure. ## Why the vertex equation holds

Let $I$ contain source and interior ports, and give the collecting ports potential zero. On $I$ use the integer grounded Laplacian $L$, whose diagonal counts all incident seams, including seams to collectors. Every connected component in the enrolled interior reaches a collector. The quadratic form of $L$ sums squared potential differences on interior seams and squared potentials on seams to the collectors, so $L$ is positive definite. Solve $Lz=g\,e_0$ over the integers by clearing the denominator of the rational solution for a unit right-hand side. This calculation predicts the records independently. A positive maximum principle, applied to the connected interior with a positive source, gives positive $z$ there. Each supplied integer vector is checked against the neighbor list at every vertex. Define

\[
 Z=16\sum_{v\in I}z_v+g,\qquad
 \pi_v=16z_v/Z \quad(v\in I),\qquad
 \pi_b=Z^{-1}\sum_{v\sim b}z_v \quad(b\text{ a collector}).
\]

Summing $Lz$ gives $\sum_b\pi_b=g/Z$. These weights sum to one. Direct balance at an interior vertex follows by inserting the transfer probability 1/16, the unused calendar probability, and the emission on the appointment after collection. At a collector every incoming flight occupies it for exactly one appointment. This proves stationarity; finite connectedness, positive hopping and idle probability give uniqueness and convergence. An independently observed occupied flight $v\to w$ therefore has frequency $z_v/Z$. For an ordinary edge the net current is $(z_v-z_w)/Z$, and the same formula holds at a collector with $z_w=0$. Its divergence is exactly $g/Z$ at zero and zero at every other interior vertex. This proves the steady-sourcing premise of the finite layered graph up to depth six on all three federations. It also proves it on any connected finite source domain with an accessible collecting boundary and the same symmetric contact clock. An unconstrained emitter on a closed finite carrier would have to accumulate load. ## A source for which every aperture in a shell agrees
