# High spatial frequencies on the commissioned strand

This author experiment compares the positive lattice square root with a finite-range hopping forecast on the three exposed v3 validation geometries. The native strand, dimer clock, independent coupling measurements and shot budgets come from the [collective experiment](../../candidate_laws/interval_echo/openwave/row_30_collective/README.md). The earlier comparison of a parabola and the known v3 results motivated the choice of rival and wavelength range. The frozen confirmation and its public draw have their own terms.

With measured rest frequency m, squared speed c² and u = 4 sin²(k/2), the rival frequency is m + c²u/(2m) − c⁴u²/(8m³). A polynomial of degree two in the nearest-neighbour lattice Laplacian is a hopping operator with range two. Its frequency agrees with the lattice square root through order k⁴. There are no fitted coefficients. At the nominal meter values m = 15 and c² = 16 the forecasts differ by more than twice the existing 0.0048 frequency tolerance at k > 2.208. The previous acquisitions enrolled lower wave numbers.

Use every strand previously reserved for v3 validation, with its same mode count and standing-wave launch profiles reflected around π/2. For M terminal addresses this changes launch number i to M+1−i. Each temporal product and each addressed spatial sign is acquired through the original physical two-time transfer and binary-outcome budgets. Use the frozen temporal reader, even-hold spatial fit and odd-hold full-profile check. The correct foil uses the high launch labels. Require held pair sums to close within 0.0048, each measured spatial profile to pass and the high launch labels to fail. The native line contrast must exceed 0.4, the zero-coupling contrast at that line must stay below 0.02 and the same control must give contrast above 0.4 at frequency 2. Report all boards even if a condition fails. On boards with resolved signals, compare the two fixed forecasts to every enrolled frequency with the same absolute tolerance 0.0048. This tests differences at lattice wavelengths, outside the low-k continuum claim.
