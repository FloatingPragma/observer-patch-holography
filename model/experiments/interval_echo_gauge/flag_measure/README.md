# Measuring a face and its marked corner

A guarded twelve-port Interval Echo cell starts with a sharp double and two singles. Three final positions identify the occupied face if the three pairs share material seams. The double labels one corner. An instrument on the unrestricted load register gives **one face in eleven launches**. Conditional on a face, each of the twenty faces has weight 1/20 and each of its corners has weight 1/3. The sampler receives no list of faces, vertices per face or desired corner weights. On one 4,194,304-launch cohort, 380,626 starts had three pairwise-adjacent terminal positions. The 20 face counts ranged from 18,793 to 19,259; the 60 face/corner counts ranged from 6,211 to 6,481. Of the four sharp-start and forward/reverse-path cohorts, all passed simultaneous 99% checks of admission, every face probability and every within-face corner share. Three more cohorts used the actual port-to-gateway read routes of the 20-, 80- and 320-owner carriers. The other 600 configurations are reported separately.

## How the weights arise

Traverse each of the twelve sites once along an internal path. Insert site j by applying contacts successively to edges (i,i+1) for i from j−1 down to zero. The native blank-seam generator on the two orderings of any unequal pair is a constant times the identity plus (I−SWAP)/2. After an exposure t and a sharp read at the first endpoint the order interchanges with probability sin²(t/2), independently of the load difference. Equal inputs are unchanged. Choose t=2 arcsin sqrt((i+1)/(i+2)). The newly inserted label at position j then ends at each of 0 through j with probability 1/(j+1), by telescoping the successive interchange probabilities. Conditional on the inserted label's trajectory, the old labels undergo a permutation independent of their initially uniform order. Induction from one label gives uniform random order on all twelve labels. Forgetting labels of equal loads therefore gives exactly 1/660 to each possible marked triple, for any sharp starting configuration. The code checks the native nine blank columns, both starting words and both path orders. All intermediate outcomes are summed; the terminal distribution uses all starts.

The observer reads the first rail after every contact by using the registered binary (1,1) output pointer on a separate blank seam. Against a vacant reference the pointer identifies a double; after a dark outcome a second probe against a double identifies a vacancy. Two dark readings give one unit. Each echo is undone. The inspected data rail can be transposed to either gateway by internal π exchanges, read from its adjacent prepared reference, then returned. The two neighbour owners supply the reference rails. The implementation verifies the local projector on all six input/reference combinations, every route's endpoint permutation, and all 660 final words on each physical routing. The measurement uses a scoped terminal record. Publishing the complete route and sharp-read transcript defines a different observation.

Uniform weighting also arises at equilibrium without the insertion probabilities. In repeated sweeps of the thirty edges with half-exchanges the uniform law is stationary, since every elementary map is doubly stochastic. A complete sweep has both a positive identity term and every single-edge transposition term. These transpositions connect all 660 states. The finite chain therefore has a unique stationary law and converges to it. From the sharp nonface seed the total variation errors after 1, 4 and 16 sweeps were 0.37718, 0.01954 and 0.000000171. In the changed local generator the exchange coefficient is tripled on every seam. This retains the symmetric incidence, balanced pointer, private memory, load alphabet and perfect π routing. It changes the transition probabilities at the insertion appointments. From the first face seed the forward schedule gave one face on all 4,194,304 original starts, and every changed-law bank failed the uniform-flag tests. The stationary half-contact chain alone cannot distinguish these two exchange coefficients.

The finite source has 20×3 admitted flags, hence admission 60/660. Each port is on five faces. The joint conditional measure on (face,marked corner) is 1/60 and its port marginal is 5/60=1/12. The registered proper port rotations independently act transitively on all 60 flags after the explicit isomorphism between the material seam numbering and the theorem manifest. Thus every rotation-invariant probability on admitted flags has the same weights. The controlled launch and read above exhibit such a measure from a nonsymmetric input. This realizes both hypotheses of the finite port-dual measure theorem on this observable. [Finite incidence theorem](../../../../Lean/Screen/PrimitivePortDualMeasure.lean)

## A port-function metric and compact-family readout

For each admitted event evaluate two real port functions on its one recorded corner and multiply their values. The exact mean is the diagonal bilinear form ∑ f(i)g(i)/12. For the twelve address indicators the complete empirical Gram matrix has diagonal equal to the twelve accepted corner counts divided by 380,626 and all off-diagonal entries zero. No list of metric weights enters the detector.

For the classifier, transport the support of the measured triangles to the registered carrier. Supply the carrier's oriented face convention and use its alternating face bracket. On an oriented face (a,b,c) the coefficient of c in [a,b] is +1 with cyclic permutations and antisymmetry. These signs are apparatus conventions. The measured port metric assigns the squared residual of an upper tensor entry with output o and inputs i<j the factor w(o)/(w(i)w(j)), from the output metric and the two inverse input metrics. Weighted least squares on all 792 coordinates searches the full registered families F and G and the comparison plane P. The best fitted coefficients lie in the prescribed compact sign strata. The supplied finite classifier and nearest-distance loss define the decision. Current commutators of physical gauge fields require their own measurement.
