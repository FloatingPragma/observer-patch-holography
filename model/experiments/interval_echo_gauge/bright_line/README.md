# A two-mode doublet and the phase of the third mode

A source at a single port selects a line in the positive pair triplet. Its orthogonal plane carries a faithful two-dimensional representation of a subgroup of the registered current action. A sequence of two antipodal port pulses reverses this plane to within 0.033 in operator norm, while leaving the entire one-load sector fixed. The corresponding pair seam distributions at a cross-axis source are 0.914918 apart in total variation. The weak interaction and a spatial spin are separate identifications. The construction here uses the supplied cell current service and its calibrated pulse durations. [Registered joint group](../joint_currents/README.md) · [Design](PROTOCOL.md)

## What subgroup acts on the plane?

Hold both rotational triplets fixed inside the joint current group. Write U for the three-by-three unitary on the first one-load triplet. In the first pair triplet require the source line to stay invariant. The two other modes transform by a matrix B in U(2), and the line acquires a phase w. The parent group's determinant relation reads

$$\det B\,w=(\det U)^2.$$

Conversely every triple obeying this equation is realized in the ideal connected group. The rank-one source projector can be found by projecting the ordinary odd pair star at port 1 into its first enrolled triplet. This source has nonzero overlap there. This defines the bright line within the enrolled material triplet. Other source ports populate its orthogonal plane as well; the terminal experiment keeps the entire unfiltered star. Preserving this line and parking the two rotational triplets restrict the control menu. Its image has dimension 13. Additional phase choices give different twelve-dimensional subgroups.

* Keep the registered uniform control circle and both independent traceless groups. They act as \((zA,z^2 V,z^2)\) for \(A\in SU(3), V\in SU(2), |z|=1\). The determinant relation holds. If this action is the identity, the line gives \(z^2=1\) and the colour determinant gives \(z^3=1\). Thus \(z=1, A=I, V=I\). This subgroup is the direct product with trivial kernel.
* Fix the bright vector, so \(w=1\). The resulting subgroup is isomorphic to \(U(3)\times SU(2)\), since \(V=(\det U)^{-1} B\) has determinant one. Its primitive \(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\) presentation has kernel of order three.
* Tie the line phase to the colour determinant, \(w=\det U\). Then \(\det B=\det U\). Complex conjugating B identifies this group with \(S(U(3)\times U(2))\). Its action on colour, plane and line in covering coordinates is \((z^{-2}A,z^{-3}V,z^{-6})\). The kernel is generated by \((e^{2\pi i/3}I_3,-I_2,e^{i\pi/3})\). Surjectivity follows by choosing any sixth root of \((\det U)^{-1}\); each of the six choices yields one lift. Selecting this relation requires a physical control on the relative phase. ## Do the measured phases lock?

Use D, the sum of currents at port 1 and its antipode, and S, the sum at every port. S acts as N times the identity on the first triplet with N loads. D acts on the one-load triplet with eigenvalues 1/2 on the source line and zero on the two other modes. Indeed a site has first-triplet squared projection 3/12 and the opposite site has the negative vector by oddness. Antipodal inversion reverses the orientation of the two edge circulations, so their compressions sum to zero on these odd bands. In each enrolled triplet a proper fivefold turn about the chosen axis has three distinct complex characters. Each of the two addressed generators commutes with that turn and preserves the triplets individually. They therefore commute with each other, and the two successive physical pulses implement D exactly. The real occupation sum is scalar on the real two-plane and preserves its axis. D annihilates the second triplet and spectator modes on both carriers.

For the pair, write β for the eigenvalue of D on its source line. The trace of D on the first triplet is one by rotational transitivity, so the plane eigenvalue is (1−β)/2. The [rational certificate](certificate.py) gives **0.719927 < β < 0.719929**. It verifies directly that the enrolled quartic F of the integer pair Hamiltonian K annihilates the raw odd star. For its lowest isolated root t, dividing F(x) by x−t and applying the quotient to the star gives a vector proportional to its spectral projection. Rational interval arithmetic encloses the ratio of its antipodal occupation to its squared norm. A D pulse at exposure π therefore preserves the line but gives different colour determinant and bright phases.

An observer can distinguish the phases using only terminal outcomes. At port 1 the prepared state has a single bright component in its first triplet. All other components keep their phase under D or S. A sharp return to either original port (one load) or to an edge in either original star (pair) has probability affine in the cosine of this phase because all source vectors and terminal projectors are real. Independent sham and π-phase arms calibrate the two endpoints of this affine relation. The one-load bright phase of D equals its determinant phase. The phases can thus be compared using separate preparations at fixed load.

Each setting enrolled 131,072 launches. The intervals have simultaneous 99% sampling coverage by a union bound over the six return rates. They also include the finite-order error in the phase anchors. S is implemented by 2,048 symmetric address sweeps with the product bound from the parent study. Keeping all raw outcomes, including pair quiets, gives disjoint intervals. In the native D arm the colour determinant is exactly i, so the real parts of all its integer powers belong to {−1,0,1}. The pair interval excludes all three values.

The controller can instead execute D+cS for the same duration. Choosing c=β−1/2 equalizes the two phase rates since their values are 1/2+3c and β+2c. Choosing c=−β/2 fixes the bright vector. These settings use the independently calculated pair eigenvalue. They demonstrate accessible calibrated programs. The underlying current service also executes D alone and preserves the line while violating the determinant condition. A source law choosing one of the restricted menus, a physical loop identification, and a coupling to matter need further evidence.

## Resolving the doublet centre

Apply both addressed pulses for 4πn with n=241. This is the first enrolled n at which 2n times the plane eigenvalue lies within 0.003 turns of a half integer. The colour triplet completes an integer number of turns. The plane phase lies near −1 and the bright phase is the inverse square of the plane phase. Their deviations give an operator distance below 0.033 from the ideal plane sign flip. On a fresh ordinary pair star at port 0 the predicted distance from sham is 0.914918 in total variation, and the sampled distance is 0.914978. For one load the predicted distance is zero and the sampled distance is 0.001411. The ordinary full-address return distributions change when the plane sign is visible relative to the unchanged modes. At zero occupation gain, the opposite circulations cancel exactly on both carriers. This is an identification of a controllable plane sign in material triplets. The long pulse duration and declared axis do not establish a source-selected weak gauge connection or the physical gauge group.
