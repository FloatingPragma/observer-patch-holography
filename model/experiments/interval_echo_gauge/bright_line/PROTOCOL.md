# Bright line and a possible weak doublet

Use the registered isolated-cell current service at gain one on the full one-load and pair preparations. Use source port 1 and its antipode for the summed control D, and all twelve addresses for S. The known 23-dimensional image and projection of a port source into each enrolled triplet motivate this test. A preliminary native matrix inspection found D is axial, with colour eigenvalues 0,0,1/2 for one load and a double eigenvalue near 0.140 for the pair. The pair source at 1 projects onto its remaining bright line. These numbers are exposed design information.

Test whether preserving the bright line also enforces equality between its phase and the determinant phase of the first one-load triplet. Use an exposure of π for D and a sham. Compare a native control D+cS with c chosen to satisfy that equality, and one chosen to fix the bright phase. Coefficients are controller settings computed from the exposed native bands. Measure ordinary one-load port and two-load seam words, all 30 seams and quiet, at zero free time. Enroll the odd source at port 1 and at the smallest distinct nonantipodal address. Also run symmetric addressed pulse words for a pair weak central half-return: choose the smallest positive integer n≤512 for which 2n times the pair plane eigenvalue is within 0.003 turns of a half integer. Use 4πn D. Compare the complete native, sham and occupation-gain-zero histograms. For the half-return compare with the ideal operation reversing the dark plane and leaving both bright axes fixed; include the spectator and the full original launch in every probability. Resolve changes on fresh synthetic launches with fixed seed and 131072 per arm. The dark-plane flip could fail to give distinct sharp seam counts or move the colour sector appreciably. The supplied source axis, calibration and requirement on the bright phase are under test as assumptions, with no natural selection of these controls presumed.

For the π pulse D the preparation at port 1 has only its bright component in the first triplet. Enroll two phase anchors in each sector, sham and S applied for π/N where N is the number of loads. Record returns to either antipodal launch port (one load) or any seam in either original five-seam star (pair). Both anchors use the same ordinary detector. Fit the cosine of the D phase by linear interpolation of these return rates; real preparation amplitudes and real terminal occupation projectors give an affine cosine independently of which sector is tested. The one-load bright phase of D is also its determinant phase since D vanishes on its plane. Disjoint confidence intervals reject the bright/determinant lock under D alone.
