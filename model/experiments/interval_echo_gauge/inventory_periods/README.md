# Which joint phases do native contacts permit?

On a connected protected register of M≥2 blank ternary ports, arbitrary timing of native contacts and one reachable private reference gives an exact control group. It contains the independent special-unitary group on each inventory of zeros, singles and doubles. Its remaining observable determinant phases are generated by **two** continuous parameters. A detector restricted to total load two can report a finite cyclic ambiguity even when a different material inventory distinguishes the very same pulses. The result concerns this specified control menu. Identifying a Standard Model gauge transformation requires a source-selected action on the physical charge carriers. ## Determinant lattice and its kernel

Write n₀,n₁,n₂ for an inventory, D for its multinomial dimension and τ for eight times the accumulated private reference exposure. Let σ count the total exposure of all addressed seams, in native units. The determinant in this block is exp[−i(Aτ+Bσ)], where

\[
 A=D(n_1+4n_2)/M, \qquad
 B=D(17n_0n_1+65n_0n_2+17n_1n_2)/[M(M-1)].
\]

The first trace counts the load squared at one site. For one bond, a matched pair costs zero and each mixed ordering contributes a diagonal of one half plus eight times the squared difference. Summing over the two orders gives 17, 65 and 17. The native embedded matrices have integer traces. Multiplicativity of the determinant extends the formula to any timed word, including noncommuting pulses. Conversely one private and one seam pulse give every point on this two-parameter phase torus. Dividing an arbitrary block unitary having these determinants by the unitary of those pulses leaves independent special unitaries in every block, already generated by the native control theorem. This identifies the entire group on this material domain, including relative scalar phases.

In the normalized times τ/(2π), σ/(2π), all determinants are one exactly on the integer lattice. The all-single word gives A=1,B=0. The single and double on otherwise vacant registers give B=17 and 65, which are coprime. At fixed total load two there are just two inventory rows, (4,65) and (M−1,17(M−2)). The first row is primitive. Their determinant is 3M−71, always nonzero at integer M. The resulting invisible subgroup of the time torus is cyclic of order |3M−71| by integer row and column reduction. This kernel is for the determinant detector. A nontrivial special-unitary motion can be seen by a different detector on the same inventory.

## A third material preparation resolves the period

Four connected private ports give a two-load period of order 59. Turn on the private reference and one incident bond for normalized times 6/59 and 55/59, respectively. The two-load rows predict unit determinant. The held single-load row (1,17) predicts the phase exp(+6πi/59). The two-arm meter prepares an input word on one arm and an independently set output word on the other. The first arm receives the pulse. Two final interferometer settings, sorted by the output load word, measure the real and imaginary parts of every matrix entry. All other words remain counted. Routing, controlled exposures, phase standard and Born readout are supplied apparatus. This is a synthetic observer experiment. [Protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Producer](measure.py)

The measured determinants were **0.999956−0.000188i** for one double and **1.000167+0.000044i** for two singles. Their simultaneous 99% disk radii were 0.00674 and 0.01521. For one single the same word gave **0.949443+0.314008i**, excluding unity. Reversing the pulse order preserved the three determinant phases. The exchange-only controls failed all three native forecasts. A commutator gave unit determinants on every enrolled inventory and a one-load off-diagonal transition probability of 0.50007. This last reading separates the determinant kernel from the kernel of the full action. The joint confidence bound uses 680 quadrature banks of 2²⁸ starts, a Hoeffding bound on each signed mean and the column expansion of the determinant about a unitary matrix.
