# Recovering expansion from local counts and an independent clock

Use the published four-layer carrier logs at Fibonacci sizes 5 and 8. Choose the first receiving site with the largest first-layer inbox, then use its same incoming site addresses at every round. Count the actual writes and verify their registered versions. All readers use this same finite aperture across the four epochs. Site population, source neighbourhood and conformal cadence come from the layered construction. Its original alphabet carries one write per site and round.

Test a separately specified homogeneous experiment on this aperture. Before reading any result, fix conformal spacings 1/8 and 1/16, four epochs indexed from zero, and three smooth scale histories. They all start at scale one and expansion rate one. Use dust, coasting and de Sitter. The comoving cube has unit volume, each site occupies 1/q³, and each conformal epoch has width 1/d. Pool q³d independent exposures of each site and epoch. This gives one reference four-volume unit per aperture site at every cadence and board size. A counter of constant physical density 262144 per proper four-volume unit then produces a Poisson count with flat-reference mean 262144. A separately calibrated comoving clock reports integrated elapsed time with resolution one part in a billion of the time unit. Keep the physically sampled counts, the per-interval clock readings and the unweighted write totals. Only the simulator uses the profile to prepare the marker and clock records. The inverse reads aperture totals, intervals and calibrations. The light-cone order and ordinary writes carry no scale weighting.

Use the fourth root of mean marker count divided by the flat-reference mean for the scale. Infer proper-time slopes on adjacent intervals, their centered derivative, and the local deceleration parameter at both interior epochs. Classify a value below −0.25 as accelerating, one above +0.25 as decelerating, and values in between as consistent with coasting at this resolution. A correct reconstruction requires both interior estimates to be within 0.12 of the proper-time reference on all four aperture/cadence pairs. Cross-check every independent clock interval against the trapezoidal integral of the volume-reconstructed scale. Allow a deterministic error of 9 times the cube of the conformal step divided by 12. This bound uses the second-derivative ceiling 9 for the declared smooth menu. Add six single-epoch Poisson standard errors propagated by the fourth-root derivative, conservatively using the larger endpoint error times the conformal step, and one clock count of rounding. This statistical margin is an instrument threshold for this finite sample.

Couple its Poisson draws to the de Sitter run to exhibit the identical possible record. At least one independent clock interval in each panel must reject this particular alias. An exact static control has zero expansion rate, so its deceleration parameter is undefined.

A separate amplitude run uses fresh record streams at conformal spacing 1/16 on both apertures. Supply three pure de Sitter expansion rates of 1/2, 3/4 and 5/4 in the experiment's time unit, and a dust control with initial rate one. The producer has the prepared rate, and the inverse receives only marker and elapsed-clock counts. Estimate a constant rate from the log ratio of the first two scales divided by the first elapsed interval. Forecast both later scales using the independent elapsed time since the second epoch. Require 2% relative accuracy on the prepared rate and less than 0.2% error in both subsequent scale forecasts. Dust must miss at least one forecast per aperture. Under the additional reference hypothesis of pure spatially flat de Sitter gravity with light speed one, report three times the square of the inferred rate in inverse clock units squared. These magnitude thresholds were fixed after the unit-rate inverse study and before acquiring the independent amplitude streams.
