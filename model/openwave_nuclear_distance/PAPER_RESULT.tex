\paragraph{Nuclear parity and an actual spatial colour interaction.}
Exact native Casimir and cell-swap projectors resolve all $2520$
colour-singlet six-quark states, or $240$ states at fixed zero spin and
isospin projections. On the fixed four-input action, outward spectral
bounds put the even-parity $(S,I)=(1,0)$ and odd-parity $(0,0)$ ground lines
above the two-nucleon threshold by about $0.19121\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
A separately declared fifth-input control fits the deuteron binding
$2.22456640\,\mathrm{MeV}$ through the edge weak-current coefficient;
it also binds the extra odd channel by about $2.22452542\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
Native cell parity distinguishes these channels without identifying a
continuum orbital momentum.
For three quarks the explicit native colour isometry gives
$C_X^2=\frac23n_X(3-n_X)$. Thus the same positive edge-colour law on a
four-cycle costs $8\kappa/3$ for an adjacent quark--diquark pair and
$16\kappa/3$ for an opposite pair. Complete 120-, 364- and 816-state
singlet spectra and occupation records retain the resulting finite-range
spatial attraction and geometry-dependent energies. They supply no
continuum confinement or graph-independent nuclear calibration.
