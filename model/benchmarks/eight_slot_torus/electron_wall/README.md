# Electron spin on the eight-slot torus and the fifteen-port wall

On a torus with each public and private period at least three, the free constituent and the fully mature compact admit only a positive rotation-commutator sign when the rotation carries the resolved addresses and preserves one native update. A spin-sensitive hopping law on a private dimer chain gives a different result. Its light wall mode disperses, has a negative spinor commutator, moves a signed electric flux and splits in a magnetic field. The same mode's orbital motion fixes the magnetic units. For every finite chain its weak-field magnetic factor is **strictly below two**. The laboratory electron lies above two. This rules out the undressed wall mode as a complete electron model, including lengths outside the numerical scan. No empirical constant enters either walk, the preparation, the clocks or the decision rules. The direction of the laboratory discrepancy is a known comparison, not a blind prediction. [Laboratory measurement](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.071801)

## Which source the rotation test covers

The echosahedral observer exposes twelve mutually distinguishable port atoms, thirty internal seams and twenty oriented triangular faces. At one fixed public and private address, the free-walk socket space has dimension eight. Twelve nonzero orthogonal port projectors need total rank at least twelve. A one-address, one-register relabelling of these sockets cannot preserve all twelve sharp port readings. An encoding across registers, addresses or services would have to intertwine the repairs and reproduce the joint detector records on its stated preparations. The screen-microphysics paper supplies no such map. The rotation result concerns the eight-slot torus and the magnetic exclusion concerns the fifteen-port wall. **Resolved-address lemma.** Let all four coordinate periods be at least three. Resolve the joint public and private address by projectors $P_x$. A unitary which fixes these projectors and commutes with one update $U$ is scalar on each of the stated individual rails. To see this, the nonzero block $P_yUP_x$ has range the single socket pointing back from $y$ to $x$. Varying $x$ through the eight neighbours forces the unitary at $y$ to be diagonal in sockets. Every coin entry is nonzero. Its row for the hop from $x$ to $y$ therefore equates all eight diagonal entries at $x$ with one entry at $y$. Connectedness gives one common phase. On the direct sum of the two mature rails an off-diagonal intertwiner would, by irreducibility and polar decomposition, give a unitary equivalence. The same socket-range argument forces it to preserve the moduli of the coin entries. The two observed off-diagonal moduli differ (0.24879618 and 0.24877731). Hence the joint commutant consists of independent rail phases. [Native columns and geometry](native.py)

The canonical half-turns about perpendicular public axes commute. Any other unitary implementing either address permutation and intertwining the same update differs from its canonical lift by an element of this commutant. Canonical turns preserve the rails, so the two lifts also commute. Rephasing an isolated quarter-turn can change its fourth power. It cannot change the half-turn commutator. Relative to a coherent bypass the commutator has amplitude $+1$ on these preparations. Under private-blind readout a pair of opposite nonzero private harmonics gives two equivalent copies, intertwined by reflection of private position and the two private sockets. Their multiplicity can carry supplied spin matrices. A private translation resolves the copies and a spin-independent public probe gives them identical motion. The lemma makes no claim about a nonlocal deformation of a many-body defect, or apparatus that changes the process on the path. [Counterexample and controls](test_electron_wall.py)

## A local change, and its controls

This explicit control keeps the native private route and shows why reading a winding offset as a spinor rest mass fails. [Eight-socket control](port_control.py)

The experimental construction **spinor-private-wall-v1** retains the reflection followed by local inverse-paired routing. Its carrier, coin weights, private boundaries, spin transport, two link representations and apparatus are supplied. It has three periodic public axes, $L$ open private cells with orbitals A and B, two spin states and 15 ports. The spinor rotation acts by the spatial rotation on coordinates and ports and the ordinary Pauli lift on spin. Thus a spinor is an architectural choice. ## The wall pole and its full spectrum

With zero connection, $D=V^\dagger R V$ has the Fourier symbol

$$
12D(k)=\tau_z\sum_j\sigma_j\sin k_j+
\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\M^\dagger&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M=aI-2S,\quad a=1+\sum_j(1-\cos k_j),\quad S_{t+1,t}=1 .
$$

The wall block acts as identity on spin. In the translational private bulk its off-diagonal symbol winds once around zero at $a=1$, and zero times at $a>2$. The termination cuts a strong dimer at each end. On a **finite** chain the two boundary states hybridize. Write $s_i(a)$ for all singular values of $M$. Anticommutation gives all discriminant roots $\pm\sqrt{\sum_j\sin^2k_j+s_i(a)^2}/12$, each twice for spin. The one-step phases on the plane spanned by $Vv,RVv$ for each root $d$ are $\pm\arccos d$. All $|d|<1$ since $a\le7$, $\|M\|\le9$. The remaining $52L$ states per momentum split into $26L$ each at $+1$ and $-1$ in the step spectrum. Indeed $R$ has equal positive and negative ranks and each bright plane uses one of each. The orthogonal complement has zero weight for these preparations. Both carrier signs and all private bands enter the spectral account. At a private endpoint the measured return after $n$ steps is the diagonal entry of $T_n(D)$, where $T_n$ is the Chebyshev polynomial. At rest that endpoint excites every private singular line. [Full-step eigenphase and endpoint checks](test_electron_wall.py)

The light pole has $m=s_{\min}(1)>0$. At $a=1$, $MM^\dagger$ is the tridiagonal matrix with diagonal 5 and off-diagonal $-2$, minus 4 on the first diagonal. Interlacing places its second eigenvalue above 1. The first lies below 1 for $L\ge2$ since $\|M^{-1}e_0\|>1$. Closing the private chain gives minimum 1. Omitting the public Wilson hopping and its compensating home term restores eight equal corners. For $a>0$, $M^{-1}_{ij}=2^{i-j}/a^{i-j+1}$ for $i\ge j$. Both leading singular vectors of the inverse can be taken strictly positive, so its largest singular value has strictly negative derivative in $a$. Hence $s'_{\min}(a)>0$ and $k=0$ is the unique public minimum modulo periods. Reversing the row index of $2^{1-L}M(1)^{-1}$ gives the truncated outer product $2^{-i}2^{-j}$ on $i+j<L$. Its norm tends to $4/3$, giving $m\sim(3/2)2^{-L}$. The corresponding left and right wall vectors occupy opposite endpoints.

The recorded positive rate relative to a supplied $\pi/2$ carrier is $E_0=\arcsin(m/12)$ per service. It varies with the private extent. The architecture supplies neither a physical energy unit nor a species-specific extent. Fitting $L$ to a known mass would consume the comparison. The finite pole and its mirror are consistent with domain-wall localization. Physical identification also requires an observer-built source, reference and detector. [Domain-wall construction in lattice theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9206013)

## Two measurements of the magnetic factor

The rest pole also has an integer check. Set $x=m^2$. The leading principal minors of $MM^\dagger-xI$ satisfy $P_0=1$, $P_1=a^2-x$ and $P_j=(a^2+4-x)P_{j-1}-4a^2P_{j-2}$. Interlacing isolates the lowest root in $(0,1)$ at $a=1$. Bisection uses integer signs, and interval evaluation of $-P_a/(2P_x)$ encloses $m\,s'_{\min}(1)$. The replay emits exact dyadic bounds on the squared pole and on $1-g/2$. At $L=32$ the relative error of the double-precision singular value can exceed $10^{-8}$. At $L=64$ the exact calculation resolves $m\simeq8.13152\times10^{-20}$ and $1-g/2\simeq4.18770\times10^{-37}$, even though ordinary floating-point subtraction reports zero deficit. [Integer and rational replay](wall_pole.py)

Put $\eta=s'_{\min}(1)>0$. The small-momentum rate satisfies $E=E_0+A|k|^2+O(|k|^4)$. Coupling a link to a transverse field gives the effective spin term $-q C\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot B$. In the same service and momentum units,

$$
 A=\frac{1/m+\eta}{2\sqrt{144-m^2}},\qquad
 C=\frac{1}{2m\sqrt{144-m^2}},\qquad
 g=\frac{2C}{A}=\frac{2}{1+m\eta}.
$$

For the kinetic term expand $s_{\min}(a)=m+\eta |k|^2/2+O(|k|^4)$ in the positive rate. For the spin term the two wall orbitals at rest are $(u,\pm v)/\sqrt2$ for the left and right singular vectors of $M$. The kinetic matrices exchange them. Second-order elimination across the $2m$ splitting gives $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pi})^2/(2m)=(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^2-q\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot B)/(2m)$ on the positive branch. The scalar Wilson term contributes $\eta\boldsymbol{\Pi}^2/2$. Differentiating the common $\arcsin$ clock gives the denominator. The sign and normalization also follow from two different finite instruments.

1. **Signed current.** A $y$-link grating $A_y(x)=h\cos(2px)$ scatters the incident $-p$ wall mode into $+p$ at the same rate. The odd part of the one-step amplitude is $-iq\,\sigma_z b\sin(p)/(12r)$, where $r^2=\sin^2p+s_{\min}(2-\cos p)^2$ and $b$ is the finite Fourier coefficient of $\sin A_y$ for the imposed grating at $2p$. Diagonalizing the two wall orbitals at $\pm p$ gives the $\sin(p)/r$ factor. The derivative of the effective rate on this degenerate pair is $1/\sqrt{1-(r/12)^2}$ times the measured discriminant derivative. Dividing by the field gradient gives $C$ as $p,h\to0$. Four reference phases measure the complex overlap $z$ with probabilities $|z+i^j|^2/8$. The unmatched port weighs $(1-|z|^2)/2$. Each spin-matched or orthogonal receiver uses a separate preparation. The 192 count vectors total 13,194,139,533,312 simulated trials, aggregated by multinomial sampling. All five outcomes are retained. The maximum residual against the independent Fourier forecast is 2.73 marginal counting standard deviations. [Frozen grating](protocol.json) · [Counts](counts.json)

Each reader retains its weight on all six returned lines around the rest frequency and the aggregate outside them. Spin splitting is compared with the two same-spin orbital spacings. In weak flux the latter are $2A|B|$ and the former is $2C|B|$. In the finite instrument the magnetic factor uses twice the spin splitting divided by the mean orbital spacing. ### Finite clock and uncertainty

A $b$-bit register prepares uniform service times $0,\ldots,T-1$, where $T=2^b$. Controlled applications of the walk and an inverse Fourier transform give a bin $j$. Folding its phase to $\pi/2-|\arg U|$ combines the two carrier signs. The acquisition uses the complete 768 eigenvectors per grade at period 128. It samples the exact finite-counter marginal. A separate test evolves the full port state coherently at every counter time and checks every Fourier bin on a small carrier. The eight preparations at period 128 supply 16,392 simulated readings. Their median frequencies give magnetic intervals [2.1051, 2.1366] for both charges. Every occupied bin is retained. [Clock design](phase_clock.json) · [Histograms](clock_counts.json) · [Controlled-step comparison](test_phase_clock.py)

At the large periods a resource bound uses the weight on each assigned line and allows the entire complement to lie against it. For an approximate unit eigenvector with raw-root residual $\epsilon$, its norm outside a radius $d$ is at most $\epsilon/d$ by the spectral theorem. A probe of overlap $\sqrt w$ therefore puts at least $(\max(0,\sqrt w-\epsilon/d))^2$ inside that interval. The calculation uses $d=10^{-6}$ for the unscaled magnetic matrix and adds $10^{-9}$ to the numerical residual. For an exact eigenphase the rectangular Fourier counter misses by four or more bins with probability at most $1/6$: on each side the tail is bounded by $\sum_{j\ge4}1/(4j^2)$. Converting the raw interval through $\arcsin(r/12)$ and adding four bin widths bounds the single-trial error with the resulting majority probability $p$. For 2049 independent trials the median misses with probability at most $\exp[-2(2049)(p-1/2)^2]$. Summing over both charges and all 48 line readers gives a failure bound below $10^{-25}$ under the stated residual budget. The counter uses 20 bits at period 128 and 27 at 16384. The latter costs 134,217,727 controlled services per trial and confines the finite-frequency estimator to [1.8246, 1.8480]. This last interval is a resource forecast. The acquired histograms are on the 20-bit clock. [Finite-clock implementation and resource replay](phase_clock.py)
