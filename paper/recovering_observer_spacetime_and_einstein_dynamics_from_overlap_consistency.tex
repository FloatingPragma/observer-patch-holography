\begin{abstract}
We give conditions under which observations on overlapping patches admit
a common spacetime description. A rank-three sector of finite screen
responses supplies three spatial components. On a specified radial product,
a free field on a smooth static Lorentzian metric reproduces every finite
admissible menu of scalar-wave observations to any prescribed tolerance.
For gravitational dynamics we show how directional stress measurements,
modular charges and fixed-volume entropy stationarity determine the
Einstein equation up to a cosmological constant, provided their geometric
normalizations and conservation laws hold on the same refinement tower.
In an executable spherical model, finite patches compare a prepared
modular charge with the energy of propagating matter and measure energy
currents and directional torques at shared boundaries. A covariant
stress tensor requires a common physical metric and momentum transport.
\end{abstract}

 =\int v^2\,d\Gamma(w)+\frac{E(v)}{n^2}.
\]
The same identity holds for the gradient energy with $|\zeta|^2dx$ in place
of $d\Gamma(w)$. Comparing the two and letting $n\to\infty$ bounds
$\int v^2d\Gamma(w)$ between positive multiples of $\int v^2|\zeta|^2dx$ for
every cutoff, so $\Gamma(w)$ has a density between those multiples of
$|\zeta|^2$. Polarization gives measurable coefficients, and the chain rule
reconstructs the form from them.

\paragraph{The wave action and its metric.}
In local coordinates write $\mu\,d^3x$ for $\rho\nu$ and $a^{ij}$ for the
coordinate components of $a$. The wave action
$S(\phi)=-\tfrac12\int C^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi\,d^4x$
with coefficient density $C=\mu\operatorname{diag}(-1,a)$ has weak equation
$\mu\,\partial_t^2\phi=\partial_i(\mu a^{ij}\partial_j\phi)$, that is
$\partial_t^2\phi=-B\phi$. Since $\det C=-\mu^4\det a$, the tensor
\[
 g=\sqrt{-\det C}\;C^{-1}=\mu\sqrt{\det a}\,\operatorname{diag}(-1,a^{-1})
\]
satisfies $\det g=-\mu^4\det a$ and $\sqrt{-\det g}\,g^{-1}=C$. The weak
equation is therefore the scalar wave equation $\Box_g\phi=0$, and $g$ has
signature $(-,+,+,+)$ almost everywhere. When $\mu$ and $a$ are smooth, $g$
is a smooth static Lorentz metric on $\mathbb R\times X$ and the product
wave is exactly its free scalar field with the radial Dirichlet condition.

\paragraph{Readouts.}
The field theory of a generator $B$ is the quasi-free ground or thermal
state, at a fixed inverse temperature for the static time, on the Weyl
algebra of time-zero field and momentum smearings $F=(f,h)$ with
$f\in\mathcal H$ and $h\in V$. Its two-point functions, its symplectic form
and the time evolution of smearings are given by products of the functions
\[
 c(B)B^{-1/2},\quad c(B)B^{1/2},\quad \cos(t\sqrt B),\quad
 \frac{\sin(t\sqrt B)}{\sqrt B},\quad \sqrt B\sin(t\sqrt B),
\]
with $c(\lambda)=\coth(\beta\sqrt\lambda/2)$ in the thermal state and
$c=1$ in the ground state. A finite admissible menu is a finite list of
outcome probabilities of instruments whose effects are finite polynomials in
Weyl operators of finitely many listed smearings at finitely many times in
a bounded interval $[-T,T]$. Finite Weyl words and finite adaptive
instruments with Weyl feedback are of this kind, together with their
classical records. Since $W(F)W(F')=e^{-i\sigma(F,F')/2}W(F+F')$ and a
quasi-free state has $\omega(W(F))=e^{-Q(F)/2}$, every probability of the
menu is a finite combination of exponentials of the quantities
$\langle u,q(B)v\rangle$ for the displayed functions $q$, the listed
smearings $u,v$ and the listed time differences.

\begin{proposition}[Smooth effective field descriptions]
\label{prop:smooth-effective-descriptions}
Assume (S1) and (S2). Smoothing the angular coefficients while retaining the
radial Dirichlet interval gives norm-resolvent convergence of the transported
generators, with common gap $(\pi/L)^2$. For every fixed finite admissible
menu of observer readouts on bounded wave times and every
$\varepsilon>0$, a smooth static $(1+3)$-dimensional Lorentzian metric carries
a free scalar field whose complete predictions for the menu agree within
$\varepsilon$ with those of the product wave. If the readouts carry stated
source and clock errors, these add to $\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Smoothing.} Mollify $\rho$ and $a_{\rm s}$ in a fixed finite atlas of
$S^2$ with a partition of unity and normalize. Along a sequence
$\varepsilon\to0$ this gives smooth positive $\rho_\varepsilon\to\rho$
uniformly, with $\sup|\nabla\rho_\varepsilon|$ bounded and
$\nabla\rho_\varepsilon\to\nabla\rho$ almost everywhere, and smooth
$a_{{\rm s},\varepsilon}\to a_{\rm s}$ almost everywhere with uniform
ellipticity constants, since mollification averages positive forms. Neither
depends on $r$. Let $B_\varepsilon$ be the generator of
$(\rho_\varepsilon,a_{{\rm s},\varepsilon})$ on
$\mathcal H_\varepsilon=L^2(X,\rho_\varepsilon\nu)$ with the same domain $V$;
it obeys $B_\varepsilon\geq(\pi/L)^2$.

\emph{One Hilbert space.} Put $w_\varepsilon=\sqrt{\rho_\varepsilon/\rho}$
and $U_\varepsilon f=w_\varepsilon f$. Then
$\|U_\varepsilon f\|_{\mathcal H}^2=\int|f|^2\rho_\varepsilon\,d\nu$, so
$U_\varepsilon:\mathcal H_\varepsilon\to\mathcal H$ is unitary. The
transported generator $\widetilde B_\varepsilon=U_\varepsilon B_\varepsilon
U_\varepsilon^{-1}$ has the form
$\widetilde E_\varepsilon(f)=E_\varepsilon(f/w_\varepsilon)$, with the same
domain $V$ because $w_\varepsilon^{\pm1}$ are Lipschitz and bounded, and
gap $(\pi/L)^2$ because the radial Poincar\'e inequality holds on every
screen fibre. Moreover $w_\varepsilon^{\pm1}\to1$ uniformly,
$\nabla w_\varepsilon$ is uniformly bounded, and
$\nabla w_\varepsilon\to0$ almost everywhere. The smearing of the smooth
theory is $U_\varepsilon^{-1}f$; it has the support of $f$ and converges to
$f$ in $\mathcal H$, and in $H^1$ for $f\in V$.

\emph{Mosco convergence.} Let $f_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup f$ weakly in
$\mathcal H$ with $\liminf\widetilde E_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon)<\infty$.
Along a subsequence the energies are bounded. Uniform ellipticity and
$\nabla f_\varepsilon=w_\varepsilon\nabla(f_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon)
+(f_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon)\nabla w_\varepsilon$ bound $f_\varepsilon$ in
$H^1$, so by Rellich a further subsequence converges weakly in $H^1$ and
strongly in $L^2$ to $f\in V$. The difference
$\nabla(f_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon)-\nabla f_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon
=-f_\varepsilon\nabla w_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon^2$ tends to zero in $L^2$:
split $f_\varepsilon\nabla w_\varepsilon=(f_\varepsilon-f)\nabla w_\varepsilon
+f\nabla w_\varepsilon$ and use the uniform bound for the first term and
dominated convergence for the second. Hence
$\xi_\varepsilon=\nabla(f_\varepsilon/w_\varepsilon)\rightharpoonup\nabla f$.
With $M_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon a_\varepsilon$ and $M=\rho a$, the
pointwise inequality
$M\xi\cdot\xi\geq2\xi\cdot\psi-M^{-1}\psi\cdot\psi$ for bounded vector
fields $\psi$ gives
\[
 \liminf\widetilde E_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon)
 \geq2\int\nabla f\cdot\psi\,d\nu-\int M^{-1}\psi\cdot\psi\,d\nu,
\]
because $M_\varepsilon^{-1}\to M^{-1}$ almost everywhere with a uniform
bound. The supremum over $\psi$ is $E(f)$. For the recovery sequence take
$f_\varepsilon=f\in V$: then
$\nabla(f/w_\varepsilon)\to\nabla f$ almost everywhere with an integrable
square majorant, and dominated convergence gives
$\widetilde E_\varepsilon(f)\to E(f)$. Thus $\widetilde E_\varepsilon$
Mosco-converges to $E$, and Mosco's theorem gives strong resolvent
convergence $\widetilde B_\varepsilon\to B$~\cite{mosco1994}.

\emph{Norm-resolvent convergence.} For $\|u\|\leq1$ the vector
$v=(\widetilde B_\varepsilon+1)^{-1}u$ obeys
$\widetilde E_\varepsilon(v)+\|v\|^2=\langle u,v\rangle\leq1$, so the
resolvents map the unit ball into one bounded subset of $H^1$, which is
relatively compact in $\mathcal H$. Suppose
$\|(\widetilde B_\varepsilon+1)^{-1}-(B+1)^{-1}\|\geq\delta>0$ along a
subsequence, attained up to $\delta/2$ at unit vectors
$u_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup u$. Compactness of $(B+1)^{-1}$ gives
$(B+1)^{-1}u_\varepsilon\to(B+1)^{-1}u$. A further subsequence has
$(\widetilde B_\varepsilon+1)^{-1}u_\varepsilon\to k$, and for every $\phi$,
$\langle(\widetilde B_\varepsilon+1)^{-1}u_\varepsilon,\phi\rangle
=\langle u_\varepsilon,(\widetilde B_\varepsilon+1)^{-1}\phi\rangle
\to\langle u,(B+1)^{-1}\phi\rangle$ by strong resolvent convergence. Hence
$k=(B+1)^{-1}u$, a contradiction. The convergence is in norm.

\emph{Readouts.} Norm-resolvent convergence gives
$\|q(\widetilde B_\varepsilon)-q(B)\|\to0$ for every continuous $q$ on
$[(\pi/L)^2,\infty)$ that vanishes at infinity, and strong convergence for
every bounded continuous $q$~\cite{reedsimon1980}. The gap keeps
$c(\lambda)$, $\lambda^{-1/2}$ and $\sin(t\sqrt\lambda)/\sqrt\lambda$
continuous and bounded on the spectrum; the last two, and the product
$c(\lambda)\lambda^{-1/2}$, vanish at infinity, and $t\mapsto\sin(t\sqrt\lambda)/\sqrt\lambda$ is Lipschitz in the
supremum norm, so that convergence is uniform for $|t|\leq T$. The bounded
function $\cos(t\sqrt\lambda)$ converges strongly, uniformly for $|t|\leq T$
on each fixed smearing in $V$, because
$|\cos(t\sqrt\lambda)-\cos(t'\sqrt\lambda)|\leq|t-t'|\sqrt\lambda$ and
$\|\widetilde B_\varepsilon^{1/2}h\|^2=\widetilde E_\varepsilon(h)$ is
bounded. A product function of the menu is unbounded only between two
momentum smearings $h,h'\in V$, and there it is at most a constant times
$\sqrt\lambda$, since $c$ is bounded on the spectrum. Split it with a
continuous cutoff $\chi_K$ equal to one on $[0,K]$ and zero beyond $2K$. The
cut-off part is bounded, continuous and of compact support. Since
$\sqrt\lambda\leq\lambda/\sqrt K$ for $\lambda\geq K$, the tail is bounded
by a constant times
$K^{-1/2}\widetilde E_\varepsilon(h)^{1/2}\widetilde E_\varepsilon(h')^{1/2}$,
uniformly in $\varepsilon$ because $\widetilde E_\varepsilon(h)\to E(h)$. Every quantity $\langle u,q(\widetilde
B_\varepsilon)v\rangle$ of the menu therefore converges to
$\langle u,q(B)v\rangle$, uniformly in the listed times, and so do the
finitely many probabilities built from them. One $\varepsilon$ serves the
whole menu.

\emph{Metric.} For that $\varepsilon$ the coefficient density
$C_\varepsilon=\mu_\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(-1,a_\varepsilon)$ is
smooth, so $g_\varepsilon=\sqrt{-\det C_\varepsilon}\,C_\varepsilon^{-1}$ is
a smooth static Lorentz metric on $\mathbb R\times X$ whose free scalar field
with radial Dirichlet condition has generator $B_\varepsilon$. Its
predictions for the transported menu equal
$\langle u,q(\widetilde B_\varepsilon)v\rangle$ through $U_\varepsilon$, and
they lie within the requested tolerance of the product wave. Source and clock
errors of the readouts enter both sides identically and add by the triangle
inequality.
\end{proof}

The proposition proves a common finite-menu approximation. It does not
select the radial architecture, which is a declared design input, it gives no
smoothing rate for an unknown limiting tensor, and it produces no responsive
metric, curvature, interacting continuum or laboratory identification. One
smooth metric exact for every observation is not asserted. In this sense the
proposition is the field part of the relativistic $3{+}1$D description stated
with the hypothesis of this paper; with the rank-three spatial carrier of the
source quotient and the causal and count limits of
Appendix~\ref{app:source-causal-order}, that description is fixed by the
predictions for the observers' admissible readouts. The state of the
simulation is no spacetime, and the screen and its carrier are hardware of
the computation.

\paragraph{Travelling clocks of the executable model.}
The main candidate law of the executable model, Interval Echo~\cite{ophmodel},
uses the contrast-memory seam interaction at coupling $\kappa=8$ and gives a
finite observer patch a clock that it carries: a five-position hand, an
elapsed count and the number of its own repair opportunities since its last
verified handoff. A handoff closes an interval, and a bounded first-return
walk on local fair draws decides whether the closing mark advances the hand;
the clock reads no speed and no target table. The observers measure the
motion themselves: a home patch times a reciprocal light pulse and obtains
the one-way light time $d$, a pulse triggered by the arrival of the
traveller at the far end gives the outward duration $T_1$, and the rest of
the trip is the return duration $T_2$. At reunion both owners read the
traveller's and the home counts on one common seam, and their ratio is
compared with the proper-time forecast
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{T_1^2-d^2}+\sqrt{T_2^2-d^2}}{T_1+T_2}.
\]
On three journeys with outward and return leg speeds $(1/2,1/6)$,
$(1/6,1/2)$ and $(1/4,1/4)$ of the signal speed, each with $524{,}288$
paired journeys, the residuals
are ${-0.00105\pm0.00068}$, ${+0.00019\pm0.00052}$ and ${+0.00087\pm0.00064}$ (one
standard error). The dilation is $3.2$ to $4.4$ percent, so the travelling
clock runs slow by the relativistic factor, resolved at dozens of standard
errors and within about one part in a thousand of the forecast. Embeddings
of $1{,}280$ and $5{,}120$ cells give the same material handoff sequence. The
closing filter is calibrated on the reunion ratio of one supplied reciprocal
relay journey; the guide, custody transfers, gate selection, relative
cadence and read windows are supplied services, and the hand size, the
register widths and the walk budget are designed. Clock capacity behaves as
it does for a physical clock: on handoffs of eight services the three-bit
clock overflows, and a four-bit clock, which costs one more cell at each
checkpoint, extends the working range from seven to fifteen services and
restores the forecast, ${0.99005\pm0.00193}$ against $0.99216$. The causal and
count limits of this appendix and premise (G1) further require a jointly
authenticated causal order of the candidate's events whose counts converge
to volume.

\subsection{Finite modular covariance and compressed responses}
\label{sec:finite-modular-covariance}

Geometric modular covariance requires a comparison beyond the existence
of a finite modular clock. The following finite identity states a necessary
boundary of that comparison, independently of the repair scheduler.

\begin{proposition}[Finite modular dilation and its compression boundary]
\label{prop:finite-modular-dilation}
Let $G,P$ be Hermitian matrices on a finite Hilbert space, let
$U(s)=e^{isG}$, and let $\kappa\in\mathbb R$. Then
\[
 \|i[G,P]-\kappa P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =\|[G,P]\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\kappa^2\|P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
 \qquad
 \|U(s)PU(s)^*-e^{\kappa s}P\|
 \geq |e^{\kappa s}-1|\,\|P\|.
\]
For an orthogonal projection $Q$, write $G_Q=QGQ$, $P_Q=QPQ$ and
$B_Q=i(QG(1-Q)PQ-QP(1-Q)GQ)$. Then
\[
 Q\,i[G,P]Q=i[G_Q,P_Q]+B_Q,
 \qquad
 \|Q(i[G,P]-\kappa P)Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\|B_Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \geq |\kappa|\,\|P_Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cyclicity gives $\operatorname{Tr}(P\,i[G,P])=0$, so the cross term
in the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm vanishes. Unitary invariance of
the operator norm and the reverse triangle inequality give the
finite-parameter bound. Inserting $Q+(1-Q)$ into each product gives
the compressed identity. Apply the first identity to the compressed
matrices, then the triangle inequality, for the last bound.
\end{proof}

For an example, let $\sigma$ be a faithful diagonal reference with weights
$2/225$ and $2/45$ on two configurations $a$ and $b$, and take
$G=\log\sigma$; thus $s$ is the dimensionless modular parameter. On the
two-state corner of $a$ and $b$ declare
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}1&i/2\\-i/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,\qquad
 |\psi\rangle=(|a\rangle+|b\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\]
and extend $P$ by zero. It is positive, with nonzero eigenvalues
$1/2,3/2$. For $\kappa=\log(5)/2$, the projected first derivative
is exactly $\kappa P_Q$, supplied entirely by $B_Q$; the internal
compressed commutator is zero. Nevertheless
\[
 \langle\psi|U(s)PU(s)^*|\psi\rangle
 =1+\tfrac12\sin(s\log5)\ne e^{s\log(5)/2}
\]
in general. At $s=1/\log5$ the two values are approximately $1.420735$
and $1.648721$. The declared probe is not identified with physical null
momentum.

Consequently a fixed finite Hilbert algebra cannot supply nonzero
global modular dilation. A spectral projection of $G$ has $B_Q=0$ and
retains the same obstruction. Restricted continuum comparisons must
specify states, observable domains and finite parameter windows, retain
the compression boundary, and control the actual responses on one
refining family. Unbounded continuum generators are not excluded by
these bounded-operator statements.
