\paragraph{A controlled massless relativistic sector of the unchanged action.}
The same Weyl/contact family admits a simultaneous eight-valley continuum
comparison on every fixed finite-particle momentum domain.  Set
\[
 H_a=d\Gamma(h_a)+U(a)D,\qquad
 h_a(k)=-\frac va\sum_{j=1}^3\sigma_j\sin k_j,
 \qquad D=\sum_x n_{x\uparrow}n_{x\downarrow},
\]
with fixed $v>0$ and finite $U(a)\ge0$.  This declares the scaling $c=v/a$;
it adds no mass term, energy subtraction or time reversal.  For
$\nu=\pi b$, $b\in\{0,1\}^3$, let $C=[-\Lambda,\Lambda]^3$ and
$a\Lambda<\pi/2$.  With both Fourier measures normalized by $(2\pi)^{-3}$,
\[
 (W_af)(\nu+ap)=a^{-3/2}f_b(p),\qquad
 W_a:\bigoplus_b L^2(C,\mathbb C^2)\longrightarrow
 L^2(\mathbb T^3,\mathbb C^2)
\]
is an isometry, with zero output outside the eight disjoint patches.
There is no additional factor $1/\sqrt8$.  Its exterior powers give an
isometry $W_a^{\le N}$ on all particle numbers through a fixed $N$, with
the vacuum and every valley retained.

Writing $s_j=(-1)^{b_j}$ and $\chi_b=-s_1s_2s_3$, the limiting blocks are
\[
 h_b(p)=-v\sum_j s_j\sigma_jp_j,
 \qquad E_\pm(p)=\pm v|p|,
 \qquad E^2-v^2|p|^2=0.
\]
All eight continuum masses are zero.  Four blocks have each chirality.
The proper frame $R_b=-\chi_b\operatorname{diag}(s)$ has a constant
$SU(2)$ lift and spin $S_{b,j}=R_{b,jj}\sigma_j/2$, so
$h_b=2\chi_bv S_b\cdot p$.  The positive-energy helicity is $\chi_b/2$;
the negative-energy helicity is $-\chi_b/2$.  At $p=0$ the two spin states
are degenerate and helicity is undefined.  In these frames an opposite
pair has $\alpha_j=\operatorname{diag}(-\sigma_j,\sigma_j)$.
With off-diagonal identity $\beta$, the matrices
$\gamma=(\beta,\beta\alpha_1,\beta\alpha_2,\beta\alpha_3)$ satisfy the
Clifford relations for signature $(1,-1,-1,-1)$, checked exactly.
Thus the limiting differential equation is massless Weyl/Dirac in
coordinate $x^0=vt$, with the Clifford relations verified.\footnote{For
the standard Weyl equation and helicity convention, see D.~Tong,
\emph{Quantum Field Theory}, Sections 4.4 and 4.7,
\url{https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S4.html}.}
The fixed cutoff and the lattice regulator are not Lorentz invariant;
neither the valley pairing nor this spin frame selects physical species.

\paragraph{The full interacting evolution has an explicit comparison bound.}
The sine remainder gives, uniformly on $C$,
\[
 \|h_a(\nu+ap)-h_b(p)\|
 \le\frac{va^2}{6}\sum_j|p_j|^3
 \le\frac{va^2\Lambda^3}{2}.
\]
For $a\Lambda\le1$ and $p\ne0$, both spectral-projector errors are at
most $a^2\Lambda^2/6$.  The squared-energy defect lies between
$-v^2a^2\sum_jp_j^4/3$ and zero.  No projector at the degenerate node
is assigned.

Let $\Omega_a$ project onto the initial Fock band and
$d=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$.  On the infinite lattice the site-diagonal band
weight is exactly $f_a=8(a\Lambda/\pi)^3$.  The positive countable
partial sums defining $D$ are bounded by $d$, hence converge strongly
and satisfy $D^2\le dD$.  On band states each annihilation has norm at
most $\sqrt{f_a}$ and preserves the band for the remaining particles.
Summing its squared norms gives
$\Omega_aD\Omega_a\le f_a d$, separately on each up/down number sector,
and therefore $\|D\Omega_a\|\le d\sqrt{f_a}$.
This proves the infinite-volume attachment of the finite dilute-band
estimate without substituting a continuum fraction for a finite count.

Duhamel's formula yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \big\|e^{-itH_a}W_a^{\le N}
       -W_a^{\le N}e^{-itd\Gamma(h_{\rm W})}\big\|
 &\le\min\{2,|t|\epsilon_a\},\\
 \epsilon_a&=\frac{Nva^2\Lambda^3}{2}
       +|U(a)|d\sqrt{8(a\Lambda/\pi)^3}.
 \end{split}
\]
The left evolution is the full source Hamiltonian: no band projection is
inserted and every outgoing contact state remains present.  The estimate
also holds with arbitrary untouched references; it bounds half trace
distance and individual probability errors, capped by one.
For fixed $N,\Lambda,v$ and bounded times it tends to zero whenever
$U(a)=o(a^{-3/2})$.  At $N\le1$ the contact is exactly zero for every
$U(a)$.  Thus the proved limit is free; the critical pair scaling
$U\sim a^{-2}$ and finite-density limits are outside this conclusion.
For $N=2$, $\Lambda=v=U=t=1$ and $a=1/64$, the complete-vector bound
is less than $0.001237$ and the projector bound is $1/24576$.

The finite source check retains all 128 one-particle Fourier columns on
a periodic $4^3$ cube.  An eight-valley two-particle state there has exact
$\|D\psi\|^2=1/8$ and
$\|(1-\Omega)D\psi\|^2=7/64$, so the contact escape is real.
Propagation on all 8,128 two-particle states at $t=1/8$ gives a numerical
vector difference about $0.0441641$, below the rigorous
$\sqrt{1/8}/8$.  These matrix diagnostics and exact source identities
do not constitute an additional native preparation or detector execution.
