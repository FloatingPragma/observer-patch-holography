\subsection{Action measure, record production and geometric density}
\label{sec:source-action-density-identification}

A supplied scalar response operator contains more information about its
action measure than an unweighted event count. It does not identify
that measure with physical volume. The distinction can be tested on the
same source-addressed family, with its preparation, protected records,
read law and model clock kept explicit.

\begin{proposition}[Identification of normalized action measure]
\label{prop:source-action-measure-identification}
Let $A$ be a finite real operator whose nonzero off-diagonal support is
connected. Suppose a positive diagonal matrix $M=\operatorname{diag}(m_i)$
makes $MA$ symmetric. The equations
\[
 m_iA_{ij}=m_jA_{ji},\qquad \sum_i m_i=1
\]
determine $m$ uniquely. If the two directed coefficients on every support
edge are negative, a spanning tree constructs the candidate through
$m_j/m_i=A_{ij}/A_{ji}$; the remaining edges test consistency.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For two positive solutions $m,n$, multiplying their edge equations and
cancelling a nonzero coefficient gives $m_i n_j=m_j n_i$. Thus $m_i/n_i$
is constant along every path. Connectivity makes the constant global,
and normalization sets it to one. Tree products produce the candidate;
positivity and every remaining edge equation certify its extension to
the whole graph. Multiplying both $M$ and the stiffness $MA$ by one
positive constant leaves $A$ unchanged, so reciprocity cannot identify
an absolute action scale.
\end{proof}

This reconstruction also has a finite conditioning bound. If each
directed edge coefficient has relative error at most $\epsilon<1$,
each inferred edge ratio changes by a factor between $r^{-1}$ and $r$,
where $r=(1+\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon)$. For a tree of depth $d$, the
root-relative weights change by factors in $[r^{-d},r^d]$. Normalizing
their sum gives factors in $[r^{-2d},r^{2d}]$. This bounds the tree
estimate; noisy cycle disagreement remains a model-consistency test.

On the authenticated $q=5$ scalar family, the independent certificate
recovers all $64$ normalized weights from the directed operator alone,
checks its $144$ undirected edges, and compares the result with the
separately reconstructed golden dual-cell quadrature. The tree has depth
$9$ and $81$ remaining cycle edges. The parent mass vector is used for
this comparison, not by the reconstruction algorithm. Operator and
action selection remain supplied inputs.

\paragraph{A production control on the same finite family.}
Two preparations, with amplitude multipliers $1/2$ and $1$ relative to
the retained source, give $2{,}944$ independently replayed field writes.
They have the same response operator and field ancestry; their initial
energies differ by a factor of four. Local observer records read the
actual writer, immutable version and value at the same site. Their extra
read/write operations are charged separately and have no identified
physical durations. The union of these local certificates is not a
construction of remote record transport to one observer.

For an interior field-ancestry interval $I$, let
$v_e=\tau m_{i(e)}$, where $\tau=\sqrt5/35$ is the retained action tick.
Declare a production multiplier $r>0$ and an integer exposure budget
$K$. The finite local production rule
\[
 n_e=\lfloor Krv_e\rfloor,\qquad
 \widehat V_r(I)=\frac{1}{Kr}\sum_{e\in I}n_e
\]
has the exact deterministic enclosure
\begin{equation}
 0\le \sum_{e\in I}v_e-\widehat V_r(I)
 <\frac{|I|}{Kr}.
 \label{eq:source-calibrated-production-error}
\end{equation}
The proof sums $0\le Krv_e-n_e<1$. Four controls cross the two source
strengths with $r=1,2$ at $K=4096$. All $324$ local observer records are
replayed. Changing $r$ changes raw density while the field order and
action measure stay fixed; dividing by the declared production law
recovers the same finite measure within
\eqref{eq:source-calibrated-production-error}. Neither that production
law nor the identification of $\tau m_i$ with physical four-volume is
derived by this test. No Poisson reference or old causal count-clock
theorem is substituted for the actual field ancestry.

\paragraph{What a specified conformal action adds.}
Consider the separate control metric
\[
 g=u(x)\,(-dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2),\qquad u>0,
\]
and the canonical massive scalar action in these coordinates. Its kinetic weight scales as $u$,
whereas its metric four-volume scales as $u^2$. In the finite control,
$M_i(u)=u_iM_i(1)$, each undirected gradient coefficient is multiplied
by $(u_i+u_j)/2$, the Dirichlet boundary coefficient by its interior
$u_i$, and the unit-mass potential coefficient by $u_i^2$.
These terms define one symmetric quadratic action and reproduce the
original operator at $u=1$.

Reciprocity determines the relative profile $u_i/u_j$ after division by
the reference weights. The full operator supplies more: at an interior
site with all six neighbours and the specified unit mass potential,
\[
 \sum_j A(u)_{ij}=u_i.
\]
Gradient terms cancel in this row sum. It calibrates the common profile
factor; all eight interior sites provide independent consistency checks.
Thus no additional value of $u$ is imported into this reconstruction.
The unit scalar mass and the conformal action class are essential
inputs. The controls $u=1$ and $u=1+x/2$ recover both profiles exactly
while retaining the same nonzero support and hence the same layered
support order. Their normalized kinetic and four-volume measures differ
for the nonconstant profile.

For an identified affine control $u=a+bx$, its specified continuum
metric has scalar curvature
\[
 R=-6\Omega^{-3}\Box\Omega
   =\frac{3b^2}{2u^3},\qquad \Omega=\sqrt u,
\]
with signature $(-+++)$ and the displayed curvature convention.
The nonzero control curvature is obtained through the supplied action
and metric dictionary, not from an unweighted ordering statistic. No
law makes either profile respond to the simulated source strength.

\paragraph{Scalar readouts do not determine tensor response.}
The same finite action also permits a direct comparison of directional
responses. Write its potential as $V=G_x+G_y+G_z+V_m$, including every
interior and Dirichlet edge, and consider the retained neighbouring
configurations $q_-,q$. Their discrete action contribution is
\[
 L_d=T_d-\tau V,\qquad
 T_d=\frac{(q-q_-)^TM(q-q_-)}{2\tau}.
\]
The potential uses the right endpoint $q$. Summing these contributions gives the recorded centred update through
the discrete Euler--Lagrange equation. At fixed configurations, a stretch
of coordinate axis $k$ by $s$ changes the contribution to
\[
 L_d(s)=sT_d-\tau\{s(V-G_k)+G_k/s\},\qquad
 \left.\frac{dL_d}{d\log s}\right|_{s=1}
 =T_d-\tau(V-2G_k).
\]
An $x/y$ permutation commutes with the complete cubic source operator.
It preserves $T_d$, $V$, the action measure and the isotropic sum of
these derivatives, but interchanges unequal directional responses.
The certificate uses the two retained configurations and independently
reassembles the stretched-grid action at $s=2$ and $s=1/2$; two thirds
of their difference equals the displayed derivative exactly. Kinetic
variation is included. These are finite action responses, with no
identification of a physical stress source or gravitational coupling.

The connectedness and normalization theorem, common-scale ambiguities,
finite production-error bound and conformal calibration algebra are
checked in Lean; the source histories, exact
controls and an independent verifier accompany it~\cite{ophrepo}. They identify a relative action
measure and a profile within a specified conformal action class. They do
not select the population, action, clock, production grammar, storage,
source-to-metric update or physical tensor coupling. Those inputs are
needed before the recovered quantity can be used as a sourced physical
volume or an Einstein response.
