\section{Filled source vacuum and positive excitation energy}
\label{app:filled-source-vacuum}

The supplied massive walk and fermionic source compiler admit a filled
vacuum experiment. Its bounded observer-like patches have local state,
reflecting boundaries, resolved readback, repair operations and public
records. The graph, masses, canonical anticommutation relations, coherent
controls and external schedule are hypotheses. Particle/hole normal ordering
and Slater preparation are standard; relevant precedents are
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03466}{Gupta and Short's discrete-time Dirac sea}
and \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04789}{Kivlichan et al.'s fermionic rotations}.

\paragraph{Reflected gaps and the Fock generator.}
Let $\tau=\sqrt3a/c$, $\mu=m\tau\in(0,\pi/2)$ and
$U=e^{-i\mu\beta}U_0$. A flight leaving a finite cube reflects into the
opposite-chirality bank at its starting cell. The flight permutation $S$
satisfies $\beta S\beta=S^*$; exchanging the precoin and postcoin gives
\[
 \beta U_0\beta=U_0^*,\qquad \operatorname{Re}U=\cos\mu\operatorname{Re}U_0.
\]
Hence every eigenphase lies in
$[-\pi+\mu,-\mu]\cup[\mu,\pi-\mu]$, including at the boundary.
Put $h=i\operatorname{Log}U/\tau$, $P=\mathbf1_{h<0}$ and $Q=I-P$.
For the Slater state $\Omega_P$ filling precisely the negative modes,
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_{\rm vac}&=\operatorname{Tr}(hP),\qquad
 H_{\rm sea}=d\Gamma(h)-E_{\rm vac}I,\\
 H_{\rm sea}&=\sum_{e_j>0}e_jc_j^*c_j+
                  \sum_{e_j<0}|e_j|c_jc_j^*\ge0,\\
 e^{-in\tau H_{\rm sea}}&=e^{in\tau E_{\rm vac}}\Gamma(U)^n.
 \end{split}
\]
The filled state is the unique ground vector on the active logical Fock
space. Helpers have specified blank inputs and return conditions; no
apparatus ground-state uniqueness is asserted. The identity holds in every
parity sector, with external spectators, and gives the complete
even-observable instrument. Its excitation spectrum comprises all subset
sums of $|e_j|$. The empty-vacuum accounting observable $d\Gamma(|h|)$ has
the same partition function and a different vacuum and negative-mode dynamics.

\paragraph{Local vacuum comparison.}
Write $K=(U^*-U)/(2i)=\sin(\tau h)$ and $\delta=\sin\mu$.
Then $|K|\ge\delta$ and $P=(I-\operatorname{sign}K)/2$.
For the boundary comparison take the infinite-lattice $K,P$, and embed
the decoupled reflecting interior and exterior on that same lattice.
For a bounded one-Lipschitz cell-distance function $d$,
$\|e^{sd}Ke^{-sd}-K\|\le e^s-1$. Taking $s=\log(1+\delta/2)$,
the resolvent identity and sign integral give
\[
 \|(P-\widetilde P)\mathbf1_A\|\le(2/\delta)e^{-sr},\qquad
 r=\left\lceil\frac{\log(4/(\delta\epsilon))}{\log(1+\delta/2)}\right\rceil,
\]
where $\widetilde P$ belongs to the decoupled reflecting interior and
exterior, and $A$ is $r$ layers from their interface. Both pieces have the
same gap. At $a=L/q$, choosing $\epsilon=q^{-12}$ costs physical margin
$O(\log q)$ and $M=O(q^3\log^3q)$ modes around a fixed experiment.
No comparison of global sea-vector fidelities is required.

In a low-momentum chart, set $\mathcal R=I_2\otimes C$,
$H_m(k)=m\beta+(c/3)\alpha\cdot k$ and
$E(k)=\sqrt{m^2+(c/3)^2|k|^2}$. A second-order one-step Taylor bound yields
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 z_a(k)=\frac{(m\tau+\sqrt3a|k|)^2}{2\tau},\qquad
 \|K/\tau-\mathcal R H_m(k)\|\le z_a(k),\\
 P_0(k)=\tfrac12(I-\mathcal R H_m(k)/E(k)),\qquad
 \|P_a(k)-P_0(k)\|\le\min\{1,\pi z_a(k)/(4m)\}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Sampling and Fourier-tail bounds give $O(\sqrt a)$ convergence for each
fixed $H^2$ packet. The boundary estimate transfers this to finite cubes;
Wick determinants give convergence for fixed finite polynomials in smeared
fields. The $\mathcal R=+1$ sector has negative Dirac filling, whereas
$\mathcal R=-1$ has positive Dirac filling. All eight components, both bands
and all valleys are retained. The full limit is this quasi-free state,
not one ordinary Dirac vacuum.

\paragraph{Finite-energy operations and complete readout.}
For normalized $f$, put $N_f=c(f)^*c(f)$, $p=\langle f,Pf\rangle$,
$e_+=\langle Qf,|h|Qf\rangle$ and $e_-=\langle Pf,|h|Pf\rangle$.
Applying $e^{i\theta N_f}$ to the sea costs
\[
 \Delta E=4\sin^2(\theta/2)[p e_+ +(1-p)e_-]\le4\langle f,|h|f\rangle.
\]
The complete quantum-nondemolition read $\{N_f,I-N_f\}$ has channel
$\mathcal D_f(\rho)=N_f\rho N_f+(I-N_f)\rho(I-N_f)$ and obeys
\[
 \|\mathcal D_f^*(H_{\rm sea})-H_{\rm sea}\|\le2\|hf\|,
 \qquad \|hf\|\le\frac{\pi}{2\tau}\|(I-U)f\|.
\]
For fixed compact $H^2$ modes with $\mathcal Rf=f$ away from the boundary,
$\|(I-U)f\|=O(a)$. Thus the complete read has uniformly finite mean
excitation cost, retaining both outcomes and the full compact CAR fields.
This bound does not control rare conditional outcomes or intermediate
mode-gathering drive work.

For two masses, use $v=c/3$, $\gamma=(1-|u|^2/v^2)^{-1/2}$ and
$k_j=\gamma m_j u/v^2$. Translate the entire compact Gaussian mode,
including carrier and cutoff: $g_j(t)=T_{ut}g_j(0)$, and set
$f_\theta(t)=(g_1(t)+e^{i\theta}g_2(t))/\sqrt2$.
The normalized sampled initial modes have carrier $e^{ik_j\cdot x}\xi$
with common unit spinor $\mathcal R\xi=\xi$ and
$H_{m_j}(k_j)\xi=\gamma m_j\xi$.
Prepare deterministically $P+|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|$, where
$\chi=Qf_0(0)/\|Qf_0(0)\|$. The actual binary probability is
\[
 p_\theta(t)=\langle f_\theta(t),Pf_\theta(t)\rangle+
                |\langle f_\theta(t),U^n\chi\rangle|^2,\qquad t=n\tau.
\]
The background is retained without subtraction or postselection. The
rigid-packet reference is $[1+\cos((m_2-m_1)t/\gamma+\theta)]/2$ because
$\Delta E-u\cdot\Delta k=\Delta m/\gamma$. Translating only the envelope
defines a different read with frequency $\gamma\Delta m$.
For $c=3$, $(m_1,m_2)=(100,100.1)$, $|u|=0.6$, Gaussian width $10$,
cutoff half-width $80$ and $a=10^{-9}$, outward interval bounds give
probability error below $0.038$ and native-tick swing above $0.923$
through $2\pi\gamma/\Delta m+\tau$. This three-dimensional reflecting
apparatus is an analytic finite-size witness. Separate periodic packet
controls and small reflecting operators are executed. The read has charged
duration and exports its result to a live archive; its center is prescribed
by the external schedule.

\paragraph{Preparation, protection and energy ambiguity.}
An occupied-orbital unitary is prepared by adjacent-row rotations routed
on the connected source graph, with every syndrome repaired and retained.
At most $O(M^3)$ native rotations and depth suffice, including fermionic
swaps and restored helper banks. With angle synthesis and scheduling, a
conservative request envelope is $G=O(q^9\log^{16}(2q))$.
At fixed positive admitted local noise strength, the supplied protection
and archive compiler uses $O(\log\log q)$ protection levels and
$O(\log q)$-bit archive words. If $s$ is its fixed per-level size factor
and $\kappa=\lceil\log_2s\rceil$, synthesis to $q^{-64}$ gives complete
joint decoded-instrument error $O(q^{-55}\log^{36+2\kappa}(2q))$.
Since $0\le H_{\rm sea}\le M\pi/\tau$, the corresponding mean excitation
error is $O(q^{-51}\log^{39+2\kappa}(2q))$. Preparation, idles and retained
outcomes are included. Offline orbital computation and physical drive work
are not assigned native energy costs.
With $N=O(\log q)$ archive bits per word and $k$ protection levels,
the complete diagnostic storage-time envelope is
$O(G^3N^6s^{3k}\log q)=O(q^{27}\log^{55+3\kappa}(2q))$.
These resources include the enlarged preparation prehistory; its duration
is not held fixed as resolution increases.

Neither the sea nor these reads select a unique energy interpolation.
With $\Pi_{\rm hi}=\mathbf1_{|h|>\pi/(2\tau)}$, the alternative
\[
 h_{\rm UV}=h+(2\pi/\tau)\operatorname{sign}(h)\Pi_{\rm hi}
\]
has the same $U$ and $P$, and $|h|\le|h_{\rm UV}|\le5|h|$.
It preserves the finite-energy read class; on every fixed smooth admitted
mode, the energy difference is at most $8\tau\|hf\|^2/\pi\to0$.
Its ultraviolet excitation costs differ despite identical integer-time
reads, vacuum and infrared limits. The positive stroboscopic generator
therefore supplies neither an apparatus pulse Hamiltonian nor interacting
vacuum stability or empirical energy calibration. Complete derivations and
independent finite operator/circuit checks are supplied in
\path{code/m1_sea_energy}.
