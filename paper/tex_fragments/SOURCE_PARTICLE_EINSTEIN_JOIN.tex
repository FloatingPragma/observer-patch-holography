\subsection{Matter action, regional entropy and the geometric equation}
\label{sec:source-particle-einstein-join}

A finite particle spectrum and a responsive geometric readout do not by
themselves specify the stress or entropy of a common spacetime theory.
The curvature calculation makes this distinction explicit. In signature
$(-,+,+,+)$ and units $\hbar=c=1$, use the same complex Higgs doublet
$H$ as in the conditional matter action, write $X=H^\dagger H$, and specify
\begin{equation}
 S=\int\sqrt{-g}\,[F(X)R+\mathcal L_{\rm min}],\qquad
 F(X)=F_0-\xi X,\quad F_0>0.
 \label{eq:source-particle-curvature-action}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L_{\rm min}$ contains the canonical gauge, Higgs, fermion
and Yukawa terms. The covariant extension, metric, spin structure and
curvature coupling $\xi$ are supplied data. Let $T_{\rm min}$ be the
Hilbert stress of this same minimally coupled action. Metric variation
gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_{ab}:=2FG_{ab}
       +2(g_{ab}\Box-\nabla_a\nabla_b)F=T_{{\rm min},ab}.
 \label{eq:source-particle-metric-equation}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the additional matter stress relative to $2F_0G$ is
$T_{\xi,ab}=2\xi[XG_{ab}+(g_{ab}\Box-\nabla_a\nabla_b)X]$.
It obeys $\nabla^aT_{\xi,ab}=-\xi R\nabla_bX$; this sector is not
separately conserved. Total classical conservation uses all matter
field equations, retaining gauge forces and Yukawa exchange. It does
not establish a regulated quantum Ward identity.

The same curvature derivative
$\partial(FR)/\partial R_{abcd}
 =(F/2)(g^{ac}g^{bd}-g^{ad}g^{bc})$
gives the Wald functional and its area coefficient,
\begin{equation}
 S_W=4\pi\int_{\partial B}F\,dA,\qquad
 \eta(X)=4\pi F(X).
 \label{eq:source-particle-wald-coefficient}
\end{equation}
If $F_0=1/(16\pi G_0)$, then
$\eta=1/(4G_0)-4\pi\xi X$. Positivity of $F$ is a domain condition;
a varying Higgs norm does not give a constant area coefficient.
The Noether-charge functional in
\eqref{eq:source-particle-wald-coefficient} is not, by definition, the
entropy of the finite source state.

\begin{proposition}[A controlled regional first variation]
\label{prop:source-particle-generalized-volume}
Vary about flat spacetime and constant $F=F_*>0$, with variation parameter
$s$ and $f=\partial_sF|_0$. On the slice orthogonal to a unit timelike
vector $u$, use radial normal coordinates and spatial metric tangent
$h_{ij}=\partial_sg_{ij}|_0$, with $h(0)=\partial h(0)=0$ and
$h_{ij}(x)x^j=0$. Define
\[
 W=\int_B\frac{3F-F_*}{2F_*}\,dV,\qquad
 C_\ell=\frac{4\pi\ell^4}{15}.
\]
If fourth directional derivatives of $\operatorname{tr}h$ and $f$ on
$B_\ell$ are bounded by $M_h,M_f$ for every Euclidean unit direction, then
\begin{align}
 \delta S_W-\frac{8\pi F_*}{\ell}\delta W
   &=-2\pi C_\ell(2F_*\delta G_{uu}-2\Delta_3 f)+\mathcal R_\ell,
       \label{eq:source-particle-ball-identity}\\
 |\mathcal R_\ell|
   &\leq\pi^2\ell^6\left(\frac37F_*M_h+\frac{20}{21}M_f\right).
       \label{eq:source-particle-ball-error}
\end{align}
All variations are first derivatives at $s=0$; curvature and Laplacian
values on the right are evaluated at the ball centre.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The curvature derivative contracts with the slice normal and spatial
projector to $3F/2$, with reference coefficient $F_*/2$; its
Noether generalized volume is therefore $W$ above.
Also $\delta W=\delta V+3\int_Bf/(2F_*)$ and
$\delta S_W=4\pi(F_*\delta A+\int_{\partial B}f)$.
Spherical moments give the constant and quadratic scalar terms
$4\pi\ell^2f(0)+(2\pi/3)\ell^4\Delta_3f$ on the boundary and
$(4\pi/3)\ell^3f(0)+(2\pi/15)\ell^5\Delta_3f$ in the ball.
The constant term cancels. The identity
$\Delta_3\operatorname{tr}h(0)=-(4/3)\delta G_{uu}(0)$ gives the metric term.
Integrating the Taylor remainders $M|x|^4/24$ yields
\eqref{eq:source-particle-ball-error}.
\end{proof}

The scalar variation is constrained by the Higgs coupling. For $\xi\ne0$
and reference $H=(0,w)$ with real $w\ne0$, the path
$H_s=(0,w-sf/(2\xi w))$ realizes $\delta F=f$; for $\xi=0$ it requires $f=0$.
Ordinary fixed proper volume instead leaves
$16\pi^2\ell^2f(0)-2\pi C_\ell(2F_*\delta G_{uu}-5\Delta_3f)
+O(\ell^6)$. Even $f(0)=0$ does not remove the coefficient mismatch.
Fixed generalized volume is a distinct admissibility premise.
The Wald construction and generalized volume follow their
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9307038}{Noether-charge}
and \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.04374}{regional first-law}
formulations; the exact action, tensor and moment checks are in the
repository~\cite{ophrepo}.

For a geometric inference, the same state and action must supply
$\delta S_{\rm bulk}=2\pi C_\ell\delta T_{{\rm min},uu}+e_{\rm bulk}$
and fixed-$W$ stationarity with error $e_{\rm stat}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 |\delta(\mathcal E_{uu}-T_{{\rm min},uu})|
 \leq\frac{|e_{\rm bulk}|+|e_{\rm stat}|+|\mathcal R_\ell|}
               {2\pi C_\ell}.
 \label{eq:source-particle-entropy-residual}
\end{equation}
Ten timelike probes spanning the symmetric tensors determine the full
residual, provided they use common metric, stress, state and clock data.
Nonminimal boundary counterterms must be assigned consistently between
bulk entropy and $S_W$. With $f=0$ this is the constant-coefficient
linearized Einstein normalization; finite nonlinear regional deficits
and errors $o(\ell^4)$ are required for a nonlinear inference. A common
isotropic vacuum term becomes a spacetime constant only with constant
gravitational coupling and the matched total Ward and Bianchi identities.

An interacting continuum join must transport
one source's field algebra, currents, stress and state into the regional
