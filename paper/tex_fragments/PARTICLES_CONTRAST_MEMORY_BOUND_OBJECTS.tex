\section{The bound objects of the executable model}
\label{sec:cms-objects}

The executable model of the theory runs candidate repair and memory laws on one fixed
carrier and is specified with its proofs in the executable-model paper \cite{ophmodel}.
Its main candidate law, Interval Echo, takes the seam interaction of the reference
candidate, the contrast-memory law A-contrast-memory-spherical-v1, at coupling $\kappa=8$
as its elementary interaction and adds a clock that a finite observer patch carries; the
scalar channel is the second candidate law. A travelling patch keeps a hand, an elapsed
count and the number of its own repair opportunities since its last verified handoff. At
reunion with a home patch the ratio of the two elapsed counts matches the relativistic
proper-time forecast that the observers compute from their own light-pulse and arrival
records, a slowing of $3.2$ to $4.4$ percent, within about one part in a thousand on the
tested journeys. The closing filter of the clock is calibrated on the reunion ratio of a
supplied reciprocal relay journey \cite{ophmodel}. This section reads the bound objects of
the contrast-memory seam interaction as particle candidates, and Interval Echo inherits
them together with the binding theorem. The section states what they are, which
observable about them the model reproduces, how their response compares with the
complete-response premise of Section~\ref{sec:finite-supply}, and how they could meet the
class map of Section~\ref{sec:defect-particle-classes}. Statements about the
specification, the binding theorem, the emitter and the reader are proved in
\cite{ophmodel} and are quoted here.

\subsection{What a test of the model is}
\label{sec:cms-test}

A test of the model compares distributions of admissible observer records with what
observers in our world record. The state of the simulation does not contain the physical
Universe or its spacetime. Ports, seams, cells and federations are hardware of the
computation; they form no physical space, and no test asks that the carrier be a
three-dimensional lattice. Physical space, time, fields and particles are effective
descriptions that the observers inside the computation use for the records they agree
on. A difference between microscopic state encodings is therefore no failure of the
model, and a resemblance between them is no success.

The bridge is proved in the theory. Observers who inhabit the simulation can describe
their experiences as relativistic $3+1$-dimensional continuum spacetimes: for every
finite admissible menu of observer readouts, a smooth $(1+3)$-dimensional Lorentzian
field theory reproduces their complete predictions within any tolerance, on the premises
of the proposition on smooth
effective field descriptions of the flagship \cite{flagship}, and the spacetime paper
gives the rank-three spatial carrier and the causal and count limits \cite{ophEinstein}.
What the executable model has to supply is the premises of those theorems, with its own
local clocks and records. It does not have to rebuild three dimensions inside its state.
The executable-model paper lists the finite theorems whose premises its outputs supply as
they stand \cite{ophmodel}.
Every object below is read on seams and faces by observers through their ports; none of
them sits or moves in space.

State vectors, levels of the law, the evolution parameter and positions in a sequence of
events are tools of the calculation. Statements that use them are marked as audit. No
common clock is assumed: each port counts the events of its own seams, and that count is
a clock of the observer that owns the port.

\subsection{Carrier, loads and the law}

A cell is one twelve-port icosahedral carrier, the echosahedron, with $12$ ports, $30$
seams and $20$ faces \cite{ophmicro}; one cell is one observer, and its twelve ports are
its interfaces. A federation joins complete cells by external
seams that form a partial matching on the ports; in the spherical federations every
cell has three external ports, so a port has five or six seams. The federations used
below have $20$, $80$ and $320$ cells. Every port holds a load $n_v\in\{0,1,2\}$, and
every seam has a private memory of eleven states, coherent working memory held by the
owners of its two ports, which is no public record. On every seam the owners write the
native seam event into the memory, apply a phase that depends on the recorded contrast
of the two loads and reverse the write. On blank memory this echo acts on the loads
exactly as
\begin{equation}
 H_\kappa=\frac12\sum_e\bigl(I-\mathrm{SWAP}_e\bigr)+\kappa C,\qquad
 C=6\sum_v n_v^2-2\sum_{e=\{a,b\}}n_an_b,
 \label{eq:cms-law}
\end{equation}
for every real $\kappa$, and it keeps a blank memory blank \cite{ophmodel}. The coupling
$\kappa$ is one dimensionless number, the agreement energy in units of the exchange. A
seam whose two ports both hold a load is an agreed seam and lowers $C$ by $2$. The law
conserves the number of loads and their multiset. On loads $0$ and $1$ the seam term is
the ferromagnetic XXZ interaction with anisotropy $1+2\kappa$, and the binding below is
of the droplet kind known for that interaction
\cite{nachtergaele2001,fischbacher2018}. The model claims no interaction of its own;
its content is the realization of the law by a private memory, the constants on this
carrier with loads up to two, and the tests on agreed records.

\subsection{The pair on a seam}

A pair is two loads on the two ends of one seam (Figure~\ref{fig:cms-cell}a). A single
load persists by conservation of the number of loads, so the pair is the smallest object
whose persistence is an outcome of the law. Let $P$ project on the span of the pairs on a
seam in the sector of two loads and $Q=I-P$, which contains every separated pair and
every doubled load. On every federation of complete cells with a matching of external
seams, the binding theorem of \cite{ophmodel} gives
\[
 PH_\kappa P\le(10\kappa+6)P,\qquad QH_\kappa Q\ge12\kappa\,Q,\qquad
 \|QH_\kappa P\|\le\sqrt{10},
\]
and for $\kappa>3$, for every state that starts in $P$ and every value of the evolution
parameter,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\text{no pair on a seam})\le\frac{40}{(2\kappa-6)^2}.
 \label{eq:cms-binding}
\end{equation}
The bound does not depend on the number of cells. The proof combines ordered-block
min-max with the Sylvester equation for ordered spectra. With the constants measured on
$20$, $80$ and $320$ cells, the largest level of the adjacent block of the exchange
$5.70$ and the coupling $2.08$, the bound becomes $17.3/(2\kappa-5.70)^2$.

\paragraph{Emitter and reader.}
A local emitter with one fuel state at a seam writes the pair with a stated error in
norm; at $\kappa=128$ and emitter coupling $64$ the probability of no pair, a doubled load
or fuel is at most $64009/25600000\approx0.0025004$ at every reading on every seam of the spherical family. Every
seam has two ends, and at each end the observer that owns the port copies the predicate
``both ends hold one load'' into a private pointer; a courier carries one copy, and a
record is admitted only when both copies are valid and agree. The complete outcome set is one record per seam and one
outcome ``no pair''; fuel left in the emitter counts as ``no pair''. The seam memory stays
private and unread, the seam records are committed at the reading, and each reading uses
a fresh run, so reading never holds the object in place \cite{ophmodel}.

\paragraph{The face object.}
Three loads on the three ports of one face form the face object
(Figure~\ref{fig:cms-cell}b); its record is the conjunction of the agreed records of the
three seams of the face. Started on one face at $\kappa=8$, the outcome ``no agreed face''

\paragraph{Local events without a common clock.}
One event is one seam acting once,
$\exp\bigl(-i\tau\bigl[\tfrac12(I-\mathrm{SWAP}_e)-2\kappa n_an_b\bigr]\bigr)$. Each port
counts the events of its own seams, and that count is a clock of the observer that owns
it. Two programs in which every port sees its events in the same order give the same seam
records exactly, so no order between events on seams that share no port enters a record. At
$\kappa=8$ and event angle $1/64$, rounds in which every seam acts once in a fresh random
order keep the pair, with largest probability of ``no pair'' $0.0217$ on one cell and
$0.0188$ on $20$ cells; rounds in which half of the carrier acts twice give $0.0470$ and
$0.0268$, while the counts at different ports differ by a factor of up to $2.6$. Seams that
act independently at random lose the pair at these angles, and on one cell the
loss falls with the angle: $0.505$, $0.184$ and $0.085$ at $1/64$, $1/256$ and $1/1024$.

is not held ($0.015$, $0.11$ and $0.32$ after $64$, $512$ and $2048$ rounds, against $0.010$
to $0.018$ unread). The binding rests on the regular phase between successive events of a
seam, and an observer that serves each of its seams once per round supplies that
regularity with its own count.

\subsection{The port response and the complete-response premise}
\label{sec:cms-response}

The gauge theorem of Section~\ref{sec:finite-supply} consumes twelve port generators that
are independent, closed under commutators, with a positive trace form, and with the
rotations of the cell acting through them \cite{ophGauge}. On one cell under the
contrast-memory seam interaction, which Interval Echo shares, the response is computed on
the two triplets that are odd under the antipodal map: for one load the slow level
$\mathbf3$ and the fast level $\mathbf3'$ of the graph Laplacian, for the bound pair its
two lowest odd triplets. A port service is a weak operation at one port by the observer
that owns it, lasting long against the inverse level spacings of the law. Directions are counted modulo the common phase, which changes no
record probability. These statements are derivation and audit on the operators of the
law; no observer reads the matrices.

The plain services of the law, the load of each port, the exchange of each seam and the
twist of each port, give seventeen generators for one load and for the bound pair at
$\kappa=8$: two copies of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, one on each triplet, and one common number.
The two triplets are locked. Every port operator with rational entries acts on the fast
triplet as the conjugate under $\sqrt5\mapsto-\sqrt5$ of its action on the slow triplet,
so a symmetric part that is no number on one triplet is no number on the other. One
declared service, the load weighted by the energy below the top one-load level together
with the twist,
\[
 F_p=\tfrac12\bigl\{n_p,\,E_{\rm top}-\tfrac12L\bigr\}+J_p,\qquad
 E_{\rm top}=\tfrac12\bigl(5+\sqrt5\bigr),
\]
with $L$ the graph Laplacian of the cell and $J_p$ the circulation round the five
neighbours of $p$, gives twelve with every
check of the premise: $\mathfrak u(3)$ on the slow triplet and $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ on the
fast one, the Lie type \eqref{eq:finite-gauge-type}; a positive trace form with
eigenvalues between $0.5$ and $7.24$; all $66$ brackets inside the span with residual
$9\times10^{-15}$; and the $720$ covariance identities for the $60$ rotations with
residual $2\times10^{-15}$. The weighting and the twist are supplied, and the law's own
events provide neither. On the bound pair, twelve needs the weighting from the pair's own
$\mathbf3'$ level, that is knowledge of the target level; the contrast-memory construction
exhibits no service that gives twelve on the composite without it.

Interval Echo selects its port services with the rotations of the cell \cite{ophmodel}.
Let $P$ be the positive spectral projector of the sum of the permutation operators of the
twelve proper rotations that turn the five neighbours of a port by one edge. The service
at port $p$ is the band-diagonal part of $Pn_pP+J_p$ at unit gain, on one load and, lifted
to the sector of two unit loads, on the bound pair at $\kappa=8$, whose retained triplets
are the levels of the prepared source in which both loads occupy one seam with
probability above one half. On
each sector the twelve services are independent and close under all $66$ commutators, and
the $720$ port-rotation conjugation checks pass with residuals below $10^{-11}$. A signed
pulse at one port changes the distribution of the bound pair's seam readings by up to
$0.11$ in total variation. On $20$ and $80$ cells, prepared overlap transport generates
all $60$ proper rechartings with a matching unit-gain current implementer. The ring
orientation, the preparations, the protected spectral service and the unit gain are
supplied. Completeness of the physical control menu is a separate premise: with the bare
load of one port added to the rotation-selected services, the visible closure has
seventeen directions.

Two couplings have exact features, and no selection of the coupling is claimed from
them. At $\kappa=1$ the triplet levels of the pair are $2-\sqrt5$, $(7-\sqrt5)/2$,
$2+\sqrt5$ and $(7+\sqrt5)/2$, counted from $12\kappa$; on the second and the fourth the
load of every port is exactly $1/6$ and the twist vanishes. At $\kappa=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ a pair
on a seam costs exactly what two free loads in the slow level cost, $5-\sqrt5$ above
$12\kappa$.

\subsection{A proposed reading of classes}
\label{sec:cms-class-reading}

The law has one conserved number, the number of loads, and loads add when objects merge.
If one load is given class $1$, an object of $N$ loads has class
$N\bmod 6$: the pair class $2$, the face object class $3$, two faces class $4$, and the cap
of six loads class $0$. This is a proposed reading. It was formed with the table of
objects in view, no run of the model tests it, and the law holds no record of a class.
Three computations would test it: the link register of the class theory with $q=6$ joined
to the contrast-memory law, so that six loads and none carry the same class in the
records; the reaction table of the register of
Table~\ref{tab:defect-reactions} against the merging of objects; and the count of states
per class against the multiplicities $(4,6,6,4,6,6)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:defect-particle-classes}. Each is a comparison of record distributions in
the sense of Section~\ref{sec:cms-test}.

\subsection{A second object on a second candidate law}
\label{sec:cms-cage}

The same carrier holds the second candidate law, the scalar channel, with one mode on every
seam and on every pair of seams at a port \cite{ophmodel}. Its frequencies obey
$\omega^2=m^2+\lambda$ with $\lambda$ a level of the graph of seams, and its reference state
has every negative level filled, so that one object more and one object fewer are its two
signs. On a ring of four seams with alternating signs the law holds an object and couples
it to nothing else. The excitation of this cage is kept for every interval and every
start, a local detector with four analyzers reads it from counts at five waits, and a
changed law with the same alphabet, routing and symmetry loses it: particle stability for
a second object. After a finite pulse the seam record of the cage swings with no drive at
one line, which an observer reads at $4.8990$ of its own reference and finds equal to the
level difference of the two modes read separately: a prepared clock. The pulse supplies
the phase and the work, the level of a cage is flat, and the law holds no term between two
objects. The scalar channel and the contrast-memory seam interaction act on different
state spaces, and their results are not added up.

\subsection{Supplied parts and limits}
\label{sec:cms-scope}

\paragraph{Supplied.}
The following parts are design choices of the construction: the coupling $\kappa$, with
computed values $0$, $0.5$, $1$, $2$, $4$, $8$, $16$, $64$ and $128$; the coherent generator
on the private seam memory; the reference registers of the degree term on ports with five
seams; the emitter with its fuel, coupling and duration; the evolution parameter of the
runs with a continuous parameter, while as local events the pair needs each seam to act
once per round or the limit of small event angle; the tangent-frame routing of the
federation; and the weighted port service of Section~\ref{sec:cms-response}. In Interval
Echo the guide, custody transfers, gate selection, relative cadence and read windows of
the clock journeys are supplied services, the hand size, register widths and walk budget
of the clock are designed, and the settings of the rotation-selected port service are
supplied.

\paragraph{Limits.}
The contrast-memory construction does not supply a selection of $\kappa$; a second sign of
load, since the interaction has no antiparticle and no annihilation; a record of currents
that later events read, that is a
seam connection with its own energy; the class of an object as a record; the twelve port
currents on the bound object from the law's own services; one limiting speed for all
objects, since the bound pair is a Galilean composite whose branch on a chain is
$E(K)=\Delta-\cos^2(K/2)/\Delta$ with $\Delta=1+2\kappa$; one dictionary for the rest
energy and the inertia of free clusters, whose kinetic ratios at $\kappa=8$ differ from
their rest-energy ratios; a refinement map that preserves
seams; an observer clock normalized to proper time; and the regional response that the
Einstein theorem consumes \cite{ophEinstein}. Interval Echo adds to this interaction an
observer-carried clock whose reunion readings follow the proper-time forecast on its
tested journeys, and the rotation-selected port currents on the bound pair. Clock
comparison on held clock constructions and directions, one common measurement of current
response and stress on one bound excitation, and the identification of physical particle
species lie outside its results \cite{ophmodel}. These limits are measured against the test of
Section~\ref{sec:cms-test}. None of them asks the model to contain three dimensions in its
state.
