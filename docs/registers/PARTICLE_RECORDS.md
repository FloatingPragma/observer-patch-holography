# Which particle can two observers identify?

Two preparations belong to the same observable class for a specified set of experiments if every calibrated sequence of measurements gives the same joint outcome probabilities, including refusals. Finite count banks test that equivalence to their stated resolution. An additional detector can split a class whose earlier records agreed.

A particle assignment starts with the source setting, the receiver's clicks, the outcomes when the source is off, and the intervening material and clock. A second observer calibrates their own instruments, then compares outcome distributions for the same physical preparations. The catalogue below gives the measurements available in the charged and neutral lepton search. The [full particle inventory](../../model/evaluation/SCORECARD.md#standard-model-particle-identifications) lists the remaining families and the distinct observations each requires.

Of the accepted caps, 611 displayed two disjoint triangular cores. The axial material quadrupole vanishes about the cell centre and changes when measured about the centre of the occupied sites. Assigning the readings to a deuteron requires independently identified proton and neutron sources and an electric quadrupole detector. ## Comparing two descriptions of one experiment

If Alice describes the source by ρ, the route by U and the receiver by D, Bob's probability is tr(D UρU†). If the two endpoints independently change coordinates by A and B, these three objects become AρA†, BUA† and BDB†. The trace is unchanged. This requires matching the physical detector axes. Keeping an old component number while rotating the instrument changes the question it asks. A change of family coordinates likewise transports the family energy projectors. A gauge transformation alone cannot turn one rest energy into a different one. A receiver in the five-mode exterior construction can register three different responses from an electrically neutral source. A negative charged daughter marks one weak direction, a positive charged daughter marks its conjugate, and silence in both directions marks a third class. Calibration with charged standards sets the efficiency separately for each sign. A measured silence inherits that efficiency and the finite sample size. Naming the daughter electron, muon or tau requires an independent identification of that charged particle.

Even complete precision on these total currents has a limitation. In the declared electroweak construction one can prepare an oppositely charged pair from a weak doublet and a weak singlet whose *entire* unresolved gauge response equals that of the direct lepton doublet. The equivalence is exact for currents transported to one frame and survives a common motion of the centre. Accessing the electric charge of one constituent can split the two descriptions. This example gives an explicit reason to keep the table indexed by the measurements actually performed. For a mixture, even identical overall rates of charged daughters and of resolved constituents can conceal different preparations. Recording the daughter sign and the constituent meter on the same arrival distinguishes the examples in the certificate. An independent arrival count is needed to measure the population that reached the detector and stayed dark. Whether the native moving pair realizes those currents is a separate experimental question.
