This register records the canonical PR-numbered premises, with their types, intended dispositions, consuming lanes, statements, and evidence paths. It is not an exhaustive list of the hypotheses of every result. Result-specific assumptions and imported results are declared in the [claim registry](../../claims/claim_registry.yaml), with named assumptions defined in the [assumption dictionary](../../claims/assumption_dictionary.md). Claim-registry validation rejects unknown premise references, missing per-claim premise classifications, and undefined assumption names. An explicit non-consumer of these canonical rows can require its own result-specific assumptions. The full input budget must be read from each result and its dependencies, not from this register alone. Dispositions such as `axiomatize` and `remove` describe intended treatment; they do not certify a completed adoption into the specification or a completed removal. P and N are the only proposed fundamental free numerical parameters; measured calibrations, fits, targets, and transport payloads remain separate flagged empirical inputs and must be included in the input budget of the results that use them. Registering a premise legitimizes its use inside conditional compositions; it does not make the premise true, derived, or physical. Disposition changes, including adoption into the specification, require an explicit scientific rationale, never a silent edit.

Each evidence path has one explicit primary role: `statement` locates the premise interface, `conditional_consumer` uses that premise in a conditional result, `no_go` proves a source-selection or derivation obstruction, and `external_input` identifies imported mathematics or data. These roles describe evidential function only; they create no implicit reverse-consumer edge or lane ownership.

Retired ids keep their number and are never reused: PR-83 (commit-populated event base), retired when the finite causal carrier was derived from authenticated source order (PR-81, PR-82).

The current-implementation column says what the executable construction in `model/` does with each premise: derives it from native records, constructs an instance on declared inputs, proves an obstruction, takes it as a declared input, or does not address it. It certifies no physical premise.

## What the dispositions mean

### remove

A remove row is consumed as a declared premise until the executable specification supplies it and observers recover that supply from their records. The row leaves the register only through such a supply on the same domain; every composition citing it is conditional on it, and any bounded no-go attached to the row records exactly what the specification fails to supply.

### axiomatize

An axiomatize row is a candidate for adoption into the executable specification. Adoption is a recorded register decision with rationale and a specification-wide dependency and countermodel review, never a silent edit; the row keeps its source-selection no-gos as the evidence of what the specification does not supply, and an adopted row stays on the register with its decision record.

### import

An import row is a flagged external input that stays on the register: classical external mathematics, pinned empirical data packets, and the two numerical inputs P and N. An import is never presented as a derivation; replacing an import with a source-side result is a recorded disposition change.

Register rows keep the source-selection no-gos proved against them as permanent evidence of what the executable specification does not supply.
