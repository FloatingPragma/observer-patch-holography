# Observer spacetime and predictions without a laboratory

## Primary objective: identify the observed Universe

The objective is to show that the observations of an OPH world admit the
spacetime and physical descriptions measured in our Universe. A carrier is
a mathematical component of the model; constructing a physical carrier or
an emulator is **not a prerequisite**. A paired native-event/laboratory-clock
experiment is one optional way to test an implementation. It is not the only
way to identify emergent physical time, and observing microscopic native
repair events in nature is not required by the phenomenological route.

The working hypothesis is that our measured records have an effective
description in the OPH observer statistics. Derive its consequences and
compare them with existing measurements. Assuming this correspondence is
useful for developing a candidate; assuming that the comparisons succeed
would not establish it. The
[construction target](../../model/CONSTRUCTION.md#completion-target-and-acceptance-criteria)
already concerns effective observations, not a fundamental manifold or
fundamental fields.

### What can be attached to the simulated output

Use the model's accessible records, rather than host execution time:

- **Time:** one observer's recorded event count supplies an internal clock.
  Its comparisons with other clocks are stochastic laws, with finite
  uncertainty, rather than a universal deterministic conversion.
  Choosing an event-count unit needs no independent timestamp calibration.
- **Space:** a declared current-response protocol recovers a dimensionless
  spatial Gram matrix. For that readout, squared distances are
  `d(i,j)^2 = G[i,i] + G[j,j] - 2 G[i,j]`. A factorization of the rank-three
  slow-band Gram supplies three plotting coordinates up to orthogonal frame
  choice. Selecting that band remains part of the interpretation; the full
  resistance readout has rank eleven.
- **Their joint content:** the existing
  [twelve-clock comparison](../../model/native_clock/COMPARISON.md) uses one
  continuing total-two history. Local movement counts have a common
  asymptotic rate, and their cross-covariances distinguish all three native
  distance shells. No independent oscillator, laboratory timestamp or
  federation enters this result. Finite-sample inference of those covariance
  classes still needs its own uncertainty analysis.

The [visible incident-clock construction](../../model/laboratory_clock/README.md#geometry-and-fields-from-visible-incident-clocks)
uses the ordered union of two local tapes without a hidden attempt or movement
counter. Between A ticks, B's count has mean one and variance `8/5` for adjacent
ports or `2` otherwise. All 66 pair laws recover the source graph and its
rank-three Gram; a fixed zero-count classifier has an explicit finite-sample
error bound. With the stationary total-two preparation, that same geometry and
native repair predict the joint local occupation probabilities after two A
ticks: `14/407`, `7/407` and `58/3663` in the three distance classes. Identity
repair preserves every clock law but fails these field predictions. This is a
joint dimensionless correspondence within one source. Identifying the same
records in measured data is a hypothesis to test, rather than a prerequisite
for defining the clocks or deriving their predictions.

Uniform scheduling can be relaxed within the IID class. The
[weighted reconstruction](../../model/relative_observer_geometry/README.md)
uses all oriented pair means and terminal coincidences to determine the
positive seam probabilities. Normalization follows because each source event
ticks exactly two endpoint clocks. The recovered weights predict the local
field law, while a matched cyclic control shows that those pair statistics
do not establish temporal independence. No hidden attempt counter enters
this inference.

The [collective regional construction](../../model/laboratory_clock/README.md#collective-clocks-on-the-same-carrier)
counts the union of each region's visible events once. Under uniform IID
scheduling it derives the complete count law for every region pair, with
normalized variance at least `1/(30N)` for distinct event sets and `2/(29N)`
at equal mean rates. Regions exposing identical events define one clock.
The [public comparison](../../model/predictions/README.md#collective-regional-optical-attachment)
tests all distinct pairs under an explicit direct optical readout hypothesis,
including its count normalization, bounded error and selection cost. It rejects
that conjunction; it does not derive the published atomic/servo apparatus or
exclude every collective clock. Collective tape access is stronger than an
isolated primitive port's tape.

The [active modular clock](../../model/transfer_modular_probe/README.md)
also makes each native incident event trigger one fixed coherent phase step.
Closed histories provide its faithful native-derived reference. The coherent
probe and carrier-pause scheduler are declared apparatus; passive repair has
a modular-automorphism obstruction. Mapping two measured optical transitions
directly to its three-mode energy gaps fails the public absolute-ratio test
under the [specified common-scale attachment](../../model/predictions/README.md#minimal-modular-transition-optical-attachment).

The separate [faithful encoded-mode clock](../../model/observer_time_fields/REFINEMENT.md#faithful-state-clock-compatibility)
has a joint state/response test: matching polarization axes and
`log((1+r)/(1-r))=b*Omega` with common `b` relate noise, response and modular
rate. This tests a declared preparation on the full encoded algebra.
A spatial-patch modular flow and an atomic apparatus correspondence require
additional observation maps; assigning seconds alone does not supply them.

Thus records can already carry an observer-relative clock and a finite
spatial interpretation. An event labelled by a local count and a spatial
coordinate is an internal chart. The individual clocks' mean rate equality
does not establish simultaneity, message delivery, a common metric tensor,
physical bulk dimension or Lorentz transformations. In particular, the
twelve points in a three-coordinate embedding are not an established
three-dimensional spatial continuum. A holographic bulk interpretation, if
used, needs a map to bulk observables rather than equating ports with bulk
locations.

The [native warm-up theorem](../../model/record_laws/README.md#preparation-by-finite-native-warm-up)

unavailable to the subsequent protocol. A common complete continuation then
inherits that error; for full-carrier input it must retain domain failures.
This does not prepare coherent probes or source apparatus.

Keep one complete native instrument, sector, continuing-state preparation,
observer access contract and scheduler fixed. Start with overlapping regions
inside one carrier. Derive a **joint local spacetime correspondence** for
clock readings, field correlations and intervention responses:

1. Specify the spatial readout and each observer's clock from accessible
   records. Retain joint outcomes, hidden-event evolution, memory, failures
   and the observer's finite record resources. Do not combine results from
   different sectors as though they were one experiment.
2. Derive the same-record field response from the repair law. Determine
   whether an admissible coherent observable has propagating modes and
   compatible signal/clock relations in the claimed regime. The existing
   C/E occupation response has contracting real modes; multiplying its time
   parameter by a positive constant cannot turn those modes into oscillatory
   waves. This is a restriction on that readout, not a no-go theorem for
   every coherent, relational or effective OPH description.
3. Derive an effective action for those very observations and clocks. Check
   its predicted correlations and intervention responses against the native
   joint law. A supplied wave equation is a comparison target. A Euclidean
   transfer reconstruction or unitary dilation requires an observable
   correspondence before its evolution parameter can be identified with the
   observers' duration. The phase-dependent repair family supplies further
   candidates, but the clock statistics alone do not select its phase law.
4. Test compatibility across overlapping observer descriptions: the same
   physical event and intervention must have agreeing predicted records in
   their shared domain. Seek the appropriate local Lorentzian relations,
   signal propagation and clock comparison, with explicit error bounds and
   a declared range of scales. A metric assigned after seeing every output
   is insufficient. Failure rejects the proposed correspondence or candidate
   law in that domain, rather than requiring an artificial clock fit.

One carrier is enough to test this local proposal and expose contradictions.
An extended 3+1-dimensional effective description additionally requires a
compatible family over larger domains and controlled resolution errors.
That may be established by a theorem on an appropriate family; it does not
require the current spherical federation architecture or a numerical
simulation of the whole Universe. A twelve-port calculation alone cannot
establish that extension. Interacting matter and responsive geometry must
ultimately obey the same correspondence; they remain part of the completion
target rather than being discharged by a kinematic chart.

Formally, fix a map from each admitted preparation/intervention protocol to
an effective physical protocol. Require the complete outcome law predicted
by OPH to agree, within declared errors, with the effective law for all
protocols in the claimed domain. Use instrument-level bounds when quantum
reference outputs are part of that domain. Different incompatible
measurement settings need no common classical table of predetermined
outcomes. Coordinate, clock and field maps must serve the same family of
tests, rather than being chosen independently for each plotted curve.

### Connecting to existing observations and units

An internal clock can first use arbitrary units. Once a derived observable
is independently identified with a measured physical process, one measured
frequency or duration can fix the corresponding time scale. Additional
dimensionless clock ratios, spectra, propagation laws and intervention
responses then test the identification without a new time fit for each
observable. State all remaining dimensional scales and fitted dimensionless
parameters; choosing units does not eliminate those physical freedoms.

In SI, the [second](https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/second) is defined
through a caesium transition, and the
[metre](https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/metre) through the speed of light
and the second. OPH need not predict a different numerical value for those
unit-defining constants. It must account for the identified clock and signal
processes and their dimensionless relations. The present model has not
identified a caesium transition or electromagnetic light merely by naming
a load change a tick.

Use published measurements once the corresponding model observable exists.
First recover a constrained measured regime, such as coherent propagation
and clock comparisons; then test additional observations that were not used
to choose the interpretation or fit its parameters. Existing data can reject
or support this route without any new laboratory. Reproducing already known
relations is a necessary retrospective check; discriminating predictions
against viable alternatives add evidence. No finite collection of matches
proves a unique microscopic ontology among empirically equivalent models.

The immediate priority is therefore the joint observer/action/spacetime
correspondence above. The optional apparatus protocol and illustrative
millisecond predictions below do not replace it or block it.

## What would need to be set up

One carrier is enough. Keep three operations distinct:

1. **Identify the instrument.** Specify the load encoding, actual selective
   maps and records, preparations/resets, allowed coherent controls and
   detector errors. For an engineered emulator, these are implemented
   operations. For a proposed naturally occurring realization, they need an
   independently justified physical mapping. A detection click is not
   automatically a native attempt or a moving current.
2. **Calibrate timing separately.** Record attempt boundaries, including
   zero-current attempts, against an independent reference counter. Retain
   missed/extra-event and timestamp uncertainty. Use a fixed calibration
   subset, then hold out later endpoint readings. The existing
   [clock attachment](../../model/laboratory_clock/README.md) performs the
   bounded-error affine test. Constant duration between every attempt is an
   additional hypothesis; a few endpoint readings alone do not establish it.
   If preparation, coherent control and readout use source pulses, their
   durations need separate calibration or explicit latency bounds. A repair
   attempt and a source-pulse invocation are not the same clock resource.
3. **Predict unused observations.** Freeze the preparation, schedule,
   spatial dictionary, instrument candidate and decision rule before the
   validation trials. Predict complete arrival/readout laws, including
   timeouts and aborts. Do not estimate the clock rate from the arrival
   measurements it will subsequently be used to predict.

For a narrow classical passage demonstration, twelve occupation bits and
an independent clock can emulate the native binary dynamics. Testing the
coherent C/E distinction additionally needs a quantum encoding, record
ancillas and coherent preparation/readout. A small restricted encoding can
demonstrate that distinction without realizing all 78 source configurations.
Neither demonstration requires a federation.

The minimum retained event table contains trial ID, preparation and reset
status, instrument version, attempt number, addressed seam, coin/current
records including zeros, incident-clock count, independent timestamp and its
uncertainty, intervention/readout setting, outcome, and any abort or timeout.
The receiver's prediction uses only its declared local records and known
preparation; an audit log does not grant it hidden configuration access.

An emulator programmed to implement C is expected to reproduce C. That would
test implementation and timing. An OPH-specific physical test additionally
requires an independently constrained mapping to the physical system, with
predictions that were not installed as its programmed dynamics.

## Concrete expected passage results

Assume the unchanged native rule, independent uniform selection among the
thirty seams, fair placement coins, one initially loaded sender, and an empty
receiver at port zero. Assume a constant `tau=1 ms` per native attempt and a
known launch at an attempt boundary. This time scale is illustrative and is
neither derived from repair nor taken from the published experiments below.

The [exact passage calculation](../../model/physical_space_time/README.md)
then predicts:

| Sender / graph distance to receiver | Mean receiver incident ticks | Mean arrival duration | Arrival by 10 ms | Arrival in (10,30] ms | Arrival in (30,60] ms | Still waiting at 60 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 / distance 1 | 22 | 132 ms | 0.13546788 | 0.15637791 | 0.13960926 | 0.56854495 |
| 2 / distance 2 | 28 | 168 ms | 0.01936592 | 0.09288837 | 0.14983358 | 0.73791213 |
| 3 / distance 3 | 30 | 180 ms | 0.00403940 | 0.05765454 | 0.14229901 | 0.79600704 |

Each row is one complete four-bin probability law, rounded to eight decimal
places. The mean is for the uncapped arrival distribution; a 60 ms experiment
does not observe that mean directly and must retain its right-censored trials.
These three settings test the shell predictions under the declared symmetry.
Testing the entire passage metric needs all 132 ordered distinct pairs, or
additional held-out symmetry checks with an explicitly restricted claim.

If the assumed/calibrated cadence is instead `[0.99,1.01] ms`, the mean-duration
ranges are `[130.68,133.32]`, `[166.32,169.68]` and `[178.2,181.8] ms. Deadline
probabilities must also change. For a deadline `T`, let `F(n)` be the native
arrival CDF after `n` attempts. With elapsed timestamp error at most `delta`,
sound cumulative bounds are

```text
F(max(0,floor((T-delta)/tau_hi)))
    <= P(recorded arrival duration <= T)
    <= F(floor((T+delta)/tau_lo)).
```

This assumes the stated error bounds and attempt cadence throughout the
trial. An independently established complete-trial total-variation error
`epsilon` widens either probability endpoint by `epsilon`, clipped to `[0,1]`.
Unknown apparatus error stays unknown. The same `tau` applies at every
deadline; choosing a different rate for each histogram cell is not permitted.
The [clock package](../../model/laboratory_clock/README.md) also converts the
native first-current waiting law under this type of conditional calibration.

The table can be reproduced with the existing model, without new data:

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F
from model.physical_space_time.construction import kernel, arrival_law

P = kernel(0)["attempt"]
for sender in (1, 2, 3):
    law = arrival_law(P, sender, 0, 60)
    p = list(map(F, law["first_arrival_probabilities"]))
    bins = [sum(p[:10]), sum(p[10:30]), sum(p[30:60]),
            F(law["timeout_probability"])]
    assert sum(bins) == 1
    print(sender, [f"{float(value):.8f}" for value in bins])
```

It retains per-shot source/drain trajectories, coin outcomes and circuit

one-second independent samples or a timing-accuracy certificate. No native
observable has been independently identified with these transitions, so no
OPH fit or physical prediction score is assigned to these traces.

The [2021 BACON clock-network paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03253-4)
provides another measured ratio comparison, but its raw-data availability
statement requires a request to the authors. It should not be described as
an immediately downloadable event-record dataset.

The first two provide a practical route for work with existing data. Begin
by reproducing the publication's original observable, with its own units,
selection rules and detector model. Then derive a source-to-apparatus map
for the particular comparison being attempted. Fit any allowed clock or
detector parameters on a declared subset and test unused records or
observables, accounting for temporal dependence. This is a retrospective
benchmark when the published aggregate outcomes informed the design; it
must not be relabelled as a new prospective experiment.

If no faithful mapping exists, the dataset can still assess methods and
rule out an attempted identification. A similar waiting-time histogram
alone does not identify the native instrument. Existing ordinary quantum
models remain essential comparators.
