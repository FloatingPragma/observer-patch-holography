# Journal of the repair-law search

One entry per step of the [plan](PLAN.md), in the order the steps ran. Each
entry states what the step measured, how it changes the standing of each class
of repair laws it tested, and what it hands to the following step. The step
reports hold the evidence; the numbers below are quoted from them.

Standing of a class uses five words. **Excluded**: every member tested
contradicts a required record, and a proof or a family argument carries the
contradiction to the whole class. **Excluded on the tested band**: the
contradiction holds for the measured preparations and wavelengths.
**Disfavoured**: a measured or derived property puts the class behind a
competitor without a contradiction. **Survives**: every required record of the
step agrees within its registered tolerance. **Undecided**: the step's records
are identical for all members, or too few records exist for a verdict.

## Step 1. Clocks and spacetime from observer records

Experiments run on 19 November 2026.
[Report](relativistic-observer-records/report.md) ·
[PDF](relativistic-observer-records/report.pdf)

**Question.** Two observers leave the same place, follow different paths and
meet again. Can a candidate law make their clocks disagree by the proper-time
difference of special relativity, without being told the speed or the
slowdown factor? And do the waves that carry those clocks have a measurable
three-dimensional spatial response with one signal speed?

### What was found

**A clock carried along is no proper-time clock.** On the golden record net,
two stations recorded the same optical tags and reconstructed each journey
before the traveller's clock was opened. A register that the rule advances
only on waits matched the proper-time model on 15 of 15 journeys, and the
stations predicted 374 of 374 withheld echoes. That register has the effect
written into its rule. An exchange oscillator carried on the finest setting
read 15.948 ticks, with a 99% interval of 14.855–17.116, where the optical
upper bound on the traveller's proper time was 9.257 ticks. All 14 journeys
with enough records showed the discrepancy. Moving the oscillator only hands
its pairs to the next owner, and its internal evolution per service is the
same in motion and at rest, so it accumulates home time.
[Report section](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#what-the-integer-clock-and-the-carried-oscillator-measured)

**A clock made from the moving wave keeps proper time at long wavelengths.**
The reflected-routing law reflects the equal-slot component of eight local
amplitudes and routes each outgoing amplitude to its neighbour's opposite
slot. Three slot pairs route among spatial addresses and one within a private
cyclic register. A packet prepared in two neighbouring private modes carries
their relative phase as a clock hand. The update has no input for the packet
speed or the relativistic factor. With the speed taken from independent
arrivals, the reference speed from a separate gapless wave and the rest rate
from a stationary doublet, all sealed before the moving counts were opened,
the five long-wave preparations matched $\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$ within the
registered tolerance of 0.035. A higher-count repeat bounded the discrepancy
below 0.0068 at three of them. The short-wave preparation missed by 0.06546.
The exact one-direction dispersion $\cos E=(2+\cos k+\cos q)/4$ explains both
results: for small $k$ and $q$ it gives $E\simeq\sqrt{k^2+q^2}/2$, which ties
speed and clock rate together relativistically, and the cosine terms separate
them at short wavelengths. The finite law forecast a fresh short-wave ratio of
0.62896 and the measurement gave 0.62904.
[Clock](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#a-clock-carried-by-the-traveling-wave) ·
[Ratios](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#how-much-the-moving-clock-slowed)

**The same law has a proved flat 3+1D field limit.** Nine stations read a
complex field amplitude from interference counts on a chart the instruments
calibrate. In a fixed field sector the equal-slot projection obeys
$\cos E=\tfrac14[\cos hK_1+\cos hK_2+\cos hK_3+\cos hQ]$, and refinement gives
the free Klein–Gordon equation with signal speed $1/2$ and rest angular
frequency $|Q|/2$, with error of order $h^2$. The three axial speed
calibrations gave 0.500106, 0.500028 and 0.499845. On 120 reserved complex
values the RMS error fell from 0.012193 at $N=16$ to 0.001105 at $N=128$.
Dropping one spatial derivative raised it to 0.31–0.32. Six massless
directions bound the smallest eigenvalue of the spatial response tensor below
by 0.19305 at 99%, so no rank-two tensor of the homogeneous quadratic model
fits the measured dials.
[Field](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#how-observers-measured-a-three-dimensional-field) ·
[Proof](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#what-the-continuum-proof-establishes) ·
[Three directions](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#three-directions-made-a-measurable-difference)

**A faster channel exists in the apparatus.** The free reference wave travels
at 0.499350 radar units per home tick. The addressed service that carries a
calibration nonce travels at one unit per tick. If inhabitants can send their
own messages through it, the scalar cone is not the causal limit of all their
signals.
[Report section](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#what-universal-for-the-inhabitants-would-mean)

**Causal counts on the golden family.** A collecting observer on the finite
three-axis realization at $q=21$ received 6,482 writes and 2,904,889 ordered
pairs. The finite dimension estimate from those counts is 3.612; the value
four comes from the limit theorem.
[Report section](relativistic-observer-records/report.md#where-the-rer-geometry-theorems-enter)

**Echo ranges on a classical relay.** Six-endpoint echo experiments on the
240-port classical one-way relay carrier ran in the same step and are outside
the report. A sixteen-return timing measurement excludes a static flat
Euclidean reading of the six call-echo ranges on its quiet preparation. The
routing pointers never act on that preparation, so the verdict holds for
every pointer assignment with the same apparatus. Classical transfer clocks
on the golden net and on several ordinary classical laws reunited on 23 of
132 primary trips, with too few outward echoes for an independent prediction.

### What the step means for the search

The relativistic clock reading came from a law in which the clock is a beat
between internal modes of the moving excitation itself, and the signal and
the clock share one update and one low-energy dispersion relation of the form
$\omega^2=\Omega^2+c^2|K|^2$. A carried counter that receives the same
services as its twin at home accumulates home time. A law has to tie the rate of its material clocks to their motion
through its own dynamics. The step gives three necessary signatures for that:
a gapless signal with linear long-wave dispersion, a rank-three spatial
response in a chart the observers calibrate, and a material rate that follows
the measured motion. The quantity $R^2+(v/c)^2$, with $R$ the returned-to-rest
beat ratio, ranks one-body candidates cheaply. On the reflected-routing law
the small-wavenumber dispersion gives exactly one, and the long-wave
measurements agree within the clock tolerance.

The reflected-routing law has open obligations. Its preparation of the
clock doublet and its coherent readout are supplied, and the spontaneous
formation of a protected clock is untested. Interacting matter, the faster
addressed service, gravitational response and cosmic expansion are outside
the step. Its chart is a declared part of the experiment, and a map to the
rank-three slow band of the twelve-port carrier would need its own
measurement.

Step 1 did not run on the two laws of
[polynomial-sv](../../../model/polynomial_sv/README.md). Its results give
them concrete tests. An object of polynomial-sv whose internal swing advances
by the same amount per service whether it moves from seam to seam or rests is
a factorized clock, and the exclusion above applies to it as a proper-time
clock. Each law needs an independently timed gapless signal to fix $c$, a
moving-clock ratio measured against a rest calibration, a field response in
three calibrated directions and a race of every channel its inhabitants can
use. The scalar channel of the model is a different construction from the
scalar field of this step.

## Step 2. A race between the addressed relay and the free wave

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.
[Report](signal-race/report.md) ·
[Timing diagram](signal-race/figures/receipt-times.svg)

**Question.** Step 1 found two transport speeds in the reflected-routing
apparatus: the free reference wave at 0.499350 radar units per home tick and
the addressed relay service that carries calibration nonces at one unit per
tick. Can an inhabitant send a freshly chosen bit through the relay to a
distant observer before a signal travelling at the smooth scalar speed could
arrive? The race completes the channel audit of stage 1 of the
[plan](PLAN.md#experiments-in-dependency-order) for this apparatus.

### What was found

**The relay outruns the scalar cone.** A sender chose each bit when its own
clock reached an agreed epoch. The receiver recovered all 512 fresh choices
before the smooth scalar arrival at both radar ranges, in both directions,
with fine and with coarse receiver clocks. At range 64 the addressed bit was
readable at tick 78 on the fine clock and tick 80 on the coarse clock. The
earliest smooth arrival, granting the limiting speed of one-half and an
instantaneous launch at the choice, is tick 128. At range 128 the bit was
readable at ticks 150 and 160 against a smooth arrival at 256. A sender
restricted to one fixed marker carried every bit by choosing when to send it.
[Report section](signal-race/report.md#what-the-observer-saw)

**The sender's own clock confirms it.** At range 64 the sender held the
distant observer's stamped reply 139 ticks after the choice, and at range 128
after 275 ticks. The smooth round trips are 256 and 512 ticks. Breaking the
return path kept every remote receipt and prevented every confirmation at
home.
[Report section](signal-race/report.md#why-the-time-comparison-is-meaningful)

**The free wave carries the messages late.** At the early deadline the wave
receiver had seen zero clicks and scored 272 of 512, a balanced accuracy of
50%, which is the score of answering zero every time. At the late deadline it
recovered 512 of 512 bits at each resolution. The first free-wave click on a
bit-one trial came at ticks 137–146 at range 64 on the fine clock. The
eight-slot reflection has a strict front of one hop per update, so the finite
law allows precursors ahead of the smooth cone. Each further consecutive
forward hop multiplies a path's probability by 1/16. With 6,016 detection
trials per bit, the probability of one early click for the short-range fine
preparation is $1.40\times10^{-37}$, and integer path arithmetic bounds it by
$5.88\times10^{-35}$ for any normalized single-excitation preparation behind
the emission edge.
[Report section](signal-race/report.md#what-the-free-wave-establishes)

**Controls and custody.** A silent source, a disabled command, a broken
forward path and a silent wave each scored 50%. An independent reader
reproduced all 16,384 deadline decisions from the 8,192 receiver inboxes, and
a clean replay reproduced the archived results byte for byte. The protocol,
the instruments and the sealed decisions were committed before the issuer
tape was opened.

One accessible channel on one baseline suffices. The long-wave clock and field results of step 1 hold for the instruments they were derived on. |

### What the step means for the search

The question moves from the update rule to the access rule. The
experimenters supplied the permission to send through the relay. For an
autonomous repair law the decisive question is whether an inhabitant can gain
that permission through its own dynamics, including by triggering or delaying
an ordinary calibration message. A candidate that claims one causal speed has
to specify who can trigger calibration, routing and collection after a fresh
choice. The [plan](PLAN.md#communication-access) carries this as a
constraint on every candidate.

The same question applies to polynomial-sv. Its declared port service and
scheduler move records between cells. Any such service that an inhabitant can
trigger or delay after a fresh choice is one of its signals, and its speed
enters the inhabitants' causal geometry.

## Step 3. Two clocks on the same journey

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.
[Report](second-clock/report.md) ·
[Comparison figure](second-clock/figures/clock-comparison.svg)

**Question.** Step 1 calibrated one smooth scalar metric on the
reflected-routing law with a single clock. Does that metric predict how much
two differently built clocks slow down on the same journeys, and when a
freshly launched signal returns? This is stage 2 of the
[plan](PLAN.md#experiments-in-dependency-order).

### What was found

**Two clocks, one forecast.** The beat clock is the private-mode doublet of
step 1. The optical clock tells time by a localized pulse circulating around
the third spatial direction, read on a ring of detectors; it has no private
beat and no external phase reference. The same eight-slot update moves both,
and no update multiplies a clock rate by a relativistic factor. Velocity came
from independent records of the first clock's envelope, and each clock's rest
advance came from its own stationary calibration. The forecasts were sealed
before either moving clock was read.
[Report section](second-clock/report.md#what-traveled-and-what-was-read)

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| Forward diagonal | 0.58025 | 0.58123 | 0.57961 | 1450.04 → 1453.57 |

All ten clock readings lie inside the registered band of ±0.035 with three

**A small systematic offset.** The optical clock read below the point
forecast on every route, by 0.00065–0.00212, which exceeds its counting

speed into the unchanged metric; the axial residual shrank from −0.002056 to
−0.000247, and the five residuals fell to magnitudes of 0.00025–0.00087.
Finite pulse width and slightly different envelope speeds are the candidate
causes; the experiment did not separate them.

**Two follow-ups.** Local four-aperture readers sampled the beat clock at
20,738 clock appointments and found zero hidden whole turns, with a largest
phase difference of 0.00936 radians from the signed-return reading. Two
stationary laboratories then exchanged roles: A launched and B read, then B
launched and A read, each receiver returning its stamped result to the
sender. All four peer readings passed (A to B: 0.58353 beat, 0.58102 optical
against 0.58025; B to A: 0.58339 and 0.58107 against 0.58026). The records
and scores reproduced byte for byte.

### What the step means for the search

On these prepared journeys the smooth relativistic metric describes two
different clock readouts and a fresh signal to the tested accuracy. The two
clocks share one free propagation law. The gate is symmetric under exchange
of a spatial momentum with the private momentum, and the optical pulse speed
and the private beat probe the same dispersion from two directions, so their
agreement tests one dispersion relation through two independent records. It
does not test whether matter held together by an interaction keeps the same
time.

That is the next material test: a clock sustained by an interaction, with its
own rest calibration, read on the same held-out routes. A clock of that kind
needs two excitations that interact. In the member of the family with zero
encounter phase two excitations propagate independently and bind nothing, so
the test runs on the encounter-phase variants and can separate them. It
joins stage 2 and the two-particle test of stage 3.

## Step 4. A clock held together by an interaction

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.
[Report](interaction-clock/report.md) ·
[Finite-wavelength correction](interaction-clock/correction.md) ·
[Comparison figure](interaction-clock/figures/comparison.svg)

**Question.** The clocks of steps 1 to 3 were free excitations of one
propagation law. Does a clock held together by an interaction keep the same
time on the same journeys, and can inhabitants form such a clock from
separated constituents?

### What was found

**The interaction laws.** Two distinguishable excitations make the eight-slot
moves of the candidate. When two matching routing slots occupy the same
address, a contact operation mixes them into other matched pairs of slots,
and both constituents take the same outgoing step. Their separation stays
zero while the pair travels. One constituent carries two internal
alternatives, the rails, whose relative phase is the clock hand. In the
**motion-coupled** law the two rails have slightly different contact
reflections, π/16 and π/16 + π/2048. In the **spectator** law both rails have
the same reflection and an extra relative phase advances at every contact.
The two couplings are design inputs, fixed across all sizes, widths and
directions.

**The motion-coupled pair keeps proper time.** Its mobile phase obeys
$\cos E_\phi(k) = a(k)\cos(\phi/2)$, with $a(k)$ the mean of the four
cosines, so $E_\phi^2 \simeq (|k|^2 + \phi^2)/4$ at small phases and the
splitting between the rails falls with motion by the relativistic factor.
The forecasts were sealed from independent speed and rest-clock calibrations.

| Route and preparation | Forecast | Motion-coupled reading | Spectator reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Axial | 0.71408 | 0.70862 | 1.00002 |
| Forward diagonal | 0.58463 | 0.57744 | 0.99998 |
| Reversed diagonal, translated laboratory | 0.58457 | 0.57740 | 0.99991 |
| Mixed diagonal, wider momentum band | 0.58589 | 0.57659 | 0.99995 |
| Same, twice the circumference | 0.58352 | 0.57674 | 0.99997 |

All five motion-coupled readings lie inside the registered band of ±0.035.
The spectator clock reads one on every route: its phase counts contacts.
[Report section](interaction-clock/report.md#what-the-receiver-measured)

**The residual is finite-wavelength physics.** Every motion-coupled reading
fell below its smooth forecast, by 0.00546–0.00930, beyond counting
uncertainty. On the forward diagonal the smooth forecast is 0.584628, the
finite law at the packet's central wavelength gives 0.576056, and the finite
law propagated over the full packet and the receiver's actual signed detector
gives 0.577423. The observer recorded 0.577436 ± 0.000066, 0.19 counting
standard errors away. That comparison was retrospective. Six further flights
at other central wavelengths and packet widths had all three predictions
sealed before their readings were opened. The central-mode correction
improved all six, and the full packet-and-detector calculation predicted
every reading within 1.32 counting standard errors. The RMS errors were
0.01051 for the smooth forecast, 0.001218 for the central mode and 0.0000556
for the full calculation. The correction changes with the preparation:
widening the momentum band at order 16 changed the reading by
−0.001263 ± 0.000099, the full calculation predicted −0.001338 and the
central mode predicts zero.
[Correction](interaction-clock/correction.md#six-new-forecasts-before-opening-the-clock)

A finite-mode identity fixes the sign. With $r = 2\partial_\phi E$,
$v = \nabla_k E$ and $c = 1/2$,

$$1 - |v|^2/c^2 - r^2 = \frac{\operatorname{Var}_i(\cos k_i) + \sin^2(\phi/2)\,\operatorname{mean}_i(\sin^2 k_i)}{\sin^2 E} \ge 0,$$

so at every nonzero finite momentum the law's clock runs slower than the
smooth formula.

**Binding holds, formation fails.** The sharp matched pair stayed together in
all 262,144 trials at every measured epoch through 16 services under both
active laws. With the contact switched off, 89,932 of 262,144 stayed matched
after one step (34.31%; the exact probability is 11/32). Initially separated
excitations met in 82,663 first-step coincidences and were captured zero
times. The reason is exact: the matched coincident subspace and its
orthogonal complement are each invariant under the update and its inverse,
so a pair launched with no overlap with the protected subspace never enters
it.
[Report section](interaction-clock/report.md#binding-formation-and-collisions)

**A scalar contact phase fails as a clock.** An on-site phase of $i$ whenever
the two constituents coincide on an axial guide, with no rule that keeps them
together, gave clock ratios 0.87214, 0.52508 and 0.82583 on three prepared
pairs against forecasts of 0.97574, 0.96782 and 0.97313. The fourth pair lost
localization and clock contrast. Initially separated packets passed through
one another without sustained capture.

**The encounter phase is observable.** In a two-particle interferometer, two
coherent paths meet a second excitation a different number of times and
reunite at one detector. A blind reader identified all four scalar contact
phases, 0, +π/2, π and −π/2, in both repeats. Single-particle records are
identical for all four.

### What the step means for the search

Binding and a stable rest line are no selection rule for a clock. A law has
to tie the internal beat to the measured motion, as the motion-coupled rails
do and the spectator rails do not, and the step-1 lesson about factorized
clocks holds for bound matter as well. The smooth metric describes the bound
clock at its declared accuracy, and the finite law predicts the deviations
from it to 0.0000556 in RMS. Its sign is fixed: the law's clocks run slower
than the smooth formula at every finite momentum. Precise clocks of
inhabitants would read that deviation at short wavelengths.

The decisive obstacle is formation. Under a unitary law whose bound subspace
is invariant, scattering never produces the bound state, because nothing
carries away the difference between the separated and the bound
configuration. A law that lets inhabitants form a clock needs an encounter
that emits a separate, distinguishable record and leaves a persistent
travelling pair behind. Formation, binding and clock operation then have to
happen on the same executable.

## Step 5. A pair formed in an encounter

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.
[Report](formation-clock/report.md)

**Question.** Step 4 found that the compact pair cannot form from separated
constituents, because its bound subspace is invariant. Does a law that
exports a separate record at the encounter form the pair, and does the formed
pair carry a clock along one measurable path?

### What was found

**The successor law.** Every junction gets its own periodic wire of neutral
modes. When the two constituents meet head-on at a junction, the vertex
rotates that collision channel, at a conversion angle of π/4, into a matched
pair in the equal-slot state plus one marker on the junction's wire. The
marker moves away one link per update, and an incoming marker would reverse
the transition. Both clock rails see the same vertex. After the marker has
left, the pair evolves under the contact law of step 4. The marker is an
information carrier supplied by the law; no energy is assigned to it.

**Formation works.** At one aimed crossing, 8,145 of 16,384 launches gave a
first-encounter marker, and in every marked trial the two constituents were
detected together at the assigned time, at four terminal ages from 0 to 8
updates after the capture. Zero marked trials separated. With the vertex
disabled, or one beam offset, no first-encounter marker appeared. A receiver
that deliberately displaced one constituent reported all 8,230 marked pairs
as separated, so the detector could report either outcome.
[Report section](formation-clock/report.md#what-the-observers-recorded)

**The formed pair does not travel as one clock.** The pair's internal dial
gave the forecast local interference signal: all five coherent phase
readings, the two stationary references included, agreed with their sealed
forecasts within one counting standard error. The single-traveller test
required 90% of home arrivals in the intended winding. The records gave
81.28% on the axial route, 77.18% on the diagonal and 77.16% on the reflected
diagonal, and the independent position survey found the centre of the cohort
at 0.034 and 0.035 laps where one lap was enrolled. On the forward diagonal
the observers counted 496,041 forward, 132,953 reverse and 13,699
zero-winding arrivals. The stationary dial also advanced at a changing rate:
0.07440 ± 0.00128 rad at 736 ticks and 0.04692 ± 0.00074 rad at 896 ticks in
independent trials. No moving/rest ratio is supported by these records.

**Why it fails.** A second receiver combined the four approach layers
coherently and selected the component with the same phase on every layer. It
counted 1,977,622 forward and 530,047 backward diagonal arrivals, and
more than 99.99% of all backward arrivals came through that smooth component.
Filtering out short longitudinal wavelengths would leave 21.14% travelling
backwards. The finite gate accounts for it: the vertex emits an equal sum
over routing slots, whose one-step amplitude $a e^{-i\varphi/2}$ splits into
the two unit phases $e^{\mp iE}$ with weights
$(1 \pm a\sin(\varphi/2)/\sin E)/2$, and the two phases travel in opposite
directions. For the central diagonal mode the forward weights are 78.73% and
78.88% for the two rails.
[Report section](formation-clock/report.md#which-components-went-backwards)

### Standing of the law classes

| Class | Defining feature | Standing | Deciding records |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Compact pair with a reversible capture vertex | A head-on collision converts into a bound pair plus an outgoing marker on a junction wire. | **Survives** for formation; **fails** the single-traveller clock test as prepared | 8,145 of 16,384 launches formed a pair that stayed together; zero marks with the vertex disabled. 77–81% of arrivals in the intended winding against a registered 90%; no completed lap of the cohort's centre. |

| Vertices that emit the pair in the equal-slot state | The output of the capture is a symmetric sum over routing slots. | **Disfavoured** as a source of moving clocks | The equal-slot output splits into two opposite group velocities with weights near 79% and 21%. |

### What the step means for the search

Exporting a distinct record removes the formation obstacle: a law with a
reversible capture vertex and an outgoing marker forms a persistent pair from
separately launched constituents. Binding and carrying a clock along one path
are separate requirements, and this vertex meets the first only. The
direction a formed pair travels is set by the state the vertex emits, and an
equal-slot output populates both directions. A law that forms clocks has to
select one outgoing branch, either through the encounter's own kinematics,
with the marker carrying the recoil, or through a vertex whose output is a
single travelling branch. The capture vertex, its π/4 angle and the marker
wires are supplied; what an unprepared repair history forms is open.

### Handed to the following steps

1. With the law fixed, longer initially separated wave trains that meet over
   several approach planes and times, with earlier emissions and depletion
   tracked. The test: does the encounter select one outgoing direction, does
   the formed clock keep one rest rate across several durations, and does the
   cohort make its measured reunion?
2. The recoil frequency of the marker and both outgoing directions, recorded
   separately.
3. A bounded autonomous candidate that assigns an owner to every send,
   trigger and return operation, with the channel race of step 2 rerun on it.
4. The moving-clock and channel tests on the contrast-memory law and the
   scalar channel of polynomial-sv.

## Step 6. Three laws that form, steer and keep a clock

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.

**Question.** The pair formed in step 5 left its birthplace in both
directions. Can a reversible local change during the pair's first updates
make it travel one way, while the clock it carries keeps one rate?

### What was found

**The family.** A newborn pair carries an age counter, born at zero with
the marker. On each update the pair takes the contact coin and then a
partial hop $e^{is\theta}[\cos\theta\,I - is\sin\theta\,F]$ between the two
ends of every routed edge, with $\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{2} f\big((j+1)/M\big)$
at age $j < M$ and the full hop $F$ once mature. The search registered 26
rules: the two clock couplings of step 4, both orientations $s = \pm 1$,
linear and cosine profiles $f$, opening durations $M$ of 8, 32 and 128
updates, and an abrupt start. The age counter cycles after 8192 updates.

**Three rules survive.** Three schedules passed the directional and clock
tests on an extended feeder preparation, and their 8192-state versions
passed the doubled-size journey. All three use the motion-coupled clock and
the minus orientation.

| Law | Opening | Doubled journey, moving/rest (smooth forecast) | Correct direction among late crossings | Separated after the marker's second return |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Cosine 32 | smooth, over 32 updates | 0.57631 (0.58280) | 95.24% | 1.06% |
| Cosine 128 | smooth, over 128 updates | 0.57861 (0.58367) | 96.56% | 0.53% |
| Linear 128 | constant increment, over 128 updates | 0.57874 (0.58323) | 95.97% | 6.67% |

The direction column comes from ordinary incoming wave trains of width five
that meet through the constituent law, with depletion at every earlier
encounter, counting only trials with a registered crossing; at the late
appointment about one third of the marked cohort crossed. Absorbing the
marker at its first transit preserved every tested pair.

**The whole array reads a steady clock.** Twelve layers sent their four
phase counts to the laboratory, which summed them before reading the angle.
At beam width five the whole-array rates of the cosine-128 rule lay between
4.395 and 4.400 × 10⁻⁴ radians per home tick, where the single birth-layer
detector read 2.99 to 5.24 × 10⁻⁴. All three laws passed the duration check
at both beam widths, with at most 0.21% variation over six durations, and so
did the abrupt comparator. Widening the incoming train from three layers to
five changed the collective rate by about 31%.

**Signals and frames.** A receiver decoded 254 of 256 fresh choices carried
by an ordinary constituent at radar range five ahead of the smooth scalar
deadline, with 262,144 parallel copies per bit. The scalar speed is therefore
no exact bound on every equipped finite experiment; the open question
concerns a restricted low-energy observer with finite resources under
refinement.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosine 32, cosine 128, linear 128 | **Survive** | Directional and clock tests on the feeder preparation; 95–97% correct direction on ordinary trains; whole-array clock stable to 0.21%. |

## Step 7. The matter-wave relation of formed matter

Experiments run on 20 November 2026.
[Replay in the simulator](../../../simulator/README.md)

**Question.** Does matter formed in an encounter obey the relativistic wave
relation $\omega^2 = m^2 + c^2|k|^2$, with the rest line and the speed fixed by
independent records?

### What was found

On cubes of period 64, 128 and 256, independent stationary ensembles set the
rest frequency, echoes and translation interferometers measured each wave
vector, and the scalar standard of the unbound walk set $c$. Fourteen signed
order-three directions per period, six axial and the eight sign reflections
of (1, 2, 2), were enrolled before calibration, and the frequency
predictions were sealed before the moving frequencies were read.

| Period | Measured momentum | Measured frequency | Inside the tolerance, three laws | Changed law |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 64 | 0.29401–0.29480 | 0.176420–0.176763 | 42/42 | 0/14 |
| 128 | 0.14697–0.14764 | 0.122577–0.122687 | 42/42 | 0/14 |
| 256 | 0.07316–0.07413 | 0.104780–0.104858 | 33/42; 42/42 in the precision repeat | 0/14 |

The stationary calibrations read 0.098146–0.098234 radians per home tick.
The changed law, which moves half the spatial weight of the formed vector
into the private slots, keeps the rest gap and the scalar standard and shows
a kinetic deficit of 49.43–50.67%.

The finite law explains the result. On a plane wave the mature step keeps
the span of the formed vector $S$ and its routed image, and there
$\cos\omega = \cos m\,(2w_{\rm private} + 2w_{\rm spatial}\sum_i \cos k_i)$
with $m = \varphi_0/2$, so
$\omega^2 = m^2 + 2w_{\rm spatial}\,m\cot(m)\,|k|^2 + O(|k|^4)$. For equal
weights the coefficient is 0.24919629, against the scalar standard
$c^2 = 0.24935023$: the two speeds differ by 0.06%, and a larger box at fixed
gap does not remove that offset. The simulator reproduces every frequency
from the native step and the closed form to $3\times10^{-15}$.

### Standing of the law classes

| Class | Standing | Deciding records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosine 32, cosine 128, linear 128 | **Survive** | All held-out directions inside the tolerance at every period; the relation is common to the three and selects none. |
| Formed vector with half its spatial weight moved to the private slots | **Excluded** | Kinetic deficit near 50% at every period. |

### What the step means for the search

The long-wave relativistic relation of formed matter follows from the
transport of the candidate laws; the gap is supplied by the contact phase.
A physical continuum limit needs a common scaling of the observers' clocks,
distances and the gap, and the higher spatial gradients depend on direction.
