# 28. What Is a Particle, What Is a Wave, and Why Does It Look Like Both?

An electron lands at one spot. The spots it chooses over many landings are the spots a wave makes bright.

The argument about how one object manages both has been running since 1905, when Einstein published a paper in the *Annalen der Physik* proposing that light is emitted and absorbed in lumps of a definite size, at a moment when every optical instrument in Europe worked because light is a wave. Textbooks have mostly handled the tension by saying the object has two natures.

For the electron, the evidence for the wave started with an accident. In April 1925, in a laboratory on West Street in Manhattan, a bottle of liquid air blew apart next to an electron-scattering experiment Clinton Davisson had been running for years and that Lester Germer had joined in 1924. The bottle stood near a vacuum tube containing a nickel target. The target was hot. Air rushed in and the nickel oxidized.

They spent the following weeks baking the oxide off, heating the target for long stretches to drive the damage out, and then started the run again. The experiment was simple to describe: fire electrons at the nickel, count how many come back at each angle. Before the accident the counts had traced a smooth hill. Afterward the same apparatus gave sharp peaks at particular angles and very little in between.

Nothing about the electron gun had changed. What had changed was the metal. The prolonged heating had recrystallized the target, and where the nickel had been a mass of tiny crystals pointing every way, it had become a handful of large ones. A handful of large crystals is a diffraction grating. They rebuilt the apparatus around a single crystal. The strongest peak came at an accelerating voltage of 54 volts and an angle of 50 degrees, and the atomic spacing of nickel converts that angle into a wavelength of 0.165 nanometers, against the 0.167 nanometers Louis de Broglie's rule of 1924 assigns an electron of that speed.

Nickel settled one half of the matter. The other half was put on film in 1989 at the Hitachi Advanced Research Laboratory, where Akira Tonomura's group sent electrons past a charged wire that bends two paths back together, the equivalent of a two-slit arrangement. They ran the beam so faint that the instrument held one electron at a time. Each one landed as a single dot at a definite place on a counting detector. It took about twenty minutes of accumulating dots for the stripes to appear.

The question the nickel put, and the charged wire put again sixty-four years later, is how a spreading pattern and a single count can be one thing happening.

## One word, two objects

A value assigned at every place. That is one of the two things physics calls a field, and chapter twenty-six built one out of a table of who touches whom: a number at every port, falling off with hop distance in four exact fractions, and no space anywhere in the calculation.

The other one starts with a room.

Take a region and ask what can be answered inside it without leaving. Chapter nineteen made a question into an operation. A collection of operations you can compose, scale and add is an algebra. So attach to each region the algebra of everything answerable there, with its composition rule. Regions that contain other regions carry longer lists, because anything answerable in the small one is answerable in the large one. The small algebra sits inside the big one exactly.

The list is longer than it sounds. Everything a thermometer, a photographic plate, a photomultiplier and a wristwatch inside that region could between them establish is on it, along with every combination of those readings, and nothing whose answer would require somebody to go outside and look.

Take two regions far enough apart that no light ray leaves one and reaches the other while the questions are being put. Every operation on one side then commutes with every operation on the other. Alice asks and Bob asks, in either order, and the two orders give the same answers, so there is no fact about who went first for anybody to establish. Chapter twenty used that as a step in the Bell argument, where it is the statement that neither of them can signal to the other.

An assignment of an algebra to every region, with those two properties, is what the word field names in quantum field theory. There is no arrow at a point anywhere in it.

On the carrier that structure is exact. Two observer regions with no seam in common sit as exactly the two tensor factors of the joint description. Chapter twenty built the tensor product out of a deck of cards: four suits, thirteen ranks, dimensions multiplying, and most joint descriptions refusing to come apart into a fact about the suit and a fact about the rank. Two separated regions are the suit and the rank. Each one's questions are questions about its own factor, the two lists commute across the gap, and the pair's description is assembled by the operation that multiplies dimensions.

Everything in that construction is countable. The regions are finite collections of patches, the lists of questions are finite, and the commuting across the gap is arithmetic on the wiring rather than the limit of a sequence of approximations, so a continuum is never called for and never used. Chapter twenty-six's field is a value at every place. This one is a list of questions per region. Physics runs both under one word. The second is the one the Standard Model is written in.

Chapter twenty-two gave the second kind its quanta and its clock. Many copies of one thermal mode, stepped by an observer's count of repairs, converge to a field whose two halves fail to commute by exactly the amount a position and a momentum do, and the region's own flow acts on that field as time translation.

## Why the iron bar does not slosh

Warm one end of an iron bar and the warmth crawls along and stays, arriving late at the far end and never overshooting it. Chapter nine's repair move is that same equation written on a graph: my reading minus the average of my neighbors', a sixtieth at a time. Diffusion by itself produces no waves at all.

What produces a wave is a second quantity that the first one feeds. A pendulum has two: displacement sets the rate at which velocity changes, and velocity sets the rate at which displacement changes. The pair circles instead of settling. Repair has only the first. It settles, and that is all it does. The second quantity comes from an observer. Chapter twenty-two gave a region a small quantum memory, stepped once at every touch, and that memory carries the pair: an amplitude and its rate of change, each feeding the other, in the count the incident clock keeps. The repair supplies the stiffness and the memory supplies the swing.

Fix a direction of travel and a wavelength, and the wavelength fixes a wavenumber: how many crests fit into a set length. The transport between carriers has a redundancy, the relabelings of chapter twelve that change no comparison, and once it is removed the amplitudes that propagate are perpendicular to the direction of travel. In three-dimensional space the perpendicular directions form a plane. Two of them. Two is a rank in chapter fifteen's sense, the number of independent directions a table of numbers contains.

Write A for the amplitude in that perpendicular plane, and write the Greek letter lambda, capitalized, for the stiffness the wiring supplies at a given wavenumber k, which is the number saying how hard the carrier pushes back on a mismatch of that wavelength. Two quantities feeding each other give one equation.

$$A'' + \Lambda(k)\,A = 0$$

The double prime is the rate of change of the rate of change, counted on the observer's clock. That is the equation of a mass on a spring with the wiring supplying the spring, and its solutions oscillate at a frequency whose square is the stiffness.

The stiffness comes from the complete thirty-seam operator. At long wavelengths it approaches the square of the wavenumber. Its first correction subtracts a twentieth of the fourth power times the square of the seam spacing, and an exact bound holds across the full operator: the difference from the square of the wavenumber never exceeds that correction in magnitude. At zero wavenumber the stiffness is exactly zero, so the frequency there is exactly zero. That exact zero, with the stiffness rising as the square of the wavenumber above it, gives the long waves one limiting speed, the speed of chapter twenty-two's first response.

Shrink the seam spacing and the paired fields converge to Maxwell's equations, with an error proportional to the square of the spacing for smooth starting fields. Every field of finite total energy converges, smooth or not. A current can be included, and conservation of its charge preserves Gauss's law. A light wave is the transport between carriers rippling, read on an observer's clock.

An observer can read such a field without keeping a spare copy. It remembers a port's value, averages that port with an adjacent value, and reads the average. Twice the average minus the remembered value recovers the hidden value. Writing the recovered pair back lets the next measurement start from the same state. Applied to forty-two potential coordinates, this procedure produces the records that drive the field forward, and the fields reconstructed from those records satisfy the same action, charged paths included.

The recorded potentials also fill the solid made by joining the carrier's twenty triangular faces to its center, which gives the surface description a volume. Magnetic flux stays closed and Faraday's induction holds inside it. The volume carries its own evolution: a field that starts out satisfying Gauss's law keeps satisfying it, and an observer reading and restoring fifty-five potential coordinates carries a complete history of the volume whose action stays stationary. The same action has thirty independent radiative modes. Quantized by the usual rule they become thirty quantum oscillators, whose occupation numbers are the photons of that volume.

Matter rides on the same records. A charged field placed in the volume changes with the electromagnetic potential exactly as gauge symmetry requires, and both follow one stationary action with positive conserved energy. Start opposite charges near the center and near the boundary, let five patches record, probe and restore the state, and drive each step from their decoded records. Replaying the records reproduces all five positions and five velocities at eighty-one checkpoints to within $1.0001\times10^{-10}$ of the exact solution, which interval arithmetic encloses throughout. A field that interacts with itself through a quartic term converges to the continuum nonlinear wave equation as the solid is subdivided, and the same rate holds for charged matter in a uniform magnetic field.

The moving configuration keeps time on its own as well. Its kinetic metric and conserved energy assign a duration to every stretch of motion, whatever labels were used to order the samples, so decoded records yield the elapsed time without a single timestamp. The interacting system has its quantum description too. Removing the redundant gauge coordinates leaves a positive kinetic metric whose Laplace-Beltrami operator, the curved-space version of the ordinary Laplacian, fixes a Hamiltonian with exactly one self-adjoint extension, and neutral states evolve under it unitarily.

## Two questions, two orders

Amplitudes can cancel and probabilities cannot. That is chapter nineteen's difference and the reason there are dark bands at all. Cancellation needs a sign. A pattern that slides as one path is lengthened needs more than a sign, because it needs something that turns continuously, and the amplitude turns: it carries an angle, and the arithmetic of turning angles is the arithmetic of the square root of minus one.

The suspicion that the turning is bookkeeping is reasonable enough. Turning arrows multiply conveniently, and there might be real numbers underneath doing the work.

The carrier settles it on a two-by-two table.

The machine hands over two yes-or-no questions from behind one public reading, both real, both built out of labels an actual run produced, and the two fail to commute. It hands over two states as well, and every real question in the entire web scores those two states identically: put any of them to either state and the same odds come back. The states are different states. Nothing real tells them apart.

Build the missing question out of what is there. Start with the even split, which is the do-nothing operation halved and which as a description gives fifty-fifty odds to every question in sight. Take the two questions, call them P and Q, do them in one order and then in the other, and subtract. That is chapter nineteen's commutator. Multiply that difference by the square root of minus one and by two divided by the square root of three. Subtract the result from the even split, and call what comes out Y.

$$Y = \frac{1}{2}I - \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\,i\,(QP - PQ)$$

I is the operation that does nothing, P and Q are the two real questions the carrier supplied, and i is the square root of minus one.

Y squares to itself, making it a question in chapter nineteen's sense, with a yes, a no and no third possibility. Put it to the two states that nothing real could separate. It answers yes on one of them with certainty and no on the other with certainty.

The coefficient was not fitted to make that happen. The commutator of those two particular questions has off-diagonal entry exactly the square root of three over four, and two over the square root of three scales that entry to exactly one half, which is the value that makes the result square to itself. Two real questions and one subtraction produce the direction that neither of them held.

And the direction is unreachable from below. Close the real questions under everything real processing does to them: average them, scale them, take complements, push them through any real operation of the kind a physical process performs on a description. Every element of that closure hands the two states the same number, and Y hands them different ones, so no amount of coarse-graining can manufacture it.

Three questions then fix everything. The record question the run supplied, its rotated partner, and Y between them pin down any description of a two-outcome system whose odds add up to one, leaving nothing about it undetermined.

The direction that was missing is the phase. Ernst Stueckelberg tried the other route in 1960, writing quantum theory in a real space in *Helvetica Physica Acta*, and made it work by adding an operator that squares to minus one and commutes with every observable, which is the imaginary unit under another name.

## The leftover, named

Twelve observers wired into an icosahedron, one record out of thirty flipped, two thousand and forty-eight starting states and sixteen schedules apiece: chapter six ran all of it. Every run converged. Every run left exactly one unit of disagreement sitting in the system, movable to any location and removable from none, with no edge of the world to push it off.

That leftover is the simplest **topological defect**, and a particle is the same object in the full world.

No repair removes it because it does not live at any place. Every seam is satisfied or can be made so. What cannot be satisfied is a loop: chain the translations round it and the product comes back different from what went in. A repair works on one seam and changes the loop only by moving the discrepancy to the next seam along, the way pressing a wrinkle flat in a carpet pushes it a little further across the floor. The discrepancy can be moved anywhere and taken out nowhere, because taking it out would need one move touching every seam of the loop at once, and no repair is that large.

Now the full world. Each carrier holds a frame: the three slow directions of chapter fifteen, and the twelve dials of chapter twenty-six. Neighboring carriers compare frames across their seams, and the comparison is a **transport**, the turn that carries one carrier's frame onto its neighbor's. Where repair has finished, transport is flat: any closed chain of transports multiplies out to doing nothing. A defect is a place where that fails and cannot be made to stop failing. It has a small **core**, surroundings settled as completely as repair can settle them, and a boundary around the core whose transports multiply to a definite twist. Every seam outside the core can be brought into agreement, and the twist stays, because it belongs to the boundary as a whole and no single seam holds it. Chapter six's leftover is the case in which the twist is a single sign.

Three properties follow, and they are the three that every particle has.

The twist is conserved. Shrink the boundary or deform it without crossing the core and it reads the same twist, since each deformation is a repair passing the discrepancy along. Two defects brought together combine their twists. A defect and its mirror twist cancel, the knot comes undone and its cost is released, which is annihilation. Charges add, and particles are made and destroyed in matched pairs, for that one reason.

The twist has two parts. The transport turns both things a carrier holds, its dials and its frame. The dial part of the twist is the defect's **charge**: how far the hypercharge dial turns on the way round, and which of the three color slots and two weak slots the twist runs through. The frame part is its spin. Chapter twelve named the residue in a loop charge. The knot is where that residue sits.

The core costs something. The surroundings have to be kept settled against a core that keeps disagreeing. Every repair that touches the core finds a mismatch, resolves it, and finds the knot one seam over, so repair works at the core at every tick and gets nowhere with it. Chapter thirty-five called that a standing cost. Read on the region's own clock, the excess repair the core demands above the settled reference is the defect's **rest energy**, and that is mass.

## A full turn and a sign

The frame part of a twist is a rotation of space, and quantum amplitudes see rotations twice over. Turn a quantum state through a full circle and it can come back with its sign flipped. Only two full turns are guaranteed to restore it. Paul Dirac liked to show this with a pair of scissors tied to a chair by strings: one full turn of the scissors tangles the strings beyond untangling, and a second full turn in the same direction leaves a tangle that can be undone without turning the scissors at all. Each of the sixty rotations of the carrier therefore has two quantum versions, differing by that sign, a hundred and twenty in all.

A defect whose frame twist sits on the signed side comes back negated after a full turn. That is **spin** one half. A defect whose twist sits on the plain side comes back unchanged, and its spin is a whole number.

Now swap two identical defects. Follow the boundaries around them during the swap and they end up wound exactly as if one defect had been turned through a full circle while the other stayed put. A belt held at both ends shows it: carry one buckle round the other and back to the swap position, and the belt picks up one full twist. So the sign a full turn gives is the sign a swap gives. Swapping two spin-one-half defects negates the state, and that has a consequence nobody can avoid: two of them in the same mode would have an amplitude equal to its own negative, which is zero. That is Pauli's exclusion principle, and it is why atoms have shells and tables are solid. Whole-number spins swap without a sign and pile into one mode freely, which is why a laser beam can be bright. David Finkelstein and Julio Rubinstein proved in 1968 that knotted field configurations obey exactly this connection between spin and swapping. Here every particle is such a knot, so the connection holds for all of them.

The many-body description writes the same thing in operators. Creating two spin-one-half defects in one order gives minus what the other order gives, and creating the same one twice gives nothing. The exclusion rule is the arithmetic of the knot.

## Why the list is the list

A twist is a turn of the dials, and chapter twenty-seven found six combinations of dial turns that nothing in the world can detect. A defect whose twist failed to come home under those six would make a turn that does nothing do something. It cannot be tied. The knots the transport admits are exactly the fifteen states of chapter twenty-seven, each with its mirror twist, which is its antiparticle. Quarks are the knots whose twist runs through color slots, so they carry charges in thirds. Leptons are the knots whose twist misses color, so they carry whole charges. The roster is the list of twists that come home.

Handedness enters through the weak slots. Wu's cobalt showed that the weak force grips only one handedness, and on the knots this is the statement that the left-handed versions carry a twist through the weak slots while their right-handed partners carry none. A particle at rest has no handedness to keep, so its core has to be both at once, trading weak charge back and forth between its two halves at every tick. Something has to take that charge and hand it back. That something is the subject of the section after next.

Families are directions. The three directions of chapter twenty-seven's family band are three places a core can sit with the same twist, so the three families carry identical charges. Their masses differ, and by enormous factors, because a mass is a persistence count. A core's mismatch survives some number of ticks before repair returns to it, and a lighter defect is one whose core lasts longer between repairs. Survival over many ticks is a product of many fractions, so a small difference in how a core sits turns into a large difference in cost. The top quark outweighs the electron by a factor of more than three hundred thousand, and the two are counted in the same sixths of a charge.

Some knots can share their twist out among lighter ones. The total twist is conserved and the cost of the heavier core is released as motion. A muon does this in two microseconds, becoming an electron and two neutrinos. Those are the unstable particles, metastable knots with a lighter way to hold the same twist. Others bind. Three quark knots whose color twists add to nothing seen from far away are held together by ripples of the eight color dials, and that bundle is a proton.

## Ripples with nothing to keep

Not everything a detector counts is a knot. The transport can ripple without tying anything, and a ripple has no core. Nothing in it disagrees with its surroundings at any tick, so the repair has nothing to maintain, and a ripple has no rest energy at all. That is what a massless particle is: a wave of the transport whose quanta cost nothing to keep in existence.

Light is the ripple of the central dial. It is the wave of the earlier section of this chapter: two polarizations across its direction of travel, a stiffness that vanishes at the longest wavelengths, and one speed, the first-response speed of chapter twenty-two. Starlight that set out before the Earth formed arrives at that speed for the same reason that distance is the first answer to a disturbance. The photon is massless exactly, with no small number in a denominator holding it there, because there is no core for a small cost to be the cost of.

The eight color dials ripple too. Their ripples carry color themselves, so they grip each other, because the three and the eight do not commute, and their brackets put a term into the curvature that is quadratic in the field itself. A ripple that grips its own kind cannot spread out freely. The color field between two quark knots stays in a tube instead of thinning, the cost of the tube grows with its length, and pulling a quark out of a proton makes new knots out of the tube's cost long before the quark gets free. The gluons are massless ripples that are never seen alone. Collider detectors see them as sprays of new particles, jets, aligned with the direction the ripple left in.

A ripple in the geometry itself is gravity's wave, the settled record count of chapter twenty-five shaking. LIGO heard the first one on 14 September 2015, from two black holes merging over a billion light years away, and it arrived at the speed of light.

## The reference, read as a field

Every knot is maintained against something: the settled state its surroundings return to once repair has done its work. Call it the **reference**. It is chapter eleven's settled holding read as a background, the least informative state compatible with everything observers can check.

In an electroweak effective theory with a scalar doublet, a chosen nonzero scalar background distinguishes a weak direction. The Yukawa interaction couples left- and right-handed matter through that doublet. Interpreting the scalar as an observer reference is a proposed source connection; the existence of stable records does not by itself prove that the source selects this background. Its selection, coefficients and physical current readout must be calculated in the declared model.

Its size is the reference's stiffness, the amount of weak charge it can absorb and return per unit of volume, read in energy. The strength of the weak interaction fixes it at about 246 billion electron-volts. Everything else about electroweak physics is this reference doing what a pointing background does.

Three of the four electroweak dials turn the reference when they ripple. Their ripples have to drag it along, which is a standing cost, so they acquire mass: the two W particles and the Z, the last weighing in at 91.1876 billion electron-volts. One combination of the central dial and a weak dial leaves the reference where it is, and that combination is light. The polarization bookkeeping closes exactly. Three massless ripples with two polarizations each, plus three directions in which the reference can be turned, become three massive ripples with three polarizations each. Nine before and nine after. The directions in which the reference turns were absorbed as the third polarizations.

A ripple in the reference's size is a different thing. It turns nothing and changes how stiff the background is, and its quantum is the **Higgs boson**, spin zero because a size has no direction. The two collaborations at CERN announced it on 4 July 2012, at about 125 billion electron-volts.

In that effective description, the scalar background and Yukawa coefficients determine fermion masses. Repair persistence suggests a way to interpret those coefficients, but does not calculate the hierarchy. Neutrino masses need an additional specified mechanism; the absence of a right-handed state from the minimal roster does not fix their masses or prove a double-insertion suppression.

## One cone for every knot

Observers moving relative to each other do not agree about lengths or durations. They agree about the cone. Chapter seventeen got it from the angle-preserving maps of the sky, and chapter twenty-two got it as the first response of a wave, and in both places it has one speed for every observer and every pair of regions.

A candidate excitation's energy and motion must be read on the same clock. If its physical transformations and dispersion are Lorentz covariant, its rest energy obeys $E_0=mc^2$, and a stable massive particle has $E^2=c^2|p|^2+m^2c^4$. A shared causal cone alone does not prove this dispersion: lattice corrections and interactions can preserve a propagation bound while changing the energy–momentum relation.

The finite constructions therefore test the relation rather than assume it. One original Wilson source has a free low-momentum coefficient different from the photon coefficient; a distinct orthogonal regulator repairs that free mismatch. Complete interacting local records can be controlled at fixed spacing, but the interacting relativistic limit requires further estimates on that same source and state. The spherical Lorentz correspondence supplies useful symmetry mathematics; it does not transfer a dynamical continuum theorem to every carrier program.

## When the record gets written

Questions that refuse to commute, and a center where the answers commute, survive being read twice, and survive being copied to a neighbor: chapter nineteen supplied both halves of the answer and left them lying apart. Pinching carries a question from the first into the second by deleting every comparison that only made sense between two bins.

Between commits, no record exists of which route a pattern took. What exists is the spreading mismatch of the wave, with a phase and a frequency. Every question about it lives in the part of the algebra that refuses to commute. Amplitudes for different routes add, opposite amplitudes cancel, and cancellation is what a dark band is made of.

At a commit a record is written. A record lives in the center. Everything there commutes with everything else, the answers survive reading and copying, and they are ordinary classical numbers: this one, here, count of one.

The electron crossed the tube on West Street with no record written anywhere of which crystal plane it scattered from, and arrived at the collector where a record was written and a count went up by one. The pattern of counts is the wave. Each count is the particle. Nothing about the electron differed between the crossing and the arrival except how much of it got written down.

A which-route apparatus correlates the paths with detector states. Perfectly distinguishable detector states remove interference from the unconditioned screen record. Partial distinguishability reduces visibility only partly: for balanced paths and pure detector markers, the visibility is their overlap magnitude. An inconclusive detector outcome need not leave the original interference intact. The complete joint record, including unsuccessful readouts, is what the quantum source must predict.

A repair is such an instrument. Chapter nine's experiment puts an empty seam and a seam holding a single unit into superposition and lets one repair act. A repair that writes down only how many units crossed leaves most of the comparison standing. One that also writes the parity of the seam deletes it, and the bands go flat at three eighths. How much contrast the bands keep is a reading of what the repair writes.

Duality is a statement about when the record is written. The older question, which of two natures the thing has, carries an unstated variable, and the variable is the depth at which the reading is being taken. Fix the depth and the ambiguity goes. Nothing whatever happens to the electron at the plate. A record gets written, and a record holds a number.

## Observations first, fields after

Textbooks tell the story in one order. First there is the field, filling space with values whether or not anybody looks, and then a measurement samples it. This world runs the other way round.

First come observations: what each observer reads at its own boundary, a detector element at the collector on West Street, a grain on Tonomura's plate, the touches an incident clock counts. Where observers overlap their readings disagree, and the disagreements are repaired, seam by seam. A reading becomes a record only once it has stopped being revised, which is chapter ten's rule that agreement comes first and memory second. A record that survives every comparison it can be put to is a **fact**. Only then is there a field: the shortest description that predicts every agreed record, and what any further intervention would write. The value of a field at a place is what every detector there agrees on once the repairing is done. Before that there is nothing for it to be.

So the stripes on Tonomura's film are the field, assembled out of twenty minutes of dots that every reader of the film agrees about. The electron's wave was recovered from those records after the observers agreed. It was not sitting in the tube waiting to be sampled. Facts are emergent in the same sense as the gas law's pressure: real enough to lift a piston, and a property of settled records rather than of anything underneath them.

## The term that cannot be written

In 1919 Ernest Rutherford published four papers in the *London, Edinburgh and Dublin Philosophical Magazine* on what happens when alpha particles are fired into light gases. In nitrogen he saw scintillations at distances no alpha particle could travel, identified them as hydrogen nuclei knocked out of the nitrogen nucleus, and gave the thing its name the following year. Proton.

Chapter one told the other half. Grand unification put the eight, the three and the one inside a single simple symmetry, and the fractional charges and the family structure fell into place. The difficulty came with the carriers. Building three blocks into one simple group forces extra directions into the list of carriers. The smallest group with room for all three holds twenty-four carrier directions, which is the twelve the world has and twelve more. Every one of the extra twelve carries color and weak charge at once. A carrier of that kind couples a quark directly to a lepton, so a proton comes apart into a positron and a neutral pion.

The retained gauge construction has separate color, weak and central factors. Its gauge Lie algebra has no generator that turns a quark representation into a lepton representation. This excludes that gauge-boson mechanism within the stated field content. It does not exclude every larger construction or every baryon-number-violating interaction: higher-order operators and additional fields require their own analysis. A dimension count alone would not prove the Lie brackets or this representation statement.

The companion problem goes the same way. A monopole of the unified kind is a knot in the field configuration, formed when a large symmetry is squeezed down to a smaller one, and whether such knots exist is settled by asking which loops in the space of ways to do the squeezing can be shrunk to a point without leaving that space. Whether a loop can be shrunk to nothing is a homotopy question. The loops that refuse are what count the knots. The carrier emits the product of blocks from the first moment there is a group at all, with no earlier stage at which it was simple, so the squeezing never happens and the loops are never drawn.

The fifty thousand tons of ultrapure water under the mountain in Japan have been running since April 1996. A search through 450 kiloton-years of data, which is the mass of the water multiplied by the years it was under observation, turned up nothing of the right shape. The floor that search puts under the decay sits at 2.4 times ten to the thirty-fourth years. The retained product algebra excludes that gauge carrier within its declared field content. A full proton-lifetime prediction additionally needs the physical baryon states and all relevant interactions.

Five entries, and not one of them says how hard anything pulls. Twelve directions, thirty seams and a census of what can hold together leave the photon on the list and the forty-five states of matter on the list, and say nothing whatever about the grip of the first on the second. That grip is a number near one part in 137. It sets the size of every atom in your hand.
