## How to read the claims

The book follows a proposed complete history whose local interactions carry its computation. No external computer and no extra time play that history. Events, changing local states and records belong to the structure itself. A quantum history assigns no simultaneous outcomes to incompatible measurements nobody made.

Closure is a condition on the whole history. It says nothing about its inhabitants approaching a final instantaneous state. Space, durations, fields and matter are effective descriptions of the records those inhabitants can compare. The chapters explain that idea through concrete observer constructions, including repair, memory and complete quantum measurements.

The main executable candidate, Interval Echo, places small observers on the ports of twelve-port cells and gives each observer a clock it can carry and read. Neighbours compare what they hold through a private memory on the seam they share, and the laws under test act on those registers. Its creator specifies the law, its coupling and the preparation before a run. Its simulated observers need not invent those laws. Independent repair examples illustrate how finite observers retain records and recover rules. Each example has its own mathematical domain, and its results transfer to the model only when a proof connects the two.

The physical proposal is that a complete construction reproduces the observations of our world. The papers distinguish exact identities, finite calculations, supplied calibration values and physical identifications. A number used to choose a model parameter is an input, even when the model reproduces it accurately. The common continuum, observed particle spectrum and gravity realization require the same-source tests described there. The book explains the proposal from inside its imagined world while those mathematical distinctions remain binding.


Locality forbids one thing: information reaching a local update from anywhere but its own neighborhood. Work counters, conventional orderings and global calculations used to check a construction are tools in the hands of whoever does the checking. There is no universal clock. A repair tick, a causal order, a coordinate, a modular parameter and a clock reading are different things, and moving between any two of them takes a map with a name on it. An observer need not be conscious. The last chapters, on experience and on ethics, take further steps, and they say where each step is taken.
